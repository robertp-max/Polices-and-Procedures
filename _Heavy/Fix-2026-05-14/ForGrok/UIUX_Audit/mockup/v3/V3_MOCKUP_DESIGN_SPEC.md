# V3 Mockup Design Specification

**Project:** CareIndeed Home Health – v3 Glassmorphic Design  
**Date:** May 2026  
**Purpose:** This document is the official design spec to be used when generating individual mockups with any AI image model (Claude, Gemini, Midjourney, etc.).

---

## 1. Core Visual Language (v3)

- Premium, calm, clinical-grade glassmorphism.
- Deep, elegant dark glass as the primary aesthetic (navy/charcoal base).
- Light mode version must feel equally premium (soft, clean, subtle glass).
- Restrained accent palette: Teal (#007970) and warm orange (#E07B2C) used sparingly and strategically.
- Strong emphasis on **depth, floating elevation, and visible borders**.

**Non-negotiable rule:**  
The glassmorphism effect must be clearly visible through **4-sided breathing room** and **floating cards**. Flat, edge-to-edge, or flush designs are not allowed.

---

## 2. Glass & Layering Rules (Strict)

- **Maximum 3 layers**:
  - Layer 0: Dark atmospheric background (deep navy/charcoal)
  - Layer 1: Main working surface (can be a single glass frame **or** multiple floating glass cards)
  - Layer 2: Elevated cards, panels, drawers, modals

- **Each glass card must look like it is floating** over the background with clearly visible borders on all four sides.
- **No big main container** that wraps the entire content area. Individual elements should read as separate floating glass cards whenever possible.
- Cards must have:
  - Subtle frosted translucency
  - Soft luminous edges / inner glow
  - Clear separation from the background and from each other
- Do **not** use heavy decorative inner blur layers inside the main glass.

---

## 3. 4-Sided Border / Breathing Room (Critical)

- Every mockup **must** show clear, visible 4-sided borders around the main content area.
- The dark atmospheric background must be clearly visible framing the glass content.
- This rule applies at both the page level and the individual card level.
- The borders should be obvious enough that the glassmorphism effect is immediately readable.

**Reference behavior:** All strong Top Picks and v2 mocks show this clearly. Do not generate full-bleed or edge-touching layouts.

---

## 4. Navigation Treatment

- The full navigation system must be visible in every mockup:
  - Desktop: Left navigation rail + top bar
  - Mobile: Bottom navigation tabs + safe area
- Navigation should feel integrated with the glass language but not overpower the content.
- The navigation area itself should respect the overall glassmorphic treatment (subtle glass or clean separation).

---

## 5. Card & Element Rules

- Every interactive or content block (KPI cards, list items, panels, sections, etc.) should be treated as its own **independent floating glass card**.
- Cards must have:
  - Visible borders on all sides
  - Clear separation and breathing room from other cards
  - Consistent elevation and glass treatment
- No large single glass container that groups many elements together unless the mock is intentionally using a single-frame layout.

---

## 6. Typography & Readability

- Text must be **clearly readable** at normal viewing size.
- Use strong hierarchy (Montserrat or similar for headings, clean sans for body).
- Avoid tiny text (especially 9–12px labels inside cards).
- Maintain good contrast on both dark and light glass.

---

## 7. Color & Accent Rules

- Dark mode: Deep navy/charcoal glass base + teal + restrained warm orange accents.
- Light mode: Soft, clean glass with subtle hairlines and soft shadows.
- Use semantic colors (green/amber/red) only for status, not decoration.
- Accents should feel intentional and sparse.

---

## 8. Output Requirements (Per Mockup)

- **One screen per image** only. No collages or multi-screen mockups.
- Screen only — no phone bezels, monitor frames, hands, desks, or external environment.
- High resolution with sharp, legible text.
- Both light and dark mode versions should feel cohesive (same layout, spacing, and card language).
- Show the full interface including navigation.

---

## 10. Anti-Patterns (Do Not Do These)

- Full-bleed content with no visible borders
- One giant glass container holding all content
- Cards flush against each other or against the edges
- Heavy decorative inner blur layers
- Tiny unreadable text
- Collages with multiple screens in one image
- Device frames or environmental elements
- New visual dialects that break cohesion
