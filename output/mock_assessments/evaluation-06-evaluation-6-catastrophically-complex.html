AUTONOMIC DYSREFLEXIA — EMERGENCY PROTOCOL

THIS IS A MEDICAL EMERGENCY. Autonomic dysreflexia (AD) in this patient can be FATAL (hypertensive crisis → stroke, seizure, cardiac arrest).

Signs/Symptoms:

Sudden severe headache ("pounding")
BP >150/90 (patient's baseline is ~105/65 — ANY SBP ≥140 is concerning)
Flushing/sweating above the level of injury (face, neck, chest)
Piloerection ("goosebumps") above injury
Nasal congestion
Blurred vision
Bradycardia (may be severe — HR <50)
Anxiety, feeling of "impending doom"

IMMEDIATE ACTIONS (DO NOT WAIT FOR ORDERS):

Sit patient upright immediately (lower legs if possible) to use orthostatic effect to reduce BP.
Loosen all restrictive clothing, abdominal binder, compression stockings.
Check BP immediately — if SBP ≥150, proceed to pharmacologic intervention.
Identify and remove the trigger:
Bladder (most common): Check Foley catheter — is it kinked, clamped, obstructed? Irrigate gently with 30 mL NS. If no flow, replace Foley immediately. If straight cath needed, use lidocaine gel.
Bowel: Check for fecal impaction (apply lidocaine jelly to rectal area first to prevent worsening AD, then gently check). If impacted, disimpact carefully.
Skin: Check for pressure injury, ingrown toenail, tight clothing, strap/belt compression, temperature extremes, tight catheter leg strap.
Other: UTI, DVT, fracture (can occur with osteoporotic SCI bone from minor trauma), abdominal pathology.
If trigger cannot be identified/resolved AND SBP remains ≥150:
Administer nifedipine 10 mg — bite capsule and administer contents via PEG (rapid onset).
OR apply 2% nitroglycerin paste 1 inch to forehead or chest ABOVE the level of injury. Remove when BP normalizes.
Monitor BP every 2-5 minutes until resolved.
Call 911 if:
SBP >180 and not responding to interventions within 5 minutes.
LOC changes.
Seizure activity.
Severe bradycardia (HR <40).
Symptoms worsen despite interventions.
Notify physician — Dr. Choi or on-call provider.
Document — full event documentation including time of onset, symptoms, BP readings, interventions, trigger identified, resolution time.
