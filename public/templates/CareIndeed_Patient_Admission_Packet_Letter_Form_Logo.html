Patient Rights & Responsibilities

Regulatory Basis42 CFR §484.50; California Health & Safety Code §1599.1 et seq.; Title 22 CCR §74743; ACHC accreditation requirements

A. PATIENT BILL OF RIGHTS

As a patient of Care Indeed Home Health, you have the right to:

Dignity & Respect

Be treated with dignity, consideration, respect, and courtesy at all times by all agency personnel. Receive care that is free from discrimination based on race, color, national origin, ancestry, ethnicity, age, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, gender expression, marital status, religion, disability, medical condition, genetic information, source of payment, immigration status, or any other characteristic protected by federal, state, or local law. Have your cultural, psychosocial, spiritual, and personal values, beliefs, and preferences respected and accommodated to the extent possible.

Be addressed by your preferred name and pronouns.

Informed Participation in Care

Be informed in advance about the care and treatment to be furnished, including the disciplines that will provide care, the frequency of visits, and any changes to the plan of care, before the change is made. Participate in the development and periodic revision of your individualized plan of care, including the right to establish goals of care. Be informed of anticipated outcomes of care, including realistic expected goals for improvement and any barriers to achieving those goals. Be informed of all services available from the agency, whether covered by insurance or not.

Receive a written copy of your plan of care, including the names and contact information of all providers involved in your care.

Consent & Refusal

Give informed consent before the start of care and before any new treatment is initiated. Refuse any treatment, service, or medication after being fully informed of the expected consequences of such refusal, without being subjected to discrimination or reprisal. Revoke consent for treatment at any time. Be informed of the consequences of refusing treatment and have that refusal documented in your medical record.

Privacy & Confidentiality

Confidentiality and privacy of all information contained in your clinical record, including the right to approve or refuse the release of your records to any individual outside the agency, except as required by law or authorized under HIPAA. Expect that all communications and records pertaining to your care will be treated as confidential, consistent with HIPAA, CMIA, and applicable California law. Privacy during care provision, including the right to have visits conducted in a private setting within your home. Request restriction of certain disclosures of your PHI as permitted under HIPAA.

Receive care in a manner that maintains your personal privacy and bodily dignity.

Access to Information & Records

Receive a copy of your clinical record upon request, within the time frames required by California law (no later than 15 days for paper, 5 days for electronic format). Be charged no more than the reasonable cost of copying as permitted by California Civil Code §56.11 and Health & Safety Code §123110. Request amendments or corrections to your clinical record. Receive information, upon request, relating to the agency's policies, procedures, and charges for services.

Financial Transparency

Be informed in advance of the extent to which payment may be expected from insurance, public programs, or any other source, and the extent to which you may be responsible for any charges. Be informed, before care is initiated, of any charges for which you may be liable, including charges not covered by Medicare, Medi-Cal, or your insurance. Receive an Advance Beneficiary Notice (ABN) prior to the delivery of any service for which Medicare payment may be denied, explaining your financial liability and your options. Not be balance-billed for amounts above the Medi-Cal reimbursement rate if you are a Medi-Cal beneficiary.

Advance Directives

Be informed, both orally and in writing, of your rights under California law to formulate advance directives, including a Power of Attorney for Health Care, Living Will, and POLST. Have your advance directives followed to the extent permitted by law and agency policy. Receive care regardless of whether you have executed an advance directive.

Safety & Freedom from Harm

Be free from verbal, mental, sexual, and physical abuse, including injuries of unknown source, neglect, exploitation, humiliation, and misappropriation of property. Be free from the use of restraints or seclusion used as a means of coercion, discipline, convenience, or retaliation. Have your person, property, and belongings treated with respect. Receive care in a safe setting.

Grievances & Complaints

Voice grievances and suggest changes in service or staff without fear of reprisal, discrimination, or interruption of services. Have grievances investigated and resolved by the agency. Be informed of the contact information for the state home health hotline, its purpose, and its hours of operation. File complaints with external agencies, including CDPH, ACHC, CMS, and the Medicare BFCC-QIO, without retaliation.

Transfer & Discharge

Be informed of the agency's policies and procedures regarding transfer and discharge from services. Receive advance notice of any planned transfer or discharge, including the reasons for the transfer/discharge and your right to appeal. Not be discharged for exercising your rights, voicing grievances, or filing complaints, or for reasons of discrimination. Receive continuity of care during and after discharge, including referrals to appropriate services and providers.

Additional Rights

Choose your attending physician and be informed of any limitations on that choice. Be informed of the names, professional titles, and qualifications of all individuals providing care. Receive care from personnel who have met applicable federal, state, and professional credentialing and competency requirements. Be told about any research or experimental treatment proposed as part of your care and to refuse participation without penalty. Request and receive information about the agency's ownership and control, affiliations, and any relationships with referral sources.

Be informed of your right to access auxiliary aids and services, including interpreters, at no cost.

B. PATIENT RESPONSIBILITIES

As a patient of Care Indeed Home Health, you have a responsibility to:

Communication & Information

Provide accurate, complete, and timely information about your health history, current condition, medications (including over-the-counter and supplements), allergies, past hospitalizations, and any other matters pertaining to your health. Inform your care team promptly about any changes in your condition, symptoms, medications, physician, insurance, address, phone number, emergency contacts, or advance directive status. Notify the agency if your physician makes changes to your medications or treatment plan. Communicate openly with your care team about your concerns, questions, preferences, and expectations.

Inform the agency if you do not understand your plan of care, instructions, or any information provided.

Plan of Care Compliance

Follow the plan of care recommended by your physician and established in collaboration with your care team, or inform your clinician promptly if you are unable or unwilling to follow the plan. Accept the consequences of refusing treatment or not following the recommended plan of care. Take medications as prescribed and inform your clinician of any difficulties, side effects, or non-compliance. Use medical equipment and supplies as instructed and report any malfunctions or concerns.

Home Environment & Safety

Provide a safe, clean, and reasonably accessible environment for care delivery, including adequate lighting, clear pathways, and a suitable work area for your clinician. Inform agency staff of any safety hazards in the home, including but not limited to: the presence of firearms or weapons, aggressive or unrestrained pets, pest infestations, structural hazards, secondhand smoke exposure, illegal activity, or the presence of individuals who may pose a threat to staff safety. Maintain utilities (electricity, water, heat/cooling) necessary for safe care delivery and the operation of medical equipment.

Ensure that a working telephone or reliable means of communication is available in the home. Address safety recommendations made by your care team to the extent reasonably possible.

Scheduling & Access

Be available and present in the home for scheduled visits, or provide the agency with at least 24 hours' advance notice if you need to reschedule or cancel. Provide the agency with home access instructions (key, lockbox code, gate code, building entry procedures) if you may be unable to answer the door. Notify the agency if you will be away from home (hospitalization, vacation, temporary relocation) and provide your expected return date.

Financial Responsibilities

Provide accurate and current insurance information, including copies of insurance cards. Notify the agency promptly of any changes to your insurance coverage, including new coverage, termination of coverage, or changes to a managed care plan. Meet financial obligations as agreed in the Financial Agreement (§7) and, if applicable, the Private-Pay Service Agreement (§8), in a timely manner. Notify the agency of any financial difficulties that may affect your ability to pay.

Respect & Cooperation

Treat all agency staff with respect, dignity, and courtesy, free from verbal, physical, or sexual harassment, discrimination, or threats. Understand that Care Indeed Home Health has a zero-tolerance policy for abuse, threats, or harassment directed at staff, and that persistent violations may result in discharge from services after appropriate warnings and documentation. Cooperate with your care team in carrying out your plan of care. Notify the agency promptly if you have any dissatisfaction with the care or services provided, so that concerns may be addressed before they escalate.

Notice Of Privacy Practices (HIPAA / CMIA)

Regulatory Basis45 CFR §164.520; California Civil Code §56–56.37 (CMIA); California Civil Code §1798.100 et seq. (CCPA/CPRA, as applicable)

THIS NOTICE DESCRIBES HOW MEDICAL INFORMATION ABOUT YOU MAY BE USED AND DISCLOSED AND HOW YOU CAN GET ACCESS TO THIS INFORMATION. PLEASE REVIEW IT CAREFULLY.

A. OUR LEGAL OBLIGATIONS

Care Indeed Home Health is required by federal law (the Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 — "HIPAA") and California law (the Confidentiality of Medical Information Act — "CMIA") to:

Maintain the privacy of your protected health information (PHI), which includes any individually identifiable information about your health condition, the health care services you receive, or payment for those services. Provide you with this Notice of our legal duties and privacy practices with respect to your PHI. Follow the terms of the Notice currently in effect. Notify you in the event of a breach of your unsecured PHI, in compliance with the HIPAA Breach Notification Rule (45 CFR §§164.400–414) and California Civil Code §1798.82.

B. HOW WE MAY USE AND DISCLOSE YOUR PHI

Uses and Disclosures That Do NOT Require Your Authorization

The following categories of use and disclosure are permitted or required by law without your written authorization:

1. Treatment. We will use and disclose your PHI to provide, coordinate, and manage your health care and related services. This includes sharing information among members of your care team (nurses, therapists, aides, physicians), consulting with other health care providers involved in your care, coordinating with hospitals, skilled nursing facilities, or other agencies for transfers and referrals, and communicating with your pharmacy regarding prescriptions.

Example: Your nurse shares your wound care assessment with your physician to obtain updated orders. Your physical therapist communicates with your occupational therapist regarding your mobility and functional goals.

2. Payment. We will use and disclose your PHI to obtain payment for services rendered. This includes submitting claims and supporting documentation to Medicare, Medi-Cal, private insurance companies, managed care organizations, or other third-party payers; verifying insurance coverage and eligibility; obtaining prior authorization or precertification; collecting amounts owed; and conducting utilization review activities.

Example: We submit your OASIS data and clinical documentation to Medicare to support our claim for reimbursement. We send a claim to your Medi-Cal managed care plan with diagnosis codes and dates of service.

3. Health. Care Operations We will use and disclose your PHI for our internal health care operations as necessary to run our agency and ensure quality care. This includes quality assessment and performance improvement (QAPI) activities; outcomes measurement and analysis; patient safety activities; accreditation, certification, licensing, and credentialing activities; clinical training and education (with appropriate safeguards); business planning, management, and general administration; compliance audits and fraud prevention; legal services and risk management; and business associate oversight.

Example: We review your clinical record as part of our QAPI program to identify opportunities to improve patient outcomes. Our medical records are audited during an ACHC accreditation survey.

4. Required by Law. We will disclose your PHI when required to do so by any federal, state, or local law that compels such disclosure. This includes mandatory disease and vital statistics reporting, responses to court orders and administrative orders, and compliance with workers' compensation laws.

5. Public Health Activities. We may disclose your PHI to public health authorities for the purpose of preventing or controlling disease, injury, or disability, including reporting communicable diseases to the California Department of Public Health, reporting adverse events or product defects to the FDA, and notifying individuals who may have been exposed to a communicable disease.

6. Health Oversight Activities. We may disclose your PHI to health oversight agencies for activities authorized by law, including audits, civil, administrative, or criminal investigations, inspections, licensure and certification surveys, and other proceedings necessary for oversight of the health care system, government benefit programs, and compliance with civil rights laws. Oversight agencies include but are not limited to: CMS, ACHC, CDPH, OIG, DOJ, DHCS, and the California Attorney General.

7. Abuse and Neglect Reporting. We are required by California law (Welfare & Institutions Code §15630 et seq.) to report suspected abuse, neglect, exploitation, or abandonment of elders and dependent adults to Adult Protective Services (APS), local law enforcement, and/or the Long-Term Care Ombudsman. All Care Indeed Home Health employees are mandated reporters under California law. This disclosure does not require your authorization.

8. Judicial and Administrative Proceedings. We may disclose your PHI in response to a court order. We may also disclose PHI in response to a subpoena, discovery request, or other lawful process, provided that (a) the party seeking the information has made reasonable efforts to notify you (or your attorney), or (b) the party has obtained a qualified protective order.

9. Law Enforcement. We may disclose your PHI to law enforcement officials under limited circumstances, including: in response to a court order, warrant, subpoena, or summons; to identify or locate a suspect, fugitive, material witness, or missing person; about a suspected victim of a crime under certain limited circumstances; about a death we believe may be the result of criminal conduct; about criminal conduct occurring on our premises; and in a medical emergency when necessary to report a crime, the location of the crime or victims, or the identity, description, or location of the perpetrator.

10. Decedents. We may disclose your PHI to coroners, medical examiners, and funeral directors as necessary for them to carry out their duties. We may also disclose PHI to organ procurement organizations for the purpose of organ, eye, or tissue donation.

11. Research. We may use or disclose your PHI for research purposes if the research has been approved by an Institutional Review Board (IRB) or privacy board that has reviewed the research proposal and established protocols to ensure the privacy of your PHI. In most cases, we will ask for your specific authorization before using your PHI for research purposes.

12. To Avert a Serious Threat to Health or Safety. We may use and disclose your PHI when necessary to prevent or lessen a serious and imminent threat to your health and safety or the health and safety of the public or another person. Disclosures will be made only to someone who is able to help prevent or lessen the threat.

13. Specialized Government Functions. We may disclose your PHI for military, veterans' affairs, national security, intelligence, and protective services of the President as required by law.

14. Workers' Compensation. We may disclose your PHI as authorized and necessary to comply with workers' compensation laws or other similar programs.

15. Correctional Institutions. If you are an inmate of a correctional institution, we may disclose your PHI to the institution or its agents when necessary for your health and safety, the health and safety of others, or the administration and maintenance of safety at the institution.

16. Communication with Family, Friends, and Others Involved in. Your Care Unless you object, we may disclose your PHI to a family member, other relative, close personal friend, or any other person you identify who is involved in your care or payment for your care. We will limit the disclosure to PHI that is directly relevant to that person's involvement. If you are unable to agree or object due to incapacity or emergency, we will use our professional judgment to determine whether disclosure is in your best interest.

17. Appointment Reminders, Treatment Alternatives, and Health-Related Benefits. We may use and disclose your PHI to contact you about appointment reminders, treatment alternatives, or other health-related benefits and services that may be of interest to you.

Uses and Disclosures That REQUIRE Your Written Authorization

All uses and disclosures not described above require your signed, written authorization. Specifically, authorization is required for:

Marketing — Any communication about a product or service that encourages you to purchase or use the product or service, unless the communication is face-to-face or involves a promotional gift of nominal value. Sale of PHI — Any disclosure of PHI where we receive direct or indirect remuneration from a third party in exchange for the PHI. Psychotherapy Notes — Uses or disclosures of psychotherapy notes (if any are maintained), except for certain limited treatment, payment, and operational purposes. Any other use or disclosure not described in this Notice.

You may submit an authorization using the form provided in §5 of this packet or by requesting one from our Privacy Officer.

Revocation of Authorization: You have the right to revoke any authorization you have given in writing at any time. To revoke an authorization, you must submit your request in writing to our Privacy Officer. Your revocation will be effective upon receipt, except to the extent that Care Indeed Home Health has already taken action in reliance on the authorization prior to receiving your revocation.

C. YOUR RIGHTS REGARDING YOUR PHI

Under HIPAA and California law, you have the following rights with respect to your PHI:

1. Right to Inspect and Copy (Right of Access)

You have the right to inspect and obtain a copy of your PHI that is maintained in a "designated record set," which includes your clinical/medical record and billing records. To exercise this right, you must submit a written request to our Privacy Officer.

We will respond to your request within 15 calendar days (California law) or 30 calendar days (HIPAA), whichever is shorter. If your records are maintained electronically, you may request an electronic copy in the format of your choice, if readily producible, or in another mutually agreed-upon electronic format. We may charge a reasonable, cost-based fee for copying, as permitted by California Civil Code §56.11 and Health & Safety Code §123110. The fee shall not exceed $0.25 per page (paper) or $0.50 per page for records copied from microfilm, plus reasonable clerical costs.

We may deny your request in limited circumstances. If we deny your request, you will receive a written explanation of the denial and information about how to request a review of the denial.

2. Right to Amend

You have the right to request that we amend your PHI if you believe it is inaccurate or incomplete. Your request must be in writing, directed to our Privacy Officer, and must include the reason you are requesting the amendment.

We will respond within 60 days of receiving your request (one 30-day extension permitted with written notice). We may deny your request if the PHI: (a) was not created by us; (b) is not part of the designated record set; (c) would not be available for your inspection; or (d) is accurate and complete. If we deny your request, we will provide a written explanation and inform you of your right to submit a written statement of disagreement, which will be included in your record.

3. Right to an Accounting of Disclosures

You have the right to request a list of certain disclosures we have made of your PHI. The accounting will include disclosures for the six-year period prior to your request (or since April 14, 2003, whichever is later) and will exclude disclosures made:

For treatment, payment, and health care operations. To you or your personal representative. Pursuant to your authorization. For national security, intelligence, or correctional purposes. As part of a limited data set. Incident to an otherwise permitted use or disclosure. We will respond within 60 days of your request (one 30-day extension permitted). The first accounting in any 12-month period is free; we may charge a reasonable, cost-based fee for additional requests.

4. Right to Request Restrictions

You have the right to request that we restrict certain uses or disclosures of your PHI for treatment, payment, or health care operations, or to persons involved in your care.

We are not required to agree to your request, except in one circumstance: if you pay for a service entirely out-of-pocket (without submitting to insurance) and you request that we not disclose PHI related to that service to your health plan for payment or operations purposes, we must comply with that restriction. If we agree to a restriction, we will comply unless the information is needed for emergency treatment. Any agreed-upon restriction may be terminated by mutual agreement or by either party with written notice.

5. Right to Request Confidential Communications

You have the right to request that we communicate with you regarding your PHI by alternative means or at alternative locations. For example, you may ask that we call you only at a specific number, use a different mailing address, or communicate via a particular email address.

We will accommodate reasonable requests. We will not ask you the reason for your request. You must specify the alternative means or location and explain how payment, if applicable, will be handled under the alternative communication method.

6. Right to a Paper Copy of This Notice

You have the right to receive a paper copy of this Notice at any time, even if you previously agreed to receive it electronically. To obtain a paper copy, contact our Privacy Officer or ask any member of your care team.

7. Right to Be Notified of a Breach

You have the right to be notified if there is a breach of your unsecured PHI. A "breach" is an unauthorized acquisition, access, use, or disclosure of PHI that compromises the security or privacy of the information. We will notify you in writing without unreasonable delay and no later than 60 days after discovery of the breach. The notification will describe the breach, the types of information involved, steps you should take to protect yourself, what we are doing to investigate and mitigate, and contact information.

D. CALIFORNIA-SPECIFIC RIGHTS (CMIA)

In addition to your rights under HIPAA, the California Confidentiality of Medical Information Act (Civil Code §56–56.37) provides the following protections:

No disclosure without authorization — Your medical information shall not be disclosed by a health care provider without your written authorization, except as specifically permitted by law. The CMIA authorization requirements are in certain respects more restrictive than HIPAA.

Mental health records — Additional protections apply to mental health records under the Lanterman-Petris-Short Act (Welfare & Institutions Code §5328).

HIV/AIDS information — Information regarding HIV testing and results is subject to additional confidentiality protections under Health & Safety Code §120975–121020.

Substance abuse treatment records — Records of substance abuse treatment are subject to federal confidentiality requirements under 42 CFR Part 2, which generally require patient consent for disclosure even for treatment, payment, and operations purposes.

Genetic information — Genetic test results are subject to additional protections under Civil Code §56.17.

Private right of action — You may bring a civil action against any person or entity that negligently or intentionally discloses your medical information in violation of the CMIA. Remedies include:

Compensatory damages

Punitive damages (for willful violations)

Attorneys' fees and costs

Statutory damages of $1,000 per violation (for negligent release) or $3,000 per violation (for knowing and willful release)

Injunctive relief

Employer restrictions — Your employer may not use your medical information for employment decisions except as specifically authorized.

A. WHAT ARE ADVANCE DIRECTIVES?

Advance directives are legal documents that allow you to express your wishes about future medical treatment and to designate someone to make health care decisions on your behalf if you become unable to do so. Under California law, the following types of advance directives are recognized:

1. Advance Health Care Directive (AHCD) A written document that may include both of the following:

Power of Attorney for Health Care — Designates another person (your "agent" or "health care proxy") to make health care decisions on your behalf when you are unable to make them yourself. Your agent has the authority to make any health care decision you could make, including decisions about life-sustaining treatment, unless you specifically limit that authority.

Individual Health Care Instructions (Living Will) — Written instructions that express your wishes regarding specific types of health care, including your desires about life-sustaining treatment, artificial nutrition and hydration, pain management, organ donation, and disposition of remains.

2. Physician Orders for Life-Sustaining Treatment (POLST) A medical order (not merely a directive) completed by a health care provider in consultation with you (or your representative). The POLST form specifies the types of life-sustaining treatment you wish to receive or decline, including CPR, intubation/mechanical ventilation, antibiotics, artificial nutrition, and comfort measures. The POLST is intended for individuals with serious illness or advanced frailty and is recognized by emergency medical personnel.

3. Do Not Resuscitate (DNR) Order A physician order that instructs health care providers and emergency medical personnel not to initiate cardiopulmonary resuscitation (CPR) in the event of cardiac or respiratory arrest. In California, a valid pre-hospital DNR must be on the state-approved POLST or equivalent form to be honored by paramedics and emergency responders.

4. Mental Health Advance Directive A document that allows you to express your wishes about mental health treatment, including hospitalization, medication, and electroconvulsive therapy, and to appoint an agent for mental health decisions.

A. WHAT IS HOMEBOUND STATUS?

To be eligible for Medicare-covered home health services, you must meet the Medicare homebound (confined to the home) criteria. You are considered homebound if:

Criterion 1 — There exists a normal inability to leave the home, AND leaving home requires a considerable and taxing effort.

This may be due to:

A condition that restricts your ability to ambulate (paralysis, amputation, severe weakness, orthopedic impairment, stroke effects, severe shortness of breath, etc.) Need for assistive devices (walker, wheelchair, crutches, cane) or the assistance of another person to leave home A medical condition that makes leaving home medically contraindicated (e.g., immunosuppression, unstable cardiac condition, risk of falls) Cognitive or psychiatric impairment that renders you unable to safely leave home unassisted (e.g., dementia, severe anxiety, agoraphobia)

Dependence on life-sustaining technology that is not easily portable

And

Criterion 2 — Absences from the home are infrequent, of short duration, or to receive medical treatment.

The following activities generally do not disqualify you from homebound status:

Attendance at adult day care programs (licensed/certified/accredited/state-regulated)

Attendance at religious services

Trips for medical treatment (doctor's appointments, dialysis, chemotherapy, radiation, etc.) Short, infrequent walks or trips outside (e.g., trip to the barber, brief walk around the block)

Attendance at funerals, graduations, or other unique events

Important: Homebound status is a clinical determination made by your care team and physician based on your individual circumstances. Meeting the homebound criteria requires both Criterion 1 AND Criterion 2.

B. HOW HOMEBOUND STATUS IS ASSESSED AND DOCUMENTED

Ongoing Assessment. Your homebound status will be reassessed at each skilled visit and documented in the visit note. Clinicians will assess and record specific clinical findings that support homebound status, including:

Nature of the condition that restricts mobility or the ability to leave home Specific functional limitations (e.g., ambulates with rolling walker, maximum distance 30 feet with fatigue) Taxing effort required to leave home (e.g., needs assistance of one person to transfer from wheelchair to car; develops severe dyspnea with minimal exertion)

Frequency and purpose of any absences from home

Recertification. Homebound status is recertified by your physician at each 60-day recertification period. Your physician must certify that you meet the homebound criteria as a condition of continued Medicare coverage.

Changes in Status. If your functional status improves to the point that you no longer meet the homebound criteria, your care team will:

Notify you and discuss the implications for your Medicare home health benefit. Communicate with your physician regarding your changed status. Provide you with written notice before any change in service. Assist you in identifying alternative resources or settings for continued care.

A. PROGRAM OVERVIEW

Care Indeed Home Health maintains a comprehensive Infection Prevention and Control (IPC) Program designed to prevent and control the transmission of infections among patients, staff, caregivers, and visitors. The program is supervised by a qualified professional and is integrated into our QAPI program.

Our IPC Program includes:

Written infection control policies and procedures reviewed at least annually

Ongoing surveillance of infections and communicable diseases

Staff education and competency evaluation in infection prevention

Compliance with OSHA/Cal-OSHA bloodborne pathogen and aerosol transmissible disease standards

Antibiotic stewardship activities

Outbreak investigation and response protocols

Coordination with local and state public health authorities

B. STANDARD PRECAUTIONS

Standard Precautions are the minimum infection prevention practices that apply to all patient care, regardless of suspected or confirmed infection status. All Care Indeed Home Health staff are trained in and will practice the following:

1. Hand Hygiene

Staff will perform hand hygiene (handwashing with soap and water or use of alcohol-based hand sanitizer containing at least 60% alcohol) before and after every patient contact, after removing gloves, after contact with body fluids or contaminated surfaces, and before any clean/aseptic procedure.

Hand hygiene is the single most important measure to prevent the spread of infection.

2. Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

Gloves — Worn when contact with blood, body fluids, mucous membranes, non-intact skin, or contaminated items is anticipated. Changed between tasks and between patients. Masks and Respiratory Protection — Worn when there is risk of splash/spray to the face, when caring for patients with respiratory infections (N95 or higher for aerosol transmissible diseases as indicated), and during procedures that generate aerosols. Gowns — Worn when contact with blood, body fluids, or contaminated surfaces is anticipated, and when the staff member's clothing may become contaminated.

Eye Protection (goggles/face shield) — Worn when splash or spray of blood or body fluids to the eyes is anticipated.

3. Sharps Safety

Needles, lancets, and other sharps are never recapped, bent, or broken by hand. All sharps are disposed of immediately after use into an approved, puncture-resistant sharps container. Sharps containers in the patient's home are provided by the agency and are disposed of according to local regulations.

4. Safe Injection Practices

Single-use vials are used for one patient and discarded after use. Multi-dose vials, if used, are dedicated to a single patient whenever possible and stored per manufacturer's instructions. Injection equipment (syringes, needles, prefilled devices) is never used on more than one patient.

5. Respiratory Hygiene / Cough Etiquette

Patients and household members with respiratory symptoms are encouraged to cover coughs and sneezes, use tissues (disposed immediately), and perform hand hygiene.

Staff will don a mask when caring for patients with respiratory symptoms, and provide the patient with a mask if appropriate.

6. Environmental Cleaning

Reusable patient care equipment is cleaned and disinfected between patients using EPA-registered hospital-grade disinfectants. Single-use items are disposed of after one use.

Staff will clean and disinfect the work area and equipment at each visit as needed.

7. Linen and Laundry

Contaminated linens are handled with minimum agitation to avoid aerosolization. Patient/family is instructed on proper handling of soiled linens (washing in hot water with detergent).

8. Waste Management

Regulated medical waste (blood-soaked materials, sharps, etc.) is segregated, contained, and disposed of in accordance with California Medical Waste Management Act (Health & Safety Code §117600 et seq.) and local regulations. General waste is disposed of according to standard household practices.

F. PATIENT AND CAREGIVER ROLE IN INFECTION PREVENTION

You and your caregivers play a critical role in preventing infections:

A. DEFINITIONS

Telehealth — The mode of delivering health care services using information and communication technologies to facilitate the diagnosis, consultation, treatment, education, care management, and self-management of a patient at a distance from the health care provider. In the context of home health, telehealth includes real-time audio/video visits between the patient (at home) and a clinician (at the agency or remote location).

Remote Patient Monitoring (RPM) — The use of connected digital technologies to collect physiological data (vital signs, weight, blood glucose, oxygen saturation, etc.) from the patient at home and electronically transmit that data to the care team for assessment, monitoring, and clinical decision-making.

Store-and-Forward — The transmission of recorded health information (images, data, documents) through secure electronic communication for review by a provider at a later time.
