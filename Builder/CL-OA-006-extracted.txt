Purpose This policy establishes the priority ranking of evidence sources that authorized OASIS assessors and all clinical documentation authors at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. must apply when making clinical coding decisions, completing OASIS assessments, and creating or resolving discrepancies in clinical documentation. In home health, the same patient's clinical status may be described — sometimes differently — across multiple concurrent documentation sources: the referral discharge summary from a hospital, prior home health records from another agency, therapy evaluations, nursing visit notes from earlier in the same episode, the patient's own self-report, and the clinician's direct physical examination at the current assessment encounter. When these sources conflict, the assessor does not have discretion to simply choose the source that produces the most convenient coding result. The choice of evidence source must follow a defined, defensible, and clinically sound hierarchy that places the highest weight on contemporaneous direct clinical observation and the lowest weight on unverified secondary sources. The absence of a documented evidence hierarchy creates three specific and serious failure modes. First, it enables financial motivation to influence source selection — an assessor who has no defined rule for choosing between sources may gravitate toward whichever source produces a higher payment group, consciously or unconsciously. Second, it creates inconsistency across assessors and across time, producing OASIS data that does not accurately represent the patient population and distorting the agency's quality measures. Third, it eliminates the audit trail that would allow the Director of Nursing, the OASIS Quality Reviewer, or a CMS surveyor to evaluate whether the assessor's coding decision was clinically sound. This policy implements 42 CFR § 484.55(b) — which requires the comprehensive assessment to accurately reflect the patient's current health status — and the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual's foundational principle that OASIS responses must be grounded in clinical evidence collected during the assessment encounter, not inferred from external records. 3. Scope This policy applies to: All registered nurses (RNs) authorized to conduct comprehensive OASIS assessments per CL-OA-003 The OASIS Quality Reviewer who validates evidence source use during quality reviews The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager who oversees assessment accuracy All clinical disciplines who contribute clinical information to the assessing RN during the assessment process The Compliance Officer who reviews evidence source selection in the context of audits or compliance investigations This policy governs evidence source selection for all OASIS data items at all assessment time points (SOC, ROC, RECERT, FU/SCIC, TRN, DC) and for all clinical documentation decisions where multiple evidence sources exist and must be prioritized. It does not alter the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual's item-specific instructions — where the Guidance Manual specifies a particular data collection method for a specific item, that specification governs. This policy establishes the framework that applies when the Guidance Manual does not specify a single source or when multiple applicable sources must be reconciled. 4. Policy Statement 4.1 All OASIS coding decisions at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall be grounded in evidence drawn from the highest available level of the Documentation Hierarchy defined in Section 5 of this policy. When evidence from a higher-level source is available and applicable, it shall govern the coding decision over evidence from a lower-level source. 4.2 No OASIS item response shall be selected on the basis of a lower-level evidence source when a higher-level source provides directly applicable clinical information for that item during the applicable look-back period. 4.3 When evidence sources conflict — that is, when a higher-level source and a lower-level source describe the patient's status differently for the same OASIS item — the assessor shall apply the evidence hierarchy per Section 5, document the conflicting sources in the assessment narrative, explain why the higher-level source was selected, and initiate the conflicting source reconciliation process per CL-OA-008 if the conflict has clinical implications beyond the immediate coding decision. 4.4 The evidence source used to support every OASIS coding decision shall be identifiable in the assessment narrative documentation. It is not sufficient to code an OASIS item accurately if the clinical record does not contain documentation that a reviewer or surveyor can use to verify the coding decision. An OASIS response that cannot be traced to a specific, documentable evidence source is an unsupported response, regardless of its clinical plausibility. 4.5 Financial considerations — including PDGM payment group optimization, episode management, or revenue targets — shall not influence evidence source selection. Selecting a lower-level evidence source because it produces a more favorable payment outcome, when a higher-level source is available and applicable, constitutes data manipulation and a potential violation of the False Claims Act. Any direction from revenue cycle personnel, management, or consultants to select evidence sources on the basis of payment optimization rather than clinical accuracy shall be reported to the Compliance Officer immediately per CO-CP-006. 4.6 The Documentation Hierarchy applies to all clinical documentation decisions, not exclusively to OASIS coding. When a clinician must choose between conflicting sources to document a clinical finding in a visit note, plan of care, or assessment narrative, the same hierarchy applies. Consistent application of the hierarchy across all documentation types reinforces the internal consistency of the clinical record. 4.7 The Director of Nursing shall incorporate evidence source documentation into the monthly OASIS quality audit per CL-OA-002, verifying that audited assessments contain traceable evidence references for high-risk item categories and that evidence source selection is consistent with this policy. 5. Definitions Term Definition Evidence Source Any document, observation, test result, or verbal report that provides clinical information relevant to an OASIS coding decision or a clinical documentation entry. Documentation Hierarchy The priority ranking system established by this policy that governs the relative weight assigned to different categories of evidence sources when making OASIS coding decisions and resolving source conflicts. Direct Clinical Observation The assessing RN's own first-hand observation of the patient performing an activity, exhibiting a symptom, or demonstrating a functional level during the assessment encounter. The highest-weight evidence source. Physical Examination Finding A clinical finding obtained through hands-on physical examination by the assessing RN during the assessment encounter — including auscultation, palpation, range of motion testing, strength testing, and neurological assessment. Standardized Tool Administration A validated assessment instrument administered by the assessing RN during the assessment encounter in strict accordance with the validated protocol — including BIMS, PHQ-2/PHQ-9, MAHC-10, and other CMS-required tools. Patient Self-Report Information provided verbally by the patient about their own symptoms, functional abilities, medication use, or history. A primary but subjective source that requires corroboration for OASIS items requiring objective verification. Caregiver Report Information provided verbally by a caregiver (family member, paid caregiver, or household member) about the patient's functional abilities, symptoms, or history. Treated as corroborative with patient self-report rather than as an independent primary source. Internal Clinical Record Documentation generated within the current Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. home health episode — including prior visit notes, prior assessments, physician communication notes, medication reconciliation records, and laboratory results obtained during the episode. External Clinical Documentation Documentation generated by healthcare providers or facilities outside Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. — including hospital discharge summaries, SNF discharge records, physician office notes, specialist reports, and prior home health agency records. Inference A coding decision based on logical extrapolation from available evidence rather than on direct clinical observation, patient report, or documented clinical findings. The lowest-weight evidence basis; requires explicit documentation of the inferential reasoning. Evidence Traceability The ability of a reviewer to identify, in the clinical record, the specific documentation or observation that supported a given OASIS coding decision. Required for all OASIS responses per Section 4.4. 6. The Documentation Hierarchy — Evidence Source Priority Ranking 6.1 Hierarchy Structure The following hierarchy governs evidence source selection at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Sources are listed from highest priority (Level 1) to lowest priority (Level 6). When multiple sources are available, the highest applicable level governs. LEVEL 1 — DIRECT CLINICAL OBSERVATION DURING THE ASSESSMENT ENCOUNTER  (Highest Priority) Definition: The assessing RN directly observes the patient performing the activity, demonstrating the functional level, or exhibiting the symptom being coded — during the current assessment visit, within the applicable look-back period. Application: For GG functional items (GG0130, GG0170), Level 1 evidence is obtained by asking the patient to perform the activity during the visit and directly observing the performance — not by asking the patient or caregiver to describe what they can do. For wound assessment items (M1311–M1350), Level 1 evidence is obtained by undressing all wound dressings and directly observing wound status. For behavioral observation items (M1710, M1720, M1740), Level 1 evidence includes directly witnessing behavioral symptoms during the visit. Documentation Standard: The visit note shall describe what the assessor directly observed — the specific activity, the level of assistance required, the qualitative characteristics of the performance, and any safety concerns identified. Documentation shall be specific enough that a second clinician reviewing the record could independently verify the observation. Examples of Adequate Level 1 Documentation: &quot;Patient directly observed ambulating 25 feet in the hallway with rolling walker. Required standby assist for balance at the turn. Gait was slow, wide-based, and slightly unsteady but no loss of balance. Completed the distance in 48 seconds.&quot; &quot;Stage 2 pressure injury directly observed on the right heel — dressing removed for assessment. Wound measures 2.1 cm x 1.8 cm x 0.2 cm. Wound bed 80% granulation tissue, 20% yellow slough. Periwound skin is intact without maceration. No drainage. No odor.&quot; Examples of Inadequate Level 1 Documentation: &quot;Patient ambulates with walker.&quot;  (No observation detail; does not establish GG performance level) &quot;Wound healing well.&quot;  (No objective wound assessment data) LEVEL 2 — PHYSICAL EXAMINATION FINDINGS DURING THE ASSESSMENT ENCOUNTER Definition: A clinical finding obtained through hands-on examination by the assessing RN during the current assessment visit — not observed activity performance, but assessed physical status. Application: Applicable to items where the assessor performs an examination rather than observing activity performance. Examples include: lung auscultation findings for respiratory items; cardiac auscultation and peripheral edema palpation for cardiac items; skin turgor and mucous membrane assessment for hydration/nutritional items; neurological reflex testing; range of motion measurement; muscle strength grading; sensory testing (monofilament); blood pressure and vital signs measurement. Documentation Standard: The examination finding shall be documented with the specific technique used, the specific finding obtained, and the specific anatomical location or measurement. A finding documented as &quot;WNL&quot; without supporting examination detail is inadequate. Examples of Adequate Level 2 Documentation: &quot;Lung auscultation: bibasilar crackles present posterior fields bilaterally, approximately one-third of the way up from the bases. No wheezes. No pleural rub.&quot; &quot;Right lower extremity edema — pitting 2+ to mid-calf. Left lower extremity — trace non-pitting edema at ankle. Skin intact bilaterally, no breakdown.&quot; &quot;Active range of motion — right shoulder flexion to 95 degrees (limited by pain; patient reports 8/10 pain at endpoint). Strength — right deltoid 3+/5 per MMT.&quot; LEVEL 3 — STANDARDIZED ASSESSMENT TOOL RESULTS FROM THE ASSESSMENT ENCOUNTER Definition: Results from standardized assessment instruments administered by the assessing RN during the current assessment visit, in strict accordance with validated administration protocols. Application: Applies specifically to CMS-required standardized tools — BIMS (for M1700), PHQ-2/PHQ-9 (for M1730/M1750), MAHC-10 (where applicable), and any other CMS-mandated tool for the current OASIS version. Also applies to validated discipline-specific tools used during the assessment (Timed Up and Go test, Berg Balance Scale, functional reach, etc.). Documentation Standard: The specific tool name and version, all individual item scores, the total score, and the clinical interpretation of the score shall be documented. The administration conditions shall be noted — particularly any deviation from the standard protocol (patient fatigue, interpreter used, partial administration with reason). See CL-OA-011 for standardized tool administration requirements. Critical Rule: A standardized tool score is only Level 3 evidence if the tool was administered in accordance with its validated protocol during the current assessment encounter. An abbreviated BIMS, a BIMS administered without the word repetition component, or a BIMS inferred from prior assessments does not constitute Level 3 evidence for the current assessment. LEVEL 4 — PATIENT SELF-REPORT WITH CORROBORATION Definition: Information provided verbally by the patient about their own symptoms, functional abilities, medication use, or history — when corroborated by at least one of: (a) physical examination findings; (b) caregiver report; (c) internal clinical record entries; or (d) external clinical documentation. Application: Patient self-report is a primary evidence source for many OASIS items — particularly symptom items (pain, dyspnea, anxiety), activity items with look-back periods (where direct observation of the activity at the assessment is not feasible), and history items. However, self-report alone is not sufficient for items where objective verification is clinically feasible and required by the CMS Guidance Manual. Uncorroborated Patient Self-Report: When a patient self-report cannot be corroborated — for example, a patient reports they are fully independent in ambulation, but no direct observation is performed and no corroborating source is available — the assessor shall document the report, note the absence of corroboration, and explain the clinical reason why direct observation was not performed. Uncorroborated self-report for items where objective verification is feasible constitutes Level 5 evidence (single source report), not Level 4. Documentation Standard: The specific patient statement, the corroborating source, and the reconciliation of any inconsistency between the patient report and the corroborating source shall be documented. LEVEL 5 — SINGLE-SOURCE REPORTS (CAREGIVER REPORT / UNCORROBORATED PATIENT SELF-REPORT / INTERNAL CLINICAL RECORD) Definition: Information from a single source that cannot be corroborated by another independent source during the current assessment. This includes: caregiver-only reports (no corresponding patient confirmation or clinical examination finding); uncorroborated patient self-report for items where corroboration is feasible; and internal clinical record entries from prior visits during the current episode, when no direct observation at the current assessment is possible. Application and Limitations: Level 5 sources are appropriate when Level 1–4 sources are not available or applicable — for example, when a patient lacks the communication capacity to self-report and no direct observation is possible, the caregiver's report of the patient's typical functional status is the best available evidence. Level 5 sources are also appropriate as the primary evidence for items with extended look-back periods, where the look-back extends beyond what the assessor can directly observe during a single visit. Caregiver Report Caution: Caregiver reports may reflect the caregiver's capabilities and preferences rather than the patient's actual abilities. A caregiver who routinely performs all ADLs for the patient may report the patient as &quot;unable to do anything&quot; even if the patient retains residual abilities. The assessor must probe for the patient's actual typical performance, not the caregiver's typical assistance level. When caregiver report and clinical examination findings conflict, the clinical examination governs (Level 2 over Level 5). Documentation Standard: The specific source, the information provided, and any limitations or qualifications of the source shall be documented. If Level 5 evidence is used for a GG functional item where direct observation is normally expected, the reason direct observation was not performed shall be documented. LEVEL 6 — EXTERNAL CLINICAL DOCUMENTATION AND INFERENCE  (Lowest Priority for Current Assessment Status) Definition: External clinical documentation: Records generated by providers outside Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. during a prior care episode or concurrent care encounter — including hospital discharge summaries, SNF records, physician office notes, specialist reports, and prior home health agency records. Inference: A coding decision based on logical extrapolation from available evidence when no direct evidence is available for the specific item — for example, inferring a patient's pain level from behavioral observations when the patient cannot self-report. Application — External Documentation: External documentation is appropriately used for: history items (diagnoses, surgical history, hospitalization history); medication list verification (cross-checked against the patient's actual medication bottles as Level 1/2 verification); establishing the patient's baseline prior to the current episode; and identifying clinical conditions documented by prior treating providers that the current assessor cannot independently verify. External documentation is not appropriate as the primary basis for OASIS items that reflect the patient's current functional status, unless the external document is contemporaneous with the current assessment time point and from a qualified clinical provider who directly assessed the patient. Critical Warning: Coding a patient's current GG functional status based on a hospital discharge summary's functional description, without direct observation at the SOC visit, is a Level 6 coding decision for a Level 1 item. This is the most common evidence hierarchy violation identified in ADR audits. The hospital's functional status description at discharge represents the patient's status at discharge — it does not represent the patient's status at the home health SOC, which may be better, worse, or the same. Inference Application: When inference is the only available basis for a coding decision (patient is comatose; patient refuses assessment; patient is transferred and not present for the TRN assessment), the assessor shall document: the specific clinical evidence available; the inferential reasoning applied; and the confidence level in the inferred response. Inference-based responses shall be flagged for OASIS Quality Reviewer review per CL-OA-002. Documentation Standard for Inference:  &quot;Direct observation of [specific activity] was not possible because [specific reason]. The available evidence — [description of available evidence] — supports a coding response of [response]. Reasoning: [specific clinical reasoning].&quot; 6.2 Evidence Hierarchy Application by OASIS Item Category OASIS Item Category Primary Evidence Level Secondary Evidence Level Common Error GG Functional Procedures 7.1 Evidence Collection During Assessment Visits Step Responsible Party Action Timeframe 7.1.1 Assessing RN Before beginning the OASIS assessment, review all available pre-visit documentation (referral records, prior notes, medication lists) to identify the items that will be assessed and to plan the observation and examination sequence that will generate the highest-level evidence for each applicable item. Do not pre-populate any OASIS response based on pre-visit documentation alone — this is a Level 6 evidence basis for items that require Level 1 or 2 evidence. Before each assessment visit. 7.1.2 Assessing RN During the assessment visit, structure the clinical encounter to generate direct observation and physical examination evidence for all GG functional items and examination-dependent items. This means asking the patient to perform activities rather than asking whether they can perform them; removing wound dressings to directly observe all wounds; conducting a full physical examination including auscultation, palpation, and neurological assessment as appropriate to the diagnoses; and administering all required standardized tools in validated protocol. During the assessment visit. 7.1.3 Assessing RN When direct observation of a specific activity is clinically unsafe, practically impossible, or refused by the patient, document the reason and note the evidence level being used as the substitute. Notify the Director of Nursing if the inability to obtain Level 1 or 2 evidence affects a high-risk OASIS item (GG items, wound items). At the time of the assessment; notification within 24 hours. 7.1.4 Assessing RN After completing the assessment visit, complete the narrative documentation before coding the OASIS responses. The narrative shall precede the OASIS responses — do not reverse-engineer narrative to match OASIS responses that were pre-populated from external sources. Within the required documentation timeframe per CL-OA-001. 7.2 Documentation of Evidence Sources in the Assessment Narrative Step Responsible Party Action Timeframe 7.2.1 Assessing RN For each OASIS item being coded, the assessment narrative shall contain documentation that a reviewer can use to verify the evidentiary basis. For Level 1 items (direct observation), this means describing what was observed in specific, objective terms. For Level 2 items (physical examination), this means documenting the specific examination finding. For Level 3 items (standardized tools), this means documenting the individual item scores and total score. For Level 4-6 items, this means documenting the source, the information provided, and any corroborating or conflicting information. In the assessment narrative; concurrent with or before OASIS coding. 7.2.2 Assessing RN When using Level 5 or 6 evidence for an item where higher-level evidence would normally be expected — for example, coding a GG item from caregiver report because the patient refused to perform the activity — include the specific notation:  &quot;Level [X] evidence used for [OASIS item] because [specific reason higher-level evidence was not available]. Evidence basis: [description of evidence].&quot; In the assessment narrative. 7.2.3 Assessing RN Never document an evidence source that was not actually used. If the wound assessment was done from a prior note (Level 6) rather than direct wound observation (Level 1), do not document a wound description in language suggesting direct observation occurred. This constitutes falsification of medical records regardless of whether the coded OASIS response happens to be accurate. At all times. 7.3 Quality Review Integration Step Responsible Party Action Timeframe 7.3.1 OASIS Quality Reviewer During each Level 2 quality review per CL-OA-002, specifically assess evidence traceability for all GG functional items and wound items in the reviewed assessment. For each reviewed item, verify: (a) the narrative contains documentation at the appropriate evidence level; (b) the coded OASIS response is consistent with the documented evidence; (c) no lower-level evidence source was used when higher-level evidence was documented in the narrative. Within the timeline specified in CL-OA-002. 7.3.2 Director of Nursing During monthly OASIS accuracy audits per CL-OA-002, include evidence traceability as a specific audit category. Calculate the percentage of audited assessments where every reviewed OASIS item has traceable, appropriate-level evidence documentation. Report this as a separate metric in the OASIS Accuracy Dashboard. Monthly. 7.3.3 Director of Nursing When an audited assessment reveals Level 6 evidence used for GG functional items without documented clinical justification, treat this as a Priority 1 finding requiring individual coaching within 7 calendar days and targeted retraining within 14 calendar days. Report the finding to the Compliance Officer if the pattern appears across multiple assessors or assessments. Coaching within 7 calendar days; Compliance Officer notification for patterns. 8. Compliance &amp; Audit Considerations 10.1 How Compliance Is Measured Compliance Indicator Measurement Method Acceptable Standard GG functional items coded from Level 1 direct observation when the patient was present and capable Monthly OASIS audit — cross-reference GG responses with assessment narrative observation documentation ≥95% of GG items with documented direct observation or clinically justified exception Wound items coded from direct wound observation Monthly OASIS audit — verify narrative contains wound description at Level 1 standard ≥98% of wound items coded from direct observation at the assessment encounter Standardized tool scores documented at item level Monthly audit of BIMS and PHQ-2/PHQ-9 documentation ≥98% of tool administrations with item-level scores documented Level 5/6 evidence uses documented with explicit notation Audit of assessments where Level 5/6 evidence was the primary source ≥95% of Level 5/6 uses with explicit evidence level notation No evidence of revenue-motivated source selection Compliance Officer quarterly review of coding patterns Zero confirmed instances 10.2 What Surveyors and Auditors Will Look For CMS OASIS validation surveyors will independently assess patients in the survey sample and compare their clinical findings to the submitted OASIS data. The most common discrepancy they identify — and the one most directly linked to evidence hierarchy violations — is GG functional items coded at a higher level of dependence than the patient's direct performance demonstrates. When a surveyor observes a patient ambulate 100 feet independently with a cane and the submitted OASIS shows &quot;Substantial/Maximal Assistance&quot; for walking, the inevitable conclusion is that the coding was not based on direct observation. Surveyors will ask the assessing RN to describe the evidence basis for the GG responses. If the assessor cannot identify a specific direct observation or examination finding — and the narrative does not document one — the assessment will be cited as inaccurate. ADR auditors from the MAC will specifically examine assessment narratives for the presence of specific, objective clinical descriptions that corroborate OASIS responses. A narrative that says &quot;patient requires assistance with mobility&quot; without describing what was observed does not adequately support a specific GG functional item response. Auditors are trained to recognize when narratives have been copied from external sources (hospital discharge language appearing in the SOC narrative) or when the narrative descriptions are generic and non-specific. 10.3 Common Failure Points Failure Point Risk Mitigation SOC GG items coded from hospital discharge functional description without SOC direct observation ADR denial; OASIS inaccuracy; potential upcoding Train all assessors on the non-transferability of discharge status to SOC status; require GG observation documentation in EHR before OASIS can be locked Wound items coded from prior visit notes at RECERT without current wound observation OASIS inaccuracy at RECERT; quality measure distortion Include wound observation confirmation as mandatory pre-lock checklist item per CL-OA-019 BIMS coded from clinical impression rather than formal tool administration M1700 inaccuracy; behavioral health PDGM classification error Annual BIMS competency demonstration required; OASIS Quality Reviewer verifies item-level score documentation Assessment narrative describes patient performing activity but GG response does not reflect the described performance Internal inconsistency between Level 1 observation and OASIS coding Director of Nursing monthly audit includes narrative-to-GG cross-reference as a required audit step 11. Purpose This policy requires that every OASIS item response submitted by Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. be supported by verifiable, contemporaneous clinical documentation within the medical record — with a traceable evidentiary basis that a qualified reviewer can independently evaluate and validate. Evidence-based OASIS coding substantiation is the operational bridge between the clinical evidence collected during the assessment encounter (governed by CL-OA-006) and the OASIS data item response that is transmitted to the CMS repository (governed by CL-CA-003). The gap between these two processes — the space where a clinician has collected valid clinical evidence but failed to document it in a manner that supports the coded response — is one of the most consequential compliance vulnerabilities in home health. CMS has made unambiguous through survey guidance, ADR audit methodology, and quality reporting standards that an OASIS response is not credible without supporting documentation in the clinical record. This principle is not merely a documentation preference — it is the operational mechanism by which the federal government verifies that Medicare payments for home health episodes are grounded in genuine clinical need. When the documentation does not support the coded response, the response is, by definition, unsupported — and an unsupported OASIS response in a submitted claim creates False Claims Act exposure regardless of whether the clinician believed the response was clinically accurate. This policy establishes the specific documentation standards that constitute adequate substantiation for each major category of OASIS items, the pre-submission verification process that ensures substantiation before locking, and the audit methodology that validates substantiation across the agency's submitted assessments. 3. Scope This policy applies to: All authorized OASIS assessors who complete and authenticate OASIS assessments The OASIS Quality Reviewer who conducts pre-submission and post-submission accuracy reviews The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager who oversees assessment documentation quality The Compliance Officer who reviews substantiation adequacy in the context of audits and compliance investigations Medical Records staff who verify clinical record completeness in relation to submitted OASIS data 4. Policy Statement 4.1 Every OASIS item response transmitted to the CMS repository by Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall be supported by contemporaneous clinical documentation within the patient's clinical record that: (a) was created at or near the time of the assessment encounter; (b) describes specific clinical observations, examination findings, or validated tool results that support the coded response; and (c) is traceable — meaning a qualified reviewer can identify the specific documentation entry and understand how it supports the specific OASIS response. 4.2 OASIS responses shall not be coded on the basis of clinical plausibility, diagnostic inference, or general knowledge of the patient's condition without specific supporting documentation. The fact that a patient with a particular diagnosis &quot;would typically&quot; exhibit a particular functional level is not documentation — it is assumption. Every response requires specific documentation. 4.3 The narrative clinical documentation shall be written before or concurrently with OASIS coding, not after. An OASIS response coded first, followed by narrative documentation reverse-engineered to support the coded response, does not constitute contemporaneous evidence-based substantiation. The documentation integrity requirement per CO-DC-001 and the audit trail maintained by the EHR will reflect the sequence of documentation. 4.4 Substantiation documentation shall be specific to the item being coded. Documentation that supports one OASIS item does not automatically substantiate a related item. Each item requires its own evidentiary basis unless the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual explicitly provides for grouped assessment. 4.5 The OASIS Quality Reviewer shall assess substantiation adequacy as a core component of every Level 2 quality review per CL-OA-002. An assessment where the OASIS Quality Reviewer cannot identify adequate substantiation for a coded response shall be treated as an accuracy deficiency and managed through the correction process per CL-OA-002. 4.6 Adequate substantiation is distinct from accurate coding. An OASIS response may be accurate — correctly reflecting the patient's clinical status — while being inadequately substantiated — because the documentation does not clearly connect the clinical evidence to the coded response. Both accuracy and substantiation are required. An accurately coded but inadequately substantiated OASIS assessment is a documentation compliance failure under 42 CFR § 484.110 and a billing compliance vulnerability. 5. Definitions Term Definition Coding Substantiation The clinical documentation that supports, explains, and justifies a specific OASIS item response — distinct from the evidence itself, which is defined in CL-OA-006. Substantiation is the recorded documentation of the evidence. Contemporaneous Documentation Documentation created at or near the time of the clinical encounter it describes — specifically, within the documentation timeframe required by CL-CD-004 (24 hours for visit notes; 5 calendar days for OASIS assessments). Traceable Evidence Documentation in the clinical record that a qualified reviewer can identify, locate, and use to independently evaluate the basis for a coded OASIS response. Adequate Substantiation The minimum standard of documentation that a qualified reviewer — including a CMS surveyor or MAC ADR auditor — would accept as credibly supporting a specific OASIS item response. Defined at item-category level in Section 6. Inadequate Substantiation Documentation that is too vague, too generic, too brief, or too disconnected from the specific OASIS item to serve as credible evidence of the coded response. Reverse-Engineered Documentation Documentation created after an OASIS response has been coded, with the purpose of justifying the pre-existing coded response rather than reflecting contemporaneous clinical observation. Explicitly prohibited by this policy. Narrative-OASIS Alignment The state in which the specific clinical descriptions in the assessment narrative are consistent with and support the selected OASIS item responses. A fundamental requirement of this policy and of CMS guidance. Documentation Gap The absence of documentation that would be expected to support a specific OASIS response — for example, the absence of a wound description when M1311 indicates a pressure injury is present. 6. Item-Category Substantiation Standards 6.1 GG Functional Assessment Items (GG0130 Self-Care; GG0170 Mobility) The GG items are the highest-risk OASIS category for substantiation deficiencies. They directly drive PDGM functional level classification, which affects Medicare payment across all episode types. ADR auditors examine GG item substantiation in virtually every home health claim audit. Minimum Adequate Substantiation Standard for Each GG Item: The narrative documentation shall contain ALL of the following elements for each GG item that is being coded: The activity or action being assessed — stated explicitly (not implied) What the patient actually did during the assessment — described in observable terms (not &quot;can perform&quot; or &quot;is able to&quot;) The level of physical assistance provided — described in the same terms as the OASIS response option (no helper needed; setup or clean-up only; supervision or touching assistance; partial/moderate/substantial/maximal/dependent) Qualitative characteristics of the performance — speed, safety, quality, consistency The surface, environment, or equipment context — where applicable (type of floor surface for ambulation; type of bed for bed mobility; type of stairs for stair climbing) Any safety concerns or supervision requirements — identified during the activity observation Adequate Substantiation Examples — GG0170B Walking 10 Feet: Adequate:  &quot;Patient walked 10 feet on the linoleum kitchen floor, starting from a standing position at the counter. Patient required me to provide physical contact at the right elbow for balance (touching assistance) throughout the 10-foot distance. Walking speed was approximately 15 seconds for the distance. Gait was shuffling, wide-based; right foot cleared the floor adequately. Patient did not lose balance. No verbal cueing was required.&quot; Inadequate:  &quot;Patient ambulates with assistance.&quot; — Does not describe the specific performance, the specific assistance level, or any observable characteristics. Inadequate:  &quot;Patient requires moderate assist with ambulation per discharge summary.&quot; — Uses Level 6 external documentation for a Level 1 observation item without documentation that direct observation occurred. Inadequate:  &quot;Patient ambulates with rolling walker, unsteady.&quot; — Does not establish the level of assistance; &quot;unsteady&quot; does not map to a specific GG response option. Documentation Requirement for GG Items Where Observation Was Not Possible: When a GG item cannot be assessed through direct observation (patient refuses; patient is medically unable to attempt the activity; the activity cannot safely be assessed at the visit), the assessor shall document: The specific reason direct observation was not performed What substitute evidence was used (caregiver report, prior visit notes, clinical examination) The specific information from the substitute source that supports the coded response The OASIS Quality Reviewer shall be notified of GG items coded without direct observation for pre-submission review 6.2 Wound Assessment Items (M1311–M1350) Minimum Adequate Substantiation Standard: For each wound for which an OASIS wound item is coded, the narrative shall document: Wound location — specific anatomical site Wound etiology — the clinical basis for the wound type classification Wound stage or classification — per NPIAP (for pressure injuries) or applicable system; with the specific observable features that determine the stage Wound measurements — L x W x D in centimeters, measured with a disposable tool at the current assessment Wound bed description — percentage of each tissue type (granulation, epithelialization, slough, eschar, necrotic) Drainage — type and amount Periwound skin — specific description of the surrounding tissue Signs of infection — specific indicators assessed and present or absent Whether a dressing was removed — explicit statement that dressings were removed for wound assessment (demonstrates direct observation) Critical Substantiation Rule — No Wound Item Without Wound Removal: An OASIS wound item coded on the basis of a dressing change note that does not document dressing removal and direct wound observation is inadequately substantiated. Documentation that states &quot;wound care performed per orders&quot; without wound description details does not substantiate wound OASIS items. Stage Determination Substantiation: For pressure injury staging, the narrative shall document the specific tissue visible in the wound bed that determines the stage. A Stage 3 pressure injury requires documentation of full-thickness skin loss with exposure of subcutaneous tissue. A Stage 4 requires documentation of full-thickness skin and tissue loss with exposure of fascia, muscle, tendon, ligament, bone, or cartilage. Unstageable requires documentation of obscured wound bed (slough or eschar covering the wound base). An OASIS wound stage response without the specific tissue description that determines the stage is inadequately substantiated. 6.3 Behavioral Health Items — M1700 (Cognitive Function) / BIMS Minimum Adequate Substantiation Standard: For M1700 and all BIMS-derived items, the narrative shall document: BIMS administration confirmation — explicit statement that the BIMS was administered during the current assessment encounter Word repetition component score — the three words used and the number repeated correctly Temporal orientation component scores — the patient's responses to year, month, and day of week questions; each scored individually Word recall component score — the number of words recalled from the initial repetition Total BIMS score — calculated from the components Cognitive domain interpretation — &quot;Cognitively Intact (13–15)&quot; / &quot;Moderately Impaired (8–12)&quot; / &quot;Severely Impaired (0–7)&quot; When BIMS Cannot Be Administered: If the patient is unable to complete the BIMS (comatose, severe aphasia, refuses), document: the specific reason BIMS could not be administered; the Staff Assessment for Mental Status (SAMS) process used instead; the SAMS component responses; and the resulting cognitive function response per CMS OASIS-E2 guidance for the SAMS pathway. 6.4 Behavioral Health Items — M1730/M1750 (Depressive Feelings / Depression) Minimum Adequate Substantiation Standard: For M1730 and M1750, the narrative shall document: PHQ-2 administration confirmation — explicit statement that the PHQ-2 was administered PHQ-2 item 1 response — patient's verbatim or closely paraphrased answer and the numeric score assigned (0–3) PHQ-2 item 2 response — patient's verbatim or closely paraphrased answer and the numeric score assigned (0–3) PHQ-2 total score PHQ-9 administration (if PHQ-2 ≥3) — all 9 item scores and the total score PHQ-9 item 9 response and any safety assessment — documented separately per CL-SD-022 When PHQ Cannot Be Administered: Document the specific reason and the clinical observation basis for the M1730/M1750 responses selected. 6.5 Medication Management Items (M2001, M2003, M2010) Minimum Adequate Substantiation Standard: For M2001 (Drug Regimen Review), the narrative shall document: Confirmation that a drug regimen review was conducted — explicit statement with the methodology (physical inventory of medication bottles, comparison against prescription list, patient interview) The specific potential clinically significant issues identified — or explicit statement that no issues were identified, with the specific checks performed (interactions, allergies, duplications, dose errors, contraindications) The medications reviewed — the complete medication list reviewed, or reference to the medication reconciliation worksheet per CL-SD-013 For M2003 (Medication Follow-Up), the narrative shall document whether the physician was contacted within one calendar day of the drug regimen review for any identified potential clinically significant medication issues — with the date and method of contact, or with the reason contact was not made if M2001 was coded as &quot;no potential issues identified.&quot; For M2010 (High-Risk Medication Education), the narrative shall document the specific high-risk medications that were the subject of education, the specific educational content provided, and the patient's/caregiver's demonstrated understanding. 6.6 Homebound Status Substantiation While homebound status is not a single OASIS item, it is the foundational eligibility determination that underlies every OASIS assessment and every Medicare claim. Homebound status substantiation shall meet the following minimum standard: The assessment narrative shall document both CMS homebound criteria with patient-specific clinical language: Criterion 1 (Condition-based normal inability to leave home): The specific medical condition or combination of conditions that restricts the patient's ability to leave the home — described in terms of specific functional limitations, symptoms, or physical constraints that result from the illness or injury. Criterion 2 (Considerable and taxing effort required): The specific functional limitations that make leaving home require considerable and taxing effort — described in observable, measurable terms that a reviewer can evaluate. See CL-CA-005 for the complete homebound status documentation standards. An inadequate homebound substantiation — generic language, diagnosis-only documentation, or copied language from a prior assessment — is not only a documentation deficiency but a potential basis for recoupment of the entire episode's claims. 6.7 Clinical Severity and Symptom Items Minimum Adequate Substantiation Standard: For symptom and severity items (dyspnea, pain, incontinence, etc.), the narrative shall document: The specific assessment method used — validated scale, clinical examination, patient report The patient's specific reported symptom description — verbatim or closely paraphrased The objective clinical findings — vital signs, auscultation findings, observation of effort, scale scores The functional impact — how the symptom limits the patient's activities The frequency and context — when the symptom occurs, what triggers or relieves it 7. All three must align. A high-dependence GG response with a narrative that does not describe high-dependence performance, for a patient who presents as functionally capable during the survey interview, fails the triangulation test. Specificity of GG documentation — auditors are trained to reject generic mobility documentation. &quot;Patient requires assistance with ambulation&quot; does not substantiate a specific GG response option. The documentation must mirror the language of the OASIS response option — if the response is &quot;Substantial/Maximal Assistance,&quot; the documentation must describe the level of physical assistance that corresponds to that response. BIMS component documentation — auditors frequently note total BIMS scores documented without individual component scores. This pattern suggests the BIMS was not formally administered; a clinician who informally estimates a total BIMS score cannot produce individual component responses. Contemporaneousness — auditors examine timestamps and documentation dates. An assessment narrative that was documented significantly after the assessment date, or that appears to have been populated from external sources rather than current clinical observation, fails the contemporaneousness requirement. Absence of wound documentation when wound items are coded — M1311 coded as &quot;yes, pressure injury present&quot; without a corresponding wound assessment narrative at the current assessment time point is an immediate substantiation deficiency. 9.3 Common Failure Points Failure Point Risk Mitigation GG narrative uses general mobility language rather than activity-specific observation language ADR denial; OASIS accuracy deficiency EHR assessment template includes activity-specific observation fields as required entries for each GG item; Director of Nursing monthly audit targets GG documentation specificity BIMS total score documented without component scores M1700 substantiation deficiency; suggests informal administration Annual BIMS competency includes demonstrated documentation of all components; OASIS Quality Reviewer validates component documentation at every SOC review Wound OASIS items coded but no wound observation narrative at the current time point M1311–M1350 substantiation deficiency Pre-submission checklist item requires wound observation documentation verification before lock; OASIS Quality Reviewer flags wound items without current assessment documentation Homebound narrative copied from prior assessment without update CL-CA-005 violation; survey citation EHR configuration prevents copy-forward of homebound narrative field; Director of Nursing monthly audit includes homebound documentation freshness check Assessment narrative drafted after OASIS responses are coded Reverse-engineering suspicion; integrity concern EHR workflow requires narrative field completion before OASIS module becomes available for response entry; audit trail timestamps compared in monthly audit 10. Purpose This policy establishes the standardized reconciliation process that authorized OASIS assessors at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. must follow when documentation from referring facilities, prior care settings, or internal records contains conflicting clinical information that affects OASIS coding decisions. Documentation conflicts are not an exception in home health — they are a routine feature of the care environment. A patient is discharged from a hospital where the discharge summary describes them as requiring moderate assistance with bed mobility, but the home health SOC nurse observes them moving in bed with minimal physical contact. The hospital's medication reconciliation list includes a medication the patient has not taken for three months. The prior home health agency's discharge summary describes a Stage 3 pressure injury on the left heel, but the SOC nurse directly observes a wound that appears consistent with a Stage 2. The prior visit note describes SpO2 of 94% on room air, but the current assessment finds SpO2 of 88%. Each of these conflicts creates a documentation decision with clinical, coding, billing, and compliance dimensions. The wrong choice — defaulting to the external document without explaining the conflict, or ignoring the prior internal record without acknowledging the change — produces either an inaccurate OASIS response, an inadequately substantiated response, or both. The right choice requires a defined, defensible process that: identifies the conflict explicitly; applies the evidence hierarchy from CL-OA-006 to determine which source governs; documents the reconciliation process; codes the OASIS on the basis of the governing source; and initiates clinical follow-up when the conflict has patient safety or care plan implications. This policy implements the documentation integrity requirements of 42 CFR § 484.110, the accuracy requirements of 42 CFR § 484.55(b), and the cross-verification requirement established in CL-OA-013. 3. Scope This policy applies to: All authorized OASIS assessors who encounter conflicting documentation sources during comprehensive assessments The OASIS Quality Reviewer who identifies conflicting source issues during quality reviews The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager who provides clinical adjudication when conflicts cannot be resolved by the assessor All clinical disciplines who may identify conflicts between documentation sources during routine visits and must report them to the assessing RN Physicians who may need to be notified of clinically significant conflicts per Section 6.4 4. Policy Statement 4.1 When documentation from two or more sources provides conflicting information about the same clinical finding or patient status — and that finding or status is relevant to an OASIS coding decision — the conflict shall be identified, documented, and resolved through the standardized process established in Section 6 of this policy before the OASIS response for the affected item is finalized. 4.2 Ignoring a known documentation conflict and coding from a single source without acknowledging or resolving the conflict is prohibited. Selecting the source that produces a more favorable payment outcome, without clinical justification for the selection, violates the evidence hierarchy established in CL-OA-006 and may constitute data manipulation per CO-FA-003. 4.3 The resolution of every documentation conflict affecting an OASIS coding decision shall be documented in the assessment narrative. The documentation shall identify: the conflicting sources; the nature of the conflict; the evidence hierarchy level of each source; the source selected to govern the coding decision; and the clinical reasoning supporting the selection. Conflicts resolved through clinical judgment rather than evidence hierarchy shall be documented through the clinical reasoning process per CL-OA-012. 4.4 When a documentation conflict has clinical implications beyond OASIS coding — such as a conflict between the referral diagnosis and the SOC clinical findings that suggests a new undiagnosed condition, or a conflict between the prior wound documentation and the current wound assessment that suggests clinical deterioration or improvement — the conflict shall trigger the appropriate clinical response per Section 6.4, including physician notification per CL-CP-005 when clinically indicated. 4.5 When a documentation conflict cannot be resolved by the assessing RN through application of the evidence hierarchy, the RN shall escalate to the Director of Nursing for clinical adjudication before locking the assessment. The Director of Nursing's determination represents the agency's official coding position for the affected item. 4.6 Documentation conflicts that appear systemic — occurring repeatedly across multiple patients, involving the same referring facility, or reflecting consistent discrepancies between internal records and clinical findings — shall be reported to the Director of Nursing and flagged for quality review. Systemic conflicts may indicate a population-level change in patient acuity, a facility's documentation practices, or a gap in the agency's clinical process that requires a QAPI response per QA-PG-001. 5. Definitions Term Definition Documentation Conflict A situation in which two or more documentation sources provide different information about the same clinical finding, patient status, diagnosis, medication, or functional ability — where the different information cannot be simultaneously accurate and where the conflict affects an OASIS coding decision or a clinical care decision. External Source Conflict A conflict between an external clinical document (hospital discharge summary, SNF records, prior HHA records, physician notes) and the assessing RN's current clinical findings or internal clinical records. Internal Source Conflict A conflict between two documents within the current Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. episode — such as a conflict between a prior visit note and the current assessment findings, or between a physician order and the plan of care medication list. Clinical Deterioration Conflict A conflict in which more recent information indicates that the patient's clinical status has declined from what was previously documented — requiring immediate clinical attention beyond OASIS coding. Clinical Improvement Conflict A conflict in which more recent information indicates that the patient's clinical status has improved from what was previously documented — potentially affecting continued eligibility or plan of care goals. Irresolvable Conflict A conflict that cannot be resolved through application of the evidence hierarchy, additional clinical assessment, or clinical reasoning — requiring Director of Nursing adjudication and, if clinically significant, physician consultation. Reconciliation Documentation The specific narrative entry in the clinical record that identifies the conflicting sources, explains the conflict, describes the resolution process, and justifies the coding decision made in light of the conflict. 6. Procedures 6.1 Conflict Identification — Pre-Visit and During-Visit Step Responsible Party Action Timeframe 6.1.1 Assessing RN Before each assessment visit, review all available pre-visit documentation (referral records, discharge summary, prior visit notes, medication lists) and identify any information that could conflict with clinical findings expected at the current assessment — for example, a discharge functional status description that may differ from the patient's current functional status; a wound description from a prior note; a medication list from a facility that may not reflect the patient's current home medications. Create a &quot;conflict watch list&quot; of items to monitor during the assessment. Before each assessment visit. 6.1.2 Assessing RN During the assessment visit, compare direct clinical findings against the pre-visit documentation for each item on the conflict watch list. When a conflict is identified — the observed finding differs materially from the documented expectation — document both the observed finding and the prior documentation, and note the conflict in the assessment narrative. Note the evidence hierarchy level per CL-OA-006 for each conflicting source. At the time of conflict identification. 6.2.2 Assessing RN Apply the evidence hierarchy to determine the governing source. In all cases where the conflict involves the assessing RN's direct clinical observation at the current assessment (Level 1 or 2) versus any external or prior internal document (Level 5 or 6), the current direct observation governs. Document this determination explicitly. At the time of conflict identification. 6.2.3 Assessing RN When the conflict involves two sources at the same hierarchy level — for example, two prior visit notes that conflict with each other — apply the following tie-breaking rule: the more recent source governs, provided it is internally consistent and not contradicted by physical examination findings. If the more recent source is contradicted by physical findings, the physical findings govern regardless of recency. At the time of conflict identification. 6.2.4 Assessing RN When the conflict involves functional status described in a hospital discharge summary (Level 6) versus the assessing RN's direct SOC observation (Level 1) — which is the most common conflict type — the SOC direct observation governs categorically. In the assessment narrative; before locking. 6.3.2 Assessing RN The reconciliation documentation shall be specific enough that a reviewer — including a CMS surveyor — can follow the assessor's reasoning and independently evaluate whether the conflict resolution was appropriate. Conflicts requiring immediate clinical action (beyond documentation) include: (a) Current clinical findings indicating a significant decline from the last documented status — suggesting clinical deterioration; (b) Current clinical findings indicating a new condition or complication not documented in the referral; (c) Medication conflicts suggesting the patient has been taking a medication differently than prescribed; (d) Wound conflicts suggesting significant progression or new wound development since the last documented assessment; (e) Vital sign conflicts suggesting instability not captured in prior documentation. At the time of conflict identification; before the visit concludes. 6.4.2 Assessing RN For conflicts with clinical implications: (a) Address the immediate patient safety concern at the visit; (b) Notify the physician within the timeframe required by the applicable clinical protocol (immediately for emergent findings; within 24 hours for significant changes per CL-CP-005); (c) Update the plan of care per CL-CP-002 if the conflict reflects a change in the patient's clinical status that affects care delivery; (d) Document the clinical response in the visit note separately from the OASIS reconciliation documentation. During or immediately following the visit; physician notification per CL-CP-005 timelines. 6.4.3 Assessing RN If the documentation conflict suggests a wound has been incorrectly staged in prior documentation — for example, if a wound previously documented as Stage 2 now appears to be a Stage 3 on direct observation — do not attempt to reconcile the conflict through staging — stage the wound accurately based on current direct observation per the NPIAP system, document the current stage with supporting clinical observations, and notify the physician within 24 hours. At the time of wound assessment; physician notification within 24 hours. 6.5 Escalation for Irresolvable Conflicts Step Responsible Party Action Timeframe 6.5.1 Assessing RN If a documentation conflict cannot be resolved through application of the evidence hierarchy — for example, because the assessor is genuinely uncertain whether the current clinical findings represent the patient's typical functional status or an atypical assessment-day performance — do not lock the assessment. Contact the Director of Nursing for clinical adjudication. Before locking; within the applicable OASIS completion deadline. 6.5.2 Director of Nursing Review the conflicting sources and the assessor's documentation. Provide clinical adjudication guidance — which source should govern and why. Document the adjudication guidance in the Director of Nursing's communication note in the patient's clinical record. Within 24 hours of escalation; adjusted for the OASIS completion deadline. 6.5.3 Director of Nursing If the conflict involves a clinical finding of significant concern — a potential new diagnosis, an unexpected deterioration, or a medication safety issue — contact the physician within the timeframes required by CL-CP-005. Document the physician contact, the information communicated, and the physician's response. Within 24 hours of identifying the clinically significant conflict; immediately for emergent concerns. 7. Purpose This policy requires that all SOC and ROC OASIS assessments at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. reflect the patient's clinical and functional status at the time of the actual assessment encounter — not at the time of discharge from the referring facility, not at the time the referral was received, not as described in the hospital's discharge summary, and not as the patient's condition was documented at a prior point in the care continuum. The point-in-time principle is one of the most foundational and most frequently violated rules of OASIS data collection. Its violation is also among the most consequential: when an assessment is coded to reflect discharge status rather than SOC status, it over-represents functional impairment at the start of the episode and over-represents functional improvement at discharge — producing inflated quality measure outcomes that misrepresent the agency's clinical performance. More significantly, coding a patient's SOC functional status from a hospital discharge summary rather than from direct clinical observation at the SOC visit frequently produces artificially high PDGM functional impairment scores — which generate higher PDGM payments. When this pattern is systematic, it constitutes the textbook definition of upcoding: claiming payment for a level of service complexity that was not present at the point of care delivery, based on documentation of a prior clinical state that no longer reflected the patient's actual status. The False Claims Act does not require intent to defraud — it requires only that a claim was submitted that the submitter knew, or should have known, was false or fraudulent. A systematic practice of coding SOC functional status from discharge summaries rather than direct observation, when the agency's assessors are trained and authorized to conduct direct observation, satisfies that standard. This policy establishes the point-in-time assessment requirement as an explicit, non-discretionary coding rule at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. — supplementing the broader evidence hierarchy in CL-OA-006 by specifically addressing the most common and highest-risk application of that hierarchy. 3. Scope This policy applies to all authorized OASIS assessors conducting SOC and ROC assessments, the OASIS Quality Reviewer, and the Director of Nursing. The point-in-time principle also applies at all other OASIS time points (RECERT, FU/SCIC, TRN, DC), where it is addressed in the respective time-point policies. This policy focuses specifically on the SOC and ROC context because these are the time points where the transition from a prior care setting creates the most significant risk of time-point confusion. 4. Policy Statement 4.1 Every SOC and ROC OASIS assessment at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall reflect the patient's clinical and functional status at the time of the SOC or ROC assessment visit — the actual date the assessing RN conducted the comprehensive assessment in the patient's home. For items with defined look-back periods, the look-back is measured backward from the SOC/ROC assessment date, not from the patient's discharge date. 4.2 The patient's functional status, symptom status, clinical severity, and behavioral health status as documented in the hospital or SNF discharge summary represents the patient's status at the time of hospital or SNF discharge — not at the time of the home health SOC assessment. These are different points in time, and the patient's clinical status may have changed substantially during the interval between discharge and the SOC visit. 4.3 The common clinical changes that occur between hospital discharge and home health SOC include: functional improvement from early mobility programs initiated in the hospital; functional decline from deconditioning that occurs during the interval; medication changes at discharge that affect functional or cognitive status; wound progression or healing; pain improvement from discharge analgesics or pain regression as IV medications are transitioned to oral; and anxiety or depression that emerges in the home environment but was not apparent in the institutional setting. 4.4 The assessing RN shall conduct and document a direct clinical assessment of the patient's current functional status during the SOC or ROC visit — using direct observation, physical examination, and standardized tool administration — and shall code all OASIS items from that direct clinical assessment. The discharge documentation from the referring facility shall be used for history items (diagnosis history, surgical history, hospitalization history) but shall not substitute for direct observation of current functional status. 4.5 When a patient's direct SOC/ROC clinical assessment reveals functional abilities greater than described in the discharge summary — a patient who performs GG activities at a lower assistance level than the discharge documentation would suggest — this is not a discrepancy to be &quot;corrected&quot; by using the discharge documentation instead. It is expected clinical information reflecting the normal trajectory of recovery and the patient's actual status at the assessment time point. The direct observation governs. 4.6 When a patient's direct SOC/ROC clinical assessment reveals clinical deterioration or a new finding not described in the discharge summary — a new wound, a higher assistance level for mobility than documented at discharge, a new behavioral symptom — this information shall be coded from the direct observation and shall trigger appropriate clinical responses per CL-CP-002 and CL-CP-005. 5. Definitions Term Definition Point-in-Time Assessment The OASIS data collection principle that each assessment reflects the patient's status at the specific, defined moment of the assessment encounter — not at any prior or subsequent time point. Assessment Time Point The specific clinical event that defines when the assessment is conducted and from which all look-back periods are measured — for SOC assessments, this is the date of the first billable visit. SOC Assessment Date The date of the first billable home health visit — the date that establishes the beginning of the look-back period for all SOC OASIS items with look-back requirements. ROC Assessment Date The date of the first billable visit following a patient's return from an inpatient facility — the date that establishes the ROC look-back period. Discharge Status The patient's functional, clinical, and cognitive status as documented at the time of discharge from a hospital, SNF, or other inpatient facility. Represents a prior time point; not equivalent to SOC or ROC assessment status. Look-Back Period The CMS-defined timeframe prior to the assessment date during which clinical information is relevant to coding a specific OASIS item — measured from the SOC/ROC assessment date, not the discharge date. Defined in detail in CL-OA-010. Status Drift The clinical change in a patient's functional or clinical status that occurs during the interval between hospital discharge and the home health SOC assessment visit. A normal feature of post-acute care transitions; must be captured through direct SOC assessment rather than assumed from discharge documentation. 6. Procedures 6.1 Pre-Visit Preparation — Point-in-Time Orientation Step Responsible Party Action Timeframe 6.1.1 Assessing RN Before each SOC or ROC visit, review the available referral documentation to understand the patient's clinical history, but do not use this review to pre-populate any functional or clinical severity OASIS responses. Pre-visit documentation serves the purpose of clinical preparation — identifying which assessments to perform, which standardized tools to administer, which conditions to evaluate — not the purpose of OASIS pre-coding. Before the assessment visit. 6.1.2 Assessing RN Note the interval between the patient's hospital or SNF discharge date and the SOC/ROC assessment date. Intervals greater than 48 hours represent a meaningful &quot;status drift&quot; window — clinical conditions may have changed substantially. Document the interval in the assessment narrative. Before or at the beginning of the assessment visit. 6.1.3 Assessing RN Remind yourself of the point-in-time principle before beginning the assessment: you are assessing the patient's status today, in this home, at this moment in time. You are not confirming the hospital's assessment findings. You are not extending the hospital stay's clinical picture into the home. You are conducting an independent, original clinical assessment. Before beginning the assessment. 6.2 Assessment Execution — Direct Current-Status Evaluation Step Responsible Party Action Timeframe 6.2.1 Assessing RN For every GG functional item applicable to the SOC OASIS, ask the patient to perform the activity during the assessment visit. Observe the performance. Code from the observation. If the patient's performance demonstrates greater independence than the discharge documentation suggested, code the greater independence. If the patient's performance demonstrates greater dependence than the discharge documentation suggested, code the greater dependence. The direct observation governs in both directions. During the assessment visit. 6.2.2 Assessing RN For clinical severity and symptom items — respiratory status, cardiac status, pain, nutritional status, bladder and bowel function — conduct the physical examination and patient interview that generates Level 1 or 2 evidence for the current time point. Do not assume clinical stability from the discharge documentation; assess current status directly. During the assessment visit. 6.2.3 Assessing RN For behavioral health items — administer the BIMS and PHQ-2/PHQ-9 during the current assessment visit, regardless of what behavioral health assessments the hospital may have conducted. The SOC BIMS reflects the patient's cognitive status in the home environment at the SOC time point. These are not interchangeable. During the assessment visit. 6.2.4 Assessing RN Conduct a complete skin and wound assessment at the SOC visit by undressing all dressings. Look-back periods are a fundamental structural feature of the OASIS instrument. They exist because many clinically important conditions — such as falls, incontinence episodes, behavioral symptoms, pressure injury occurrence, and high-risk medication use — cannot be fully assessed through a single-point-in-time clinical examination. They require information about what has happened to the patient over a defined preceding interval. The look-back period establishes the boundary of that interval — precisely enough that two assessors evaluating the same patient at the same time, using the same clinical information, should arrive at the same response for look-back-period items. When look-back periods are ignored — when an assessor codes a 7-day look-back item based on information from 30 days prior, or uses information from within the look-back period that was actually generated at a prior care setting rather than during the applicable home health period — the result is an OASIS response that does not accurately describe the patient at the applicable time point. This produces payment classification errors and quality measure distortions. The integrity of the entire OASIS program as a quality measurement and payment instrument depends on every agency applying look-back periods correctly and consistently. 3. Scope This policy applies to all authorized OASIS assessors, the OASIS Quality Reviewer, and the Director of Nursing. It governs all OASIS assessment time points where look-back period items are applicable. 4. Policy Statement 4.1 Every OASIS item with a CMS-defined look-back period shall be coded using only information that falls within that look-back period as measured backward from the applicable assessment date per the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual. Information outside the look-back period shall not be used as the basis for coding, regardless of its clinical relevance. 4.2 Look-back periods are measured from the assessment date — the date of the SOC visit, the ROC visit, the RECERT visit, or the FU assessment — not from any other reference point. For SOC assessments following hospital discharge, this means the look-back begins at the SOC assessment date and extends backward from that date. 4.3 When a look-back period overlaps with a prior care setting (hospital, SNF, prior home health episode), events that occurred within the look-back window at the prior care setting are included in the assessment. The setting in which an event occurred does not exclude it from the look-back window. An incontinence episode during a hospital stay that falls within the 14-day look-back of the SOC assessment date is within scope. 4.4 Information from outside the look-back period shall not be used to code look-back items, even when that information is more clinically compelling or more severely represents the patient's condition than information within the look-back window. The look-back boundary is a hard boundary. 4.5 When no information about the look-back period is available — for example, when a patient is transferred and the only functional information is from clinical records predating the look-back — the assessor shall document the limitation, apply the best available evidence within the look-back window, and use clinical reasoning documentation per CL-OA-012 to explain the coding decision. 5. Purpose This policy requires that all CMS-mandated standardized assessment tools — including the BIMS, PHQ-2/PHQ-9, and MAHC-10 — be administered by authorized assessors at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. in strict accordance with their validated administration protocols, and that any deviation from the validated protocol is explicitly documented with a clinical justification. Standardized assessment tools are not interchangeable clinical judgment instruments — they are psychometric instruments whose validity, reliability, and normative scoring frameworks depend entirely on administration according to the validated protocol. A BIMS administered without the word repetition component does not produce a valid BIMS score. A PHQ-9 where the assessor substitutes their own questions for the validated 9 items does not produce a valid PHQ-9 score. An abbreviated or modified tool produces data that is incomparable to the normative framework, cannot be interpreted using the validated scoring ranges, and cannot be relied upon for OASIS coding purposes. Beyond the psychometric validity issue, there is a compliance dimension: CMS OASIS item responses that are keyed to standardized tool scores — M1700 (BIMS), M1730 and M1750 (PHQ) — are required to be derived from the validated tools, not from clinical impression or informal assessment. Coding M1700 from clinical observation of the patient's general orientation without administering the BIMS is a coding rule violation per the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual, regardless of whether the resulting response is clinically plausible. 3. Scope This policy applies to all authorized OASIS assessors who administer standardized assessment tools, the OASIS Quality Reviewer who validates tool administration, and the Director of Nursing who oversees tool competency. 4. Policy Statement 4.1 The BIMS, PHQ-2/PHQ-9, and MAHC-10 (where applicable) shall be administered during every applicable OASIS assessment time point at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc., unless a documented clinical exception applies per Section 6.3. 4.2 Each tool shall be administered exactly as specified in its validated protocol and as required by the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual. No item shall be omitted, reworded, reordered, or supplemented with non-protocol questions. The validated administration protocol for each tool is specified in Section 6 of this policy. 4.3 Tool administration results shall be documented at the individual item level — not only as total scores — to demonstrate that each component of the tool was administered and scored individually per the validated protocol. 4.4 An OASIS response that is keyed to a standardized tool result shall not be coded from clinical impression, from prior assessment scores, from other assessors' reports, or from informal screening — unless a documented clinical exception applies per Section 6.3 and the applicable CMS guidance for the alternative pathway. 4.5 All authorized OASIS assessors shall demonstrate competency in the administration of every required standardized tool before conducting independent OASIS assessments, per CL-OA-003. Annual re-competency shall include re-demonstration of BIMS administration as a mandatory component. 5. Definitions Term Definition Validated Protocol The specific, published administration instructions for a standardized assessment tool — including the exact wording of items, the sequence of administration, the response option structure, the scoring methodology, and the normative interpretation framework — that constitute the tool as validated in the published research literature and as required by CMS. BIMS (Brief Interview for Mental Status) A CMS-developed standardized cognitive screening tool consisting of three components: word repetition (3 words), temporal orientation (year, month, day of week), and word recall. Scored 0–15. Required for coding M1700 when the patient can communicate. PHQ-2 (Patient Health Questionnaire — 2 Item) A validated two-question depression screening instrument requiring verbatim question reading and a 0–3 frequency response scale. Required as the initial screening for coding M1730 and M1750. PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire — 9 Item) A validated nine-question depression assessment instrument required when PHQ-2 score is ≥3 or when clinical presentation indicates depression. MAHC-10 (Missouri Alliance for Home Care — 10 Item) A validated fall risk assessment tool used in the OASIS context for applicable items. Clinical Exception A documented clinical circumstance in which the validated administration protocol cannot be completed in full — including patient refusal, communication limitations, cognitive inability, or medical instability — requiring use of an alternative pathway specified in the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual. SAMS (Staff Assessment for Mental Status) The alternative cognitive assessment pathway used when a patient cannot complete the BIMS — consisting of staff observation-based assessments of cognitive function. Used to code M1700 when BIMS cannot be administered. 6. Validated Administration Protocol: Step 1 — Introduction (do not skip): Say to the patient:  &quot;I am going to ask you some questions about your memory and thinking. Some of these questions will be easy, and some will be harder. Let's start with a short memory test.&quot; Step 2 — Word Repetition Component (Component A — scored 0–3): Say:  &quot;I am going to say three words, and I would like you to repeat them back to me right now, and remember them for a few minutes. The three words are: [sock], [blue], [bed].&quot;  Use these specific words. Do not substitute. Do not provide multiple repetitions — the words are stated once. Ask the patient to repeat them. Record how many of the three words the patient repeated correctly (0, 1, 2, or 3). Purpose This policy requires that when an OASIS coding decision involves clinical judgment beyond direct observation — because the evidence is ambiguous, the patient's performance varied across the look-back period, the assessment tool could not be administered in full, or the CMS Guidance Manual does not unambiguously resolve the specific clinical scenario — the authorized assessor at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. documents the specific clinical reasoning that supports the selected response. Clinical reasoning documentation is both the compliance defense and the clinical integrity mechanism for judgment-based coding decisions. Every OASIS item has a defined, correct response for every patient's clinical situation — but some items require clinical judgment to determine what that correct response is. When a patient's performance varies from day to day, and the item requires coding &quot;typical&quot; status, the determination of what is &quot;typical&quot; requires judgment. When a patient can complete 60% of a self-care activity independently but requires physical assistance for the remaining 40%, the selection of the appropriate GG response option requires judgment about the assistance level category. When the CMS Guidance Manual's response option definitions do not cleanly map to the patient's specific presentation, the assessor must apply clinical reasoning to identify the best-fit response. Clinical reasoning documentation does not excuse inaccurate coding — it documents the reasoning behind accurate coding that required judgment to arrive at. A well-documented reasoning process for a difficult coding decision is a clinical integrity statement. An undocumented judgment call that happens to produce an accurate response is a compliance vulnerability, because it cannot be evaluated or defended. 3. Scope This policy applies to all authorized OASIS assessors, the OASIS Quality Reviewer, and the Director of Nursing. 4. Policy Statement 4.1 When an OASIS coding decision requires clinical judgment beyond the direct application of documented clinical observations to a CMS Guidance Manual response option — specifically, when any of the triggering conditions in Section 5 apply — the assessor shall document the clinical reasoning that supports the selected response before locking the assessment. 4.2 Clinical reasoning documentation is required for judgment-based coding decisions; it is not required for routine coding decisions where the clinical evidence directly maps to the response option without ambiguity. The requirement is triggered by the conditions in Section 5, not applied universally to every item. 4.3 Clinical reasoning documentation shall include: the specific coding question being resolved; the relevant clinical evidence available; the alternative response options considered; the clinical reasoning applied to select among the options; and the selected response with its basis. The documentation shall be specific enough that a qualified OASIS assessor reviewing the record can evaluate whether the reasoning is clinically sound. 4.4 Clinical reasoning documentation does not substitute for clinical evidence. An assessor cannot document reasoning in place of conducting the assessment that would generate the evidence. If a GG item requires direct observation and the patient refused to perform the activity, the appropriate response is to document the refusal and apply the substitute evidence protocol per CL-OA-006 — not to document reasoning about what the patient's performance level &quot;probably is&quot; based on their diagnosis. 4.5 The OASIS Quality Reviewer shall specifically review clinical reasoning documentation during Level 2 quality reviews for all items coded under the triggering conditions in Section 5. Reasoning documentation that is absent, vague, or internally inconsistent shall be treated as an accuracy deficiency per CL-OA-002. 5. Triggering Conditions for Clinical Reasoning Documentation Clinical reasoning documentation is required when any of the following conditions apply to a coding decision: Condition 1 — Variable Patient Performance Across the Look-Back Period: The patient's performance of an activity or exhibition of a symptom varied significantly across the look-back window — some days better, some days worse — and the assessor must determine which response option best represents &quot;typical&quot; performance. The reasoning documentation shall describe the range of performance observed or reported, the frequency and pattern of better vs. worse days, and the basis for the typical performance determination. Condition 2 — Partial Activity Completion: The patient completes part of an activity independently and requires physical assistance for another part, and the activity is not sub-divided at that level in the OASIS item. The reasoning documentation shall describe the specific portions of the activity completed independently and with assistance, and the basis for the selected GG response option that most closely represents the overall assistance level. Condition 3 — Atypical Assessment Day Performance: The assessor has reason to believe that the patient's performance during the assessment visit was atypical — better or worse than their usual performance — due to a transient factor (acute pain, unusual fatigue, anxiety about the assessment, medication timing). The reasoning documentation shall describe the evidence of the atypical performance, the evidence about the patient's typical performance, and the assessment of which more accurately represents the patient's functional level per the CMS coding rules. Condition 4 — Standardized Tool Partial Administration: The standardized tool could not be administered in full due to a clinical exception per CL-OA-011 Section 6.3, and the assessor must code the OASIS item using an alternative pathway. The reasoning documentation shall describe the specific component(s) not administered, the clinical exception, and the alternative evidence used. Condition 5 — CMS Guidance Ambiguity: The CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual does not unambiguously resolve the coding decision for the patient's specific clinical situation. The reasoning documentation shall describe the specific ambiguity, the steps taken to resolve it (including any consultation with the Director of Nursing or OASIS contractor per CL-OA-004), and the clinical reasoning applied to the best-fit response. Condition 6 — Caregiver Performs Activities for Patient: The patient's caregiver routinely performs activities for the patient, making it difficult to assess the patient's actual performance ability versus their habitual assistance level. The reasoning documentation shall describe the evidence about the patient's actual ability (from direct observation, patient self-report, and caregiver report) versus the habitual assistance level, and the basis for the coded response. Condition 7 — Inferred Coding for Transfer OASIS: The Transfer OASIS is being completed retrospectively without the assessing RN having been present at the time of transfer. The reasoning documentation shall describe the sources used (last available visit note, patient/caregiver contact, hospital contact), the limitations of those sources, and the clinical reasoning applied to each coded response. 6. Clinical Reasoning Documentation Structure The following structured format shall be used for all clinical reasoning documentation: [Item Code] — Clinical Reasoning Entry: &quot;[OASIS item code and name]: Clinical judgment required for this coding decision. [Triggering condition]: [Describe the specific triggering condition]. Available evidence: [Describe the specific clinical evidence available — source, content, and evidence hierarchy level per CL-OA-006]. Alternative responses considered: [Identify the response options that were candidates]. Coding reasoning: [Explain why the selected response most accurately represents the patient's status per the applicable OASIS item definition]. Selected response: [State the response selected and confirm it is consistent with the CMS Guidance Manual definition for that response option].&quot; Example — Condition 1 (Variable Performance): &quot;GG0170B (Walking 10 Feet) — Clinical Reasoning Entry: Clinical judgment required. Triggering condition: Variable performance across the 7-day look-back. Available evidence (Level 4 — patient self-report corroborated by caregiver): Patient reports that on most days she walks from bedroom to bathroom (approximately 15 feet) without physical assistance, holding the wall for support. However, on two days this week (per caregiver report), patient required hand-holding assistance from the caregiver at the bathroom threshold due to increased dizziness following medication change on 7/3. Direct observation today (7/5): Patient walked 15 feet to the bathroom without physical contact assistance; required verbal cueing at the threshold. Alternative responses considered: 04 (Supervision or Touching Assistance — reflects typical performance most days and direct observation today) vs. 03 (Partial/Moderate Assistance — reflects the two days with hand-holding). Coding reasoning: Per CMS OASIS guidance for GG items, code reflects the patient's usual performance — the level that most commonly describes the patient. Patient performs independently or with supervision/touching on 5 of 7 days. The 2-day variance was associated with a new medication that has now been dose-adjusted. Typical performance is supervision/touching assistance. Selected response: 04 — Supervision or Touching Assistance.&quot; 7. Purpose This policy requires that prior to finalizing any comprehensive OASIS assessment at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc., the assessing registered nurse must reconcile assessment findings against all available documentation sources — including the referral record, physician orders, prior visit notes, medication reconciliation records, discipline-specific evaluations, and any external clinical documentation from referring facilities — to ensure that the completed assessment is internally consistent, externally corroborated where applicable, and free from material contradictions that would undermine its accuracy, integrity, or billing defensibility. Cross-document verification is the quality control bridge between data collection and submission. Without it, individually accurate OASIS responses may conflict with each other or with the broader clinical record in ways that neither the assessor nor the OASIS Quality Reviewer would detect without a structured side-by-side comparison. A functional status OASIS response that conflicts with the medication list (e.g., a patient coded as independent in medication management who has a documented cognitive impairment severe enough to require supervised medication dispensing), a wound item response that conflicts with the wound care visit note from two days prior, or a discharge summary clinical picture that conflicts with the SOC homebound documentation — each of these represents a cross-document inconsistency that creates survey deficiency risk, ADR audit vulnerability, and clinical care coordination failure simultaneously. This policy implements 42 CFR § 484.55(b) — requiring the comprehensive assessment to accurately reflect the patient's current health status — and 42 CFR § 484.110 — requiring clinical records to be complete, accurately written, and promptly completed. It is a companion policy to CL-OA-007 (Evidence-Based OASIS Coding Substantiation) and CL-OA-008 (Conflicting Documentation Source Resolution), which establish the substantiation and conflict resolution standards that cross-document verification enforces. 3. Scope This policy applies to: All registered nurses (RNs) authorized to conduct comprehensive OASIS assessments per CL-OA-003 The OASIS Quality Reviewer who conducts pre-submission and post-submission accuracy reviews The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager who oversees assessment completeness and accuracy All clinical disciplines whose documentation outputs are subject to cross-verification against OASIS assessments 4. Policy Statement 4.1 Prior to locking any comprehensive OASIS assessment, the assessing RN shall conduct a structured cross-document verification review using the Cross-Document Verification Checklist (Appendix A) to confirm that the completed assessment is consistent with all available documentation sources and free from material contradictions. 4.2 Cross-document verification shall include comparison of the completed OASIS assessment against each of the following document categories, to the extent each category is available at the time of assessment finalization: (a) referral documentation including hospital discharge summaries, SNF records, and physician office notes; (b) the current physician order set; (c) the plan of care in development or the prior plan of care (for ROC and RECERT time points); (d) the medication reconciliation record per CL-SD-013; (e) all visit notes created during the applicable look-back period; (f) discipline-specific evaluation reports (PT, OT, SLP, MSW) if available; (g) laboratory and diagnostic results available in the clinical record. 4.3 When the cross-document verification identifies a material inconsistency between the completed OASIS assessment and another document in the clinical record, the assessing RN shall resolve the inconsistency before locking the assessment, applying the documentation hierarchy per CL-OA-006 and the conflict resolution process per CL-OA-008. The resolution shall be documented in the assessment narrative per CL-OA-012. 4.4 The cross-document verification checklist (Appendix A) shall be completed and documented in the EHR for every OASIS assessment at every applicable time point. A checklist that is not completed is treated as a missing verification — equivalent to not having performed the verification. 4.5 The OASIS Quality Reviewer shall specifically assess cross-document verification checklist completion and adequacy as part of every Level 2 quality review per CL-OA-002. An assessment where the checklist is absent, incomplete, or where the reviewer identifies an unresolved inconsistency not flagged by the checklist shall be treated as a quality deficiency requiring correction. 4.6 Cross-document verification is not a guarantee of accuracy — it is a structured process for detecting detectable inconsistencies. The verification does not substitute for the underlying evidence-based coding standards of CL-OA-006 and CL-OA-007. Both the evidence quality and the cross-document consistency are required. 5. Definitions Term Definition Cross-Document Verification A structured review process in which the assessing RN compares the completed OASIS assessment item responses against all available documentation sources to identify and resolve material inconsistencies before locking the assessment. Material Inconsistency A discrepancy between an OASIS item response and a document in the clinical record that is clinically significant — meaning it reflects a genuine difference in documented clinical status rather than a difference in documentation style or format. Minor differences in terminology that describe the same clinical finding are not material inconsistencies. Internal Consistency The property of an OASIS assessment in which all items that logically relate to each other (e.g., functional mobility items, wound items, cognitive function items) produce a coherent clinical picture without contradicting each other. External Consistency The property of an OASIS assessment in which the item responses are consistent with the clinical information in other documents in the patient's record — including the physician orders, the plan of care, medication lists, and visit notes. Cross-Document Verification Checklist The structured form (Appendix A) that the assessing RN completes for every OASIS assessment to document that each category of available documentation has been reviewed and compared against the OASIS responses. Assessment Finalization The process of reviewing, correcting, and locking the completed OASIS assessment in the EHR, making it the definitive submitted record for the applicable assessment time point. 6. EHR mobile or dual-screen workflow is strongly recommended to enable simultaneous document review. During verification. 6.1.3 Assessing RN Open the Cross-Document Verification Checklist (Appendix A) in the EHR quality review module. The checklist shall remain open throughout the verification process. Surveyors will compare the OASIS clinical grouping diagnoses against the physician's current orders and the discharge summary diagnoses. Purpose This policy requires that all medication-related OASIS assessment items at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. be coded based on the patient's actual medication-taking behavior — what the patient is actually doing with their medications in their home — rather than on the prescribed medication regimen alone. This distinction is one of the most clinically significant and most consistently misapplied rules in OASIS data collection. The prescribed regimen describes the physician's intent. The patient's actual behavior describes clinical reality. For medication management OASIS items, clinical reality governs. A patient who has been prescribed a 10-medication daily regimen but who consistently takes only 4 of those medications, or who takes the correct medications but at incorrect doses or intervals, is not managing their medications per the prescribed regimen — and coding them as such produces an inaccurate OASIS item response that misrepresents the patient's risk level, understates the complexity of their care needs, and may contribute to the type of medication management failure that results in hospital readmission. The medication management OASIS items exist precisely to capture this gap — to distinguish between what is ordered and what is actually happening in the home. This policy establishes the assessment methodology, documentation standards, and coding guidance for all medication-related OASIS items, with specific focus on the distinction between the prescribed regimen and actual patient behavior, as required by 42 CFR § 484.55(b) (accurate reflection of current health status) and the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual medication item-specific instructions. 3. Scope This policy applies to all authorized OASIS assessors who complete medication management OASIS items, the OASIS Quality Reviewer, and the Director of Nursing. It also applies to all clinical disciplines who perform medication reviews during routine visits and who may identify medication adherence information relevant to OASIS coding. This policy governs OASIS coding for medication items at all applicable time points: SOC, ROC, RECERT, and DC. 4. Policy Statement 4.1 All medication management OASIS items shall be coded based on the patient's actual medication-taking behavior during the applicable look-back period — not based on the prescribed medication regimen, the physician's medication order, or the patient's stated intent to take medications as prescribed. 4.2 The assessment of actual medication-taking behavior shall be conducted through: (a) direct interview of the patient using standardized, open-ended questions about their medication use; (b) physical inventory and inspection of all medication bottles, containers, organizers, patches, and injectable supplies in the patient's home; (c) caregiver report of observed medication administration during the look-back period; (d) review of pharmacy refill history when accessible. 4.3 A drug regimen review per M2001 shall be conducted at every SOC, ROC, and RECERT assessment and shall compare the prescribed regimen — all physician-ordered medications — against the patient's actual medication inventory and self-reported adherence behavior, identifying all potential clinically significant medication issues per the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual definition of that term. 4.4 When a potential clinically significant medication issue is identified during the drug regimen review, the assessing RN shall contact the patient's physician within one calendar day of the assessment date, as required for M2003 coding. Documentation of the contact — including whether it occurred within the required timeframe — shall be present in the clinical record before the medication OASIS items are finalized. 4.5 Medication management OASIS items shall not be coded from the medication list alone. The medication list is a necessary input to the drug regimen review, but it does not constitute the assessment of actual patient behavior required for M2020 (Oral Medication Management), M2030 (Management of Injectable Medications), and related items. 4.6 The coded response for M2020, M2030, and related items shall reflect the patient's actual performance of medication management tasks — whether the patient is independently managing their medications, requiring assistance, unable to manage, or not applicable. The response shall be grounded in direct observation or corroborated patient/caregiver report per CL-OA-006, not in the assessor's inference about what a patient with particular diagnoses &quot;should&quot; be able to do. 5. Definitions Term Definition Prescribed Regimen The complete set of medications ordered by a physician or allowed practitioner for a patient — including drug name, dose, route, frequency, and indication — as reflected in the physician's current medication orders and the medication reconciliation record. Actual Patient Behavior The patient's real-world medication-taking practices as assessed during the assessment encounter — including which medications the patient actually takes, at what doses, at what frequencies, and with what level of assistance. Actual patient behavior is assessed through direct interview, physical inventory, and caregiver report, not inferred from the prescribed regimen. Drug Regimen Review The systematic comparison of all physician-ordered medications against all medications the patient is actually taking, intended to identify potential clinically significant medication issues including adverse drug reactions, duplicate therapy, drug-drug interactions, incorrect dosing, contraindicated medications, and medications prescribed without a documented indication. Required for M2001. Potential Clinically Significant Medication Issue Per the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual: any medication-related situation that the assessor determines warrants physician notification, including: adverse drug reactions; drug-drug interactions with potential for patient harm; medications prescribed at doses inconsistent with the patient's current clinical status (e.g., renal function); duplicate therapy; and medications taken without a current physician order. Medication Adherence The degree to which a patient takes their medications as prescribed — including the correct medication, at the correct dose, by the correct route, at the correct frequency, and for the correct duration. Medication Non-Adherence Any deviation from the prescribed regimen — including missed doses, incorrect dosing, incorrect timing, use of discontinued medications, or failure to take medications at all. Medication Management Ability The patient's functional capacity to independently manage their medication regimen — including the physical ability to open containers, read labels, and self-administer; the cognitive ability to remember and understand the regimen; and the organizational ability to maintain a consistent schedule. M2001 OASIS item: Drug Regimen Review. Asks whether the home health clinician reviewed all the patient's medications during the assessment visit. M2003 OASIS item: Medication Follow-Up. Asks whether a physician was contacted within one calendar day of the drug regimen review when a potential clinically significant medication issue was identified. M2010 OASIS item: Patient/Caregiver High-Risk Drug Education. Asks whether the patient/caregiver received education about high-risk medications as defined by CMS. M2020 OASIS item: Management of Oral Medications. Assesses the patient's ability and performance in managing their oral medication regimen. M2030 OASIS item: Management of Injectable Medications. Assesses the patient's ability and performance in managing injectable medications (e.g., insulin, enoxaparin). 6. Procedures 6.1 — Drug Regimen Review at Each Assessment Time Point (M2001) Step Responsible Party Action Timeframe 6.1.1 Assessing RN Before beginning the drug regimen review, compile the complete prescribed medication list from: (a) the physician's current medication orders; (b) the medication reconciliation worksheet per CL-SD-013; (c) the referring facility's discharge medication list (for SOC/ROC). This constitutes the &quot;prescribed regimen&quot; baseline against which actual behavior will be compared. Before the drug regimen review during the assessment visit. 6.1.2 Assessing RN Conduct the physical medication inventory: ask the patient/caregiver to bring ALL medications to the assessment area — prescription bottles, over-the-counter medications, vitamins, supplements, herbal products, patches, inhalers, injectable medications, and eye/ear drops. Visually inspect each: drug name, dose, prescriber, fill date, quantity remaining. Note any medication in the home that does not appear on the prescribed list, and any prescribed medication not present in the home. During the assessment visit. 6.1.3 Assessing RN Conduct the medication adherence interview using time-anchored, open-ended questions: &quot;Tell me how you take each of your medications — which ones do you take, when, and how many?&quot; &quot;Are there any medications you've stopped taking or decided not to take?&quot; &quot;Are there any medications you take more or less often than prescribed?&quot; Document the patient's responses verbatim or in close paraphrase — do not document only the conclusion. During the assessment visit. 6.1.4 Assessing RN Compare the physical inventory and adherence interview results against the prescribed regimen. For each prescribed medication, document: (a) whether the medication is present in the home; (b) whether the patient reports taking it; (c) whether the dose, route, and frequency match the prescription; (d) whether the quantity remaining is consistent with expected adherence (e.g., a 30-day supply with 5 pills remaining when the last refill was 28 days ago is consistent with adherence; 5 pills remaining when the last refill was 5 days ago suggests non-adherence). During the assessment visit. 6.1.5 Assessing RN Systematically review the complete medication list for potential clinically significant medication issues per the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual definition: (a) medications the patient is taking without a current physician order; (b) medications with known interaction risk with other medications on the list; (c) medications with doses that appear inappropriate given the patient's current clinical status; (d) duplicate therapy (two medications from the same drug class prescribed without documented rationale); (e) medications that appear to be causing an adverse reaction based on the patient's reported symptoms. During the assessment visit. 6.1.6 Assessing RN Code M2001 based on whether the drug regimen review was completed as described above. M2001 is a process measure — it is coded based on whether the review occurred, not on its findings. If the review was completed, M2001 = 0 (No problems found) or 1 (Problems found). If the review was not completed (patient unable to produce medications, patient refused), M2001 must be coded accordingly per the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual. Document the review methodology and findings per Section 6.4. After the drug regimen review is complete. 6.2 — Physician Notification for Identified Issues (M2003) Step Responsible Party Action Timeframe 6.2.1 Assessing RN When a potential clinically significant medication issue is identified during the drug regimen review, contact the patient's attending physician within one calendar day of the drug regimen review date. &quot;One calendar day&quot; means: if the drug regimen review is completed on July 5, physician contact must occur no later than July 6. Document the date and time of contact. Within one calendar day of the drug regimen review. 6.2.2 Assessing RN Document the physician contact including: the specific issue communicated; the physician's response; any orders received; and any clinical guidance provided. This documentation must be present in the clinical record before M2003 is finalized. Within 24 hours of the physician contact. 6.2.3 Assessing RN Code M2003 based on whether physician contact occurred within one calendar day when M2001 = 1 (problems found). M2003 is a process measure coded on timing: if physician was contacted within one calendar day, M2003 = 0 (Yes); if not contacted within one calendar day, M2003 = 1 (No). The reason for non-contact must be documented in the clinical record. After physician contact status is confirmed. 6.2.4 Assessing RN If the drug regimen review finds no potential clinically significant issues (M2001 = 0), M2003 is coded as &quot;NA&quot; — physician contact is not required when no issues are identified. Do not conflate M2001 = 0 (no issues found) with a failure to conduct the drug regimen review. At the time of M2003 coding. 6.3 — Oral and Injectable Medication Management Assessment (M2020, M2030) Step Responsible Party Action Timeframe 6.3.1 Assessing RN Assess M2020 (Oral Medication Management) based on the patient's actual performance of oral medication management tasks during the look-back period — not on what the patient could theoretically do. Ask: &quot;Do you take your own medications by yourself, or does someone help you?&quot; &quot;Do you set up your own medications, or does someone else prepare them for you?&quot; Observe the patient attempt to access and take a medication during the visit if clinically feasible. During the assessment visit. 6.3.2 Assessing RN Apply the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual response option definitions exactly as written for M2020: (0) Able to independently take the correct oral medication(s) and proper dosage(s) at the correct times; (1) Able to take medication(s) at the correct times if individual dosages are prepared in advance by another person; (2) Unable to take medication unless administered by another person. Select the response that reflects the patient's actual performance — not their best possible performance or the level the assessor believes they should achieve. At the time of M2020 coding. 6.3.3 Assessing RN Assess M2030 (Management of Injectable Medications) only when the patient is currently prescribed injectable medications (insulin, enoxaparin, other injectables). Assess the patient's actual performance of injectable medication management: &quot;Who prepares your injectable medication?&quot; &quot;Who administers it?&quot; &quot;Have you been taught to give it yourself?&quot; Observe if clinically feasible. Apply the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual response options exactly. During the assessment visit when injectables are prescribed. 6.3.4 Assessing RN Document the specific evidence supporting the M2020/M2030 coding: who was observed or interviewed, what they reported or demonstrated, and how that evidence maps to the selected response option. Documentation of &quot;patient manages medications independently&quot; without the specific basis for that conclusion is inadequate per CL-OA-007 substantiation standards. In the assessment narrative; before locking. 6.4 — Documentation Requirements for Medication OASIS Items Step Responsible Party Action Timeframe 6.4.1 Assessing RN Document the drug regimen review in the assessment narrative with the following elements: (a) the review methodology (physical inventory conducted, interview conducted, pharmacy profile accessed); (b) the number of medications reviewed; (c) any medications present in the home but not on the prescribed list; (d) any prescribed medications not present in the home; (e) any adherence concerns identified; (f) any potential clinically significant issues identified with specific description; (g) physician notification status (date, time, method, outcome) if issues were found. In the assessment narrative; before locking. 6.4.2 Assessing RN For M2020 and M2030, document: (a) what was directly observed or reported about the patient's medication management behavior; (b) whether the patient manages medications independently, with assistance, or requires full administration by another; (c) if the patient uses a medication organizer — who fills it and how frequently; (d) for injectables — who prepares and who administers. The documentation must be specific enough that the response option selected is the only defensible choice given the documented evidence. In the assessment narrative; before locking. 6.4.3 Assessing RN For M2010 (High-Risk Drug Education), document: (a) which high-risk medications as defined by CMS are in the patient's regimen; (b) that education specific to those medications was provided; (c) the specific educational content delivered; (d) the patient's/caregiver's demonstrated understanding. M2010 requires that education be specific to the patient's high-risk medications — general medication safety education does not satisfy this requirement. In the assessment narrative; before locking. 6.5 — Escalation and Exception Handling Condition Escalation Path Corrective Action Timeframe Patient is unable to produce any medications for review (no medications accessible in the home) Assessing RN documents and notifies Director of Nursing Document the specific reason no medications are accessible. Purpose This policy requires that for all OASIS items derived from multi-component standardized assessment tools — specifically the BIMS, PHQ-2, PHQ-9, and MAHC-10 — the individual component scores are correctly calculated, summed, and applied to the corresponding OASIS item responses in strict accordance with the scoring algorithms defined in the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual and the validated tool-specific scoring protocols at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Scoring methodology integrity is the technical precision layer of OASIS accuracy. A BIMS can be administered correctly — all three components in the correct sequence, using the correct items — but scored incorrectly, producing a total score that does not match the component responses. A PHQ-9 can be administered with the correct items in the correct order but have the frequency response options mis-scored (e.g., assigning 2 points for &quot;Several days&quot; instead of 1 point), producing a total score that systematically overstates or understates symptom severity. These scoring errors are distinct from administration errors (governed by CL-OA-011) and are distinct from coding errors (governed by CL-OA-004) — they are arithmetic and algorithmic failures in translating correctly administered tool results into the scores that drive OASIS item responses. 3. Scope This policy applies to all authorized OASIS assessors who administer and score the BIMS, PHQ-2/PHQ-9, and MAHC-10, and to the OASIS Quality Reviewer and Director of Nursing who validate scoring accuracy. 4. Policy Statement 4.1 The scoring of all multi-component standardized assessment tools shall follow exactly the scoring algorithms specified in the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual and the validated tool-specific scoring guides. No agency-created scoring shortcuts, simplified scoring algorithms, or score estimation methods shall be used. 4.2 Individual component scores shall be calculated independently for each component before the total score is computed. Total scores shall not be estimated from clinical impression or from recall of prior administration results. 4.3 The OASIS item response derived from each standardized tool result shall correspond exactly to the score range-to-response mapping specified in the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual. When a total score falls at a boundary between two response categories, the CMS guidance for that specific boundary shall govern the response selection. 4.4 All component scores and total scores shall be documented in the assessment narrative per CL-OA-011 before the derived OASIS item is coded. The coded OASIS response and the documented scores shall be internally consistent — an assessor cannot document a BIMS total of 14 (Cognitively Intact) and code M1700 at the Moderate Impairment level without a documented clinical explanation. 5. Purpose This policy requires that all clinical findings, observations, assessment data, standardized tool results, and clinical reasoning supporting OASIS assessment item responses at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. be documented at or near the time of the clinical encounter — not reconstructed from memory after the fact, not interpolated from subsequent clinical contacts, and not reverse-engineered from the OASIS item responses that were selected before the documentation was written. The contemporaneous documentation requirement is the foundational integrity standard of clinical recordkeeping. It applies broadly across all clinical documentation per CL-CD-001 and CL-CD-004, but carries heightened significance in the OASIS context because OASIS documentation directly drives regulatory compliance, billing classification, and public quality reporting in ways that make reconstruction-based documentation not merely a documentation shortcut but a clinical integrity failure with measurable systemic consequences. The EHR audit trail — which is immutable, timestamped, and accessible to CMS surveyors, MAC auditors, and OIG investigators — records when every entry was created and when every OASIS response was selected. A visit that occurred on July 5 with OASIS documentation created on July 9 is visible in the audit trail as a non-contemporaneous entry. An assessor who coded the OASIS responses on July 5 and then wrote the supporting narrative on July 9 is documenting in reverse sequence — and the audit trail will show this. This policy establishes the specific standards, timelines, and accountability requirements that prevent non-contemporaneous documentation from occurring and address it definitively when it does. 3. Scope This policy applies to all authorized OASIS assessors, the Director of Nursing, the OASIS Quality Reviewer, and Medical Records staff. It governs the timing and sequence of all clinical documentation created in connection with comprehensive OASIS assessments at all applicable time points. 4. Policy Statement 4.1 All clinical findings, physical examination results, patient and caregiver interview responses, standardized tool component scores and totals, and clinical reasoning entries that support OASIS item responses shall be documented in the EHR within 24 hours of the clinical encounter at which they were obtained. The 24-hour standard is the absolute maximum — same-day documentation is the agency's expected standard. 4.2 Clinical documentation supporting OASIS items shall be created in a sequence that reflects the actual clinical process: observations are made, data is collected, documentation is written, and OASIS responses are selected based on the documented evidence. The reverse sequence — selecting OASIS responses first and then writing documentation to support them — constitutes reverse-engineered documentation and is explicitly prohibited. 4.3 Late entry documentation — clinical narrative created after the 24-hour documentation standard of CL-CD-004 — is permissible when genuinely required by a documented clinical circumstance (clinical emergency during the visit, EHR system outage, assessor medical emergency). Late entries shall comply with the late entry standards of CO-DC-003, including the required attestation language identifying the actual date and time of documentation and the reason for the delay. Late entries that are created for the purpose of providing post-hoc justification for OASIS responses already coded do not constitute valid clinical documentation regardless of their content or the attestation applied. 4.4 OASIS item responses shall not be locked in the EHR without completed, authenticated supporting documentation in the assessment narrative. An OASIS item that is coded without corresponding narrative documentation at the time of coding is an unsupported response per CL-OA-007. The EHR workflow shall enforce narrative completion as a prerequisite for OASIS response entry where technically feasible. 4.5 The audit trail generated by the EHR for all OASIS-related documentation entries is a compliance record. It shall be maintained per CO-DC-001 and CO-HP-007, shall not be altered or deleted, and shall be available for review by the Compliance Officer, Director of Nursing, OASIS Quality Reviewer, CMS surveyors, and authorized audit entities. 5. Definitions Term Definition Contemporaneous Documentation Documentation created at or within 24 hours of the clinical encounter it describes, reflecting actual clinical observations and findings as they occurred — not reconstructed from memory after a longer interval. Non-Contemporaneous Documentation Documentation created more than 24 hours after the clinical encounter it describes, without a documented clinical justification for the delay. A form of late entry requiring compliance with CO-DC-003. Reverse-Engineered Documentation Documentation created for the specific purpose of justifying OASIS responses that were selected before the documentation was written. An integrity violation regardless of the accuracy of the underlying documentation. Distinguished from contemporaneous documentation in which the sequence is: observe → document → code. Late Entry Documentation created after the deadline applicable to the type of entry (24 hours for visit notes and OASIS supporting narratives per CL-CD-004), documented with a clear attestation identifying it as a late entry per CO-DC-003. Permissible when genuinely required by a documented external circumstance. Audit Trail Timestamp The EHR-generated, immutable record of the date and time each documentation entry was created, modified, or authenticated — maintained per CO-DC-001. Documentation Sequence The order in which clinical documentation entries are created in the EHR — the sequence of: (1) clinical encounter occurs; (2) clinical findings are documented; (3) OASIS items are coded from the documented findings. The correct sequence is 1→2→3; reverse-engineering inverts to 1→3→2. 6. Purpose This policy establishes the complete validated competency assessment process — including all assessment instruments, performance standards, evaluation methodologies, evaluator qualifications, documentation requirements, remediation protocols, and ongoing competency maintenance standards — for all clinicians authorized to independently conduct comprehensive OASIS assessments at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. This policy is the operational companion to CL-OA-003 (OASIS Clinician Authorization &amp; Competency), which defines the authorization structure and the OASIS Competency Program curriculum. CL-OA-018 defines how competency is measured, what tools are used to measure it, what passing standards apply, who is qualified to evaluate, and what happens when an assessor does not meet competency standards. The distinction between having completed a training program and having demonstrated competency is clinically and legally significant. An assessor who has sat through an OASIS training module has met a training attendance requirement. An assessor who has demonstrated OASIS competency through validated performance evaluation has met a clinical governance standard. Only the latter qualifies an assessor to independently produce OASIS assessments that are submitted to the federal government as the basis for Medicare payment. This policy establishes the standards that distinguish training attendance from demonstrated competency. 3. Scope This policy applies to all registered nurses who are candidates for OASIS assessor authorization, all currently authorized OASIS assessors, the Director of Nursing who serves as the designated Competency Evaluator, and the OASIS Quality Reviewer who has evaluator responsibilities for certain competency domains. 4. Policy Statement 4.1 All clinicians must demonstrate competency across the domains defined in Section 5 before being authorized to independently conduct OASIS assessments. Competency demonstration is distinct from training completion — it requires performance evaluation against defined standards, not merely attendance at or completion of training content. 4.2 Competency shall be evaluated by the Director of Nursing or a designated evaluator who: (a) holds current OASIS assessor authorization; (b) has administered the specific competency evaluation instruments used in this policy for at least 12 months; (c) has been designated in writing by the Director of Nursing as qualified to conduct competency evaluations for the specific competency domain being assessed. 4.3 Competency evaluation results — including all scores, evaluator observations, and determination of pass or fail — shall be documented using the OASIS Competency Validation Record (Appendix A) and filed in each assessor's personnel file per HR-WM-007. 4.4 Annual competency re-validation is required for all authorized assessors. The assessor may conduct OASIS assessments only in a supervised capacity during the suspension period. 4.6 An assessor who fails the same competency domain twice in any 12-month period shall have their OASIS assessor authorization revoked and shall be required to complete the full initial OASIS Competency Program before re-applying for authorization. 5. Purpose This policy establishes the structured pre-submission quality review process that every authorized OASIS assessor at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. must complete for every OASIS assessment at every applicable time point before locking and transmitting the assessment to the CMS repository. The pre-submission quality review is the final quality control gate between the assessor's work product and the federal data system — the last opportunity to identify and correct errors before they become compliance deficiencies, payment classification errors, or quality measure distortions. The pre-submission quality review is not a post-completion re-read. It is a structured, checklist-driven systematic review that verifies specific, predefined quality criteria for each major OASIS item category. Its purpose is to catch the categories of errors that are most common, most consequential, and most detectable at the pre-submission stage: items left at default values without clinical justification; internal inconsistencies between related item groups; items where the documented evidence does not support the coded response; standardized tool scores that are inconsistent with the coded OASIS item; and homebound status documentation that does not meet the standards of CL-CA-005. This policy establishes the complete Pre-Submission Quality Review Checklist (Appendix A), the required completion standards for each checklist section, the documentation requirements for the completed review, and the escalation process when the review identifies issues that cannot be resolved before the OASIS completion deadline. 3. Scope This policy applies to all authorized OASIS assessors, the OASIS Quality Reviewer, and the Director of Nursing. The Pre-Submission Quality Review Checklist (Appendix A) is the operative document of this policy — no assessment shall be locked without the checklist being completed. 4. Policy Statement 4.1 Every OASIS assessment at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall undergo a structured pre-submission quality review using the Pre-Submission Quality Review Checklist (Appendix A) before the assessment is locked in the EHR. The review shall be conducted by the assessing RN as a self-review after completing all OASIS item selections and all supporting narrative documentation. 4.2 The Pre-Submission Quality Review Checklist is a required clinical record document — its completion shall be recorded in the EHR OASIS quality review module with the assessor's electronic signature and the date and time of completion. An OASIS assessment that is locked without a completed, authenticated checklist is a documentation compliance deficiency. 4.3 When the pre-submission quality review identifies a correctable issue — an item response that is not supported by documentation, an internal inconsistency, a missing required element — the assessor shall correct the issue before locking the assessment. Do not lock the assessment with known uncorrected issues. 4.4 When the pre-submission quality review identifies an issue that cannot be immediately resolved by the assessing RN — for example, an ambiguous clinical situation requiring Director of Nursing adjudication, or a missing document that requires clinical judgment about its absence — the assessor shall document the issue in the checklist, escalate to the Director of Nursing, and delay locking until the issue is resolved or the Director of Nursing authorizes locking with the documented limitation. 4.5 The OASIS Quality Reviewer's Level 2 review per CL-OA-002 shall include verification that the Pre-Submission Quality Review Checklist was completed for the reviewed assessment and that the checklist findings are consistent with the Level 2 reviewer's independent assessment of the assessment quality. 4.6 The pre-submission quality review is the assessor's responsibility and accountability. It is not a substitute for the OASIS Quality Reviewer's Level 2 review — both are required. The pre-submission review catches issues the assessor can self-identify; the Level 2 review provides independent expert validation. 5. Definitions Term Definition Pre-Submission Quality Review The structured, checklist-driven review of a completed OASIS assessment conducted by the assessing RN before locking, using the Pre-Submission Quality Review Checklist (Appendix A) to verify completeness, internal consistency, evidence substantiation, and homebound documentation adequacy. Default Value The pre-populated or default response option that the EHR may apply to an OASIS item before the assessor makes a selection. An item left at its default value without a clinical basis for that value is not a valid assessment response. Internal Inconsistency A contradiction between two or more OASIS item responses within the same assessment that cannot simultaneously be true given the patient's clinical presentation — for example, coding a patient as requiring maximal assistance for all GG mobility items while coding them as having no pressure injuries, without clinical explanation. Completeness Check The verification that all OASIS items applicable to the current assessment time point have been addressed with a valid response — either a clinician-selected response or a documented not-applicable designation with clinical basis. Evidence Check The verification that the clinical narrative in the assessment contains documentation that supports the coded OASIS response for each item reviewed, per the substantiation standards of CL-OA-007. Pre-Submission Quality Review Checklist The structured form (Appendix A) that the assessing RN completes for every OASIS assessment before locking, systematically verifying quality criteria for each major OASIS item category. 6. Pre-Submission Quality Review — Required Review Sections The Pre-Submission Quality Review Checklist (Appendix A) consists of the following five review sections, each addressing a distinct quality dimension: Section 1 — Completeness Check Purpose: Verify that all applicable OASIS items for the current time point have been addressed with a clinician-selected response. Required Actions: Open the CMS OASIS-E2 item applicability table for the applicable time point (SOC, ROC, RECERT, FU, TRN, or DC). For each applicable item, verify that a response has been selected and that the response is not a default value that was not reviewed. For any item left blank, not applicable, or at a default value, verify that the clinical record contains a documented basis for that status (the patient's condition makes the item genuinely not applicable; the patient could not complete the required assessment). Flag and correct any item that appears incomplete without a documented clinical basis. Checklist Recording: Rate each item category as: ☐ Complete ☐ Incomplete — Issue Identified and Corrected ☐ Incomplete — Director of Nursing Consultation Required Section 2 — Internal Consistency Check Purpose: Verify that logically related OASIS items produce a coherent clinical picture without internal contradictions. Required Item-Pair Consistency Reviews: Item Pair Consistency Check GG0130 (Self-Care) and GG0170 (Mobility) The overall pattern of self-care and mobility independence should be clinically coherent — a patient who requires maximal assistance for all self-care activities would typically also require assistance for most mobility activities, absent a specific clinical explanation for the discrepancy M1700 (Cognitive Function / BIMS score) and M2020 (Oral Medication Management) A patient with severe cognitive impairment (BIMS 0–7) would not be expected to independently manage complex oral medication regimens — a discrepancy between high cognitive impairment and high medication management independence requires clinical explanation M1700 (Cognitive Function) and M1710/M1720 (Confusion/Anxiety frequency) The frequency of confusion/anxiety behavioral items should be consistent with the overall cognitive function rating GG mobility items and M1311 (Pressure Injury presence) A patient with high GG mobility independence (independent ambulation, transfer) would not typically develop pressure injuries attributable to immobility — any pressure injury in a highly mobile patient requires documented clinical explanation Respiratory severity items and GG0170 mobility activity items A patient with the highest respiratory impairment (dyspnea at rest) would be expected to have significant GG mobility limitations — a highly mobile patient also coded as having severe resting dyspnea requires clinical documentation of how the patient achieves that mobility level despite the respiratory severity Checklist Recording: Rate each pair as: ☐ Consistent ☐ Inconsistency Identified and Resolved (resolution documented in narrative) ☐ Inconsistency — Director of Nursing Consultation Required Section 3 — Evidence Substantiation Check Purpose: Verify that the clinical narrative contains documented evidence supporting the coded OASIS response for each high-risk item category, per CL-OA-007. PHQ-9 total → M1730/M1750 PHQ-9 total maps to the correct M1730/M1750 response per CMS guidance. Arithmetic verified (sum of 9 items). PHQ-9 Item 9 If PHQ-9 Item 9 score ≥1, suicidality assessment documentation present in clinical record. Checklist Recording: For each tool: ☐ Score arithmetic correct; response mapping correct ☐ Arithmetic/mapping error identified and corrected ☐ PHQ-9 required but not administered — immediate action required before locking Section 5 — Look-Back Period and Time-Point Accuracy Check Purpose: Verify that look-back periods were correctly applied and that the assessment reflects the correct OASIS time point. Compliance &amp; Audit Considerations 9.1 How Compliance Is Measured Compliance Indicator Measurement Method Acceptable Standard Pre-Submission Quality Review Checklist completed and authenticated before every OASIS lock EHR quality review module completion log ≥98% of locked assessments with completed, authenticated checklist All five checklist sections completed for each assessment Checklist completeness audit ≥98% of checklists with all five sections completed Issues identified in checklist are appropriately resolved before locking OASIS Quality Reviewer Level 2 review finding correlation ≥95% of issues identified in checklists have documented resolutions Assessments with consultation-required flags are not locked before Director of Nursing authorization EHR lock timestamp vs. Director of Nursing communication note timestamp 100% Level 2 review does not identify material issues absent from pre-submission checklist Quality Review Log audit — new issues identified at Level 2 not present in pre-submission checklist ≤5% of Level 2 reviews identify material issues not flagged in pre-submission checklist 9.2 What Surveyors and Auditors Will Look For CMS surveyors conducting OASIS validation surveys will request evidence of the agency's OASIS quality assurance process. The Pre-Submission Quality Review Checklist — when consistently completed and when it demonstrates a pattern of assessors actively identifying and resolving issues before submission — is direct evidence of a functioning OASIS quality governance program. An agency that cannot demonstrate a systematic pre-submission review process has no structural mechanism for preventing OASIS errors that are detectable before submission. ADR auditors will look for evidence that errors that could have been caught before submission were not caught — particularly internal consistency errors (BIMS score inconsistent with M1700 coding) and substantiation gaps (GG items with no activity-specific observation documentation). These are precisely the errors a properly conducted pre-submission review should catch. When an ADR auditor finds them, the absence of a documented pre-submission review suggests the agency lacked the quality governance to prevent them. 9.3 Common Failure Points Failure Point Risk Mitigation Pre-submission review treated as a checkbox exercise without genuine item-by-item review Errors that should be caught before submission are not caught; Level 2 review finds issues that were reviewable at pre-submission Time allocation minimums in Section 6; Director of Nursing reviews checklist completion times in monthly audit Section 4 (tool score consistency) skipped because assessor believes scores are correct PHQ/BIMS scoring errors not caught; M1700/M1730 miscoding Make Section 4 mandatory with required score recalculation; OASIS Quality Reviewer validates score recalculation at Level 2 Assessor locks assessment before completing Section 3 (evidence substantiation) because the narrative feels adequate Substantiation gaps not identified; OASIS locked without supporting narrative EHR workflow requires Section 3 completion rating before lock button is enabled No documentation that the checklist was completed — assessors sign but do not complete No evidence of compliance with the review requirement EHR checklist requires each section to be rated before the attestation signature can be applied 10.
