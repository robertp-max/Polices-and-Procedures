# Module M06 Blueprint

## 1. Module Overview
- Module ID: M6
- Module Name: Fraud, Waste, and Abuse
- Track: A (Core Compliance Foundation)
- Version: 1.0.0
- Status: Drafted
- Estimated Duration: 25-28 minutes
- Training Type: Awareness-level
- Target Roles: RN, LVN, CNA, HHA, Admin, DON, Compliance, HR
- Policy Source: FWA Prevention Policy, False Claims Act Policy, Billing Compliance Policy

### Detailed Overview
Fraud, waste, and abuse (FWA) in healthcare causes significant harm to patients, payers, and agencies. This module gives all workforce members the awareness needed to recognize FWA, understand the applicable laws, and report concerns appropriately.

### Detailed Description
Four sections cover FWA definitions and prevention, the False Claims Act and Anti-Kickback Statute, documentation and billing alignment, and overpayment rules and exclusion screening. A 7-question assessment covers key legal and behavioral scenarios.

## 2. Learning Objectives
- Define fraud, waste, and abuse in home health.
- Identify prohibited conduct under the False Claims Act and Anti-Kickback Statute.
- Recognize billing red flags and documentation integrity requirements.
- Explain overpayment repayment obligations and exclusion screening.

## 4. Section A - Fraud, Waste, and Abuse Prevention

**Lessons Covered:** 33

**Plain Language Content**
- Fraud: intentional misrepresentation or deception to obtain unauthorized payment.
- Waste: overuse or inefficient use of resources without deceptive intent.
- Abuse: practices that are inconsistent with sound clinical or business standards and result in unnecessary costs.
- All three can result in government penalties, exclusion from federal programs, and criminal liability.

**Home Health Examples**
- RN documents a visit that never occurred to meet a productivity requirement — this is fraud.
- Admin fails to check authorizations, resulting in services delivered beyond approved limits — this is waste.
- A clinician schedules more visits than the care plan supports without clinical justification — this is abuse.

**Role Awareness Cues**
- All roles: report suspected FWA through the compliance hotline or supervisor.
- Clinical roles: never document services not delivered.
- Compliance: maintain FWA monitoring and trend reporting.

---

## 5. Section B - False Claims Act and Anti-Kickback Statute

**Lessons Covered:** 34, 35

**Plain Language Content**
- The False Claims Act (FCA) creates civil and criminal liability for knowingly submitting false claims to Medicare or Medicaid.
- Penalties include up to three times the false claim amount plus per-claim fines.
- The Anti-Kickback Statute (AKS) prohibits receiving or offering anything of value to induce or reward referrals to federally funded programs.

**Home Health Examples**
- Submitting a claim for a skilled visit that the patient refused is a False Claim.
- Offering a physician's office staff lunches in exchange for referrals violates the Anti-Kickback Statute.
- Billing for a higher level of service than documented violates both statutes.

**Role Awareness Cues**
- All roles: do not participate in financial arrangements that could influence referrals.
- Clinical roles: ensure documentation supports every submitted claim.
- Admin/Compliance: screen all referral relationships for AKS compliance.

---

## 6. Section C - Documentation-to-Claims Alignment and Billing Red Flags

**Lessons Covered:** 36, 38

**Plain Language Content**
- Every billed service must be supported by complete, accurate, and contemporaneous documentation.
- Billing red flags: billing for services when patient is hospitalized, duplicate billing, billing without documented physician orders, billing above the care plan frequency.
- Incomplete documentation is not merely an administrative issue — it creates billing liability.

**Home Health Examples**
- RN discovers a patient was hospitalized on a date when a visit was already billed; reports discrepancy to compliance immediately.
- Admin flags a claim for a date when scheduling records show no visit was conducted.
- Compliance reviews documentation-to-claims audit reports for discrepancies monthly.

**Role Awareness Cues**
- Clinical roles: document every visit completely, on time, and accurately.
- Admin: monitor billing accuracy and flag discrepancies for review.
- Compliance: run documentation-to-claims audits and investigate findings.

---

## 7. Section D - Overpayment, Repayment, and Exclusion Screening

**Lessons Covered:** 37, 39

**Plain Language Content**
- When an overpayment is identified, federal law requires it to be reported and returned within 60 days.
- Failure to return a known overpayment can itself become a False Claim.
- Exclusion screening: the agency cannot employ or contract anyone excluded from federal health programs; screening must occur at hire and periodically thereafter.

**Home Health Examples**
- Compliance identifies a billing error resulting in overpayment and initiates a repayment process within the 60-day window.
- HR runs exclusion screening on all new hires through the OIG exclusion database before employment begins.
- Admin re-screens vendors and contractors on a regular schedule.

**Role Awareness Cues**
- Compliance: run and document exclusion screens; manage overpayment processes.
- HR: embed exclusion screening into the hire process.
- All roles: report suspected overpayments or improperly paid claims immediately.

---

## 8. Summary
- Fraud is intentional; waste and abuse may not be — all three are prohibited and reportable.
- FCA and AKS create serious legal liability for billing and referral misconduct.
- Every claim must match documentation; report billing red flags immediately.
- Return overpayments within 60 days and verify exclusion status before hire.

## 9. Assessment

**Format:** 7 MCQ | Pass: 80% (6/7)

**Q1.** Fraud in home health is best defined as:
- A. Billing for services that were actually provided
- B. Intentionally billing for services not provided or falsifying documentation for financial gain
- C. Making scheduling mistakes
- D. Being late to a patient visit
- **Correct: B** | Fraud requires intent and results in improper payment.

**Q2.** The False Claims Act applies to:
- A. Internal agency accounting only
- B. Knowingly submitting false or fraudulent claims to federal health programs
- C. Documenting patient care thoroughly
- D. Using electronic health records
- **Correct: B** | FCA applies to claims submitted to Medicare, Medicaid, and other federal programs.

**Q3.** Anti-Kickback prohibitions include:
- A. Paying for referrals or receiving compensation for directing patients to specific services
- B. Accepting standard conference registrations
- C. Providing discounts to patients
- D. Scheduling multiple visits per week
- **Correct: A** | AKS prohibits financial arrangements that could influence referrals.

**Q4.** A billing red flag includes:
- A. Documenting completed visits accurately
- B. Billing for services on a date when the patient was hospitalized
- C. Updating care plans quarterly
- D. Submitting claims within timely filing limits
- **Correct: B** | Billing while the patient was hospitalized is a classic fraud indicator.

**Q5.** The 60-day repayment rule requires:
- A. All claims must be billed within 60 days
- B. Identified overpayments must be reported and returned within 60 days
- C. Patients have 60 days to review their EOB
- D. The agency has 60 days to respond to audit findings
- **Correct: B** | Federal law requires prompt return of identified overpayments.

**Q6.** Exclusion screening ensures:
- A. Employees are checked for drug use
- B. No excluded individuals or entities are employed or contracted by the agency
- C. All vendors are licensed
- D. Background checks are completed within 30 days
- **Correct: B** | Excluded individuals cannot participate in federally funded programs.

**Q7.** Waste differs from fraud in that:
- A. Waste always involves intent to deceive
- B. Waste is overuse or inefficient use of resources without intentional deception
- C. Waste is not subject to compliance oversight
- D. Waste applies only to clinical staff
- **Correct: B** | Waste lacks fraudulent intent but still creates compliance and financial risk.
