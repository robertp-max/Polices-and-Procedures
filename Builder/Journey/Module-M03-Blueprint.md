# Module M03 Blueprint

## 1. Module Overview
- Module ID: M3
- Module Name: Ethics and Respectful Communication
- Track: A (Core Compliance Foundation)
- Version: 1.0.0
- Status: Drafted
- Estimated Duration: 22-25 minutes
- Training Type: Awareness-level
- Target Roles: RN, LVN, CNA, HHA, Admin, DON, Compliance, HR
- Policy Source: Code of Ethics, Cultural Competency Policy, Mandatory Reporting Policy

### Detailed Overview
Ethical, respectful practice is a cornerstone of home health care. This module covers cultural awareness, dignified care, patient autonomy, abuse and neglect identification, and ethical decision-making as applied in home-based care delivery.

### Detailed Description
Learners work through four sections covering cultural communication, patient dignity and autonomy, mandatory reporting obligations, and ethics. Real home health scenarios build recognition of ethical issues and correct responses. A 6-question assessment validates knowledge.

## 2. Learning Objectives
- Apply culturally competent communication practices.
- Uphold patient dignity and autonomy in all interactions.
- Identify and report suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation.
- Recognize ethical obligations in home health care delivery.

## 3. Screen Flow and Time Budget
- Intro: 2 min
- Section A - Cultural Awareness and Communication: 6 min
- Section B - Patient Dignity and Autonomy: 5 min
- Section C - Abuse, Neglect, and Mandatory Reporting: 7 min
- Section D - Ethics in Home Health: 4 min
- Summary: 2 min
- Assessment: 3 min
- Total: 29 minutes (adjusted to 25 with concise delivery)

## 4. Section A - Cultural Awareness and Respectful Communication

**Lessons Covered:** 17, 18

**Plain Language Content**
- Respectful care requires awareness of the patient's cultural background, language, and communication preferences.
- Use qualified interpreters when language barriers exist; do not rely on family members to translate.
- Avoid assumptions about beliefs, preferences, or practices based on cultural background.

**Home Health Examples**
- An HHA adjusts her communication approach when caring for a patient whose primary language is Spanish and requests a qualified interpreter.
- A CNA learns that a patient from a specific cultural background prefers same-gender caregivers and accommodates the request.
- Admin ensures translation services are available at the time of intake.

**Role Awareness Cues**
- All clinical roles: ask about communication and cultural preferences early and document them.
- Admin: arrange interpreter services at intake.
- DON/Compliance: ensure cultural competency practices are trained and monitored.

---

## 5. Section B - Patient Dignity and Autonomy

**Lessons Covered:** 19

**Plain Language Content**
- Every patient has the right to participate in their care decisions regardless of cognitive or physical status.
- Care is delivered in a way that preserves dignity, privacy, and individual preferences.
- Autonomy means the patient can accept or refuse care, even when the clinical team disagrees.

**Home Health Examples**
- An RN includes the patient in care planning discussions, documents their preferences, and updates the care plan accordingly.
- A CNA closes the door during personal care to preserve privacy.
- HHA allows the patient to choose the order of morning care activities.

**Role Awareness Cues**
- All roles: interact with patients as individuals, not conditions.
- Clinical roles: document patient preferences and honor them in the care plan.
- DON: monitor care delivery practices for dignity compliance.

---

## 6. Section C - Abuse, Neglect, Exploitation, and Mandatory Reporting

**Lessons Covered:** 20

**Plain Language Content**
- Abuse includes physical, emotional, sexual, or financial harm inflicted on a patient.
- Neglect is failure to provide necessary care.
- Exploitation is taking advantage of a patient's vulnerability for personal gain.
- All home health staff are mandatory reporters; suspicion alone triggers the reporting obligation.

**Home Health Examples**
- HHA observes unexplained bruising and reports to the supervising nurse immediately.
- CNA notices a family member taking cash from the patient's wallet and reports to the supervisor.
- RN observes a patient who appears malnourished and un-bathed and reports as potential neglect.

**Role Awareness Cues**
- All roles: report any suspicion to the supervisor immediately; do not investigate independently.
- Clinical roles: document observations objectively and specifically.
- Compliance/DON: initiate agency investigation protocol and mandatory APS/CPS reporting.

---

## 7. Section D - Ethics in Home Health

**Lessons Covered:** 21

**Plain Language Content**
- Ethical practice means acting in the patient's best interest, being honest, and avoiding conflicts of interest.
- Ethical dilemmas in home health often involve disagreements between patient wishes and family preferences.
- When ethical concerns arise, escalate through the supervisor or agency ethics process.

**Home Health Examples**
- RN faces a situation where a family wants to withhold a diagnosis from the patient; escalates to DON for ethical guidance.
- HHA is offered a personal gift by a patient; declines per agency policy and reports the offer.
- Admin staff member is asked to alter documentation; refuses and reports to compliance.

**Role Awareness Cues**
- All roles: know the difference between following orders and acting unethically.
- Clinical roles: document ethical concerns and escalate appropriately.
- Compliance: maintain an accessible ethics consultation pathway.

---

## 8. Summary
- Cultural competency and interpreter use protect patient rights and ensure safe communication.
- Patient dignity and autonomy must be actively upheld.
- Report any suspicion of abuse, neglect, or exploitation immediately.
- Ethical conduct means acting in the patient's best interest at all times.

## 9. Assessment

**Format:** 6 MCQ and scenario-MCQ | Pass: 80% (5/6)

**Q1.** A patient speaks limited English. The appropriate action before providing care instructions is:
- A. Skip the instructions
- B. Use a qualified interpreter per agency policy
- C. Ask a family member to interpret
- D. Provide English-only written materials
- **Correct: B** | Qualified interpreters ensure accuracy and protect patient rights.

**Q2.** An HHA observes unexplained bruising on a patient. The mandatory action is:
- A. Note it in personal records only
- B. Ask the family what happened first
- C. Report to the supervising nurse immediately per mandatory reporting policy
- D. Wait to see if more injuries appear
- **Correct: C** | Suspicion alone triggers mandatory reporting; do not investigate independently.

**Q3.** Which behavior best reflects patient dignity?
- A. Making care decisions without asking the patient
- B. Including the patient in care planning and respecting their preferences
- C. Rushing through care to complete more visits
- D. Discussing patient details in the hallway
- **Correct: B** | Dignity requires active inclusion and individualized respect.

**Q4.** A caregiver makes assumptions about a patient's preferences based on cultural background. The ethical concern is:
- A. No concern; assumptions are acceptable
- B. Cultural assumptions compromise person-centered care
- C. Only medical decisions require cultural awareness
- D. Cultural differences are administrative matters
- **Correct: B** | Care must be individualized and free from cultural assumptions.

**Q5.** What does ethics in home health require of all staff?
- A. Completing tasks quickly
- B. Acting honestly and in the patient's best interest at all times
- C. Following only direct orders
- D. Deferring all decisions to the physician
- **Correct: B** | Ethical conduct requires honesty and patient-centered decision-making.

**Q6.** A patient's family asks the nurse not to tell the patient about their diagnosis. The ethical obligation is:
- A. Follow the family's request
- B. Escalate to DON and follow agency ethics process; patients have a right to information about their condition
- C. Tell the patient immediately without consulting the team
- D. Document informally and defer the conversation
- **Correct: B** | Patient rights to information must be balanced through proper ethical process.
