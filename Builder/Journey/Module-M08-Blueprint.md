# Module M08 Blueprint

## 1. Module Overview
- Module ID: M8
- Module Name: Employee Health and Exposure Response
- Track: B (Clinical and Patient Care)
- Version: 1.0.0
- Status: Drafted
- Estimated Duration: 25-28 minutes
- Training Type: Awareness-level
- Target Roles: RN, LVN, CNA, HHA, Admin, DON, Compliance, HR
- Policy Source: Bloodborne Pathogen Policy, TB Policy, Exposure Response Policy, Employee Health Policy

### Detailed Overview
Employee health requirements protect staff, patients, and the public. This module covers occupational exposure risks, vaccination programs, communicable disease reporting, and the procedures for responding to occupational exposure events.

## 2. Learning Objectives
- Identify bloodborne pathogen and TB exposure controls.
- Apply exposure response procedures after an occupational incident.
- Explain communicable disease reporting obligations.
- Complete employee health clearance requirements.

## 3. Screen Flow and Time Budget
- Intro: 2 min | Section A: 7 min | Section B: 6 min | Section C: 5 min | Section D: 5 min | Summary: 2 min | Assessment: 3 min
- Total: 30 minutes

---

## 4. Section A - Bloodborne Pathogens and TB

**Lessons Covered:** 43, 44

**Plain Language Content**
- Bloodborne pathogens (HIV, Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C) are transmitted through blood and certain body fluids.
- Use safety-engineered sharps devices; never recap needles; dispose of sharps immediately in approved containers.
- TB screening is required at hire and annually; identify any patient with TB risk before care begins.

**Home Health Examples**
- RN uses a safety needle and immediately disposes of it in the sharps container after insulin administration.
- HHA reports a patient with a persistent productive cough to the supervising nurse before providing care.
- New hire completes TB test within 7 days of hire per HR onboarding protocol.

**Role Awareness Cues**
- All clinical roles: follow sharps safety and TB screening protocols without exception.
- HR: track TB testing compliance for all clinical staff.
- DON: escalate TB risk flags immediately.

---

## 5. Section B - Aerosol Transmissible Diseases, Hepatitis B, and Communicable Disease

**Lessons Covered:** 45, 46, 47

**Plain Language Content**
- Aerosol transmissible diseases (e.g., tuberculosis, measles) require respiratory precautions (N95 or equivalent).
- The agency must offer Hepatitis B vaccination to at-risk employees at no cost; staff may decline in writing.
- Notifiable communicable diseases must be reported to California Department of Public Health per mandated timelines.

**Home Health Examples**
- RN discovers a patient is on airborne precautions in a prior facility record; uses N95 for the visit.
- New clinical hire accepts Hep B vaccine series during onboarding.
- Compliance files a communicable disease report after a confirmed TB exposure incident.

**Role Awareness Cues**
- Clinical roles: recognize ATD risk and escalate before entering.
- HR: document Hep B vaccination offers and declinations.
- Compliance: manage communicable disease reporting to public health.

---

## 6. Section C - Exposure Response

**Lessons Covered:** 48

**Plain Language Content**
- If an exposure occurs (needlestick, splash, or contact with infectious materials), wash the area immediately with soap and water.
- Report the exposure to the supervisor immediately — same day, same shift.
- The agency activates a post-exposure evaluation, which may include testing, prophylaxis, and follow-up.

**Home Health Examples**
- CNA sustains a needlestick while disposing of sharps; washes the site and calls the supervisor within minutes.
- RN receives a splash of blood in the eye during wound care; irrigates with water and reports immediately.
- HR coordinates post-exposure evaluation appointment and documents the incident.

**Role Awareness Cues**
- All clinical roles: know the exposure response steps and report immediately.
- HR: coordinate post-exposure evaluation and documentation.
- Compliance: track exposures for OSHA recordkeeping and trend analysis.

---

## 7. Section D - Employee Health Clearance and Return-to-Work

**Lessons Covered:** 49, 50

**Plain Language Content**
- All new clinical staff must complete required health screenings before providing patient care.
- Clearance includes TB test, Hep B vaccination offer, and any other required health documentation.
- Return-to-work after illness or injury requires documented medical clearance before resuming patient care duties.

**Home Health Examples**
- New HHA completes onboarding health clearance including TB test before first patient assignment.
- RN recovering from influenza obtains physician clearance before returning to field visits.
- HR maintains a clearance tracker and notifies the DON when clearances are complete or expired.

**Role Awareness Cues**
- Clinical roles: complete clearance requirements and return-to-work documentation.
- HR: maintain clearance records and send timely renewal reminders.
- DON: do not assign staff to patient care until clearance is confirmed.

---

## 8. Summary
- Use safety devices for sharps; complete TB screening at hire and annually.
- Accept or formally decline Hep B vaccination; report notifiable diseases per law.
- Report any occupational exposure immediately and activate post-exposure protocol.
- Clearance is required before first patient care assignment and after illness.

## 9. Assessment

**Format:** 7 MCQ | Pass: 80% (6/7)

**Q1.** Which practice reduces bloodborne pathogen exposure?
- A. Recapping needles after use
- B. Using safety-engineered sharps and disposing immediately in approved containers
- C. Keeping used sharps in pockets until disposal
- D. Wiping sharps before disposal
- **Correct: B** | Engineered devices and immediate disposal are primary controls.

**Q2.** TB screening is required because:
- A. It is a regulatory formality only
- B. Home health staff may be exposed to patients with active or latent TB
- C. All staff have TB immunity
- D. TB is no longer a clinical concern
- **Correct: B** | Exposure risk in home settings requires ongoing screening.

**Q3.** After a needlestick, the immediate first action is:
- A. Finish the visit, then report
- B. Wash the area, report to supervisor immediately, and follow exposure protocol
- C. Apply a bandage and monitor
- D. Contact the patient's physician first
- **Correct: B** | Immediate washing and reporting are the required first steps.

**Q4.** The Hepatitis B vaccination offer must be:
- A. Provided only after a known exposure
- B. Made to at-risk employees at agency expense before or during onboarding; declination must be documented
- C. Optional with no documentation required
- D. Only for new hires under 30
- **Correct: B** | OSHA requires the vaccination offer and documentation of acceptance or declination.

**Q5.** Return-to-work after illness requires:
- A. Self-reporting that the employee feels better
- B. Medical clearance per agency policy before resuming patient care
- C. Supervisor verbal approval only
- D. A 24-hour waiting period only
- **Correct: B** | Formal clearance protects patients and staff.

**Q6.** Communicable disease reporting means:
- A. Informing coworkers only
- B. Reporting notifiable conditions to public health per California law and agency policy
- C. Only informing the patient's family
- D. Logging in personal notes
- **Correct: B** | California mandates reporting notifiable diseases to public health.

**Q7.** Employee health clearance before first patient care requires:
- A. A handshake agreement with the supervisor
- B. Completion of required screenings, vaccinations, and health documentation per agency and regulatory standards
- C. An ID badge only
- D. One TB test with no follow-up
- **Correct: B** | Pre-employment health requirements protect patients and staff.
