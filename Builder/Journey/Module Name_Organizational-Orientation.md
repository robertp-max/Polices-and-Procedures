# 1. Module Overview

**Module ID:** M1  
**Module Name:** Organizational Orientation (Compressed)  
**Training Type:** Fast onboarding, awareness-level  
**Estimated Duration:** 24-28 minutes (within 20-30 minute limit)  
**Source Basis:** Policy and Procedure (PP) awareness content only  
**Delivery Mode:** LMS self-paced with section navigation and progress tracking

**Detailed Overview**

This module gives new workforce members a fast, high-level orientation to how the agency operates, what standards must be followed, and how to safely engage with systems and reporting channels from day one. It establishes a shared baseline across clinical and non-clinical roles before deeper role-specific and regulatory modules.

**Detailed Description**

Learners complete one intro screen, four focused content sections, one summary screen, and a short assessment. Content emphasizes practical expectations: understanding mission and structure, acting ethically, reporting concerns, following workforce rules, and protecting agency data. The design uses concise screens, real home health examples, and clear accountability language to support immediate readiness and auditable completion.

**Target Audience**

- RN
- LVN
- CNA
- HHA
- Admin staff
- Director of Nursing (DON)
- Compliance staff
- HR staff
- IT staff

**Learning Objectives**

- Explain agency mission, structure, and scope of services.
- Identify ethics and compliance reporting pathways.
- Apply workforce expectations for timekeeping, attendance, and policy acknowledgment.
- Demonstrate baseline IT acceptable use and security awareness.
- Complete module requirements for onboarding progression and system enablement.

**Screen Flow and Time Budget**

- Intro screen: 2 minutes
- Section A - Organization: 5 minutes
- Section B - Ethics and Compliance Awareness: 8 minutes
- Section C - Workforce Rules and Expectations: 4 minutes
- Section D - IT and Security Basics: 4 minutes
- Summary screen: 2 minutes
- Assessment (6 items): 3 minutes

Total: 28 minutes

# 2. Section A - Organization

**Lessons Covered:**  
1) Agency mission, vision, values  
2) Organizational structure and reporting lines  
3) Scope of services

**Plain Language Content**

- Mission, vision, and values define how we serve patients at home with safety, respect, and reliability.
- Reporting lines clarify who to contact first for clinical, operational, HR, IT, or compliance issues.
- Scope of services defines what care and support the agency provides and what is outside agency authorization.

**Home Health Examples**

- An HHA is unsure whether a requested household task is covered; they verify service scope with supervisor before proceeding.
- An LVN sees a scheduling conflict affecting a visit window and escalates through the line manager.
- Admin receives a patient family request outside normal service scope and routes it to clinical leadership.

**Role Awareness Cues**

- Clinical roles (RN, LVN, CNA, HHA): know service boundaries and clinical escalation path.
- Operational roles (Admin, HR, IT): route requests to correct owners and avoid unauthorized commitments.
- Leadership and control roles (DON, Compliance): reinforce consistent decisions and documented escalation.

**Section Completion Check (non-graded)**

- Learner confirms: "I understand who I report to and what services our agency provides."

---

# 3. Section B - Ethics & Compliance Awareness

**Lessons Covered:**  
4) Code of conduct and ethics  
5) Corporate compliance program  
6) Compliance hotline and reporting  
7) Non-retaliation and whistleblower protection  
8) Employee grievance process  
9) Disciplinary process overview

**Plain Language Content**

- The code of conduct defines expected behavior with patients, families, coworkers, and partners.
- The compliance program exists to prevent, detect, and address policy violations.
- Concerns can be reported through supervisor channels or hotline pathways.
- Non-retaliation protects good-faith reporters.
- Grievance and disciplinary processes are structured, documented, and role-neutral.

**Home Health Examples**

- CNA observes questionable documentation timing and reports through approved compliance channels.
- Admin experiences repeated unprofessional behavior and uses grievance process steps.
- RN reports a potential policy deviation without fear of retaliation.

**Role Awareness Cues**

- All roles: report concerns promptly and in good faith.
- Supervisors and DON: receive concerns, document actions, and escalate appropriately.
- Compliance and HR: investigate, track outcomes, and ensure non-retaliation controls.
- IT: preserve system evidence when incidents involve systems or communication logs.

**Section Completion Check (non-graded)**

- Learner confirms: "I know how to report concerns and understand non-retaliation protections."

---

# 4. Section C - Workforce Rules

**Lessons Covered:**  
10) Timekeeping, attendance, and visit verification  
13) Policy acknowledgment and training compliance expectations

**Plain Language Content**

- Accurate timekeeping and attendance are required for payroll integrity, operational planning, and compliance.
- Visit verification must reflect real service delivery events as required by agency policy.
- Policy acknowledgment and assigned trainings are mandatory onboarding obligations.

**Home Health Examples**

- HHA corrects a missed clock action through approved correction workflow, not informal edits.
- RN completes policy acknowledgment before first independent patient assignment.
- Admin tracks unresolved onboarding tasks and flags accounts for restricted access.

**Role Awareness Cues**

- Clinical roles: maintain truthful visit/time records and complete required acknowledgments.
- Admin and HR: monitor completion status and resolve attendance/documentation exceptions.
- DON and Compliance: enforce expectations consistently across teams.

**Section Completion Check (non-graded)**

- Learner confirms: "I understand timekeeping standards and onboarding completion expectations."

---

# 5. Section D - IT & Security

**Lessons Covered:**  
11) IT acceptable use, email, mobile device, and social media  
12) Security awareness: passwords, phishing, device protection

**Plain Language Content**

- Agency systems are for authorized business use only.
- Email and mobile device use must follow policy, including safe handling of work information.
- Social media activity must not disclose protected agency or patient-related information.
- Use strong passwords, protect devices, and report suspicious messages immediately.

**Home Health Examples**

- Staff member receives a fake password reset email and reports it to IT/security.
- Clinician leaves a device in a vehicle and follows immediate incident reporting steps.
- Employee avoids posting work-related details that could expose confidential information.

**Role Awareness Cues**

- All roles: follow acceptable use and report security concerns quickly.
- IT: triage reported threats and guide response actions.
- Compliance and HR: coordinate policy and corrective actions when misuse occurs.

**Section Completion Check (non-graded)**

- Learner confirms: "I can identify basic security risks and know how to report them."

---

# 6. Summary

**Key Takeaways**

- Know the agency purpose, structure, and authorized service scope.
- Follow the code of conduct and use compliance reporting channels when needed.
- Complete required timekeeping, attendance, and policy/training obligations.
- Protect systems, devices, and communications through safe IT practices.

**Accountability Reinforcement**

- Every role is responsible for safe, ethical, and policy-aligned conduct.
- Reporting concerns early protects patients, staff, and the agency.
- Module completion is required before full onboarding and work authorization.

# 7. Assessment

**Assessment Format:** 6 questions, mixed multiple-choice and simple scenarios  
**Passing Standard:** 80% (5 of 6 correct)

**Q1. Which option best describes why mission, vision, and values are included in onboarding?**

- A. They are optional background information.
- B. They provide a shared standard for decisions and patient service behavior.
- C. They replace policy requirements.
- D. They only apply to leadership roles.

**Correct Answer:** B  
**Rationale:** Mission, vision, and values align daily decisions across all roles.

**Q2. A family asks an HHA to perform a task outside the documented service plan. What should happen first?**

- A. Perform the task to maintain satisfaction.
- B. Ask a coworker for informal approval.
- C. Verify scope with supervisor/clinical lead before acting.
- D. Ignore the request and leave.

**Correct Answer:** C  
**Rationale:** Service requests outside scope must be validated through proper reporting lines.

**Q3. You suspect a policy violation. What is the most appropriate action?**

- A. Wait to see if it happens again.
- B. Report through supervisor or compliance hotline per policy.
- C. Post about it in a staff group chat.
- D. Confront the person publicly.

**Correct Answer:** B  
**Rationale:** The compliance program requires prompt, policy-based reporting.

**Q4. Which statement is true about non-retaliation?**

- A. It protects only anonymous reports.
- B. It applies only to clinical staff.
- C. It protects good-faith reporting from retaliation.
- D. It guarantees no follow-up investigation.

**Correct Answer:** C  
**Rationale:** Non-retaliation supports safe reporting across all roles.

**Q5. A staff member receives an unexpected email asking for immediate password reset via external link. Best next step?**

- A. Click link quickly to avoid lockout.
- B. Forward to coworkers for confirmation.
- C. Report as suspicious to IT/security and follow security guidance.
- D. Delete and take no further action.

**Correct Answer:** C  
**Rationale:** Potential phishing attempts must be reported for containment and guidance.

**Q6. Which onboarding element is required before unrestricted system and scheduling access?**

- A. Optional reading completion
- B. Module 1 completion and passing assessment
- C. Supervisor verbal reminder only
- D. Completion of unrelated advanced modules

**Correct Answer:** B  
**Rationale:** Module 1 is a mandatory gate for onboarding progression and access enablement.

# 10. Implementation Blueprint (JourneyV1 Component)

**Component Name:** JourneyV1.Module.M1.OrganizationalOrientation  
**Component Type:** LMS modular training component  
**State Model:** NotStarted -> InProgress -> CompletedPass or CompletedFail

## 10.1 Screen Map

1. Intro screen (M1-S00)
2. Section A screen (M1-S01)
3. Section B screen (M1-S02)
4. Section C screen (M1-S03)
5. Section D screen (M1-S04)
6. Summary screen (M1-S05)
7. Assessment screen (M1-QZ)
8. Completion state screen (M1-END)

## 10.2 UI/UX Functional Requirements

- Persistent top progress bar with section markers.
- Left section navigation with lock/unlock states.
- Next/Back controls with section completion persistence.
- Assessment start button enabled only after all content sections are viewed.
- Completion screen shows pass/fail, score, timestamp, and next actions.

## 10.3 Timing Controls

- Target seat time: 24-28 minutes.
- Soft pacing cue per content screen: 3-8 minutes based on section.
- No forced long timers; learner-driven progression.

## 10.4 Assessment Engine Rules

- Question count: 6
- Pass threshold: >= 80%
- Scoring formula: (correct / total_questions) * 100
- Retry policy: 2 retries after initial attempt
- Remediation on fail: show missed objective domains and direct learner to relevant section

## 10.6 Gate Check Pseudocode

```text
IF module_id == "M1" AND status != "CompletedPass"
THEN
	deny_onboarding_completion = true
	deny_system_access = true
	deny_scheduling = true
	deny_patient_assignment = true

	allow_onboarding_completion = true
	allow_system_access = true
	allow_scheduling = true
	allow_patient_assignment = true
```

- TrainingAssigned (on assignment)
- SectionCompleted (on each section confirmation)
- AssessmentSubmitted (on each attempt)
- ModuleCompleted (on pass/fail completion)
- GateDecisionReturned (on access/scheduling checks)
