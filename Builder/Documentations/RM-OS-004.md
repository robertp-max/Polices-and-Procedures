## 2. Purpose

This policy establishes the Heat Illness Prevention Program for Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. as required by:

- **California Code of Regulations, Title 8, §3395** — Heat Illness Prevention (applicable to outdoor places of employment)
- **California Code of Regulations, Title 8, §5141.1** — Heat Illness Prevention in Indoor Places of Employment (effective January 1, 2025)

Home health field employees are exposed to heat illness risk in two distinct environments:
1. **Outdoors** — walking to/from patient residences, traveling in vehicles, working in patient yards, community settings, or unconditioned areas adjacent to residences
2. **Indoors** — working in patient residences or other structures where ambient indoor temperature may reach or exceed 82°F due to inadequate or absent air conditioning, particularly during California heat events

Heat illness is a serious, potentially fatal condition. This program establishes procedures so that all employees of the agency have access to water, shade, cool-down rest periods, training, and effective emergency response in the event of suspected heat illness.

---

## 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

* All employees of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. who perform work duties in outdoor environments or in indoor environments where temperatures may reach or exceed 82°F
* Job classifications primarily affected: Registered Nurse (RN), Licensed Vocational Nurse (LVN), Physical Therapist (PT), Occupational Therapist (OT), Speech-Language Pathologist (SLP), Medical Social Worker (MSW), Home Health Aide (HHA)
* All field work settings: patient residences, community clinic visits, outdoor environments during field work, vehicles used during work duties
* High-heat conditions trigger additional requirements (≥95°F outdoor ambient temperature or ≥87°F indoor ambient temperature per §5141.1)

This program also addresses vehicle heat exposure, which is a distinct risk for employees who use personal or agency vehicles in California's climate.

## 4. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| :---- | :---- |
| Heat Illness | A serious medical condition resulting from the body's inability to regulate its core temperature, ranging from heat cramps and heat syncope (fainting) to heat exhaustion and heat stroke. Heat stroke is a medical emergency with potential to cause death or permanent disability. |
| Ambient Temperature | The temperature of the surrounding air. For outdoor work: measured by a thermometer in the shade at the work location. For indoor work: the temperature at any workstation where employees perform work. |
| Heat Index | The apparent temperature felt by the body, accounting for temperature and relative humidity. Cal/OSHA permits use of heat index in lieu of dry-bulb temperature for §3395 compliance. |
| High-Heat Condition (Outdoor) | Periods when the outdoor ambient temperature equals or exceeds 95°F (35°C) at the work location. Triggers additional procedural requirements under §3395. |
| High-Heat Condition (Indoor) | Indoor ambient temperature at or above 87°F (30.6°C) at the workstation where employees perform work, or at or above 82°F where: employees wear clothing that restricts heat removal or employees work in a high-radiant-heat environment. Triggers requirements under §5141.1. |
| Cool-Down Rest Period | Preventive cool-down rest periods provided to employees at their request, taken in a shaded area or cool area (at or below 80°F where practicable for indoor). These periods are treated as rest breaks and are compensated. |
| Acclimatization | The physiological adaptation process by which the body adjusts to work in hot conditions. New employees and employees returning from absences greater than 14 days require a progressive acclimatization period per §3395. |
| Personal Heat Illness Risk Factors | Individual factors that increase heat illness vulnerability including: age >55, obesity, certain medications (anticholinergics, diuretics, beta-blockers, stimulants), diabetes, cardiovascular disease, prior heat illness, and wearing personal protective equipment. |

## 5. Policy Statement

5.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall provide and maintain a Heat Illness Prevention Program in complete compliance with 8 CCR §3395 (outdoor workers) and 8 CCR §5141.1 (indoor workers).

5.2 The agency shall provide potable drinking water to all employees working in heat conditions, meeting the quantity and access requirements in Section 6.1.

5.3 The agency shall ensure access to shade or cooling areas for employees working outdoors in temperatures ≥80°F at all times when requested by an employee. Employees shall not be required to seek approval before taking a preventive cool-down rest period. Cool-down rest periods shall be compensated time.

5.4 The agency shall initiate an acclimatization protocol for all new employees and for any employee returning from an absence of 14 or more days who will be performing work in heat conditions.

5.5 Employees are required to report heat illness symptoms — including heavy sweating, weakness, dizziness, nausea, headache, confusion, or cessation of sweating — to their supervisor immediately. Employees shall not be discouraged or penalized for taking preventive cool-down rest periods.

5.6 For indoor work environments (patient residences) at or above 82°F: the agency shall provide employees with procedures for assessing indoor temperature, taking cool-down periods, and requesting supervisor assistance if the environment creates an unacceptable heat risk. Employees shall document indoor temperature at visits assessed as potentially heat-hazardous.

5.7 The agency shall provide heat illness prevention training to all employees before working in conditions with heat illness risk, meeting the content requirements of §3395(h) (outdoor) and §5141.1(i) (indoor).

5.8 The agency shall implement high-heat procedures whenever temperatures at or above 95°F (outdoor) or 87°F (indoor) are forecast for any location where employees will be working.

5.9 This program is a subprogram of RM-OS-001 (IIPP) and applies to all employee heat-exposure contexts.

## 6. Procedures

### 6.1 Water, Rest, and Shade — All Conditions

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.1.1 | Risk Manager / Supervisor | Ensure all field employees have access to, or carry on their person, adequate potable drinking water at all times during outdoor work or field visits in conditions ≥80°F. Adequate quantity: at least 1 quart (approximately 32 oz) of water per employee per hour of outdoor work, or equivalent access to a water source. | Continuously during outdoor work in ≥80°F conditions. |
| 6.1.2 | Field Employee | While working in high-heat conditions (≥80°F outdoor or ≥82°F indoor), drink water at frequent, regular intervals — at minimum every 15–20 minutes even if not thirsty. Do not rely on thirst as an indicator of dehydration. | Throughout heat-exposure work periods. |
| 6.1.3 | Field Employee | If working outdoors, identify shade (natural or constructed) in proximity to the work location before beginning work in temperatures ≥80°F. If no shade is available at the work location, identify a cool location (vehicle with functioning air conditioning if outdoor shade is not available) as the designated cool-down area. | Before beginning work in ≥80°F outdoor conditions. |
| 6.1.4 | Field Employee | When experiencing heat illness warning signs (dizziness, weakness, nausea, headache, irritability, clamminess, muscle cramps) — immediately move to shade or cool area and take a preventive cool-down rest period. Do not require supervisor approval; notify supervisor as soon as safely possible. | Immediately upon experiencing warning signs. |
| 6.1.5 | Supervisor | Do not impede, discourage, or penalize any employee from taking a preventive cool-down rest period. If an employee notifies a supervisor of a cool-down rest period, the supervisor shall check in on the employee within 15 minutes. | Immediately upon notification. |

### 6.2 High-Heat Procedures (Outdoor ≥95°F / Indoor ≥87°F)

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.2.1 | Risk Manager / Operations Manager | Monitor National Weather Service forecasts and California Excessive Heat Warning alerts daily during warm months (May–October) and on triggered days in other months. When ≥95°F is forecast for any geographic area where field staff are scheduled, activate High-Heat Procedures for affected areas. When patient home residences in that area may reach ≥87°F, activate Indoor High-Heat Procedures as well. | Daily monitoring during warm months; same-day activation when forecast is received. |
| 6.2.2 | Supervisor | When High-Heat Procedures are active: contact each assigned employee by phone at least once during the work shift to inquire about symptoms and confirm the employee has adequate water and access to shade/cooling. Document contact in the High-Heat Observation Log (Appendix A). | At least once per shift during activated High-Heat Procedures. |
| 6.2.3 | Field Employee | During High-Heat conditions: (a) increase drinking frequency — minimum 8 oz. every 20 minutes; (b) pre-wet a cooling towel or use cooling vest if provided; (c) schedule patient visits to avoid peak heat hours (10 AM–4 PM) where clinically feasible; (d) conduct patient care in the coolest available room of the residence; (e) if the residence is ≥87°F indoor and the patient lacks cooling (functional AC), document the environmental conditions and notify supervisor. | Throughout high-heat duty period. |
| 6.2.4 | Clinical Manager / Scheduler | During prolonged heat events (≥3 consecutive days of high-heat conditions), adjust field schedules where feasible to: (a) front-load visits to morning hours (before 10 AM); (b) reduce consecutive visit density for employees working without AC during visits; (c) ensure employee has adequate recovery time between visits. | During prolonged heat events; scheduling adjustments made at day-prior schedule review. |

### 6.3 Indoor Heat Assessment at Patient Residences

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.3.1 | Field Employee | Upon arriving at each patient residence during warm weather months (May–October) or any day when outdoor temperatures exceed 80°F: assess the indoor temperature of the work environment. If the ambient temperature (estimated) appears to be at or above 82°F: (a) document the estimated indoor temperature in the visit note; (b) identify the coolest available room for clinical activities; (c) notify supervisor if conditions appear to be ≥87°F and work duration will exceed 30 minutes. | Upon arrival at each at-risk visit. |
| 6.3.2 | Agency | Maintain a small digital thermometer in each field staff's supply kit for use in indoor temperature assessment when conditions are uncertain. Agency is not required to measure temperature at every visit, but temperature measurement shall be performed when conditions appear to be potentially hazardous. | Supply thermometers to all field clinical staff kits. |
| 6.3.3 | Field Employee | If the indoor temperature is ≥87°F and the employee will work for more than 30 minutes: (a) take a preventive cool-down period in the coolest available space (outdoors in shade if outdoors is cooler, or near open window or fan) for at least 5 minutes after every 30 minutes of clinical work; (b) notify supervisor at start of visit; (c) document cool-down periods taken and indoor temperature estimate in visit notes. | Throughout visits in ≥87°F indoor conditions. |
| 6.3.4 | Director of Nursing / Risk Manager | If repeated visits to a specific patient's residence involve sustained indoor temperatures at or above 87°F and the patient cannot secure cooling, evaluate whether the visit safety can be assured. Document assessment in the risk file and consider care plan modification (schedule change, telehealth check-in alternative for some contacts) in consultation with Clinical Manager. | Upon identification of persistent unsafe indoor heat condition. |

### 6.4 Acclimatization Protocol

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.4.1 | Supervisor / HR | Implement the acclimatization schedule for: (a) all new employees during their first 14 calendar days of outdoor work in heat conditions; (b) any employee returning from an absence of ≥14 calendar days who will be working in heat conditions. The progressive acclimatization schedule for new employees: Day 1–3: max 50% of normal heat-exposure duty; Day 4–7: max 70%; Day 8–14: max 90%; Day 15+: full duty. | Starting from first day of heat-exposed work for new employees; from first day back for returning employees. |
| 6.4.2 | Supervisor | Monitor all newly acclimatizing employees with increased supervisory contacts during the first 14 days: check-in by phone at a minimum at the start and end of each shift. Document check-ins on Acclimatization Monitoring Log (Appendix B). | Daily during first 14 days. |
| 6.4.3 | All Employees | Alert supervisor if experiencing heat illness symptoms during acclimatization period. The risk of heat illness is highest during the first days of heat exposure. Do not dismiss early symptoms, and escalate to emergency response if a co-worker or patient reports an employee appearing confused, not sweating in high heat, or unresponsive. | Continuously during acclimatization period. |

### 6.5 Emergency Response for Heat Illness

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.5.1 | Recognizing Employee or Callers | Recognize heat illness emergency warning signs: (a) loss of consciousness or unresponsiveness; (b) confusion, disorientation, slurred speech; (c) hot, dry, or flushed skin (cessation of sweating in extreme heat); (d) seizure. For ANY of these signs — CALL 911 IMMEDIATELY. Do not delay calling 911 to contact supervisor first. Move employee to shade/cool area while awaiting EMS. | Immediately upon recognizing emergency heat illness signs. |
| 6.5.2 | Responding Employee | While awaiting EMS: (a) move the affected employee to shade or cooler environment; (b) remove outer clothing if possible; (c) apply cool (not ice-cold) water to skin — wrists, armpits, neck; (d) fan the employee; (e) have employee sip cool water if conscious and able to swallow — do NOT give water to unconscious/confused persons; (f) stay with the employee until EMS arrives. | While awaiting EMS. |
| 6.5.3 | Supervisor | After being notified of a heat illness emergency: (a) confirm 911 was called; (b) notify Risk Manager and Administrator immediately; (c) contact the patient household (if incident occurred in patient home) to provide information as needed; (d) document all circumstances in the Heat Illness Incident Form (Appendix C). | Immediately upon notification. |
| 6.5.4 | Risk Manager | Following any employee heat illness incident: (a) initiate an incident investigation per RM-OS-001 Section 6.5; (b) conduct a root cause analysis per QA-AE-002; (c) identify whether program failures contributed; (d) update training or procedures within 30 days of RCA completion. | Investigation within 24 hours; RCA within 14 days; program update within 30 days. |

### 6.6 Vehicle Heat Safety

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.6.1 | All Employees | Before beginning a drive in warm weather: ensure vehicle air conditioning is functional. If vehicle AC is not functional during conditions ≥80°F outdoor temperature, notify supervisor immediately — the vehicle is a heat hazard and the employee shall not perform extended driving for work assignments without functional AC or a replacement vehicle. | Before commencing each day's field duties in warm weather. |
| 6.6.2 | Risk Manager | Maintain a protocol for agency response to employee vehicle AC failure during a high-heat period: options include reschedule visits, provide rental vehicle, reassign to telephone-contact duties until vehicle is repaired. No employee shall be required to work in a vehicle without functioning AC during High-Heat conditions (≥95°F outdoor). | Protocol in place prior to warm-weather season (by May 1 each year). |
| 6.6.3 | All Employees | Never leave any person (patient, child, dependent) in an unattended vehicle in warm weather. Employees who observe a person or animal in a hot vehicle in distress are authorized and expected to call 911. | Continuously. |

### 6.7 Escalation and Exception Handling

| Condition | Escalation Path | Corrective Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Excessive Heat Warning issued by NWS for service area | Risk Manager immediately notifies all supervisors and scheduling manager. | High-Heat Procedures activated; all field staff notified; visit schedule reviewed for resequencing. | Same day as warning issuance. |
| Employee self-reports heat exhaustion symptoms (not emergency) | Employee calls supervisor; supervisor activates check-in. | Employee moves to cool area; supervisor evaluates whether employee can continue. If in doubt, end field duties for the day; employee may request medical evaluation at no cost. | Immediately upon report. |
| Annual program review identifies deficiency | Risk Manager documents finding. | Corrective action plan per QA-AE-003; training update within 30 days; procedure update within 30 days. | Within 30 days. |

## 8. Compliance & Audit Considerations

### 8.1 How Compliance Is Measured

| Compliance Indicator | Measurement Method | Acceptable Standard |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Written program exists, current, covers both §3395 and §5141.1 | Annual review against both regulation checklists | All elements present; reviewed within 12 months |
| All employees trained before heat-exposed work | Training log audit — training date vs. first field day | 100% trained before first heat exposure |
| High-heat procedures activated when temperature conditions met | Review of High-Heat Observation Logs vs. weather records | 100% activation on ≥95°F outdoor / ≥87°F indoor days |
| Heat illness incidents investigated within 24 hours | Review of Incident Forms | 100% investigated within 24 hours |
| Acclimatization protocol applied to new employees | Review of Acclimatization Monitoring Logs | 100% documented for all new employees during first 14 days |

### 8.2 What Surveyors and Auditors Will Look For

Cal/OSHA compliance officers conducting inspection for heat-related complaints or during warm-weather enforcement initiatives will specifically verify:

1. Written Heat Illness Prevention Program exists for BOTH outdoor (§3395) and indoor (§5141.1) environments.
2. Training records confirm all employees were trained before working in heat-exposed conditions, not just at annual review.
3. High-heat procedures are documented as activated on days when threshold temperatures were met.
4. Supervisory contact logs (Appendix A) are maintained during High-Heat activations.
5. Vehicle heat safety procedures are addressed.
6. Acclimatization records for new employees are maintained.

### 8.3 Common Failure Points

| Failure Point | Risk | Mitigation |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Program covers outdoor only — not indoor §5141.1 (new 2025) | Citation for failure to implement §5141.1 | This program explicitly addresses both §3395 and §5141.1 |
| No supervisory check-in records during High-Heat conditions | Citation; inability to demonstrate high-heat procedure compliance | Appendix A log required and tracked on all High-Heat days |
| Acclimatization not implemented for new employees | Citation; heat illness risk | HRonboarding checklist triggers Acclimatization Protocol for all new field employees |
| Employees trained annually but not before first heat exposure | Training was provided too late | Training is triggered at hire, before first field day — not just at annual training anniversary |
| Vehicle AC failure not addressed | Employee exposed to heat illness risk in vehicle | Vehicle AC protocol per 6.6; supervisor notified if AC failure occurs during duty |
