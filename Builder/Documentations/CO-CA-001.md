## 2. Purpose

This policy establishes Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.'s obligations and operational procedures under the California Confidentiality of Medical Information Act (CMIA), California Civil Code §§56–56.37. The CMIA imposes requirements upon healthcare providers operating in California that are legally distinct from, and in many respects more stringent than, the federal HIPAA Privacy Rule (45 CFR Part 164, Subpart E). This policy supplements but does not replace CO-HP-001 (HIPAA Privacy Program). Where CMIA and HIPAA requirements conflict, the more protective standard shall govern, consistent with California law.

The primary purposes of this policy are to:
- Define the specific protected categories of medical information under CMIA that require heightened authorization standards beyond HIPAA;
- Establish authorization, disclosure, and access procedures for CMIA-protected information;
- Identify the $1,000 nominal damages exposure and actual damages liability for any unauthorized disclosure under Civil Code §56.36;
- Ensure staff understand the distinction between CMIA authorization requirements and HIPAA authorization requirements;
- Satisfy the California Department of Public Health (CDPH) and HCAI survey expectations for California-licensed home health agencies.

---

## 3. Scope

This policy applies to:

* All employees, contractors, volunteers, and students of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.
* All Protected Health Information (PHI) as defined by HIPAA, interpreted through the additional lens of CMIA's definition of "medical information" (Civil Code §56.05(j))
* All patient records, including paper, electronic (EHR), audio, video, and any other medium
* All workforce members who create, receive, maintain, transmit, access, or destroy records containing medical information about current, former, or prospective patients
* Business Associates and agents acting on behalf of the agency where CMIA obligations transfer

This policy does not displace or reduce any HIPAA privacy obligation. Both frameworks apply simultaneously.

## 4. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| :---- | :---- |
| CMIA | California Confidentiality of Medical Information Act, California Civil Code §§56–56.37. California's primary medical privacy statute for healthcare providers, insurers, and employers. |
| Medical Information (CMIA) | Any individually identifiable information, in electronic or physical form, in possession of or derived from a provider of health care, health care service plan, pharmaceutical company, or contractor regarding a patient's medical history, mental or physical condition, or treatment. (Civil Code §56.05(j)) |
| Provider of Health Care | A licensed professional of the healing arts or any clinic, health dispensary, or health facility that is licensed, or health care service plan licensed, under applicable licensing laws. Home health agencies licensed under California Health & Safety Code §1726 are providers of health care under CMIA. |
| Authorization (CMIA) | A specific written permission by the patient (or authorized representative) that meets the requirements of Civil Code §56.11. CMIA authorizations must be separate from HIPAA authorizations unless the document meets all requirements of both laws. |
| Sensitive Category Information | Medical information in categories afforded heightened protection under CMIA beyond the federal standard: (1) mental health and psychiatric records; (2) substance use disorder records; (3) HIV/AIDS status and test results; (4) genetic information; (5) reproductive health information; (6) sexual assault and domestic violence records; (7) sexually transmitted infection records. |
| Nominal Damages | Civil Code §56.36(b) awards $1,000 per violation regardless of whether actual harm is proven, in addition to actual damages, attorney fees, and costs. |
| Confidentiality Statement | The written statement required by Civil Code §56.10 that patients have certain rights over their medical information and describing how information may be disclosed. |

## 5. Policy Statement

5.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall comply fully with the California Confidentiality of Medical Information Act (Civil Code §§56–56.37) for all medical information of patients served in California, in addition to all applicable HIPAA requirements under CO-HP-001 through CO-HP-007.

5.2 The agency shall apply CMIA's heightened protection standards to Sensitive Category Information. Disclosure of Sensitive Category Information (as defined in Section 4) requires a valid CMIA-compliant authorization that includes all elements specified in Civil Code §56.11, even in circumstances where HIPAA would permit disclosure without authorization.

5.3 The agency shall not require any patient to authorize the disclosure of medical information as a condition of receiving treatment, except as specifically permitted by CMIA (Civil Code §56.17 — insurance verification, workers' compensation, and similar specific exceptions).

5.4 The agency shall not disclose medical information for purposes other than treatment, payment, and healthcare operations without a valid CMIA authorization, except where Civil Code §56.10(b) or (c) provides a permitted exception. Permitted disclosures must be documented in the patient's record.

5.5 All authorizations obtained by the agency for release of medical information shall meet the requirements of Civil Code §56.11 (patient/patient's representative signature, description of information, identification of recipient, purpose, expiration date, right to revoke) and, where applicable, HIPAA authorization requirements (45 CFR §164.508).

5.6 For Sensitive Category Information, the agency shall provide specific disclosure guidance to workforce members identifying which disclosures require an authorization vs. a permitted exception, and shall not rely solely on HIPAA guidance for those categories.

5.7 Any unauthorized disclosure of a patient's medical information shall be reported to the Compliance Officer within 24 hours of discovery. The Compliance Officer shall assess whether the disclosure constitutes a HIPAA breach (CO-HP-003) AND/OR a CMIA violation requiring separate notification and potential response.

5.8 The agency shall maintain a Confidentiality Statement available to all patients describing their rights under both HIPAA and CMIA, updated to reflect any legislative changes within 90 calendar days of the effective date of any such change.

5.9 Workforce members shall receive CMIA-specific training at orientation and no less than annually thereafter, covering the differences between HIPAA and CMIA requirements.

5.10 Only the most current approved version of this policy shall be considered valid. Any revision requires re-acknowledgment by all Compliance Office and clinical leadership staff within 14 calendar days.

## 6. Procedures

### 6.1 CMIA Authorization Processing

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.1.1 | Patient Intake / Clinical Staff | Upon intake, provide the patient with: (a) HIPAA Notice of Privacy Practices (CO-HP-001); (b) Agency CMIA Confidentiality Statement identifying California-specific rights; (c) explanation that certain categories of information receive heightened protection under California law. Obtain patient acknowledgment. | At or before first billable visit. |
| 6.1.2 | Clinical Staff / Compliance Officer | Before disclosing any medical information upon request, determine whether the request falls within a CMIA permitted disclosure (Civil Code §56.10(b)/(c)) or requires a valid CMIA authorization. Use the CMIA Disclosure Decision Tree (Appendix A). | Before each disclosure — no retroactive authorization accepted. |
| 6.1.3 | Clinical Staff / Compliance Officer | If the requested information is Sensitive Category Information (see Section 4), require a signed CMIA authorization even if the disclosure would otherwise be permitted under HIPAA. Do not release Sensitive Category Information based on HIPAA authorization alone. | Before disclosure of any Sensitive Category Information. |
| 6.1.4 | Compliance Officer / Administrator | Review all CMIA authorization forms at least annually to confirm they meet the requirements of Civil Code §56.11. Update forms within 30 calendar days of any legislative change affecting required elements. | Annual review; update within 30 days of legislative change. |
| 6.1.5 | Releasing Staff Member | Log all disclosures of medical information (consistent with CO-HP-006 Accounting of Disclosures procedures) including: (a) date and recipient of disclosure; (b) description of information disclosed; (c) authorization or permitted exception basis; (d) whether information includes a Sensitive Category. | At time of each disclosure. |

### 6.2 Sensitive Category Information — Heightened Handling

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.2.1 | All Clinical Staff | When documenting in the EHR, flag records that contain Sensitive Category Information using the system's designated marking/category field. If the EHR does not support flags, alert the Compliance Officer for alternative segmentation. | At time of documentation. |
| 6.2.2 | Compliance Officer | Maintain a current list of Sensitive Category Information categories (Appendix B) that is distributed to all clinical and administrative staff. Update within 30 calendar days of any statutory change to CMIA sensitive categories. | Maintain continuously; update within 30 days of change. |
| 6.2.3 | Clinical Staff / Administrative Staff | When a third-party requests records, if any records requested include Sensitive Category Information: (a) verify authorization specifically covers that category; (b) do not release even if within treatment/payment/operations unless authorization exists or a specific CMIA exception applies; (c) if uncertain, escalate to Compliance Officer before release. | Before each third-party release involving Sensitive Categories. |
| 6.2.4 | Compliance Officer | If a valid authorization for Sensitive Category Information cannot be obtained (e.g., emergency), document the applicable CMIA emergency exception (Civil Code §56.10(b)(8)) in the disclosure log with the specific factual basis for the exception. | At time of emergency disclosure. |

### 6.3 Employer Access to Employee Medical Information

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.3.1 | HR Director / Compliance Officer | The agency, in its capacity as an employer, shall NOT access any employee's medical information—including workers' compensation records, fitness for duty exam results, or health insurance claims—unless: (a) the employee provides written authorization; (b) a specific CMIA employer exception applies (Civil Code §56.20–56.245); or (c) access is required by law. | At all times—zero tolerance for unauthorized employer access. |
| 6.3.2 | HR Director | Where the agency receives employee medical information in permitted channels (e.g., FMLA certification, workers' compensation), the information shall be maintained in a separate confidential file, accessible only to persons with a need-to-know as defined in the HR-WM-007 personnel file policy, and shall not be shared with supervisors except to the extent that restrictions on work activity are necessary for compliance. | At all times. |
| 6.3.3 | HR Director | Do not make employment decisions (hiring, termination, promotion, reassignment) based on medical information obtained in any CMIA-permitted channel except where the specific medical condition is a bona fide occupational requirement. Document rationale for any employment action that intersects with known medical information. | Before any affected employment action. |

### 6.4 Unauthorized Disclosure Response

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.4.1 | Any Workforce Member | If an unauthorized disclosure of medical information is discovered or suspected, report to the Compliance Officer immediately by phone or urgent message. Do not attempt to independently remediate or contact the recipient without Compliance Officer direction. | Within 24 hours of discovery. |
| 6.4.2 | Compliance Officer | Upon receiving an unauthorized disclosure report: (a) document the date/time of discovery, discoverer, and facts known; (b) assess whether the disclosure constitutes a HIPAA breach (initiate CO-HP-003 if applicable); (c) separately assess CMIA violation under Civil Code §56.36; (d) assess whether nominal damages exposure ($1,000/violation) and/or actual damages apply; (e) consult agency legal counsel within 48 hours if actual harm is identifiable or multiple patients are affected. | HIPAA assessment: within 24 hours. CMIA assessment: within 48 hours. Legal consult: within 48 hours if actual harm indicated. |
| 6.4.3 | Compliance Officer / Administrator | If the disclosure constitutes a CMIA violation: (a) notify the affected patient per Civil Code §56.36 requirements and agency legal counsel guidance; (b) document all notifications; (c) implement corrective action per QA-AE-003; (d) report to the Governing Body at the next scheduled meeting unless severity requires emergency meeting. | Patient notice: per legal counsel guidance, typically within 30 calendar days. Corrective action: initiated within 14 days. |
| 6.4.4 | Compliance Officer | Complete a CMIA Violation Documentation Form (Appendix C) for every confirmed CMIA violation. Maintain all violation records for minimum 6 years from the date of the violation (per statute of limitations under California law). | At time of confirmed violation; retain for 6 years. |

### 6.5 Escalation and Exception Handling

| Condition | Escalation Path | Corrective Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Staff releases Sensitive Category Information without valid CMIA authorization | Immediately escalate to Compliance Officer. | Compliance Officer conducts root cause analysis; implements retraining; corrective action per QA-AE-003. Document in compliance log. | Root cause analysis within 14 days; retraining within 30 days. |
| Patient files complaint related to CMIA privacy rights | Compliance Officer receives complaint, initiates formal investigation per CO-CP-007. | Respond to patient within 14 calendar days; resolve within 30 calendar days; document resolution in compliance system. | Initial response: 14 days. Resolution: 30 days. |
| State agency (CDPH/HCAI) initiates investigation of CMIA complaint | Administrator immediately notifies legal counsel and Compliance Officer. | Preserve all relevant records; cooperate fully with investigation; prepare documentation demonstrating CMIA compliance program. | Preservation: immediate. Compliance documentation: within 7 business days of notice. |
| Legislative amendment to CMIA enacted | Compliance Officer monitors state legislation per CO-RA-001. | Update policy, authorization forms, and training materials within 90 calendar days of enacted amendment's effective date. | Within 90 days of effective date of amendment. |

## 8. Compliance & Audit Considerations

### 8.1 How Compliance Is Measured

| Compliance Indicator | Measurement Method | Acceptable Standard |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| CMIA Confidentiality Statement provided at intake | Audit of 10% of patient records quarterly — verify acknowledgment present | 100% compliance |
| Sensitive Category Information disclosures have valid CMIA authorization | Quarterly audit of disclosure log — review all Sensitive Category disclosures | 100% have valid authorization or documented CMIA exception |
| CMIA violation incidents | Count from compliance log quarterly | 0 confirmed violations; any violation triggers immediate investigation |
| Staff CMIA training completed | Training completion rate from HR records | 100% at orientation; 100% annual within calendar year |
| CMIA authorization forms are current | Annual legal review of authorization form against current Civil Code §56.11 | Form reviewed and confirmed current within 12 months |
| Employer access to employee medical information is segregated and controlled | HR file audit — verify medical information is in separate file, not combined with standard personnel file | 100% compliance |

### 8.2 What Surveyors and Auditors Will Look For

California CDPH and HCAI surveyors, as well as CMS surveyors examining applicable California overlay requirements, may assess:

1. Evidence of a CMIA-specific compliance policy distinct from HIPAA. Surveyors will look for whether the agency treats CMIA as an add-on to HIPAA rather than a parallel obligation with additional requirements.
2. Patient intake acknowledgment of California-specific privacy rights. Providing only the federal NPP without a CMIA Confidentiality Statement is a compliance gap.
3. Sensitive Category Information handling procedures. Surveyors may request examples of mental health, substance abuse, or HIV-related disclosures to verify proper authorization was obtained.
4. Staff awareness of CMIA vs. HIPAA differences. Frontline staff interviews may reveal whether staff understand that California law imposes additional restrictions.
5. Disclosure logs and authorization form completeness. Both must evidence compliance with Civil Code §56.11 elements.
6. Complaint and violation response records.

### 8.3 Common Failure Points

| Failure Point | Risk | Mitigation |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Treating CMIA as identical to HIPAA | Unauthorized disclosures of Sensitive Category Information; $1,000/violation nominal damages exposure | Staff training explicitly addresses CMIA additions; policy clearly distinguishes both frameworks |
| Releasing mental health records based on HIPAA authorization alone | Civil Code §56.36 violation; patient lawsuit | Sensitive Category checklist embedded in release process (Appendix A) |
| No CMIA-compliant patient notification at intake | CDPH/HCAI survey deficiency | CMIA Confidentiality Statement incorporated into standard intake packet |
| Employer accessing employee medical information without CMIA basis | Civil Code §56.20 violation; employee lawsuit | Separate medical file; HR policy training; zero access without authorization |
| Not updating policy after CMIA legislative amendments | Non-compliant practices continue after law changes | CO-RA-001 regulatory monitoring captures CMIA amendments; Compliance Officer owns update obligation |
