## 2. Purpose

This policy establishes the comprehensive Emergency Preparedness (EP) Program for Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. as required by 42 CFR §484.102 — Condition of Participation: Emergency Preparedness. This CoP is a standalone, separately surveyed Condition that requires a unified, documented EP program with all four required elements:

**(a) Risk assessment and EP plan** — an all-hazards approach based on the geographic service area's specific risks  
**(b) Policies and procedures** — detailing how services will be maintained through emergencies  
**(c) Communication plan** — for patients, staff, and external entities  
**(d) Training and testing** — including two exercises per year, at least one of which must incorporate an actual emergency event or a community-based exercise when available

CMS State Operations Manual Appendix B surveys emergency preparedness as a unified CoP. Fragmented coverage across individual policies (CL-PR-005, OP-FM-005, OP-SL-006, HR-TD-005) does not satisfy the CMS requirement for a documentable, integrated EP program. This policy serves as the governing EP Program document; the pre-existing policies remain in force and are cross-referenced as subcomponents.

---

## 3. Scope

This program applies to:

* All employees, contractors, volunteers, and students of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.
* All patients actively enrolled in care with the agency, from the date of admission through the date of discharge
* All operational sites: main office, any satellite/branch offices, and the full geographic service area in which home visits are conducted
* All emergency and disaster scenarios identified in the Risk Assessment (Section 6.1)

This program covers emergencies that require implementation of contingency operations, including but not limited to: natural disasters (earthquake, wildfire, flood, extreme heat events, mudslide), severe weather, public health emergencies (pandemic, disease outbreak, environmental contamination), terrorism or civil unrest, infrastructure failures (utility outages, building evacuations, telecommunications failure), and individual patient emergencies requiring care continuity.

## 4. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| :---- | :---- |
| Emergency / Disaster | Any event, natural or man-made, that disrupts or threatens to disrupt the agency's ability to provide safe, effective care to patients or threatens the safety of staff. |
| All-Hazards Approach | An EP planning methodology that addresses a broad range of risks without specifying a single type of hazard — focusing on capabilities, roles, and communication that apply across risk types. Required by 42 CFR §484.102(a)(1)(i). |
| Incident Command System (ICS) | A standardized, hierarchical approach to emergency response management that establishes clear command authority, span of control, and communication protocols. The agency uses an ICS-based structure during declared emergencies. |
| Essential Patients | Patients in the agency's active caseload who require more frequent monitoring or have physical or cognitive limitations that make them particularly vulnerable during an emergency. Identified through the Vulnerability Assessment at admission per Section 6.2. |
| Alternate Care Site | A location, other than the patient's primary residence, where care could be coordinated or services temporarily provided if the patient's home is inaccessible during a disaster. |
| Business Continuity | The agency's capacity to maintain critical operations (patient care, billing, communications) during and after an emergency event. |
| After-Action Report (AAR) | A structured review of how the agency performed during an actual or simulated emergency, identifying gaps and improvements. Required after each training exercise. |
| Continuity of Operations Plan (COOP) | The operational subplan detailing how the agency will sustain or rapidly restore essential services during and after an emergency. |

## 5. Policy Statement

5.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a comprehensive, written Emergency Preparedness Program that meets all four elements of 42 CFR §484.102 at all times. The program shall be based on an all-hazards risk assessment specific to the agency's geographic service area and patient population.

5.2 The Administrator is the designated EP Program Responsible Person. The Risk Manager is the operational EP coordinator. The Director of Nursing is responsible for clinical EP coordination. All three shall have designated alternates.

5.3 The EP Program shall be reviewed and updated no less than annually, and within 30 calendar days of any of the following: (a) a significant change in the agency's service area, patient population, or operations; (b) completion of an emergency exercise or response to an actual emergency that identifies program deficiencies; (c) a change in the regulatory EP requirements.

5.4 The agency shall conduct a minimum of two emergency preparedness exercises each calendar year: (a) at least one full-scale exercise, community-based exercise, or response to an actual emergency; and (b) at least one tabletop exercise, drills, or other exercise type, as an alternative. Where the agency participates in a community-based exercise, this shall count as the full-scale exercise requirement.

5.5 All employees shall receive EP-specific training at orientation and at least annually thereafter, sufficient to enable them to execute their roles in the EP plan.

5.6 The Governing Body shall review and approve the EP Program annually per GV-GB-001, Section 6.2.6.

## 6. Procedures

### 6.1 All-Hazards Risk Assessment

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.1.1 | Administrator / Risk Manager | Conduct a written all-hazards risk assessment covering the agency's full geographic service area. The assessment shall identify: (a) natural hazards likely in the service area (Los Angeles / Southern California context: earthquake, wildfire, flood, landslide, extreme heat event, Southern California Edison Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS) events, Santa Ana wind events); (b) man-made hazards (power grid failure, civil unrest, terrorism, regional contamination, water supply disruption); (c) public health hazards (pandemic influenza, novel pathogen emergence, regional outbreak); (d) technology hazards (EHR system failure, cybersecurity incident, telecommunications outage). | Completed annually; initial assessment within 30 days of program adoption. |
| 6.1.2 | Risk Manager | For each identified hazard, assess: (a) likelihood of occurrence (Low/Medium/High based on CalOES Hazard Mitigation Plan data for the service area); (b) potential impact on patient care operations (Low/Medium/High); (c) existing controls mitigating the risk. | As part of annual risk assessment. |
| 6.1.3 | Administrator | Use the risk assessment findings to prioritize EP planning: highest-likelihood + highest-impact hazards shall have specific, more detailed procedures in the EP plan. Low-likelihood / low-impact hazards are covered under the general all-hazards protocols. | At adoption; annually updated. |
| 6.1.4 | Director of Nursing | Integrate the community hazard assessment into the patient vulnerability assessment process (Section 6.2) to identify which patients are most at risk from the identified hazards. | Annual integration with risk assessment cycle. |

### 6.2 Patient Vulnerability Assessment and Prioritization

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.2.1 | Admitting Clinician | At admission, complete the Patient Emergency Preparedness Vulnerability Assessment (Appendix A) for every patient. The assessment shall document: (a) patient reliance on electrically powered medical equipment (ventilator, oxygen concentrator, IV infusion pump, electric hospital bed, stair lift); (b) patient mobility limitations (non-ambulatory, wheelchair-dependent, unable to self-evacuate); (c) patient cognitive limitations affecting self-protective action; (d) patient's access to emergency contacts or caregivers; (e) geographic risk of the patient's location (flood zone, high fire hazard area, hillside property). | At admission; reviewed at each recertification or significant change in condition. |
| 6.2.2 | Director of Nursing | Maintain a current list of all Essential Patients (those with electric-dependent equipment, no self-evacuation capacity, or living alone with cognitive impairment). This list shall be updated in the care management system within 24 hours of any change in patient status, and shall be available to the on-call nurse at all times. | Maintained continuously; updated within 24 hours of change. |
| 6.2.3 | Scheduler / Director of Nursing | Register patients with electrically powered life-sustaining equipment with their utility provider's Medical Baseline or Life Support program and with the local public safety agency's Access and Functional Needs (AFN) registry where available. Document registration in the patient record. | Within 14 days of admission for qualifying patients. |
| 6.2.4 | Director of Nursing | During any declared emergency or Public Safety Power Shutoff (PSPS) event: activate the Essential Patients contact protocol — direct care staff to attempt contact with all Essential Patients within 4 hours of emergency declaration; document contact attempts and patient status. | Within 4 hours of emergency activation. |

### 6.3 EP Policies and Procedures — Continuity of Care

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.3.1 | Administrator / Director of Nursing | Maintain written Continuity of Care Procedures (Appendix B) covering at minimum: (a) how scheduled visits will be prioritized and rescheduled during emergency-related service disruption; (b) how Essential Patients will be triaged for available staff capacity; (c) how abbreviated clinical visits or telephone check-ins will be authorized as an emergency substitution; (d) criteria for suspending vs. continuing services to patients in affected areas; (e) procedures for coordinating with discharging hospitals or receiving facilities if patients must be evacuated or hospitalized. | Current procedures maintained as Appendix B; reviewed annually and after each exercise. |
| 6.3.2 | Administrator | Maintain written Office/Operations Continuity Procedures (Appendix C) covering: (a) ability to process payroll, benefits, and employee communications when the main office is inaccessible; (b) access to EHR when primary server or internet is unavailable (identify alternate access method, e.g., mobile hotspot, printed backup schedule, EHR vendor's offline mode); (c) backup contact lists for all staff and key Agencies (accreditation body, Medicare administrative contractor, CDPH, local emergency management); (d) procedure for operating from an alternate site if the main office is uninhabitable. | Current procedures maintained as Appendix C; reviewed annually. |
| 6.3.3 | Director of Nursing | Maintain written procedures for how clinical documentation will be captured and preserved during an EHR or telecommunications outage, including: (a) use of agency-approved paper documentation forms; (b) required elements to be transcribed into EHR within 24 hours of service restoration; (c) chain of custody for paper records generated during outage. | Current procedures maintained as Appendix B; reviewed annually. |
| 6.3.4 | Risk Manager | Ensure the agency maintains minimum operational supply levels at all times including: (a) 72-hour supply of commonly used clinical supplies (wound dressings, IV supplies, medication management supplies) distributable to Essential Patients if resupply is disrupted; (b) 72-hour supply of PPE; (c) backup communication devices (charged portable mobile devices for key staff). | Supplies maintained at minimum levels; reviewed quarterly. |

### 6.4 Emergency Communication Plan

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.4.1 | Administrator | Maintain a current, printed Emergency Contact Directory (Appendix D) that is distributed to all supervisors and updated at least annually (or within 14 days of any contact change). The directory must contain: (a) All agency staff with primary and secondary phone contact methods; (b) Governing Body members; (c) Medicare Administrative Contractor (CGS or Palmetto, as applicable); (d) California Department of Public Health HCAI regional contact; (e) Cal OES State Operations Center (916-845-8911); (f) Local emergency management agency; (g) Local fire and law enforcement non-emergency lines; (h) HHA accreditation body (if applicable); (i) Key vendors (medical supply, pharmacy, EHR vendor). | Maintained continuously; distributed to all supervisors; updated within 14 days of change. |
| 6.4.2 | Administrator | Establish and document the staff notification tree for emergency activation: (a) Risk Manager notifies Department Heads; (b) Department Heads notify all assigned staff via the agency's designated mass-notification method (phone tree, text broadcast, or EHR notification); (c) confirm receipt and availability of all staff is documented on the Staff Notification Log (Appendix E). | Protocol established; tested in each annual exercise. |
| 6.4.3 | Director of Nursing | Establish the patient notification procedure for emergency disruption: (a) if scheduled visits cannot be made, notify the patient (or caregiver) by phone no later than 2 hours before the scheduled visit time; (b) provide safety guidance matching the emergency type (e.g., shelter-in-place, evacuation route resources); (c) document all patient contacts and outcomes in the Emergency Patient Contact Log (Appendix F). | Upon emergency activation; contact within 2 hours of missed visit. |
| 6.4.4 | Administrator | Identify and document the agency's alternate communication methods in the event of phone system and internet outage simultaneously: designated emergency FM radio station for public alerts; agency-issued satellite phone or satellite communicator for Administrator and Risk Manager; walkie-talkie/radio for on-site staff if main office is occupied during civil event. | Documented in EP plan; devices maintained and charged. |
| 6.4.5 | Administrator | When an emergency disrupts normal operations for more than 24 hours, notify the Medicare Administrative Contractor (MAC) per MAC guidance for the affected geographic area. Document the notification and retain in the EP file. | Within 24 hours of extended operational disruption. |

### 6.5 Training and Testing

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.5.1 | Risk Manager / Training Coordinator | Provide EP training to all new employees as part of orientation, before they begin patient care duties, covering: (a) their specific role in the EP plan; (b) the patient vulnerability assessment and Essential Patients protocol; (c) communication tree and staff notification procedure; (d) emergency supply location; (e) ICS structure during emergencies; (f) how to report an emergency and what to do if they are isolated during a disaster. | At orientation, before first clinical duties. |
| 6.5.2 | Risk Manager / Training Coordinator | Provide annual EP refresher training to all employees covering: (a) post-exercise lessons learned from the prior year; (b) any EP plan changes; (c) regulatory updates; (d) a scenario-based rapid runthrough of a specific emergency type. | Annually; completed within calendar year. |
| 6.5.3 | Risk Manager | Plan and execute at least two emergency preparedness exercises per calendar year (per 42 CFR §484.102(d)): (a) Exercise Type 1 — full-scale exercise, community-based exercise, OR an actual emergency response that is documented and reviewed as an exercise equivalent; (b) Exercise Type 2 — tabletop exercise with agency leadership and clinical managers, or structured drills. Both exercise types shall test the communication plan and EP procedures. | Two exercises per calendar year; at least one full-scale or community-based. |
| 6.5.4 | Risk Manager | Complete a written After-Action Report (AAR) within 30 calendar days of each exercise or actual emergency response. The AAR shall document: (a) scenario used; (b) participants; (c) strengths identified; (d) gaps or failures identified; (e) corrective actions with assigned responsible party and completion deadline; (f) plan or procedure updates required. | Within 30 days of each exercise or emergency response. |
| 6.5.5 | Risk Manager | Implement all corrective actions identified in AARs within the timeframe specified in the AAR. Unresolved corrective actions shall be escalated to the Administrator at the next management meeting. | Per AAR-specified deadlines. |
| 6.5.6 | Administrator | Present the annual EP program review, exercise results, and AAR summaries to the Governing Body for review and approval, per GV-GB-001 Section 6.2.6. The Governing Body shall review and approve the EP plan annually. | At the Governing Body's third quarterly meeting each calendar year. |

### 6.6 Escalation and Exception Handling

| Condition | Escalation Path | Corrective Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Actual emergency event declared in the service area | Risk Manager activates ICS; Administrator assumes Incident Commander role. | Execute EP communication plan. Activate Essential Patient contact protocol. Document all actions in real-time on the Emergency Operations Log. | Immediately upon declaration. |
| Annual program review not completed by due date | Risk Manager notifies Administrator. | Emergency completion convened within 14 days. Reason documented. Notify MAC if regulatory deadline is at risk. | 14 days. |
| CMS survey finds EP deficiency | Administrator convenes action meeting within 48 hours. | Develop Plan of Correction per CMS timelines. Risk Manager leads all EP corrective actions. Weekly status reports to Governing Body until resolved. | Plan of Correction per CMS-specified deadline. |
| Community-based exercise opportunity not available in a calendar year | Administrator documents unavailability in EP file. | Conduct internal full-scale exercise as substitute; document basis for substitution per CMS guidance. | In lieu of community exercise if none available. |

## 8. Compliance & Audit Considerations

### 8.1 How Compliance Is Measured

| Compliance Indicator | Measurement Method | Acceptable Standard |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Written EP program covers all 4 elements of 42 CFR §484.102 | Annual review against CMS Appendix B EP survey protocol | All 4 elements documented and current |
| Annual risk assessment completed | Review of risk assessment document | Completed within 12 months; service-area specific |
| Patient Vulnerability Assessments completed at admission | Audit of 10% of admission records quarterly | 100% completed at admission |
| Two exercises conducted annually with AARs on file | Review of exercise documentation and AARs | 2 exercises per calendar year; AARs within 30 days each |
| All EP corrective actions (from AARs) completed on time | Review of AAR corrective action tracker | 100% closed by assigned deadline |
| All employees trained at orientation and annually | Training log audit | 100% at orientation; 100% annual rate within calendar year |
| Governing Body approval of EP plan documented | Review of Governing Body minutes | Annual approval documented at third quarterly meeting |

### 8.2 What Surveyors and Auditors Will Look For

CMS surveyors conducting an Emergency Preparedness survey under the State Operations Manual Appendix B will specifically verify:

1. That a single, integrated EP program document exists covering all four required elements — not four separate policies. Surveyors are trained to ask for "your Emergency Preparedness Plan" and will note fragmentation as a program deficiency.
2. That the risk assessment was based on the agency's specific geographic service area — not a generic template. For agencies in Southern California, surveyors know the region's hazards (earthquake, wildfire, Santa Ana winds, PSPS events) and will probe whether the plan addresses them.
3. That the patient vulnerability assessment is documented for all active patients and that an Essential Patients list is maintained in real-time.
4. That two exercises were conducted in the prior calendar year, with written AARs. Missing an exercise year, or conducting an exercise without an AAR, is a common citation.
5. That staff can describe their role in the EP plan consistently with what's documented.
6. That the communication plan is written, tested, and includes all required external contacts.
7. Governing Body approval of the EP plan is documented in board minutes.

### 8.3 Common Failure Points

| Failure Point | Risk | Mitigation |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Fragmented EP coverage across 4+ policies with no governing EP Program document | CMS Condition-level deficiency — failure to have an EP Program | This policy (RM-EP-001) is the governing EP Program; the 4 existing policies are incorporated as cross-references/subcomponents |
| Risk assessment is generic (not service-area specific) | Surveyor rejects non-specific plan as non-compliant | Section 6.1 specifically addresses Southern California hazard profile |
| Essential Patients list not maintained in real-time | Critical patients unidentified during actual emergency | DON responsible for 24-hr update of list; on-call access required |
| Only one exercise conducted per year, not two | CoP deficiency | Annual exercise calendar set by January 15; two exercises planned; AARs required within 30 days |
| AAR completed but corrective actions not tracked or implemented | Recurring deficiencies across exercise years | AAR corrective action tracker maintained; escalated to Administrator if overdue |
| No documentation that patients were contacted during an exercise | Surveyor cannot verify communication plan was tested | Communication plan testing explicitly required in exercise script; Appendix F log completed |
