M01-S31 | challenge

Scene_ID: M01-S31 Slide_Type: challenge Scene_Title: Challenge 5 — Near-Miss Response

Narration_Text: Help Tess respond correctly right now, at Mr. Henderson's kitchen table. He has been double-dosing his medication for five days. Two conflicting instructions from two different visit notes. He has not yet taken today's dose. The window to act correctly is open.

Challenge_Question: Mr. Henderson has been double-dosing due to conflicting instructions in two prior visit notes. He hasn't taken today's dose yet. What is the most defensible immediate response?

Option_A: Correct his understanding at the bedside immediately, update the medication schedule in the chart, call the supervising clinician during this same visit, document the near-miss event, the corrected education, the teach-back result, and the revised care plan — all before leaving the home.

Option_B: Correct his understanding now and explain the right schedule verbally, then document the correction at the end of the day after all visits are complete to ensure you have time to document thoroughly.

Option_C: Ask the patient's family member to monitor his medication intake closely and call the agency if any confusion continues, then notify the supervising clinician at your next scheduled check-in.

Option_D: Wait to adjust the medication instructions until the next scheduled physician order update, so you are not providing instructions that contradict a prior documented order without physician authorization.

Correct_Answer: A

Operational_Rationale: Option A is the only answer that closes every risk loop at the point of care. Near-miss events require same-visit documentation because the chart must be corrected before any other clinician accesses it. The chart does not protect the next clinician from the error until the correction exists in it. Waiting until end of day (Option B) leaves the chart incorrect for every clinician who might access it before your notes are entered — including an after-hours visit, a supervisor reviewing remotely, or a family member reading a portal. That is a real documented harm pathway. Option C transfers a clinical safety function to a family member. Asking a family to watch for confusion is not medication safety management — it is liability transfer without competency. The family may not recognize a dosing error until harm has occurred. Option D inverts clinical urgency and administrative process. A patient who is actively at risk of a dosing error today cannot wait for a physician order update that may be scheduled for next week. Bedside correction with same-visit supervisor escalation and documentation is required.

Full_Debrief: Two people are responsible for what happens in the next twenty-four hours: Tess, and the next clinician who opens this chart. If Tess chooses Option B and documents at end of day, the next clinician who visits Mr. Henderson at 7 AM the following morning will open the chart, see two conflicting instructions — both still there — and make a dosing decision based on stale incorrect information. It is no longer a near-miss. It is a documented medication error with a harm outcome. Near-miss documentation is not a punitive function — it is a safety function that protects the next person who touches this patient. Every link in the chain depends on the previous link being closed. The near-miss note must contain: the specific conflicting instructions identified by visit date, the corrected dosing schedule, what Mr. Henderson demonstrated in teach-back, the supervisor's name and the time of the escalation call, and the revised care plan with next steps. Without all of those elements, the note does not meet the standard for near-miss documentation under ACHC accreditation. Surveyors reviewing this record will look for whether the near-miss was identified, whether it was escalated during the same visit, whether the patient was corrected and comprehension verified, and whether the corrected plan was transmitted to the next clinician. A well-intentioned verbal correction with a note entered the next morning fails all four criteria.
