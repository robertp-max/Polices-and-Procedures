Governing Body Authority & Responsibilities
1. Policy Header
2. Purpose
This policy establishes the authority, composition, functions, and oversight responsibilities of the Governing Body of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. The Governing Body is the ultimate authority accountable for the operation, management, fiscal viability, and regulatory compliance of the home health agency. This policy ensures the agency satisfies the requirements set forth in 42 CFR § 484.105 — Condition of Participation: Organization and Administration of Services, which mandates that a home health agency must have a governing body that assumes full legal authority and responsibility for the agency's overall operation and management.
3. Scope
This policy applies to:
All members of the Governing Body of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. (including voting and non-voting members)
The Agency Administrator
The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager
The Compliance Officer
All senior leadership personnel who report directly to the Governing Body or Administrator
All contracted management entities performing governing body functions on behalf of the agency
This policy does not apply to day-to-day clinical or operational staff except to the extent that Governing Body decisions establish requirements, standards, or directives that govern their work.
4. Policy Statement
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a designated Governing Body that holds full legal authority and responsibility for the overall operation, management, and regulatory compliance of the home health agency, as required by 42 CFR § 484.105(a).
4.2 The Governing Body shall be responsible for ensuring that Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. operates in compliance with all applicable federal, state, and local laws, regulations, and licensure requirements at all times.
4.3 The Governing Body shall appoint a qualified Administrator who is authorized to act on behalf of the Governing Body in the day-to-day management of the agency and who meets the qualifications defined in agency policy GV-OG-002 and applicable California state requirements.
4.4 The Governing Body shall ensure the appointment and ongoing oversight of a qualified Clinical Manager (Director of Nursing) responsible for all clinical services, in compliance with 42 CFR § 484.105(c).
4.5 The Governing Body shall approve and oversee the agency's:
Scope of services (GV-OG-003)
Organizational structure and reporting lines (GV-OG-001)
Annual strategic plan and operational goals (GV-OG-004)
Policy framework and all REQUIRED-tier policies (GV-PM-001, GV-PM-002)
QAPI program (QA-PG-001, QA-PG-002)
Corporate compliance program (CO-CP-001)
Annual operating budget (FN-FP-005)
Emergency preparedness plan (OP-FM-005)
4.6 The Governing Body shall meet at a frequency sufficient to fulfill its oversight responsibilities, but not less than quarterly, with meetings documented in formal minutes per policy GV-GB-002.
4.7 The Governing Body shall not delegate its ultimate accountability for regulatory compliance, patient safety, or fiscal integrity. Delegation of specific authority shall comply with policy GV-OG-005.
4.8 All members of the Governing Body shall disclose and manage conflicts of interest in accordance with policy GV-GB-003.
4.9 Only the most current approved version of this policy shall be considered valid. Superseded versions must not be used for any operational or compliance purpose. Any revision to this policy requires re-acknowledgment by all Governing Body members and senior leadership within 14 calendar days of the revised effective date.

8.2 What Surveyors and Auditors Will Look For
CMS surveyors conducting a standard survey under the State Operations Manual (SOM) Appendix B will specifically verify:
Evidence that a Governing Body exists and is functioning. Surveyors will request establishing documents and a current membership roster. A sole proprietor must demonstrate individual acceptance of governing body responsibilities.
Evidence that the Governing Body has appointed a qualified Administrator. Surveyors will review Governing Body minutes for appointment documentation and verify qualifications per California state requirements.
Evidence of Clinical Manager oversight. Surveyors will look for Governing Body minutes documenting appointment, reporting, and oversight of clinical services leadership.
Evidence that the Governing Body oversees QAPI. Surveyors will examine whether the Governing Body has reviewed, approved, and acted upon quality data. Passive receipt of reports without documented action is a common deficiency.
Evidence of policy oversight. Surveyors will verify that the Governing Body has approved the agency's policies and that a review cycle exists.
Evidence of fiscal oversight. Surveyors will review whether the Governing Body monitors the agency's financial viability and acts on adverse trends.
Meeting frequency and documentation quality. Surveyors will request all meeting minutes for the survey look-back period and assess completeness, including attendance, quorum, and documented decisions.

Board Meeting & Minutes Requirements
1. Policy Header
2. Purpose
This policy establishes the requirements for scheduling, conducting, documenting, and retaining records of all Governing Body meetings of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc., including regular quarterly meetings, special meetings, and executive sessions. Formal, complete, and audit-defensible meeting minutes are the primary documentary evidence that the Governing Body is actively exercising its oversight responsibilities as required by 42 CFR § 484.105. This policy ensures that meeting records satisfy CMS surveyor expectations under the State Operations Manual (SOM) Appendix B, support the agency's continuous survey readiness posture, and provide a defensible record of all Governing Body decisions, directives, and actions.
3. Scope
This policy applies to:
The Governing Body Chair of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.
All voting and non-voting members of the Governing Body
The Designated Secretary responsible for recording meeting minutes
The Agency Administrator
The Compliance Officer
The Clinical Manager / Director of Nursing, when presenting reports to the Governing Body
Any guest, advisor, or subject matter expert invited to attend a Governing Body meeting in any capacity
This policy does not apply to departmental meetings, staff meetings, or committee meetings held below the Governing Body level, unless those meetings are convened as a formal subcommittee of the Governing Body and require Governing Body-level documentation.
4. Policy Statement
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall convene formal Governing Body meetings no fewer than four (4) times per calendar year on a quarterly basis, as required by policy GV-GB-001 and consistent with the oversight obligations established under 42 CFR § 484.105.
4.2 Every Governing Body meeting — whether regular, special, or executive session — shall be documented in formal written minutes that capture all required elements defined in Section 6.4 of this policy. The absence of written minutes for any meeting shall be treated as equivalent to the meeting not having occurred for purposes of CMS survey review.
4.3 Meeting minutes shall be drafted within fourteen (14) calendar days of each meeting, reviewed by the Governing Body Chair, and approved by the full Governing Body at the next regular meeting. Approved minutes shall constitute the official record of the meeting.
4.4 All Governing Body meetings shall require a quorum of voting members to be present — physically or via approved teleconference or video conference — before any official business may be conducted. No motion, vote, directive, or formal decision shall be recorded as valid if made without quorum.
4.5 Meeting agendas shall be prepared and distributed to all Governing Body members no fewer than seven (7) calendar days before each scheduled meeting. Standing agenda items as defined in Section 6.3 of this policy shall appear on every regular quarterly meeting agenda.
4.6 Special meetings may be convened between regular quarterly meetings when urgent matters require Governing Body action. Special meetings require a minimum of forty-eight (48) hours advance notice to all members, except in cases of imminent patient safety threat, in which case notice shall be provided at the earliest practicable time.
4.7 Executive sessions may be convened at any regular or special meeting to address legally sensitive, personnel, or confidential matters. Executive session topics shall be noted in the regular meeting minutes; the substantive content of executive session discussions shall be documented in a separate, restricted executive session log maintained by the Compliance Officer.
4.8 All Governing Body meeting records — including agendas, minutes, attendance logs, and executive session logs — shall be retained for a minimum of seven (7) years from the date of the meeting, in accordance with policy CO-HP-007, and shall be readily accessible for CMS survey review upon request.
4.9 Minutes shall reflect substantive discussion, questions raised, responses provided, directives issued, responsible parties assigned, and deadlines established. Minutes that reflect only the receipt of reports without documented discussion or action shall be considered deficient and shall be returned to the Designated Secretary for revision before approval.
4.10 Only the most current approved version of this policy shall be considered valid. Any revision to this policy requires re-acknowledgment by all Governing Body members and senior leadership within fourteen (14) calendar days of the revised effective date.

8.1 How Compliance Is Measured
8.2 What Surveyors and Auditors Will Look For
CMS surveyors conducting a standard survey under the State Operations Manual (SOM) Appendix B will specifically verify:
Evidence that Governing Body meetings are occurring at least quarterly. Surveyors will request all meeting minutes from the survey look-back period (typically 12–24 months) and verify that four or more meetings occurred in each calendar year with documented quorum.
Quality of meeting minutes as evidence of active governance. Surveyors distinguish between minutes that document passive receipt of reports and minutes that demonstrate substantive engagement. Entries such as "QAPI report received — no action" are a common deficiency. Surveyors expect to see specific data points referenced, questions asked, and directives issued.
Evidence that all required reports were presented. Surveyors will cross-reference the agenda and minutes to verify that compliance, QAPI, financial, and administrator reports were presented at each meeting. Missing reports for any quarter are a cited deficiency.
Evidence of follow-through on directives. Surveyors will trace directives issued in one quarter's minutes to the subsequent quarter's minutes to confirm reported status. Directives that appear in minutes but are never revisited suggest a non-functional governance structure.
Agenda distribution and meeting notice compliance. Surveyors may request proof that agendas were distributed at least 7 days in advance. Email distribution records and governance file timestamps are the primary evidence.
Completeness of quorum documentation. Every set of minutes must identify the quorum requirement, the number of members present, and a declaration that quorum was achieved. Any meeting that lacks quorum documentation is at risk of being treated as non-compliant.
Executive session documentation. Surveyors will verify that executive sessions are noted in the regular minutes. They will not review the content of a properly designated executive session log; however, they will cite the absence of any notation as a documentation deficiency.
Record retention. Surveyors will request records going back to the beginning of the look-back period. Missing records from any portion of that period — whether due to poor maintenance, system migration, or inadvertent destruction — will result in a citation.

Conflict of Interest Disclosure
1. Policy Header
2. Purpose
This policy establishes the requirements for identifying, disclosing, managing, and resolving actual, potential, and perceived conflicts of interest for all Governing Body members, senior leadership, and key personnel of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Conflicts of interest — whether financial, professional, or organizational — pose a direct risk to the integrity of the Governing Body's fiduciary duty, the agency's regulatory compliance posture, and its standing with CMS and the OIG. This policy ensures that all individuals in positions of governance authority act solely in the best interest of the agency and the patients it serves, that no individual benefits personally from decisions made in their governance capacity, and that the agency maintains the transparent, documented conflict management program required by OIG Compliance Program Guidance for Home Health Agencies and expected by CMS surveyors conducting surveys under the State Operations Manual Appendix B.
3. Scope
This policy applies to:
All voting and non-voting members of the Governing Body of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.
The Agency Administrator
The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager
The Compliance Officer
Any contracted management entity or individual performing governing body functions on behalf of the agency
Senior leadership personnel who participate in budget approval, vendor selection, contract execution, or strategic decision-making on behalf of the agency
Any individual appointed to serve on a subcommittee of the Governing Body with decision-making authority
This policy does not apply to front-line clinical or administrative staff except to the extent they are appointed to a role listed above or are involved in a specific transaction or decision that gives rise to a conflict of interest subject to this policy.
4. Policy Statement
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. requires all individuals within the scope of this policy to place the interests of the agency and the patients it serves above any personal, financial, or organizational interest when exercising governance authority or making decisions on behalf of the agency.
4.2 All individuals within scope shall complete and submit a Conflict of Interest Disclosure Form (Appendix A) at the time of initial appointment, annually at the first quarterly Governing Body meeting of each calendar year, and within seven (7) calendar days of any change in circumstances that creates a new actual or potential conflict.
4.3 The Compliance Officer shall review all submitted disclosures, assess the nature and materiality of each disclosed conflict, and present a written summary of findings and management recommendations to the Governing Body within fourteen (14) calendar days of receipt.
4.4 Any individual with a disclosed conflict of interest shall recuse themselves from all discussion, deliberation, and voting on the matter to which the conflict pertains. Recusals shall be documented in Governing Body meeting minutes and in the individual's conflict of interest file.
4.5 The Governing Body shall formally act on all conflict of interest disclosures presenting a material risk to agency integrity, document its determinations in meeting minutes, and maintain a conflict management plan for each active disclosed conflict.
4.6 No Governing Body member, senior leader, or key personnel shall use their position of governance authority to obtain personal financial gain, preferential vendor treatment, employment benefits, or any other advantage for themselves or their immediate family members that is not available to the general workforce.
4.7 Failure to disclose a known conflict of interest, submission of a materially false disclosure, or failure to recuse from a matter involving a disclosed conflict shall constitute a violation of this policy and may result in removal from the Governing Body, termination of employment or contractual relationship, and referral to appropriate regulatory authorities.

Succession Planning for Key Leadership
1. Policy Header
2. Purpose
This policy establishes the requirements for succession planning for the key leadership positions of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. — specifically the Administrator, the Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager, and the Compliance Officer. Leadership vacancies in these roles, if unfilled or inadequately managed, directly jeopardize the agency's regulatory compliance, patient safety, and operational continuity. CMS requires under 42 CFR § 484.105 that a qualified Administrator and Clinical Manager are in place at all times; extended vacancies without a designated interim constitute a Condition-level deficiency risk. This policy ensures the Governing Body maintains a proactive, documented succession strategy that enables the agency to fill or cover key leadership vacancies with qualified individuals without interruption to agency operations or compliance obligations.
3. Scope
This policy applies to:
The Governing Body of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.
The Governing Body Chair
The Agency Administrator
The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager
The Compliance Officer
The HR Director (in support of succession candidate identification and readiness assessment)
This policy governs succession planning for the following key leadership positions:
Administrator — the individual responsible for day-to-day management of the agency, appointed by the Governing Body per 42 CFR § 484.105(b).
Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager — the registered nurse responsible for oversight of all clinical services, per 42 CFR § 484.105(c).
Compliance Officer — the individual designated with authority and independence to operate the corporate compliance program per policy CO-CP-002.
This policy does not govern succession planning for positions below the three key leadership roles listed above unless specifically directed by the Governing Body.
4. Policy Statement
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a current, Governing Body-approved Succession Plan (Appendix A) identifying a designated interim successor for each of the three key leadership positions at all times.
4.2 The Succession Plan shall be reviewed and approved by the Governing Body at the second quarterly meeting (Q2) of each calendar year and updated within fourteen (14) calendar days of any key leadership vacancy, change in successor availability, or material change in the agency's organizational structure.
4.3 For each key leadership position, the Succession Plan shall identify: (a) the current role holder; (b) a primary interim designee who is pre-qualified and available to assume interim responsibility on short notice; (c) a secondary interim designee as backup; (d) the qualifications confirmed for each designee; and (e) the conditions and notification process for activating the succession plan.
4.4 Upon a key leadership vacancy — whether anticipated (planned departure, leave of absence) or unanticipated (sudden resignation, incapacitation, termination) — the Governing Body shall appoint a qualified interim designee within fourteen (14) calendar days of the vacancy and a permanent replacement within ninety (90) calendar days, consistent with the escalation requirements of policy GV-GB-001 §6.5.
4.5 An interim designee appointed under this policy must meet the minimum qualifications for the role as defined in policy GV-OG-002 (Administrator), applicable California state law (Clinical Manager), or policy CO-CP-002 (Compliance Officer). No individual shall serve as interim in a role for which they do not meet minimum qualifications.
4.6 The Succession Plan shall be maintained as a privileged, restricted-access document. Distribution shall be limited to the Governing Body, the Administrator, and the Compliance Officer.
4.7 This policy shall be reviewed biennially by the Governing Body, consistent with its ESSENTIAL classification tier. Any substantive revision requires re-acknowledgment by all individuals within scope within fourteen (14) calendar days of the revised effective date.

Annual Governance Self-Assessment
1. Policy Header
2. Purpose
This policy establishes the requirements for the Governing Body of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. to conduct an annual self-assessment of its own governance effectiveness, regulatory compliance performance, and fulfillment of its oversight obligations. While not explicitly mandated as a standalone requirement by 42 CFR § 484.105, an annual governance self-assessment is the recognized industry best practice for demonstrating that the Governing Body is actively evaluating — and continuously improving — the quality and effectiveness of its governance functions. The self-assessment provides documented evidence that the Governing Body is not merely a passive recipient of reports but an active, reflective governance body that measures itself against defined standards. This evidence is increasingly relied upon by CMS surveyors assessing governance functioning under SOM Appendix B and by OIG reviewers evaluating compliance program effectiveness. This policy establishes a structured, consistent self-assessment process that produces a written record usable for continuous quality improvement, survey readiness, and strategic governance planning.
3. Scope
This policy applies to:
All voting and non-voting members of the Governing Body of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.
The Governing Body Chair (primary responsibility for process administration)
The Agency Administrator (support role — logistics, scheduling, record maintenance)
The Compliance Officer (support role — self-assessment tool preparation and results filing)
This policy governs the conduct of the annual Governing Body self-assessment only. It does not govern operational self-assessments, clinical audits, departmental performance reviews, or the QAPI program self-assessment activities, which are governed by their respective domain policies.
4. Policy Statement
4.1 The Governing Body of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall conduct an annual self-assessment of its governance effectiveness using the structured self-assessment tool provided in Appendix A of this policy.
4.2 The annual self-assessment shall be conducted at the first quarterly meeting (Q1) of each calendar year, immediately following or as part of the annual governance refresher training required by policy GV-GB-001 §10.4.
4.3 The self-assessment shall be completed individually by each Governing Body member and then reviewed collectively by the full Governing Body as a group discussion item. Individual responses shall remain confidential; only the aggregated findings and identified improvement priorities shall be documented in meeting minutes.
4.4 The Governing Body Chair shall prepare or oversee preparation of a written Governance Self-Assessment Summary Report (Appendix B) compiling the aggregated findings, identifying governance strengths, and defining a Governance Improvement Action Plan for any area rated as needing improvement.
4.5 The Governance Self-Assessment Summary Report and Improvement Action Plan shall be filed in the restricted governance file and referenced in Q1 meeting minutes as completed. Individual member assessment forms shall be maintained as privileged records accessible only to the Governing Body Chair and Compliance Officer.
4.6 At the Q1 meeting of the following year, the Governing Body shall review the prior year's Improvement Action Plan as the first step in the new self-assessment cycle, confirming which improvement actions were completed and identifying any that remain open.
4.7 This policy shall be reviewed annually. Any revision requires re-acknowledgment by all Governing Body members within fourteen (14) calendar days of the revised effective date.

8.2 What Surveyors and Auditors Will Look For
CMS surveyors assessing governance functioning under SOM Appendix B and OIG reviewers evaluating compliance program effectiveness will look for:
Evidence that the Governing Body is self-reflective and improvement-oriented. A documented self-assessment process with a written action plan demonstrates that the Governing Body takes its oversight responsibilities seriously and proactively identifies its own gaps — a hallmark of governance maturity.
Evidence that identified gaps are acted upon. Surveyors and auditors are skeptical of self-assessment processes that produce findings but no action. The Improvement Action Plan and quarterly monitoring evidence demonstrate follow-through.
Linkage between self-assessment findings and other compliance activities. Surveyors will look for evidence that critical gaps identified in the self-assessment triggered formal corrective action through the compliance program, not merely internal discussion.
Confidentiality and integrity of the process. While surveyors will not review individual member forms, they will assess whether the process appears genuine and structured versus perfunctory. A Summary Report that identifies only strengths with no improvement areas is likely to be viewed with skepticism.

1. Policy Header
2. Purpose
This policy establishes the formal organizational structure, reporting relationships, and lines of authority for Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. A clearly defined organizational structure is essential to ensure that all personnel understand their reporting relationships, that accountability is maintained at every level, and that the agency operates in compliance with the organizational and administrative requirements of 42 CFR § 484.105. The Governing Body, as the ultimate authority for the agency's operation and management, must ensure that an organizational structure exists that supports effective service delivery, regulatory compliance, and clear communication from the Governing Body through all operational levels to the point of patient care.
3. Scope
This policy applies to:
The Governing Body of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.
The Agency Administrator
The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager
The Compliance Officer
All department heads, supervisors, and managers
All clinical and non-clinical employees, contractors, and per diem staff
All individuals performing services under the direction of the agency
This policy does not apply to patients, families, or external referral sources, except to the extent that organizational structure information is shared with these parties per patient rights requirements (CL-PR-001) or interagency agreement obligations (GV-EA-001).
4. Policy Statement
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a formally documented organizational structure that defines the reporting relationships, lines of authority, and accountability for all agency functions, as required by 42 CFR § 484.105(a) and California Health and Safety Code requirements for home health agencies.
4.2 The organizational structure shall clearly delineate the relationship between the Governing Body, the Administrator, the Clinical Manager (Director of Nursing), the Compliance Officer, and all departments and positions within the agency.

The Administrator reports directly to the Governing Body (GV-GB-001, GV-OG-002)
The Clinical Manager / Director of Nursing has direct authority over all clinical operations (GV-OG-002, 42 CFR § 484.105(c))
The Compliance Officer has a direct reporting line to the Governing Body independent of the Administrator for compliance matters (CO-CP-002)
All clinical staff have a defined clinical supervision chain that terminates at the Director of Nursing
All administrative and operational staff have a defined management chain that terminates at the Administrator
4.5 No individual shall be required to report to two supervisors for the same function without a formally documented dual-reporting arrangement approved by the Administrator and documented in the individual's job description (HR-JD domain).
4.6 The organizational structure shall support the agency's defined scope of services (GV-OG-003) and shall be staffed at levels sufficient to deliver all authorized services safely and effectively (HR-WM-001).
4.7 When the agency operates branch offices or satellite locations, the organizational structure must clearly identify the supervisory relationships and reporting lines for those locations back to the central office, per policy OP-FM-002.

Policy 5 of 7: GV-OG-005
Delegation of Authority
1. Policy Header
2. Purpose
This policy defines the conditions, limitations, documentation requirements, and accountability mechanisms for delegating administrative, clinical, and operational authority within Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Delegation of authority is necessary to enable efficient day-to-day operations while maintaining clear accountability. However, delegation must be carefully controlled to ensure that the Governing Body retains ultimate accountability for the agency's operation and management as required by 42 CFR § 484.105(a), and that delegated authority is exercised only by qualified individuals acting within defined limits. This policy prevents unauthorized assumption of authority, ensures all delegations are documented and traceable, and establishes the principle that accountability cannot be delegated even when tasks and authority are.
3. Scope
This policy applies to:
The Governing Body of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.
The Agency Administrator
The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager
The Compliance Officer
All department heads, supervisors, and managers who exercise delegated authority
All individuals who have been formally designated as a "designee" for any leadership position (Administrator Designee, Clinical Designee)
This policy does not apply to clinical delegation of nursing tasks (e.g., RN delegation to LVN or HHA), which is governed by the California Nursing Practice Act and agency clinical policies (CL-SD-008, CL-SD-006).
4. Policy Statement
4.1 The Governing Body of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. delegates authority to the Administrator for the day-to-day management of the agency, subject to the retained responsibilities and limitations defined in GV-GB-001. This delegation shall be documented in writing and recorded in Governing Body meeting minutes.
4.2 The Administrator may delegate specific authority to department heads, supervisors, and designees to enable efficient operations, provided that: (a) the delegation is in writing; (b) the delegatee meets minimum qualifications for the delegated function; (c) the scope and limits of the delegation are clearly defined; (d) the delegation includes accountability mechanisms and reporting requirements; (e) the Administrator retains accountability to the Governing Body for all delegated functions.
4.3 The following authorities shall not be delegated by the Governing Body to any individual or entity:
Ultimate legal authority for agency operation and management (42 CFR § 484.105(a))
Appointment of the Administrator, Director of Nursing, and Compliance Officer
Approval of REQUIRED-tier policies
Approval of the annual QAPI plan
Approval of the annual operating budget
Approval of the scope of services
Approval of the emergency preparedness plan
4.4 The following authorities shall not be delegated by the Administrator without explicit Governing Body approval:
Authority to enter into contracts exceeding $25,000
Authority to terminate key leadership personnel
Authority to modify the organizational structure (material changes per GV-OG-001)
Authority to commit the agency to regulatory or legal settlements
Authority to expand or reduce the scope of services
4.5 All delegations of authority shall be documented using the Delegation of Authority Form (Appendix A) and maintained in the agency governance file.
4.6 Delegation does not transfer accountability. The delegating party remains accountable for the outcomes of the delegated function.
4.7 Only the most current approved version of this policy shall be considered valid.

1. Policy Header
2. Purpose
This policy mandates the periodic review and revision of all policies at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. on a defined cycle with documented evidence of review and revision. Policies that are not regularly reviewed become outdated, may not reflect current regulatory requirements, and create compliance risk during CMS surveys and audits. 42 CFR § 484.105 requires the governing body to ensure that the agency's policies and procedures are current and appropriate, and CMS surveyors evaluate whether the agency has a policy review process as part of the standard survey. This policy establishes three review cycle categories (Annual, Biennial, and Triggered) as defined in the enterprise policy taxonomy framework (EN-TG-001) and ensures that all 244 policies in the agency's framework are reviewed within their assigned cycle, with documented evidence that each review was conducted, findings were assessed, and the policy was either reaffirmed or revised.

All 244 policies in the agency's enterprise policy framework (EN-TG-001)
The Compliance Officer (policy lifecycle governance)

The Administrator (approval authority for non-REQUIRED-tier revisions)
The Governing Body (approval authority for REQUIRED-tier revisions and oversight of the review cycle)
All department heads who supervise Policy Owner/Stewards
This policy does not apply to external documents referenced in agency policies (e.g., CMS guidance, regulatory texts) or to informal departmental procedures that are not part of the formal policy framework.

4.2 A policy review constitutes a documented assessment by the Policy Owner/Steward that evaluates: (a) whether the policy reflects current federal, state, and local regulations; (b) whether the policy reflects current CMS guidance and interpretive guidelines; (c) whether the policy reflects current agency operations and practices; (d) whether the policy is internally consistent with other agency policies; (e) whether the policy's compliance indicators and common failure points are still accurate; (f) whether any incidents, audit findings, or complaints have identified deficiencies in the policy.

GV-EA-004 — Agency Licensure & Certification Maintenance
1. Policy Header
2. Purpose
This policy establishes the requirements for maintaining continuous, uninterrupted licensure, Medicare certification, Medicaid enrollment, and any applicable accreditation credentials for Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. The agency's legal authority to operate and to receive reimbursement from federal and state healthcare programs depends entirely on the currency and validity of these credentials. A lapse in any required license or certification — even for a single day — may result in termination of the agency's Medicare provider agreement, loss of reimbursement, state enforcement action, inability to admit new patients, and potential Condition-level deficiency under 42 CFR § 484.100. This policy ensures that all required credentials are tracked, renewed timely, and maintained in a state of continuous validity, and that the Governing Body exercises its oversight responsibility to confirm the agency's credential status at each quarterly meeting as required by policy GV-GB-001, Section 6.2.1.2.
3. Scope
This policy applies to:
The Governing Body of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. (oversight and quarterly verification)
The Agency Administrator (primary accountability for credential maintenance)
The Compliance Officer (regulatory monitoring and compliance verification)
The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager (clinical certification and accreditation support)
Any administrative staff assigned responsibility for license renewal processing, application submission, or credential file maintenance
This policy governs the following agency-level credentials:
California Home Health Agency License issued by the California Department of Health Care Access and Information (HCAI) — License No. 406412878
Medicare Certification (CMS Certification Number / CCN) issued by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services
Medicaid (Medi-Cal) Provider Enrollment, if applicable
National accreditation, if applicable (e.g., ACHC, CHAP, Joint Commission)
Any additional state or local permits, registrations, or authorizations required for the agency to operate lawfully within its defined service area
This policy does not govern individual employee licensure or certification (see HR-TA-004) or physician credentialing. It applies exclusively to agency-level credentials.
4. Policy Statement
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain current, valid, and uninterrupted licensure, certification, and enrollment with all applicable federal, state, and local regulatory authorities at all times as a condition of its legal authority to operate and to participate in federal and state healthcare programs.
4.2 The Administrator is directly accountable to the Governing Body for the maintenance and timely renewal of all agency credentials. No credential shall be allowed to lapse, expire, or become inactive without immediate corrective action and Governing Body notification.
4.3 The Administrator shall maintain a centralized Agency Credential Register (Appendix A) that documents every required agency-level credential, including the issuing authority, credential number, effective date, expiration date, renewal deadline, and current status.
4.4 Renewal applications for all credentials shall be submitted no later than 90 calendar days before the expiration date of each credential, unless the issuing authority specifies a different renewal window, in which case the issuing authority's timeline shall be followed.
4.5 The Administrator shall verify the agency's credential status at each quarterly Governing Body meeting and present documented confirmation that all credentials are current and valid, per policy GV-GB-001, Section 6.2.1.2.
4.6 The Compliance Officer shall independently monitor regulatory changes, licensing requirements, and certification standards that may affect the agency's credential obligations and shall notify the Administrator within 7 calendar days of identifying any new or changed requirement.
4.7 Any communication from a licensing, certification, or accreditation body — including routine correspondence, deficiency notifications, survey announcements, enforcement actions, or requests for information — shall be immediately forwarded to the Administrator and Compliance Officer and responded to within the timeframes specified by the issuing authority.
4.8 The Governing Body shall be notified immediately — within 24 hours — of any event that threatens the continuity of any agency credential, including but not limited to: (a) a credential approaching expiration without a submitted renewal; (b) a denial of renewal application; (c) a regulatory enforcement action threatening suspension or revocation; (d) a change in regulatory requirements affecting eligibility; (e) a state survey resulting in an Immediate Jeopardy finding.
4.9 All credential documents, renewal applications, correspondence with regulatory bodies, and survey-related records shall be maintained in the agency's credential file and retained for a minimum of 7 years per policy CO-HP-007.
4.10 Only the most current approved version of this policy shall be considered valid. Any revision requires re-acknowledgment by all personnel within scope within 14 calendar days of the revised effective date.

GV-EA-005 — Agency Closure or Change of Ownership
1. Policy Header
2. Purpose
This policy establishes the procedures and notification requirements for planned agency closure, merger, change of ownership (CHOW), or voluntary termination of the Medicare provider agreement of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. A change of ownership or agency closure involves significant regulatory, legal, patient safety, and operational implications that must be managed in a systematic and compliant manner. CMS requires specific notification and transition procedures under 42 CFR Part 489, Subpart B for changes of ownership and under 42 CFR § 489.52 for voluntary termination of provider agreements. California HCAI requires separate state-level notifications. Failure to comply with these requirements may result in disruption of patient services, loss of Medicare certification, financial penalties, and legal liability. This policy ensures that the Governing Body, as the ultimate authority for the agency, directs and oversees any closure, merger, or CHOW process with full regulatory compliance and with the safety and continuity of patient care as the paramount priority.
3. Scope
This policy applies to:
The Governing Body of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. (decision-making authority)
The Agency Administrator (operational execution)
The Compliance Officer (regulatory compliance coordination)
The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager (patient care transition)
The CFO / Revenue Cycle Director (financial closeout)
The HR Director (workforce notification and transition)
Legal Counsel retained by the agency
All agency personnel affected by a closure, merger, or change of ownership
This policy is activated when:
The Governing Body decides to permanently close the agency
The Governing Body decides to voluntarily terminate the agency's Medicare provider agreement
The agency undergoes a change of ownership as defined by 42 CFR § 489.18
The agency merges with or is acquired by another entity
Circumstances compel involuntary closure (e.g., regulatory enforcement, financial insolvency)
This policy does not apply to temporary service suspensions during emergencies (see OP-FM-005) or reductions in the scope of services (see GV-OG-003).
4. Policy Statement
4.1 Any decision to close the agency, voluntarily terminate the Medicare provider agreement, or initiate or accept a change of ownership shall be made exclusively by the Governing Body of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. The Administrator shall not independently initiate or agree to any such action without Governing Body authorization, documented in meeting minutes.
4.2 The Governing Body shall direct the closure, merger, or CHOW process to ensure: (a) all patients are safely transitioned to alternative providers with no disruption of essential services; (b) all regulatory notification requirements are met within mandated timeframes; (c) all employee obligations are fulfilled including appropriate notice and separation processes; (d) all financial obligations are resolved including outstanding claims, overpayments, and contractual liabilities; (e) all records are preserved and transferred in compliance with HIPAA, CMS, and California state retention requirements; (f) Legal Counsel is engaged to advise on all legal and regulatory aspects.
4.3 Patient safety and continuity of care shall be the highest priority in any closure or CHOW transition. No patient shall be discharged or transferred without a documented plan for continued care, per policy CL-CP-006 and CL-CP-007.
4.4 The agency shall comply with all CMS notification requirements for change of ownership under 42 CFR § 489.18, including notification to the CMS Medicare Administrative Contractor (MAC) and the state survey agency. Notification shall be submitted no later than the timeframes required by CMS and California state law.
4.5 The agency shall comply with all California HCAI notification requirements for change of ownership, closure, or surrender of license, including advance notice to HCAI and to all patients currently receiving services.
4.6 In the event of a change of ownership, the agency shall work cooperatively with the new owner to ensure the orderly transfer of the Medicare provider agreement, state license, patient records, and operational responsibilities, subject to CMS and HCAI approval.
