# CL-OA-006 — Documentation Hierarchy and Evidence Source Prioritization (1)



CL-OA-006 — Documentation Hierarchy and Evidence Source Prioritization

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes the priority ranking of evidence sources that authorized OASIS assessors and all clinical documentation authors at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. must apply when making clinical coding decisions, completing OASIS assessments, and creating or resolving discrepancies in clinical documentation. In home health, the same patient's clinical status may be described — sometimes differently — across multiple concurrent documentation sources: the referral discharge summary from a hospital, prior home health records from another agency, therapy evaluations, nursing visit notes from earlier in the same episode, the patient's own self-report, and the clinician's direct physical examination at the current assessment encounter. When these sources conflict, the assessor does not have discretion to simply choose the source that produces the most convenient coding result. The choice of evidence source must follow a defined, defensible, and clinically sound hierarchy that places the highest weight on contemporaneous direct clinical observation and the lowest weight on unverified secondary sources.

The absence of a documented evidence hierarchy creates three specific and serious failure modes. First, it enables financial motivation to influence source selection — an assessor who has no defined rule for choosing between sources may gravitate toward whichever source produces a higher payment group, consciously or unconsciously. Second, it creates inconsistency across assessors and across time, producing OASIS data that does not accurately represent the patient population and distorting the agency's quality measures. Third, it eliminates the audit trail that would allow the Director of Nursing, the OASIS Quality Reviewer, or a CMS surveyor to evaluate whether the assessor's coding decision was clinically sound.

This policy implements 42 CFR § 484.55(b) — which requires the comprehensive assessment to accurately reflect the patient's current health status — and the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual's foundational principle that OASIS responses must be grounded in clinical evidence collected during the assessment encounter, not inferred from external records.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

All registered nurses (RNs) authorized to conduct comprehensive OASIS assessments per CL-OA-003

The OASIS Quality Reviewer who validates evidence source use during quality reviews

The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager who oversees assessment accuracy

All clinical disciplines who contribute clinical information to the assessing RN during the assessment process

The Compliance Officer who reviews evidence source selection in the context of audits or compliance investigations

This policy governs evidence source selection for all OASIS data items at all assessment time points (SOC, ROC, RECERT, FU/SCIC, TRN, DC) and for all clinical documentation decisions where multiple evidence sources exist and must be prioritized. It does not alter the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual's item-specific instructions — where the Guidance Manual specifies a particular data collection method for a specific item, that specification governs. This policy establishes the framework that applies when the Guidance Manual does not specify a single source or when multiple applicable sources must be reconciled.

4. Policy Statement

4.1 All OASIS coding decisions at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall be grounded in evidence drawn from the highest available level of the Documentation Hierarchy defined in Section 5 of this policy. When evidence from a higher-level source is available and applicable, it shall govern the coding decision over evidence from a lower-level source.

4.2 No OASIS item response shall be selected on the basis of a lower-level evidence source when a higher-level source provides directly applicable clinical information for that item during the applicable look-back period.

4.3 When evidence sources conflict — that is, when a higher-level source and a lower-level source describe the patient's status differently for the same OASIS item — the assessor shall apply the evidence hierarchy per Section 5, document the conflicting sources in the assessment narrative, explain why the higher-level source was selected, and initiate the conflicting source reconciliation process per CL-OA-008 if the conflict has clinical implications beyond the immediate coding decision.

4.4 The evidence source used to support every OASIS coding decision shall be identifiable in the assessment narrative documentation. It is not sufficient to code an OASIS item accurately if the clinical record does not contain documentation that a reviewer or surveyor can use to verify the coding decision. An OASIS response that cannot be traced to a specific, documentable evidence source is an unsupported response, regardless of its clinical plausibility.

4.5 Financial considerations — including PDGM payment group optimization, episode management, or revenue targets — shall not influence evidence source selection. Selecting a lower-level evidence source because it produces a more favorable payment outcome, when a higher-level source is available and applicable, constitutes data manipulation and a potential violation of the False Claims Act. Any direction from revenue cycle personnel, management, or consultants to select evidence sources on the basis of payment optimization rather than clinical accuracy shall be reported to the Compliance Officer immediately per CO-CP-006.

4.6 The Documentation Hierarchy applies to all clinical documentation decisions, not exclusively to OASIS coding. When a clinician must choose between conflicting sources to document a clinical finding in a visit note, plan of care, or assessment narrative, the same hierarchy applies. Consistent application of the hierarchy across all documentation types reinforces the internal consistency of the clinical record.

4.7 The Director of Nursing shall incorporate evidence source documentation into the monthly OASIS quality audit per CL-OA-002, verifying that audited assessments contain traceable evidence references for high-risk item categories and that evidence source selection is consistent with this policy.

5. Definitions

6. The Documentation Hierarchy — Evidence Source Priority Ranking

6.1 Hierarchy Structure

The following hierarchy governs evidence source selection at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Sources are listed from highest priority (Level 1) to lowest priority (Level 6). When multiple sources are available, the highest applicable level governs.

LEVEL 1 — DIRECT CLINICAL OBSERVATION DURING THE ASSESSMENT ENCOUNTER (Highest Priority)

Definition: The assessing RN directly observes the patient performing the activity, demonstrating the functional level, or exhibiting the symptom being coded — during the current assessment visit, within the applicable look-back period.

Application: For GG functional items (GG0130, GG0170), Level 1 evidence is obtained by asking the patient to perform the activity during the visit and directly observing the performance — not by asking the patient or caregiver to describe what they can do. For wound assessment items (M1311–M1350), Level 1 evidence is obtained by undressing all wound dressings and directly observing wound status. For behavioral observation items (M1710, M1720, M1740), Level 1 evidence includes directly witnessing behavioral symptoms during the visit.

Documentation Standard: The visit note shall describe what the assessor directly observed — the specific activity, the level of assistance required, the qualitative characteristics of the performance, and any safety concerns identified. Documentation shall be specific enough that a second clinician reviewing the record could independently verify the observation.

Examples of Adequate Level 1 Documentation:

"Patient directly observed ambulating 25 feet in the hallway with rolling walker. Required standby assist for balance at the turn. Gait was slow, wide-based, and slightly unsteady but no loss of balance. Completed the distance in 48 seconds."

"Stage 2 pressure injury directly observed on the right heel — dressing removed for assessment. Wound measures 2.1 cm x 1.8 cm x 0.2 cm. Wound bed 80% granulation tissue, 20% yellow slough. Periwound skin is intact without maceration. No drainage. No odor."

Examples of Inadequate Level 1 Documentation:

"Patient ambulates with walker." (No observation detail; does not establish GG performance level)

"Wound healing well." (No objective wound assessment data)

LEVEL 2 — PHYSICAL EXAMINATION FINDINGS DURING THE ASSESSMENT ENCOUNTER

Definition: A clinical finding obtained through hands-on examination by the assessing RN during the current assessment visit — not observed activity performance, but assessed physical status.

Application: Applicable to items where the assessor performs an examination rather than observing activity performance. Examples include: lung auscultation findings for respiratory items; cardiac auscultation and peripheral edema palpation for cardiac items; skin turgor and mucous membrane assessment for hydration/nutritional items; neurological reflex testing; range of motion measurement; muscle strength grading; sensory testing (monofilament); blood pressure and vital signs measurement.

Documentation Standard: The examination finding shall be documented with the specific technique used, the specific finding obtained, and the specific anatomical location or measurement. A finding documented as "WNL" without supporting examination detail is inadequate.

Examples of Adequate Level 2 Documentation:

"Lung auscultation: bibasilar crackles present posterior fields bilaterally, approximately one-third of the way up from the bases. No wheezes. No pleural rub."

"Right lower extremity edema — pitting 2+ to mid-calf. Left lower extremity — trace non-pitting edema at ankle. Skin intact bilaterally, no breakdown."

"Active range of motion — right shoulder flexion to 95 degrees (limited by pain; patient reports 8/10 pain at endpoint). Strength — right deltoid 3+/5 per MMT."

LEVEL 3 — STANDARDIZED ASSESSMENT TOOL RESULTS FROM THE ASSESSMENT ENCOUNTER

Definition: Results from standardized assessment instruments administered by the assessing RN during the current assessment visit, in strict accordance with validated administration protocols.

Application: Applies specifically to CMS-required standardized tools — BIMS (for M1700), PHQ-2/PHQ-9 (for M1730/M1750), MAHC-10 (where applicable), and any other CMS-mandated tool for the current OASIS version. Also applies to validated discipline-specific tools used during the assessment (Timed Up and Go test, Berg Balance Scale, functional reach, etc.).

Documentation Standard: The specific tool name and version, all individual item scores, the total score, and the clinical interpretation of the score shall be documented. The administration conditions shall be noted — particularly any deviation from the standard protocol (patient fatigue, interpreter used, partial administration with reason). See CL-OA-011 for standardized tool administration requirements.

Critical Rule: A standardized tool score is only Level 3 evidence if the tool was administered in accordance with its validated protocol during the current assessment encounter. An abbreviated BIMS, a BIMS administered without the word repetition component, or a BIMS inferred from prior assessments does not constitute Level 3 evidence for the current assessment.

LEVEL 4 — PATIENT SELF-REPORT WITH CORROBORATION

Definition: Information provided verbally by the patient about their own symptoms, functional abilities, medication use, or history — when corroborated by at least one of: (a) physical examination findings; (b) caregiver report; (c) internal clinical record entries; or (d) external clinical documentation.

Application: Patient self-report is a primary evidence source for many OASIS items — particularly symptom items (pain, dyspnea, anxiety), activity items with look-back periods (where direct observation of the activity at the assessment is not feasible), and history items. However, self-report alone is not sufficient for items where objective verification is clinically feasible and required by the CMS Guidance Manual.

Uncorroborated Patient Self-Report: When a patient self-report cannot be corroborated — for example, a patient reports they are fully independent in ambulation, but no direct observation is performed and no corroborating source is available — the assessor shall document the report, note the absence of corroboration, and explain the clinical reason why direct observation was not performed. Uncorroborated self-report for items where objective verification is feasible constitutes Level 5 evidence (single source report), not Level 4.

Documentation Standard: The specific patient statement, the corroborating source, and the reconciliation of any inconsistency between the patient report and the corroborating source shall be documented.

LEVEL 5 — SINGLE-SOURCE REPORTS (CAREGIVER REPORT / UNCORROBORATED PATIENT SELF-REPORT / INTERNAL CLINICAL RECORD)

Definition: Information from a single source that cannot be corroborated by another independent source during the current assessment. This includes: caregiver-only reports (no corresponding patient confirmation or clinical examination finding); uncorroborated patient self-report for items where corroboration is feasible; and internal clinical record entries from prior visits during the current episode, when no direct observation at the current assessment is possible.

Application and Limitations: Level 5 sources are appropriate when Level 1–4 sources are not available or applicable — for example, when a patient lacks the communication capacity to self-report and no direct observation is possible, the caregiver's report of the patient's typical functional status is the best available evidence. Level 5 sources are also appropriate as the primary evidence for items with extended look-back periods, where the look-back extends beyond what the assessor can directly observe during a single visit.

Caregiver Report Caution: Caregiver reports may reflect the caregiver's capabilities and preferences rather than the patient's actual abilities. A caregiver who routinely performs all ADLs for the patient may report the patient as "unable to do anything" even if the patient retains residual abilities. The assessor must probe for the patient's actual typical performance, not the caregiver's typical assistance level. When caregiver report and clinical examination findings conflict, the clinical examination governs (Level 2 over Level 5).

Documentation Standard: The specific source, the information provided, and any limitations or qualifications of the source shall be documented. If Level 5 evidence is used for a GG functional item where direct observation is normally expected, the reason direct observation was not performed shall be documented.

LEVEL 6 — EXTERNAL CLINICAL DOCUMENTATION AND INFERENCE (Lowest Priority for Current Assessment Status)

Definition:

External clinical documentation: Records generated by providers outside Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. during a prior care episode or concurrent care encounter — including hospital discharge summaries, SNF records, physician office notes, specialist reports, and prior home health agency records.

Inference: A coding decision based on logical extrapolation from available evidence when no direct evidence is available for the specific item — for example, inferring a patient's pain level from behavioral observations when the patient cannot self-report.

Application — External Documentation: External documentation is appropriately used for: history items (diagnoses, surgical history, hospitalization history); medication list verification (cross-checked against the patient's actual medication bottles as Level 1/2 verification); establishing the patient's baseline prior to the current episode; and identifying clinical conditions documented by prior treating providers that the current assessor cannot independently verify.

External documentation is not appropriate as the primary basis for OASIS items that reflect the patient's current functional status, unless the external document is contemporaneous with the current assessment time point and from a qualified clinical provider who directly assessed the patient.

Critical Warning: Coding a patient's current GG functional status based on a hospital discharge summary's functional description, without direct observation at the SOC visit, is a Level 6 coding decision for a Level 1 item. This is the most common evidence hierarchy violation identified in ADR audits. The hospital's functional status description at discharge represents the patient's status at discharge — it does not represent the patient's status at the home health SOC, which may be better, worse, or the same.

Inference Application: When inference is the only available basis for a coding decision (patient is comatose; patient refuses assessment; patient is transferred and not present for the TRN assessment), the assessor shall document: the specific clinical evidence available; the inferential reasoning applied; and the confidence level in the inferred response. Inference-based responses shall be flagged for OASIS Quality Reviewer review per CL-OA-002.

Documentation Standard for Inference: "Direct observation of [specific activity] was not possible because [specific reason]. The available evidence — [description of available evidence] — supports a coding response of [response]. Reasoning: [specific clinical reasoning]."

10.2 What Surveyors and Auditors Will Look For

CMS OASIS validation surveyors will independently assess patients in the survey sample and compare their clinical findings to the submitted OASIS data. The most common discrepancy they identify — and the one most directly linked to evidence hierarchy violations — is GG functional items coded at a higher level of dependence than the patient's direct performance demonstrates. When a surveyor observes a patient ambulate 100 feet independently with a cane and the submitted OASIS shows "Substantial/Maximal Assistance" for walking, the inevitable conclusion is that the coding was not based on direct observation. Surveyors will ask the assessing RN to describe the evidence basis for the GG responses. If the assessor cannot identify a specific direct observation or examination finding — and the narrative does not document one — the assessment will be cited as inaccurate.

ADR auditors from the MAC will specifically examine assessment narratives for the presence of specific, objective clinical descriptions that corroborate OASIS responses. A narrative that says "patient requires assistance with mobility" without describing what was observed does not adequately support a specific GG functional item response. Auditors are trained to recognize when narratives have been copied from external sources (hospital discharge language appearing in the SOC narrative) or when the narrative descriptions are generic and non-specific.

CL-OA-007 — Evidence-Based OASIS Coding Substantiation

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy requires that every OASIS item response submitted by Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. be supported by verifiable, contemporaneous clinical documentation within the medical record — with a traceable evidentiary basis that a qualified reviewer can independently evaluate and validate. Evidence-based OASIS coding substantiation is the operational bridge between the clinical evidence collected during the assessment encounter (governed by CL-OA-006) and the OASIS data item response that is transmitted to the CMS repository (governed by CL-CA-003). The gap between these two processes — the space where a clinician has collected valid clinical evidence but failed to document it in a manner that supports the coded response — is one of the most consequential compliance vulnerabilities in home health.

CMS has made unambiguous through survey guidance, ADR audit methodology, and quality reporting standards that an OASIS response is not credible without supporting documentation in the clinical record. This principle is not merely a documentation preference — it is the operational mechanism by which the federal government verifies that Medicare payments for home health episodes are grounded in genuine clinical need. When the documentation does not support the coded response, the response is, by definition, unsupported — and an unsupported OASIS response in a submitted claim creates False Claims Act exposure regardless of whether the clinician believed the response was clinically accurate.

This policy establishes the specific documentation standards that constitute adequate substantiation for each major category of OASIS items, the pre-submission verification process that ensures substantiation before locking, and the audit methodology that validates substantiation across the agency's submitted assessments.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

All authorized OASIS assessors who complete and authenticate OASIS assessments

The OASIS Quality Reviewer who conducts pre-submission and post-submission accuracy reviews

The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager who oversees assessment documentation quality

The Compliance Officer who reviews substantiation adequacy in the context of audits and compliance investigations

Medical Records staff who verify clinical record completeness in relation to submitted OASIS data

4. Policy Statement

4.1 Every OASIS item response transmitted to the CMS repository by Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall be supported by contemporaneous clinical documentation within the patient's clinical record that: (a) was created at or near the time of the assessment encounter; (b) describes specific clinical observations, examination findings, or validated tool results that support the coded response; and (c) is traceable — meaning a qualified reviewer can identify the specific documentation entry and understand how it supports the specific OASIS response.

4.2 OASIS responses shall not be coded on the basis of clinical plausibility, diagnostic inference, or general knowledge of the patient's condition without specific supporting documentation. The fact that a patient with a particular diagnosis "would typically" exhibit a particular functional level is not documentation — it is assumption. Every response requires specific documentation.

4.3 The narrative clinical documentation shall be written before or concurrently with OASIS coding, not after. An OASIS response coded first, followed by narrative documentation reverse-engineered to support the coded response, does not constitute contemporaneous evidence-based substantiation. The documentation integrity requirement per CO-DC-001 and the audit trail maintained by the EHR will reflect the sequence of documentation.

4.4 Substantiation documentation shall be specific to the item being coded. Documentation that supports one OASIS item does not automatically substantiate a related item. Each item requires its own evidentiary basis unless the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual explicitly provides for grouped assessment.

4.5 The OASIS Quality Reviewer shall assess substantiation adequacy as a core component of every Level 2 quality review per CL-OA-002. An assessment where the OASIS Quality Reviewer cannot identify adequate substantiation for a coded response shall be treated as an accuracy deficiency and managed through the correction process per CL-OA-002.

4.6 Adequate substantiation is distinct from accurate coding. An OASIS response may be accurate — correctly reflecting the patient's clinical status — while being inadequately substantiated — because the documentation does not clearly connect the clinical evidence to the coded response. Both accuracy and substantiation are required. An accurately coded but inadequately substantiated OASIS assessment is a documentation compliance failure under 42 CFR § 484.110 and a billing compliance vulnerability.

5. Definitions

6. Item-Category Substantiation Standards

6.1 GG Functional Assessment Items (GG0130 Self-Care; GG0170 Mobility)

The GG items are the highest-risk OASIS category for substantiation deficiencies. They directly drive PDGM functional level classification, which affects Medicare payment across all episode types. ADR auditors examine GG item substantiation in virtually every home health claim audit.

Minimum Adequate Substantiation Standard for Each GG Item:

The narrative documentation shall contain ALL of the following elements for each GG item that is being coded:

The activity or action being assessed — stated explicitly (not implied)

What the patient actually did during the assessment — described in observable terms (not "can perform" or "is able to")

The level of physical assistance provided — described in the same terms as the OASIS response option (no helper needed; setup or clean-up only; supervision or touching assistance; partial/moderate/substantial/maximal/dependent)

Qualitative characteristics of the performance — speed, safety, quality, consistency

The surface, environment, or equipment context — where applicable (type of floor surface for ambulation; type of bed for bed mobility; type of stairs for stair climbing)

Any safety concerns or supervision requirements — identified during the activity observation

Adequate Substantiation Examples — GG0170B Walking 10 Feet:

Adequate: "Patient walked 10 feet on the linoleum kitchen floor, starting from a standing position at the counter. Patient required me to provide physical contact at the right elbow for balance (touching assistance) throughout the 10-foot distance. Walking speed was approximately 15 seconds for the distance. Gait was shuffling, wide-based; right foot cleared the floor adequately. Patient did not lose balance. No verbal cueing was required."

Inadequate: "Patient ambulates with assistance." — Does not describe the specific performance, the specific assistance level, or any observable characteristics.

Inadequate: "Patient requires moderate assist with ambulation per discharge summary." — Uses Level 6 external documentation for a Level 1 observation item without documentation that direct observation occurred.

Inadequate: "Patient ambulates with rolling walker, unsteady." — Does not establish the level of assistance; "unsteady" does not map to a specific GG response option.

Documentation Requirement for GG Items Where Observation Was Not Possible:

When a GG item cannot be assessed through direct observation (patient refuses; patient is medically unable to attempt the activity; the activity cannot safely be assessed at the visit), the assessor shall document:

The specific reason direct observation was not performed

What substitute evidence was used (caregiver report, prior visit notes, clinical examination)

The specific information from the substitute source that supports the coded response

The OASIS Quality Reviewer shall be notified of GG items coded without direct observation for pre-submission review

6.2 Wound Assessment Items (M1311–M1350)

Minimum Adequate Substantiation Standard:

For each wound for which an OASIS wound item is coded, the narrative shall document:

Wound location — specific anatomical site

Wound etiology — the clinical basis for the wound type classification

Wound stage or classification — per NPIAP (for pressure injuries) or applicable system; with the specific observable features that determine the stage

Wound measurements — L x W x D in centimeters, measured with a disposable tool at the current assessment

Wound bed description — percentage of each tissue type (granulation, epithelialization, slough, eschar, necrotic)

Drainage — type and amount

Periwound skin — specific description of the surrounding tissue

Signs of infection — specific indicators assessed and present or absent

Whether a dressing was removed — explicit statement that dressings were removed for wound assessment (demonstrates direct observation)

Critical Substantiation Rule — No Wound Item Without Wound Removal:

An OASIS wound item coded on the basis of a dressing change note that does not document dressing removal and direct wound observation is inadequately substantiated. Documentation that states "wound care performed per orders" without wound description details does not substantiate wound OASIS items.

Stage Determination Substantiation:

For pressure injury staging, the narrative shall document the specific tissue visible in the wound bed that determines the stage. A Stage 3 pressure injury requires documentation of full-thickness skin loss with exposure of subcutaneous tissue. A Stage 4 requires documentation of full-thickness skin and tissue loss with exposure of fascia, muscle, tendon, ligament, bone, or cartilage. Unstageable requires documentation of obscured wound bed (slough or eschar covering the wound base). An OASIS wound stage response without the specific tissue description that determines the stage is inadequately substantiated.

6.3 Behavioral Health Items — M1700 (Cognitive Function) / BIMS

Minimum Adequate Substantiation Standard:

For M1700 and all BIMS-derived items, the narrative shall document:

BIMS administration confirmation — explicit statement that the BIMS was administered during the current assessment encounter

Word repetition component score — the three words used and the number repeated correctly

Temporal orientation component scores — the patient's responses to year, month, and day of week questions; each scored individually

Word recall component score — the number of words recalled from the initial repetition

Total BIMS score — calculated from the components

Cognitive domain interpretation — "Cognitively Intact (13–15)" / "Moderately Impaired (8–12)" / "Severely Impaired (0–7)"

When BIMS Cannot Be Administered:

If the patient is unable to complete the BIMS (comatose, severe aphasia, refuses), document: the specific reason BIMS could not be administered; the Staff Assessment for Mental Status (SAMS) process used instead; the SAMS component responses; and the resulting cognitive function response per CMS OASIS-E2 guidance for the SAMS pathway.

6.4 Behavioral Health Items — M1730/M1750 (Depressive Feelings / Depression)

Minimum Adequate Substantiation Standard:

For M1730 and M1750, the narrative shall document:

PHQ-2 administration confirmation — explicit statement that the PHQ-2 was administered

PHQ-2 item 1 response — patient's verbatim or closely paraphrased answer and the numeric score assigned (0–3)

PHQ-2 item 2 response — patient's verbatim or closely paraphrased answer and the numeric score assigned (0–3)

PHQ-2 total score

PHQ-9 administration (if PHQ-2 ≥3) — all 9 item scores and the total score

PHQ-9 item 9 response and any safety assessment — documented separately per CL-SD-022

When PHQ Cannot Be Administered:

Document the specific reason and the clinical observation basis for the M1730/M1750 responses selected.

6.5 Medication Management Items (M2001, M2003, M2010)

Minimum Adequate Substantiation Standard:

For M2001 (Drug Regimen Review), the narrative shall document:

Confirmation that a drug regimen review was conducted — explicit statement with the methodology (physical inventory of medication bottles, comparison against prescription list, patient interview)

The specific potential clinically significant issues identified — or explicit statement that no issues were identified, with the specific checks performed (interactions, allergies, duplications, dose errors, contraindications)

The medications reviewed — the complete medication list reviewed, or reference to the medication reconciliation worksheet per CL-SD-013

For M2003 (Medication Follow-Up), the narrative shall document whether the physician was contacted within one calendar day of the drug regimen review for any identified potential clinically significant medication issues — with the date and method of contact, or with the reason contact was not made if M2001 was coded as "no potential issues identified."

For M2010 (High-Risk Medication Education), the narrative shall document the specific high-risk medications that were the subject of education, the specific educational content provided, and the patient's/caregiver's demonstrated understanding.

6.6 Homebound Status Substantiation

While homebound status is not a single OASIS item, it is the foundational eligibility determination that underlies every OASIS assessment and every Medicare claim. Homebound status substantiation shall meet the following minimum standard:

The assessment narrative shall document both CMS homebound criteria with patient-specific clinical language:

Criterion 1 (Condition-based normal inability to leave home): The specific medical condition or combination of conditions that restricts the patient's ability to leave the home — described in terms of specific functional limitations, symptoms, or physical constraints that result from the illness or injury.

Criterion 2 (Considerable and taxing effort required): The specific functional limitations that make leaving home require considerable and taxing effort — described in observable, measurable terms that a reviewer can evaluate.

See CL-CA-005 for the complete homebound status documentation standards. An inadequate homebound substantiation — generic language, diagnosis-only documentation, or copied language from a prior assessment — is not only a documentation deficiency but a potential basis for recoupment of the entire episode's claims.

6.7 Clinical Severity and Symptom Items

Minimum Adequate Substantiation Standard:

For symptom and severity items (dyspnea, pain, incontinence, etc.), the narrative shall document:

The specific assessment method used — validated scale, clinical examination, patient report

The patient's specific reported symptom description — verbatim or closely paraphrased

The objective clinical findings — vital signs, auscultation findings, observation of effort, scale scores

The functional impact — how the symptom limits the patient's activities

The frequency and context — when the symptom occurs, what triggers or relieves it

9.1 How Compliance Is Measured

9.2 What Surveyors and Auditors Will Look For

MAC ADR auditors examining OASIS substantiation will specifically look for:

The "triangulation" test — they will compare the OASIS response, the narrative documentation, and the patient's actual clinical status. All three must align. A high-dependence GG response with a narrative that does not describe high-dependence performance, for a patient who presents as functionally capable during the survey interview, fails the triangulation test.

Specificity of GG documentation — auditors are trained to reject generic mobility documentation. "Patient requires assistance with ambulation" does not substantiate a specific GG response option. The documentation must mirror the language of the OASIS response option — if the response is "Substantial/Maximal Assistance," the documentation must describe the level of physical assistance that corresponds to that response.

BIMS component documentation — auditors frequently note total BIMS scores documented without individual component scores. This pattern suggests the BIMS was not formally administered; a clinician who informally estimates a total BIMS score cannot produce individual component responses.

Contemporaneousness — auditors examine timestamps and documentation dates. An assessment narrative that was documented significantly after the assessment date, or that appears to have been populated from external sources rather than current clinical observation, fails the contemporaneousness requirement.

Absence of wound documentation when wound items are coded — M1311 coded as "yes, pressure injury present" without a corresponding wound assessment narrative at the current assessment time point is an immediate substantiation deficiency.

CL-OA-008 — Conflicting Documentation Source Resolution

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes the standardized reconciliation process that authorized OASIS assessors at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. must follow when documentation from referring facilities, prior care settings, or internal records contains conflicting clinical information that affects OASIS coding decisions. Documentation conflicts are not an exception in home health — they are a routine feature of the care environment. A patient is discharged from a hospital where the discharge summary describes them as requiring moderate assistance with bed mobility, but the home health SOC nurse observes them moving in bed with minimal physical contact. The hospital's medication reconciliation list includes a medication the patient has not taken for three months. The prior home health agency's discharge summary describes a Stage 3 pressure injury on the left heel, but the SOC nurse directly observes a wound that appears consistent with a Stage 2. The prior visit note describes SpO2 of 94% on room air, but the current assessment finds SpO2 of 88%.

Each of these conflicts creates a documentation decision with clinical, coding, billing, and compliance dimensions. The wrong choice — defaulting to the external document without explaining the conflict, or ignoring the prior internal record without acknowledging the change — produces either an inaccurate OASIS response, an inadequately substantiated response, or both. The right choice requires a defined, defensible process that: identifies the conflict explicitly; applies the evidence hierarchy from CL-OA-006 to determine which source governs; documents the reconciliation process; codes the OASIS on the basis of the governing source; and initiates clinical follow-up when the conflict has patient safety or care plan implications.

This policy implements the documentation integrity requirements of 42 CFR § 484.110, the accuracy requirements of 42 CFR § 484.55(b), and the cross-verification requirement established in CL-OA-013.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

All authorized OASIS assessors who encounter conflicting documentation sources during comprehensive assessments

The OASIS Quality Reviewer who identifies conflicting source issues during quality reviews

The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager who provides clinical adjudication when conflicts cannot be resolved by the assessor

All clinical disciplines who may identify conflicts between documentation sources during routine visits and must report them to the assessing RN

Physicians who may need to be notified of clinically significant conflicts per Section 6.4

4. Policy Statement

4.1 When documentation from two or more sources provides conflicting information about the same clinical finding or patient status — and that finding or status is relevant to an OASIS coding decision — the conflict shall be identified, documented, and resolved through the standardized process established in Section 6 of this policy before the OASIS response for the affected item is finalized.

4.2 Ignoring a known documentation conflict and coding from a single source without acknowledging or resolving the conflict is prohibited. Selecting the source that produces a more favorable payment outcome, without clinical justification for the selection, violates the evidence hierarchy established in CL-OA-006 and may constitute data manipulation per CO-FA-003.

4.3 The resolution of every documentation conflict affecting an OASIS coding decision shall be documented in the assessment narrative. The documentation shall identify: the conflicting sources; the nature of the conflict; the evidence hierarchy level of each source; the source selected to govern the coding decision; and the clinical reasoning supporting the selection. Conflicts resolved through clinical judgment rather than evidence hierarchy shall be documented through the clinical reasoning process per CL-OA-012.

4.4 When a documentation conflict has clinical implications beyond OASIS coding — such as a conflict between the referral diagnosis and the SOC clinical findings that suggests a new undiagnosed condition, or a conflict between the prior wound documentation and the current wound assessment that suggests clinical deterioration or improvement — the conflict shall trigger the appropriate clinical response per Section 6.4, including physician notification per CL-CP-005 when clinically indicated.

4.5 When a documentation conflict cannot be resolved by the assessing RN through application of the evidence hierarchy, the RN shall escalate to the Director of Nursing for clinical adjudication before locking the assessment. The Director of Nursing's determination represents the agency's official coding position for the affected item.

4.6 Documentation conflicts that appear systemic — occurring repeatedly across multiple patients, involving the same referring facility, or reflecting consistent discrepancies between internal records and clinical findings — shall be reported to the Director of Nursing and flagged for quality review. Systemic conflicts may indicate a population-level change in patient acuity, a facility's documentation practices, or a gap in the agency's clinical process that requires a QAPI response per QA-PG-001.

CL-OA-009 — Point-in-Time Assessment at Start of Care

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy requires that all SOC and ROC OASIS assessments at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. reflect the patient's clinical and functional status at the time of the actual assessment encounter — not at the time of discharge from the referring facility, not at the time the referral was received, not as described in the hospital's discharge summary, and not as the patient's condition was documented at a prior point in the care continuum. The point-in-time principle is one of the most foundational and most frequently violated rules of OASIS data collection. Its violation is also among the most consequential: when an assessment is coded to reflect discharge status rather than SOC status, it over-represents functional impairment at the start of the episode and over-represents functional improvement at discharge — producing inflated quality measure outcomes that misrepresent the agency's clinical performance.

More significantly, coding a patient's SOC functional status from a hospital discharge summary rather than from direct clinical observation at the SOC visit frequently produces artificially high PDGM functional impairment scores — which generate higher PDGM payments. When this pattern is systematic, it constitutes the textbook definition of upcoding: claiming payment for a level of service complexity that was not present at the point of care delivery, based on documentation of a prior clinical state that no longer reflected the patient's actual status. The False Claims Act does not require intent to defraud — it requires only that a claim was submitted that the submitter knew, or should have known, was false or fraudulent. A systematic practice of coding SOC functional status from discharge summaries rather than direct observation, when the agency's assessors are trained and authorized to conduct direct observation, satisfies that standard.

This policy establishes the point-in-time assessment requirement as an explicit, non-discretionary coding rule at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. — supplementing the broader evidence hierarchy in CL-OA-006 by specifically addressing the most common and highest-risk application of that hierarchy.

3. Scope

This policy applies to all authorized OASIS assessors conducting SOC and ROC assessments, the OASIS Quality Reviewer, and the Director of Nursing. The point-in-time principle also applies at all other OASIS time points (RECERT, FU/SCIC, TRN, DC), where it is addressed in the respective time-point policies. This policy focuses specifically on the SOC and ROC context because these are the time points where the transition from a prior care setting creates the most significant risk of time-point confusion.

4. Policy Statement

4.1 Every SOC and ROC OASIS assessment at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall reflect the patient's clinical and functional status at the time of the SOC or ROC assessment visit — the actual date the assessing RN conducted the comprehensive assessment in the patient's home. For items with defined look-back periods, the look-back is measured backward from the SOC/ROC assessment date, not from the patient's discharge date.

4.2 The patient's functional status, symptom status, clinical severity, and behavioral health status as documented in the hospital or SNF discharge summary represents the patient's status at the time of hospital or SNF discharge — not at the time of the home health SOC assessment. These are different points in time, and the patient's clinical status may have changed substantially during the interval between discharge and the SOC visit.

4.3 The common clinical changes that occur between hospital discharge and home health SOC include: functional improvement from early mobility programs initiated in the hospital; functional decline from deconditioning that occurs during the interval; medication changes at discharge that affect functional or cognitive status; wound progression or healing; pain improvement from discharge analgesics or pain regression as IV medications are transitioned to oral; and anxiety or depression that emerges in the home environment but was not apparent in the institutional setting.

4.4 The assessing RN shall conduct and document a direct clinical assessment of the patient's current functional status during the SOC or ROC visit — using direct observation, physical examination, and standardized tool administration — and shall code all OASIS items from that direct clinical assessment. The discharge documentation from the referring facility shall be used for history items (diagnosis history, surgical history, hospitalization history) but shall not substitute for direct observation of current functional status.

4.5 When a patient's direct SOC/ROC clinical assessment reveals functional abilities greater than described in the discharge summary — a patient who performs GG activities at a lower assistance level than the discharge documentation would suggest — this is not a discrepancy to be "corrected" by using the discharge documentation instead. It is expected clinical information reflecting the normal trajectory of recovery and the patient's actual status at the assessment time point. The direct observation governs.

4.6 When a patient's direct SOC/ROC clinical assessment reveals clinical deterioration or a new finding not described in the discharge summary — a new wound, a higher assistance level for mobility than documented at discharge, a new behavioral symptom — this information shall be coded from the direct observation and shall trigger appropriate clinical responses per CL-CP-002 and CL-CP-005.

CL-OA-010 — CMS Look-Back Period Compliance for Assessment Items

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy requires that all OASIS items with CMS-defined look-back periods at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. be coded using only information falling within the specified timeframe as measured from the applicable assessment date. Look-back periods are a fundamental structural feature of the OASIS instrument. They exist because many clinically important conditions — such as falls, incontinence episodes, behavioral symptoms, pressure injury occurrence, and high-risk medication use — cannot be fully assessed through a single-point-in-time clinical examination. They require information about what has happened to the patient over a defined preceding interval. The look-back period establishes the boundary of that interval — precisely enough that two assessors evaluating the same patient at the same time, using the same clinical information, should arrive at the same response for look-back-period items.

When look-back periods are ignored — when an assessor codes a 7-day look-back item based on information from 30 days prior, or uses information from within the look-back period that was actually generated at a prior care setting rather than during the applicable home health period — the result is an OASIS response that does not accurately describe the patient at the applicable time point. This produces payment classification errors and quality measure distortions. The integrity of the entire OASIS program as a quality measurement and payment instrument depends on every agency applying look-back periods correctly and consistently.

3. Scope

This policy applies to all authorized OASIS assessors, the OASIS Quality Reviewer, and the Director of Nursing. It governs all OASIS assessment time points where look-back period items are applicable.

4. Policy Statement

4.1 Every OASIS item with a CMS-defined look-back period shall be coded using only information that falls within that look-back period as measured backward from the applicable assessment date per the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual. Information outside the look-back period shall not be used as the basis for coding, regardless of its clinical relevance.

4.2 Look-back periods are measured from the assessment date — the date of the SOC visit, the ROC visit, the RECERT visit, or the FU assessment — not from any other reference point. For SOC assessments following hospital discharge, this means the look-back begins at the SOC assessment date and extends backward from that date.

4.3 When a look-back period overlaps with a prior care setting (hospital, SNF, prior home health episode), events that occurred within the look-back window at the prior care setting are included in the assessment. The setting in which an event occurred does not exclude it from the look-back window. An incontinence episode during a hospital stay that falls within the 14-day look-back of the SOC assessment date is within scope.

4.4 Information from outside the look-back period shall not be used to code look-back items, even when that information is more clinically compelling or more severely represents the patient's condition than information within the look-back window. The look-back boundary is a hard boundary.

4.5 When no information about the look-back period is available — for example, when a patient is transferred and the only functional information is from clinical records predating the look-back — the assessor shall document the limitation, apply the best available evidence within the look-back window, and use clinical reasoning documentation per CL-OA-012 to explain the coding decision.

CL-OA-011 — Standardized Assessment Tool Administration and Validity

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy requires that all CMS-mandated standardized assessment tools — including the BIMS, PHQ-2/PHQ-9, and MAHC-10 — be administered by authorized assessors at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. in strict accordance with their validated administration protocols, and that any deviation from the validated protocol is explicitly documented with a clinical justification. Standardized assessment tools are not interchangeable clinical judgment instruments — they are psychometric instruments whose validity, reliability, and normative scoring frameworks depend entirely on administration according to the validated protocol. A BIMS administered without the word repetition component does not produce a valid BIMS score. A PHQ-9 where the assessor substitutes their own questions for the validated 9 items does not produce a valid PHQ-9 score. An abbreviated or modified tool produces data that is incomparable to the normative framework, cannot be interpreted using the validated scoring ranges, and cannot be relied upon for OASIS coding purposes.

Beyond the psychometric validity issue, there is a compliance dimension: CMS OASIS item responses that are keyed to standardized tool scores — M1700 (BIMS), M1730 and M1750 (PHQ) — are required to be derived from the validated tools, not from clinical impression or informal assessment. Coding M1700 from clinical observation of the patient's general orientation without administering the BIMS is a coding rule violation per the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual, regardless of whether the resulting response is clinically plausible.

3. Scope

This policy applies to all authorized OASIS assessors who administer standardized assessment tools, the OASIS Quality Reviewer who validates tool administration, and the Director of Nursing who oversees tool competency.

4. Policy Statement

4.1 The BIMS, PHQ-2/PHQ-9, and MAHC-10 (where applicable) shall be administered during every applicable OASIS assessment time point at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc., unless a documented clinical exception applies per Section 6.3.

4.2 Each tool shall be administered exactly as specified in its validated protocol and as required by the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual. No item shall be omitted, reworded, reordered, or supplemented with non-protocol questions. The validated administration protocol for each tool is specified in Section 6 of this policy.

4.3 Tool administration results shall be documented at the individual item level — not only as total scores — to demonstrate that each component of the tool was administered and scored individually per the validated protocol.

4.4 An OASIS response that is keyed to a standardized tool result shall not be coded from clinical impression, from prior assessment scores, from other assessors' reports, or from informal screening — unless a documented clinical exception applies per Section 6.3 and the applicable CMS guidance for the alternative pathway.

4.5 All authorized OASIS assessors shall demonstrate competency in the administration of every required standardized tool before conducting independent OASIS assessments, per CL-OA-003. Annual re-competency shall include re-demonstration of BIMS administration as a mandatory component.

5. Definitions

6. Tool Administration Protocols

6.1 BIMS — Brief Interview for Mental Status

Administration Requirement: At every SOC, ROC, and RECERT assessment time point where M1700 is applicable, unless a documented clinical exception requires use of the SAMS pathway.

Validated Administration Protocol:

Step 1 — Introduction (do not skip): Say to the patient: "I am going to ask you some questions about your memory and thinking. Some of these questions will be easy, and some will be harder. Let's start with a short memory test."

Step 2 — Word Repetition Component (Component A — scored 0–3): Say: "I am going to say three words, and I would like you to repeat them back to me right now, and remember them for a few minutes. The three words are: [sock], [blue], [bed]." Use these specific words. Do not substitute. Do not provide multiple repetitions — the words are stated once. Ask the patient to repeat them. Record how many of the three words the patient repeated correctly (0, 1, 2, or 3).

Step 3 — Temporal Orientation Component (Component B — scored 0–4): Ask the following three questions in sequence:

"What year is it right now?" (Score 1 if correct; 0 if incorrect)

"What month is it right now?" (Score 1 if within 5 calendar days of a new month; otherwise 0 if correct, partial credit per OASIS guidance if within 5 days of month end)

"What day of the week is it today?" (Score 1 if correct; 0 if incorrect)

Follow the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual scoring rules for the month question — specific rules apply for responses that are close to a month boundary.

Step 4 — Word Recall Component (Component C — scored 0–3): After completing the temporal orientation questions (a pause of at least several minutes from the word repetition), say: "Now, what were those three words I asked you to remember a few minutes ago?" Record how many of the three original words the patient recalls (0, 1, 2, or 3). Do not provide cues. Do not repeat the words.

Step 5 — Score Calculation: BIMS Total Score = Component A + Component B + Component C (maximum 15)

Documentation Requirement: Document the three words used, each component score, and the total score. Example: "BIMS administered. Words: sock, blue, bed. Component A (repetition): 3/3. Component B (orientation): Year correct (1), Month correct (1), Day of week correct (1) = 3/3 total orientation. Component C (recall): 2/3 words recalled (sock, bed). BIMS Total Score: 8. Interpretation: Moderately Impaired."

6.2 PHQ-2 and PHQ-9 — Patient Health Questionnaire

Administration Requirement: PHQ-2 at every SOC, ROC, and RECERT assessment where M1730 is applicable. PHQ-9 when PHQ-2 total score is ≥3 or when clinical presentation indicates depression regardless of PHQ-2 score.

Validated PHQ-2 Administration Protocol:

Say to the patient: "I am going to ask you two questions about your mood over the past 2 weeks. Please answer based on how often you have been bothered by each of the following."

PHQ-2 Item 1: "Over the past 2 weeks, how often have you been bothered by having little interest or pleasure in doing things?"

PHQ-2 Item 2: "Over the past 2 weeks, how often have you been bothered by feeling down, depressed, or hopeless?"

For each item, use the exact response scale:

"Not at all" = 0

"Several days" = 1

"More than half the days" = 2

"Nearly every day" = 3

PHQ-2 Total Score = Item 1 + Item 2 (maximum 6)

A score of ≥3 is a positive screen requiring PHQ-9 administration.

Validated PHQ-9 Administration Protocol:

Continue from PHQ-2 (use the same 2-week look-back): "Now I have some additional questions about your mood over the past 2 weeks."

Items 3 through 9 (read verbatim using the validated 9-item PHQ-9 protocol per CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual):

"Trouble falling or staying asleep, or sleeping too much?"

"Feeling tired or having little energy?"

"Poor appetite or overeating?"

"Feeling bad about yourself — or that you are a failure or have let yourself or your family down?"

"Trouble concentrating on things, such as reading the newspaper or watching television?"

"Moving or speaking so slowly that other people could have noticed? Or the opposite — being so fidgety or restless that you have been moving around a lot more than usual?"

"Thoughts that you would be better off dead, or thoughts of hurting yourself in some way?"

Each item scored 0–3 using the same frequency scale as PHQ-2. PHQ-9 Total Score = Items 1–9 (maximum 27).

Item 9 Special Requirement: When a patient scores ≥1 on PHQ-9 Item 9, the suicidality assessment protocol per CL-SD-022 Section 6.5 is immediately triggered. Document the Item 9 response separately and clearly in the assessment narrative with notation that the suicidality assessment was initiated.

PHQ-9 Score Interpretation:

Documentation Requirement: Document each item response (0–3 score for each item), both the PHQ-2 and PHQ-9 total scores, the severity interpretation, and any Item 9 special action taken.

CL-OA-012 — Clinical Reasoning Documentation for Coding Decisions

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy requires that when an OASIS coding decision involves clinical judgment beyond direct observation — because the evidence is ambiguous, the patient's performance varied across the look-back period, the assessment tool could not be administered in full, or the CMS Guidance Manual does not unambiguously resolve the specific clinical scenario — the authorized assessor at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. documents the specific clinical reasoning that supports the selected response. Clinical reasoning documentation is both the compliance defense and the clinical integrity mechanism for judgment-based coding decisions.

Every OASIS item has a defined, correct response for every patient's clinical situation — but some items require clinical judgment to determine what that correct response is. When a patient's performance varies from day to day, and the item requires coding "typical" status, the determination of what is "typical" requires judgment. When a patient can complete 60% of a self-care activity independently but requires physical assistance for the remaining 40%, the selection of the appropriate GG response option requires judgment about the assistance level category. When the CMS Guidance Manual's response option definitions do not cleanly map to the patient's specific presentation, the assessor must apply clinical reasoning to identify the best-fit response.

Clinical reasoning documentation does not excuse inaccurate coding — it documents the reasoning behind accurate coding that required judgment to arrive at. A well-documented reasoning process for a difficult coding decision is a clinical integrity statement. An undocumented judgment call that happens to produce an accurate response is a compliance vulnerability, because it cannot be evaluated or defended.

3. Scope

This policy applies to all authorized OASIS assessors, the OASIS Quality Reviewer, and the Director of Nursing.

4. Policy Statement

4.1 When an OASIS coding decision requires clinical judgment beyond the direct application of documented clinical observations to a CMS Guidance Manual response option — specifically, when any of the triggering conditions in Section 5 apply — the assessor shall document the clinical reasoning that supports the selected response before locking the assessment.

4.2 Clinical reasoning documentation is required for judgment-based coding decisions; it is not required for routine coding decisions where the clinical evidence directly maps to the response option without ambiguity. The requirement is triggered by the conditions in Section 5, not applied universally to every item.

4.3 Clinical reasoning documentation shall include: the specific coding question being resolved; the relevant clinical evidence available; the alternative response options considered; the clinical reasoning applied to select among the options; and the selected response with its basis. The documentation shall be specific enough that a qualified OASIS assessor reviewing the record can evaluate whether the reasoning is clinically sound.

4.4 Clinical reasoning documentation does not substitute for clinical evidence. An assessor cannot document reasoning in place of conducting the assessment that would generate the evidence. If a GG item requires direct observation and the patient refused to perform the activity, the appropriate response is to document the refusal and apply the substitute evidence protocol per CL-OA-006 — not to document reasoning about what the patient's performance level "probably is" based on their diagnosis.

4.5 The OASIS Quality Reviewer shall specifically review clinical reasoning documentation during Level 2 quality reviews for all items coded under the triggering conditions in Section 5. Reasoning documentation that is absent, vague, or internally inconsistent shall be treated as an accuracy deficiency per CL-OA-002.

5. Triggering Conditions for Clinical Reasoning Documentation

Clinical reasoning documentation is required when any of the following conditions apply to a coding decision:

Condition 1 — Variable Patient Performance Across the Look-Back Period: The patient's performance of an activity or exhibition of a symptom varied significantly across the look-back window — some days better, some days worse — and the assessor must determine which response option best represents "typical" performance. The reasoning documentation shall describe the range of performance observed or reported, the frequency and pattern of better vs. worse days, and the basis for the typical performance determination.

Condition 2 — Partial Activity Completion: The patient completes part of an activity independently and requires physical assistance for another part, and the activity is not sub-divided at that level in the OASIS item. The reasoning documentation shall describe the specific portions of the activity completed independently and with assistance, and the basis for the selected GG response option that most closely represents the overall assistance level.

Condition 3 — Atypical Assessment Day Performance: The assessor has reason to believe that the patient's performance during the assessment visit was atypical — better or worse than their usual performance — due to a transient factor (acute pain, unusual fatigue, anxiety about the assessment, medication timing). The reasoning documentation shall describe the evidence of the atypical performance, the evidence about the patient's typical performance, and the assessment of which more accurately represents the patient's functional level per the CMS coding rules.

Condition 4 — Standardized Tool Partial Administration: The standardized tool could not be administered in full due to a clinical exception per CL-OA-011 Section 6.3, and the assessor must code the OASIS item using an alternative pathway. The reasoning documentation shall describe the specific component(s) not administered, the clinical exception, and the alternative evidence used.

Condition 5 — CMS Guidance Ambiguity: The CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual does not unambiguously resolve the coding decision for the patient's specific clinical situation. The reasoning documentation shall describe the specific ambiguity, the steps taken to resolve it (including any consultation with the Director of Nursing or OASIS contractor per CL-OA-004), and the clinical reasoning applied to the best-fit response.

Condition 6 — Caregiver Performs Activities for Patient: The patient's caregiver routinely performs activities for the patient, making it difficult to assess the patient's actual performance ability versus their habitual assistance level. The reasoning documentation shall describe the evidence about the patient's actual ability (from direct observation, patient self-report, and caregiver report) versus the habitual assistance level, and the basis for the coded response.

Condition 7 — Inferred Coding for Transfer OASIS: The Transfer OASIS is being completed retrospectively without the assessing RN having been present at the time of transfer. The reasoning documentation shall describe the sources used (last available visit note, patient/caregiver contact, hospital contact), the limitations of those sources, and the clinical reasoning applied to each coded response.

6. Clinical Reasoning Documentation Structure

The following structured format shall be used for all clinical reasoning documentation:

[Item Code] — Clinical Reasoning Entry:

"[OASIS item code and name]: Clinical judgment required for this coding decision. [Triggering condition]: [Describe the specific triggering condition]. Available evidence: [Describe the specific clinical evidence available — source, content, and evidence hierarchy level per CL-OA-006]. Alternative responses considered: [Identify the response options that were candidates]. Coding reasoning: [Explain why the selected response most accurately represents the patient's status per the applicable OASIS item definition]. Selected response: [State the response selected and confirm it is consistent with the CMS Guidance Manual definition for that response option]."

Example — Condition 1 (Variable Performance):

"GG0170B (Walking 10 Feet) — Clinical Reasoning Entry: Clinical judgment required. Triggering condition: Variable performance across the 7-day look-back. Available evidence (Level 4 — patient self-report corroborated by caregiver): Patient reports that on most days she walks from bedroom to bathroom (approximately 15 feet) without physical assistance, holding the wall for support. However, on two days this week (per caregiver report), patient required hand-holding assistance from the caregiver at the bathroom threshold due to increased dizziness following medication change on 7/3. Direct observation today (7/5): Patient walked 15 feet to the bathroom without physical contact assistance; required verbal cueing at the threshold. Alternative responses considered: 04 (Supervision or Touching Assistance — reflects typical performance most days and direct observation today) vs. 03 (Partial/Moderate Assistance — reflects the two days with hand-holding). Coding reasoning: Per CMS OASIS guidance for GG items, code reflects the patient's usual performance — the level that most commonly describes the patient. Patient performs independently or with supervision/touching on 5 of 7 days. The 2-day variance was associated with a new medication that has now been dose-adjusted. Typical performance is supervision/touching assistance. Selected response: 04 — Supervision or Touching Assistance."

CL-OA-013 — Cross-Document Verification Prior to Assessment Finalization

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy requires that prior to finalizing any comprehensive OASIS assessment at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc., the assessing registered nurse must reconcile assessment findings against all available documentation sources — including the referral record, physician orders, prior visit notes, medication reconciliation records, discipline-specific evaluations, and any external clinical documentation from referring facilities — to ensure that the completed assessment is internally consistent, externally corroborated where applicable, and free from material contradictions that would undermine its accuracy, integrity, or billing defensibility.

Cross-document verification is the quality control bridge between data collection and submission. Without it, individually accurate OASIS responses may conflict with each other or with the broader clinical record in ways that neither the assessor nor the OASIS Quality Reviewer would detect without a structured side-by-side comparison. A functional status OASIS response that conflicts with the medication list (e.g., a patient coded as independent in medication management who has a documented cognitive impairment severe enough to require supervised medication dispensing), a wound item response that conflicts with the wound care visit note from two days prior, or a discharge summary clinical picture that conflicts with the SOC homebound documentation — each of these represents a cross-document inconsistency that creates survey deficiency risk, ADR audit vulnerability, and clinical care coordination failure simultaneously.

This policy implements 42 CFR § 484.55(b) — requiring the comprehensive assessment to accurately reflect the patient's current health status — and 42 CFR § 484.110 — requiring clinical records to be complete, accurately written, and promptly completed. It is a companion policy to CL-OA-007 (Evidence-Based OASIS Coding Substantiation) and CL-OA-008 (Conflicting Documentation Source Resolution), which establish the substantiation and conflict resolution standards that cross-document verification enforces.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

All registered nurses (RNs) authorized to conduct comprehensive OASIS assessments per CL-OA-003

The OASIS Quality Reviewer who conducts pre-submission and post-submission accuracy reviews

The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager who oversees assessment completeness and accuracy

All clinical disciplines whose documentation outputs are subject to cross-verification against OASIS assessments

4. Policy Statement

4.1 Prior to locking any comprehensive OASIS assessment, the assessing RN shall conduct a structured cross-document verification review using the Cross-Document Verification Checklist (Appendix A) to confirm that the completed assessment is consistent with all available documentation sources and free from material contradictions.

4.2 Cross-document verification shall include comparison of the completed OASIS assessment against each of the following document categories, to the extent each category is available at the time of assessment finalization: (a) referral documentation including hospital discharge summaries, SNF records, and physician office notes; (b) the current physician order set; (c) the plan of care in development or the prior plan of care (for ROC and RECERT time points); (d) the medication reconciliation record per CL-SD-013; (e) all visit notes created during the applicable look-back period; (f) discipline-specific evaluation reports (PT, OT, SLP, MSW) if available; (g) laboratory and diagnostic results available in the clinical record.

4.3 When the cross-document verification identifies a material inconsistency between the completed OASIS assessment and another document in the clinical record, the assessing RN shall resolve the inconsistency before locking the assessment, applying the documentation hierarchy per CL-OA-006 and the conflict resolution process per CL-OA-008. The resolution shall be documented in the assessment narrative per CL-OA-012.

4.4 The cross-document verification checklist (Appendix A) shall be completed and documented in the EHR for every OASIS assessment at every applicable time point. A checklist that is not completed is treated as a missing verification — equivalent to not having performed the verification.

4.5 The OASIS Quality Reviewer shall specifically assess cross-document verification checklist completion and adequacy as part of every Level 2 quality review per CL-OA-002. An assessment where the checklist is absent, incomplete, or where the reviewer identifies an unresolved inconsistency not flagged by the checklist shall be treated as a quality deficiency requiring correction.

4.6 Cross-document verification is not a guarantee of accuracy — it is a structured process for detecting detectable inconsistencies. The verification does not substitute for the underlying evidence-based coding standards of CL-OA-006 and CL-OA-007. Both the evidence quality and the cross-document consistency are required.

CL-OA-014 — Medication Reconciliation — Prescribed Regimen vs. Actual Patient Behavior

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy requires that all medication-related OASIS assessment items at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. be coded based on the patient's actual medication-taking behavior — what the patient is actually doing with their medications in their home — rather than on the prescribed medication regimen alone. This distinction is one of the most clinically significant and most consistently misapplied rules in OASIS data collection. The prescribed regimen describes the physician's intent. The patient's actual behavior describes clinical reality. For medication management OASIS items, clinical reality governs.

A patient who has been prescribed a 10-medication daily regimen but who consistently takes only 4 of those medications, or who takes the correct medications but at incorrect doses or intervals, is not managing their medications per the prescribed regimen — and coding them as such produces an inaccurate OASIS item response that misrepresents the patient's risk level, understates the complexity of their care needs, and may contribute to the type of medication management failure that results in hospital readmission. The medication management OASIS items exist precisely to capture this gap — to distinguish between what is ordered and what is actually happening in the home.

This policy establishes the assessment methodology, documentation standards, and coding guidance for all medication-related OASIS items, with specific focus on the distinction between the prescribed regimen and actual patient behavior, as required by 42 CFR § 484.55(b) (accurate reflection of current health status) and the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual medication item-specific instructions.

3. Scope

This policy applies to all authorized OASIS assessors who complete medication management OASIS items, the OASIS Quality Reviewer, and the Director of Nursing. It also applies to all clinical disciplines who perform medication reviews during routine visits and who may identify medication adherence information relevant to OASIS coding. This policy governs OASIS coding for medication items at all applicable time points: SOC, ROC, RECERT, and DC.

4. Policy Statement

4.1 All medication management OASIS items shall be coded based on the patient's actual medication-taking behavior during the applicable look-back period — not based on the prescribed medication regimen, the physician's medication order, or the patient's stated intent to take medications as prescribed.

4.2 The assessment of actual medication-taking behavior shall be conducted through: (a) direct interview of the patient using standardized, open-ended questions about their medication use; (b) physical inventory and inspection of all medication bottles, containers, organizers, patches, and injectable supplies in the patient's home; (c) caregiver report of observed medication administration during the look-back period; (d) review of pharmacy refill history when accessible.

4.3 A drug regimen review per M2001 shall be conducted at every SOC, ROC, and RECERT assessment and shall compare the prescribed regimen — all physician-ordered medications — against the patient's actual medication inventory and self-reported adherence behavior, identifying all potential clinically significant medication issues per the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual definition of that term.

4.4 When a potential clinically significant medication issue is identified during the drug regimen review, the assessing RN shall contact the patient's physician within one calendar day of the assessment date, as required for M2003 coding. Documentation of the contact — including whether it occurred within the required timeframe — shall be present in the clinical record before the medication OASIS items are finalized.

4.5 Medication management OASIS items shall not be coded from the medication list alone. The medication list is a necessary input to the drug regimen review, but it does not constitute the assessment of actual patient behavior required for M2020 (Oral Medication Management), M2030 (Management of Injectable Medications), and related items.

4.6 The coded response for M2020, M2030, and related items shall reflect the patient's actual performance of medication management tasks — whether the patient is independently managing their medications, requiring assistance, unable to manage, or not applicable. The response shall be grounded in direct observation or corroborated patient/caregiver report per CL-OA-006, not in the assessor's inference about what a patient with particular diagnoses "should" be able to do.

CL-OA-016 — Scoring Methodology Integrity for Multi-Item Assessments

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy requires that for all OASIS items derived from multi-component standardized assessment tools — specifically the BIMS, PHQ-2, PHQ-9, and MAHC-10 — the individual component scores are correctly calculated, summed, and applied to the corresponding OASIS item responses in strict accordance with the scoring algorithms defined in the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual and the validated tool-specific scoring protocols at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Scoring methodology integrity is the technical precision layer of OASIS accuracy. A BIMS can be administered correctly — all three components in the correct sequence, using the correct items — but scored incorrectly, producing a total score that does not match the component responses. A PHQ-9 can be administered with the correct items in the correct order but have the frequency response options mis-scored (e.g., assigning 2 points for "Several days" instead of 1 point), producing a total score that systematically overstates or understates symptom severity. These scoring errors are distinct from administration errors (governed by CL-OA-011) and are distinct from coding errors (governed by CL-OA-004) — they are arithmetic and algorithmic failures in translating correctly administered tool results into the scores that drive OASIS item responses.

3. Scope

This policy applies to all authorized OASIS assessors who administer and score the BIMS, PHQ-2/PHQ-9, and MAHC-10, and to the OASIS Quality Reviewer and Director of Nursing who validate scoring accuracy.

4. Policy Statement

4.1 The scoring of all multi-component standardized assessment tools shall follow exactly the scoring algorithms specified in the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual and the validated tool-specific scoring guides. No agency-created scoring shortcuts, simplified scoring algorithms, or score estimation methods shall be used.

4.2 Individual component scores shall be calculated independently for each component before the total score is computed. Total scores shall not be estimated from clinical impression or from recall of prior administration results.

4.3 The OASIS item response derived from each standardized tool result shall correspond exactly to the score range-to-response mapping specified in the CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual. When a total score falls at a boundary between two response categories, the CMS guidance for that specific boundary shall govern the response selection.

4.4 All component scores and total scores shall be documented in the assessment narrative per CL-OA-011 before the derived OASIS item is coded. The coded OASIS response and the documented scores shall be internally consistent — an assessor cannot document a BIMS total of 14 (Cognitively Intact) and code M1700 at the Moderate Impairment level without a documented clinical explanation.

5. Scoring Algorithms — Required Methodology

5.1 BIMS Scoring Algorithm

Score-to-M1700 Response Mapping:

Total Score 13–15: M1700 = Cognitively Intact (Code per current CMS OASIS-E2 guidance)

Total Score 8–12: M1700 = Moderately Impaired (Code per current CMS OASIS-E2 guidance)

Total Score 0–7: M1700 = Severely Impaired (Code per current CMS OASIS-E2 guidance)

Scoring Integrity Rules:

Component A (word repetition) and Component C (word recall) use the same three words. The three words must be stated once in Component A and recalled without prompting in Component C. Any prompting during Component C recall results in 0 for that word.

The month temporal orientation question scoring rule for near-month-boundary responses must follow the current CMS OASIS-E2 Guidance Manual exactly — this rule has specific instructions that differ from a simple correct/incorrect binary. Assessors must reference the Guidance Manual for this item.

BIMS total score = Component A score + Component B score + Component C score. There is no weighting — all components contribute equally on a point-for-point basis.

5.2 PHQ-2 Scoring Algorithm

Scoring Rule: PHQ-2 total ≥3 = positive screen requiring PHQ-9 administration. PHQ-2 total < 3 = negative screen (PHQ-9 not required unless clinical presentation indicates depression regardless of PHQ-2 score).

5.3 PHQ-9 Scoring Algorithm

All 9 items use the same frequency response scale: Not at all = 0; Several days = 1; More than half the days = 2; Nearly every day = 3.

CL-OA-017 — Contemporaneous Documentation Requirement

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy requires that all clinical findings, observations, assessment data, standardized tool results, and clinical reasoning supporting OASIS assessment item responses at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. be documented at or near the time of the clinical encounter — not reconstructed from memory after the fact, not interpolated from subsequent clinical contacts, and not reverse-engineered from the OASIS item responses that were selected before the documentation was written. The contemporaneous documentation requirement is the foundational integrity standard of clinical recordkeeping. It applies broadly across all clinical documentation per CL-CD-001 and CL-CD-004, but carries heightened significance in the OASIS context because OASIS documentation directly drives regulatory compliance, billing classification, and public quality reporting in ways that make reconstruction-based documentation not merely a documentation shortcut but a clinical integrity failure with measurable systemic consequences.

The EHR audit trail — which is immutable, timestamped, and accessible to CMS surveyors, MAC auditors, and OIG investigators — records when every entry was created and when every OASIS response was selected. A visit that occurred on July 5 with OASIS documentation created on July 9 is visible in the audit trail as a non-contemporaneous entry. An assessor who coded the OASIS responses on July 5 and then wrote the supporting narrative on July 9 is documenting in reverse sequence — and the audit trail will show this. This policy establishes the specific standards, timelines, and accountability requirements that prevent non-contemporaneous documentation from occurring and address it definitively when it does.

3. Scope

This policy applies to all authorized OASIS assessors, the Director of Nursing, the OASIS Quality Reviewer, and Medical Records staff. It governs the timing and sequence of all clinical documentation created in connection with comprehensive OASIS assessments at all applicable time points.

4. Policy Statement

4.1 All clinical findings, physical examination results, patient and caregiver interview responses, standardized tool component scores and totals, and clinical reasoning entries that support OASIS item responses shall be documented in the EHR within 24 hours of the clinical encounter at which they were obtained. The 24-hour standard is the absolute maximum — same-day documentation is the agency's expected standard.

4.2 Clinical documentation supporting OASIS items shall be created in a sequence that reflects the actual clinical process: observations are made, data is collected, documentation is written, and OASIS responses are selected based on the documented evidence. The reverse sequence — selecting OASIS responses first and then writing documentation to support them — constitutes reverse-engineered documentation and is explicitly prohibited.

4.3 Late entry documentation — clinical narrative created after the 24-hour documentation standard of CL-CD-004 — is permissible when genuinely required by a documented clinical circumstance (clinical emergency during the visit, EHR system outage, assessor medical emergency). Late entries shall comply with the late entry standards of CO-DC-003, including the required attestation language identifying the actual date and time of documentation and the reason for the delay. Late entries that are created for the purpose of providing post-hoc justification for OASIS responses already coded do not constitute valid clinical documentation regardless of their content or the attestation applied.

4.4 OASIS item responses shall not be locked in the EHR without completed, authenticated supporting documentation in the assessment narrative. An OASIS item that is coded without corresponding narrative documentation at the time of coding is an unsupported response per CL-OA-007. The EHR workflow shall enforce narrative completion as a prerequisite for OASIS response entry where technically feasible.

4.5 The audit trail generated by the EHR for all OASIS-related documentation entries is a compliance record. It shall be maintained per CO-DC-001 and CO-HP-007, shall not be altered or deleted, and shall be available for review by the Compliance Officer, Director of Nursing, OASIS Quality Reviewer, CMS surveyors, and authorized audit entities.

CL-OA-018 — Clinician Competency Validation for OASIS Assessment

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes the complete validated competency assessment process — including all assessment instruments, performance standards, evaluation methodologies, evaluator qualifications, documentation requirements, remediation protocols, and ongoing competency maintenance standards — for all clinicians authorized to independently conduct comprehensive OASIS assessments at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. This policy is the operational companion to CL-OA-003 (OASIS Clinician Authorization & Competency), which defines the authorization structure and the OASIS Competency Program curriculum. CL-OA-018 defines how competency is measured, what tools are used to measure it, what passing standards apply, who is qualified to evaluate, and what happens when an assessor does not meet competency standards.

The distinction between having completed a training program and having demonstrated competency is clinically and legally significant. An assessor who has sat through an OASIS training module has met a training attendance requirement. An assessor who has demonstrated OASIS competency through validated performance evaluation has met a clinical governance standard. Only the latter qualifies an assessor to independently produce OASIS assessments that are submitted to the federal government as the basis for Medicare payment. This policy establishes the standards that distinguish training attendance from demonstrated competency.

3. Scope

This policy applies to all registered nurses who are candidates for OASIS assessor authorization, all currently authorized OASIS assessors, the Director of Nursing who serves as the designated Competency Evaluator, and the OASIS Quality Reviewer who has evaluator responsibilities for certain competency domains.

4. Policy Statement

4.1 All clinicians must demonstrate competency across the domains defined in Section 5 before being authorized to independently conduct OASIS assessments. Competency demonstration is distinct from training completion — it requires performance evaluation against defined standards, not merely attendance at or completion of training content.

4.2 Competency shall be evaluated by the Director of Nursing or a designated evaluator who: (a) holds current OASIS assessor authorization; (b) has administered the specific competency evaluation instruments used in this policy for at least 12 months; (c) has been designated in writing by the Director of Nursing as qualified to conduct competency evaluations for the specific competency domain being assessed.

4.3 Competency evaluation results — including all scores, evaluator observations, and determination of pass or fail — shall be documented using the OASIS Competency Validation Record (Appendix A) and filed in each assessor's personnel file per HR-WM-007.

4.4 Annual competency re-validation is required for all authorized assessors. The re-validation process is abbreviated compared to the initial validation but must include all components defined in Section 6.2. 4.5 An assessor who fails any single competency domain during initial or annual validation shall be immediately suspended from independent OASIS assessment pending remediation and re-evaluation of the failed domain. The assessor may conduct OASIS assessments only in a supervised capacity during the suspension period.

4.6 An assessor who fails the same competency domain twice in any 12-month period shall have their OASIS assessor authorization revoked and shall be required to complete the full initial OASIS Competency Program before re-applying for authorization.

CL-OA-019 — Pre-Submission Quality Review for Comprehensive Assessments

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes the structured pre-submission quality review process that every authorized OASIS assessor at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. must complete for every OASIS assessment at every applicable time point before locking and transmitting the assessment to the CMS repository. The pre-submission quality review is the final quality control gate between the assessor's work product and the federal data system — the last opportunity to identify and correct errors before they become compliance deficiencies, payment classification errors, or quality measure distortions.

The pre-submission quality review is not a post-completion re-read. It is a structured, checklist-driven systematic review that verifies specific, predefined quality criteria for each major OASIS item category. Its purpose is to catch the categories of errors that are most common, most consequential, and most detectable at the pre-submission stage: items left at default values without clinical justification; internal inconsistencies between related item groups; items where the documented evidence does not support the coded response; standardized tool scores that are inconsistent with the coded OASIS item; and homebound status documentation that does not meet the standards of CL-CA-005.

This policy establishes the complete Pre-Submission Quality Review Checklist (Appendix A), the required completion standards for each checklist section, the documentation requirements for the completed review, and the escalation process when the review identifies issues that cannot be resolved before the OASIS completion deadline.

3. Scope

This policy applies to all authorized OASIS assessors, the OASIS Quality Reviewer, and the Director of Nursing. The Pre-Submission Quality Review Checklist (Appendix A) is the operative document of this policy — no assessment shall be locked without the checklist being completed.

4. Policy Statement

4.1 Every OASIS assessment at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall undergo a structured pre-submission quality review using the Pre-Submission Quality Review Checklist (Appendix A) before the assessment is locked in the EHR. The review shall be conducted by the assessing RN as a self-review after completing all OASIS item selections and all supporting narrative documentation.

4.2 The Pre-Submission Quality Review Checklist is a required clinical record document — its completion shall be recorded in the EHR OASIS quality review module with the assessor's electronic signature and the date and time of completion. An OASIS assessment that is locked without a completed, authenticated checklist is a documentation compliance deficiency.

4.3 When the pre-submission quality review identifies a correctable issue — an item response that is not supported by documentation, an internal inconsistency, a missing required element — the assessor shall correct the issue before locking the assessment. Do not lock the assessment with known uncorrected issues.

4.4 When the pre-submission quality review identifies an issue that cannot be immediately resolved by the assessing RN — for example, an ambiguous clinical situation requiring Director of Nursing adjudication, or a missing document that requires clinical judgment about its absence — the assessor shall document the issue in the checklist, escalate to the Director of Nursing, and delay locking until the issue is resolved or the Director of Nursing authorizes locking with the documented limitation.

4.5 The OASIS Quality Reviewer's Level 2 review per CL-OA-002 shall include verification that the Pre-Submission Quality Review Checklist was completed for the reviewed assessment and that the checklist findings are consistent with the Level 2 reviewer's independent assessment of the assessment quality.

4.6 The pre-submission quality review is the assessor's responsibility and accountability. It is not a substitute for the OASIS Quality Reviewer's Level 2 review — both are required. The pre-submission review catches issues the assessor can self-identify; the Level 2 review provides independent expert validation.

5. Definitions

6. Pre-Submission Quality Review — Required Review Sections

The Pre-Submission Quality Review Checklist (Appendix A) consists of the following five review sections, each addressing a distinct quality dimension:

Section 1 — Completeness Check

Purpose: Verify that all applicable OASIS items for the current time point have been addressed with a clinician-selected response.

Required Actions:

Open the CMS OASIS-E2 item applicability table for the applicable time point (SOC, ROC, RECERT, FU, TRN, or DC).

For each applicable item, verify that a response has been selected and that the response is not a default value that was not reviewed.

For any item left blank, not applicable, or at a default value, verify that the clinical record contains a documented basis for that status (the patient's condition makes the item genuinely not applicable; the patient could not complete the required assessment).

Flag and correct any item that appears incomplete without a documented clinical basis.

Checklist Recording: Rate each item category as: ☐ Complete ☐ Incomplete — Issue Identified and Corrected ☐ Incomplete — Director of Nursing Consultation Required

Section 2 — Internal Consistency Check

Purpose: Verify that logically related OASIS items produce a coherent clinical picture without internal contradictions.

Required Item-Pair Consistency Reviews:

Checklist Recording: Rate each pair as: ☐ Consistent ☐ Inconsistency Identified and Resolved (resolution documented in narrative) ☐ Inconsistency — Director of Nursing Consultation Required

Section 3 — Evidence Substantiation Check

Purpose: Verify that the clinical narrative contains documented evidence supporting the coded OASIS response for each high-risk item category, per CL-OA-007.

Required Evidence Checks:

Checklist Recording: For each item category: ☐ Adequate substantiation documented ☐ Substantiation gap identified and corrected ☐ Substantiation gap — Director of Nursing Consultation Required

Section 4 — Standardized Tool Score Consistency Check

Purpose: Verify that standardized tool scores are documented correctly and map to the corresponding OASIS item response.

Required Score-to-Response Verifications:

Checklist Recording: For each tool: ☐ Score arithmetic correct; response mapping correct ☐ Arithmetic/mapping error identified and corrected ☐ PHQ-9 required but not administered — immediate action required before locking

Section 5 — Look-Back Period and Time-Point Accuracy Check

Purpose: Verify that look-back periods were correctly applied and that the assessment reflects the correct OASIS time point.

Required Verifications:

Confirm the assessment time point code is correct for the clinical event (SOC, ROC, RECERT, FU, TRN, or DC).

For each look-back item category, verify that the look-back window was measured from the assessment date — not from the discharge date or any other reference date.

For post-hospitalization SOC/ROC assessments, verify that the look-back window correctly encompasses the inpatient period per CL-OA-010.

Verify that point-in-time items (GG items at SOC/ROC) reflect the patient's status at the assessment visit — not at any other time point.

For RECERT assessments, verify the assessment date falls within the Day 56–60 window.

Checklist Recording: For each verification: ☐ Confirmed ☐ Issue identified and corrected ☐ Issue requiring Director of Nursing consultation

9.2 What Surveyors and Auditors Will Look For

CMS surveyors conducting OASIS validation surveys will request evidence of the agency's OASIS quality assurance process. The Pre-Submission Quality Review Checklist — when consistently completed and when it demonstrates a pattern of assessors actively identifying and resolving issues before submission — is direct evidence of a functioning OASIS quality governance program. An agency that cannot demonstrate a systematic pre-submission review process has no structural mechanism for preventing OASIS errors that are detectable before submission.

ADR auditors will look for evidence that errors that could have been caught before submission were not caught — particularly internal consistency errors (BIMS score inconsistent with M1700 coding) and substantiation gaps (GG items with no activity-specific observation documentation). These are precisely the errors a properly conducted pre-submission review should catch. When an ADR auditor finds them, the absence of a documented pre-submission review suggests the agency lacked the quality governance to prevent them.
