POLICY CO-HP-101 — HIPAA, CMIA & Sensitive Data Privacy Management

Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes the comprehensive privacy management program for Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. governing the collection, use, disclosure, retention, and destruction of protected health information (PHI), electronic protected health information (ePHI), and sensitive categories of health data. This policy ensures simultaneous compliance with the HIPAA Privacy Rule (45 CFR Part 160 and Part 164, Subparts A and E), the California Confidentiality of Medical Information Act (CMIA) (Cal. Civ. Code §§ 56–56.37), the Information Practices Act (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798 et seq.), and federal substance use disorder confidentiality requirements (42 CFR Part 2). This policy operationalizes all patient privacy rights, workforce privacy obligations, and sensitive data protections into a single, survey-defensible governance instrument.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

All members of the workforce of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. including employees, contractors, per diem staff, volunteers, students, and interns

All members of the Governing Body

The Agency Administrator

The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager

The Compliance Officer / Privacy Officer

All business associates and subcontractors who access, receive, create, maintain, or transmit PHI on behalf of the agency

All information systems, paper records, verbal communications, and electronic media containing PHI or ePHI

This policy does not apply to de-identified health information as defined under 45 CFR § 164.514(a)–(c), provided de-identification has been performed and documented per approved methodology.

4. Policy Statements

4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a comprehensive privacy program that satisfies all requirements of the HIPAA Privacy Rule (45 CFR Part 164, Subpart E), the CMIA (Cal. Civ. Code §§ 56–56.37), and 42 CFR Part 2 for substance use disorder records.

4.2 The agency shall designate a Privacy Officer with authority to develop, implement, and enforce all privacy policies and procedures. The Privacy Officer may be the same individual as the Compliance Officer, provided adequate authority and independence are maintained.

4.3 All uses and disclosures of PHI shall be limited to the minimum necessary to accomplish the intended purpose, per 45 CFR § 164.502(b) and policy CO-DG-101. Treatment, payment, and health care operations (TPO) uses are governed by role-based access profiles defining the minimum necessary for each job function.

4.4 The agency shall maintain and distribute a Notice of Privacy Practices (NPP) to all patients at the first service delivery contact, per 45 CFR § 164.520. The NPP shall describe all uses and disclosures, patient rights, and the agency's legal duties. A signed acknowledgment of receipt shall be obtained; if the patient declines to sign, the attempt and refusal shall be documented.

4.5 Patients have the following rights, which the agency shall honor within the timeframes required by HIPAA and California law:

Right to access PHI (45 CFR § 164.524) — responded to within 30 days (one 30-day extension permitted with written notice)

Right to request amendment of PHI (45 CFR § 164.526) — responded to within 60 days (one 30-day extension permitted)

Right to receive an accounting of disclosures (45 CFR § 164.528) — responded to within 60 days (one 30-day extension permitted)

Right to request restrictions on certain uses and disclosures (45 CFR § 164.522(a))

Right to request confidential communications (45 CFR § 164.522(b))

Right to receive a copy of the NPP (45 CFR § 164.520)

Right to file a complaint without retaliation

4.6 The agency shall apply heightened protections to the following categories of sensitive data, which are subject to more restrictive disclosure rules under California law and/or federal regulations:

Mental health records (Cal. Welf. & Inst. Code § 5328; CMIA)

HIV/AIDS test results and diagnosis (Cal. Health & Safety Code §§ 120975–121020)

Substance use disorder records (42 CFR Part 2)

Minor consent records (Cal. Fam. Code § 6920 et seq.)

Genetic information (GINA; Cal. Civ. Code § 56.17)

4.7 Substance use disorder records protected under 42 CFR Part 2 shall not be disclosed without the patient's specific written consent, except as permitted by 42 CFR Part 2 (medical emergency, qualified research, audit/evaluation, court order meeting § 2.64–2.67 requirements). Re-disclosure is prohibited and the agency shall include the Part 2 re-disclosure notice with any permitted disclosure.

4.8 All workforce members shall receive HIPAA privacy training within 14 calendar days of hire and annually thereafter. Training shall include CMIA requirements, sensitive data handling, and the agency's specific privacy procedures. Completion shall be documented and tracked per HR-TR-101.

4.9 Sanctions for privacy violations shall be applied consistently per CO-CP-004 (Code of Conduct & Ethics) and HR-ER-002 (Disciplinary Action & Progressive Discipline). Sanctions shall be proportionate to the severity of the violation and documented in the employee's personnel file. Failure to report a known privacy violation is itself a sanctionable offense.

4.10 Only the most current approved version of this policy shall be considered valid. Any revision requires re-acknowledgment by all workforce members within 14 calendar days of the revised effective date.

POLICY CO-BA-101 — Business Associate & Vendor PHI Management

Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes the requirements for identifying, qualifying, contracting with, monitoring, and terminating business associates and vendors who access, create, receive, maintain, or transmit protected health information (PHI) on behalf of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. This policy ensures compliance with the HIPAA Privacy Rule (45 CFR § 164.502(e) and § 164.504(e)), the HIPAA Security Rule (45 CFR § 164.314), the HITECH Act (42 U.S.C. § 17931 et seq.), and the California Confidentiality of Medical Information Act (CMIA) (Cal. Civ. Code § 56.10(c)). The agency shall not permit any entity to access PHI without an executed Business Associate Agreement (BAA) that meets all regulatory requirements.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

All third-party entities that access, create, receive, maintain, or transmit PHI on behalf of the agency, including but not limited to: electronic health record (EHR) vendors, billing/coding services, cloud storage providers, IT service providers, shredding services, interpreters/translation services, collection agencies, consultants, auditors, and legal counsel

All workforce members who engage, manage, or oversee vendor relationships involving PHI

The Compliance Officer / Privacy Officer

The Administrator

The IT Director / CISO

All subcontractor relationships through which PHI may flow downstream

This policy does not apply to: (a) disclosures to treating providers for treatment purposes (45 CFR § 164.502(a)(1)(ii)); (b) disclosures to plan sponsors per 45 CFR § 164.504(f); (c) conduit exceptions (postal services, internet service providers acting solely as conduits).

4. Policy Statements

4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall execute a compliant BAA with every business associate prior to permitting access to PHI, per 45 CFR § 164.502(e)(1) and CMIA Cal. Civ. Code § 56.10(c). No PHI shall be shared, transmitted, or made accessible to any entity until a fully executed BAA is on file.

4.2 The agency shall maintain a Business Associate Master Registry documenting all active and terminated business associate relationships, including: entity name, services provided, PHI categories accessed, BAA execution date, BAA expiration/renewal date, risk classification, and assigned internal owner.

4.3 All vendors with potential PHI access shall be classified into a risk tier prior to BAA execution:

Tier 1 — High Risk: Entities with direct, ongoing, and unrestricted access to PHI (e.g., EHR vendors, billing services, cloud hosting providers)

Tier 2 — Moderate Risk: Entities with limited or role-restricted access to PHI (e.g., consultants, auditors)

Tier 3 — Low Risk: Entities with incidental or one-time access potential (e.g., shredding services, on-site equipment maintenance)

4.4 BAA content shall include, at minimum, all elements required by 45 CFR § 164.504(e)(2) and § 164.314(a)(2), including but not limited to: (a) permitted uses and disclosures; (b) obligation to safeguard PHI; (c) obligation to report breaches; (d) obligation to make PHI available for patient access requests; (e) obligation to return or destroy PHI upon termination; (f) subcontractor requirements; (g) termination provisions.

4.5 The agency shall conduct vendor due diligence prior to BAA execution including: (a) verification of entity's HIPAA compliance program; (b) review of security controls for Tier 1 and Tier 2 vendors; (c) OIG/SAM exclusion screening; (d) reference verification for new Tier 1 vendors.

4.6 Business associate relationships shall be monitored throughout the term. Tier 1 vendors shall be reviewed annually, including confirmation that the vendor has not been excluded from federal programs and that no material changes have occurred in the vendor's security posture or services.

4.7 Upon termination of a business associate relationship, the agency shall ensure: (a) return or destruction of all PHI held by the business associate; (b) written certification of PHI destruction or return; (c) revocation of all system access within 24 hours of termination; (d) update of the Business Associate Master Registry.

4.8 Business associates are directly liable for breach notification to the agency within the timeframes specified in the BAA (not to exceed 30 calendar days of discovery, per HITECH). The agency shall include breach responsibility provisions in all BAAs.

POLICY CO-IR-101 — Security Incident Response & Breach Notification

Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes the comprehensive incident response and breach notification program for Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. covering the detection, classification, investigation, containment, remediation, breach determination, notification, and documentation of security incidents and breaches involving protected health information (PHI) and personally identifiable information (PII). This policy ensures compliance with the HIPAA Breach Notification Rule (45 CFR §§ 164.400–164.414), the HITECH Act (42 U.S.C. § 17932), California data breach notification law (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.82), and the CMIA breach provisions (Cal. Civ. Code § 56.36).

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

All incidents involving the unauthorized acquisition, access, use, or disclosure of PHI, ePHI, or PII maintained by Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.

All workforce members, business associates, subcontractors, and any entity with access to agency PHI

All information media — electronic, paper, verbal, and visual

All locations where PHI is stored, processed, transmitted, or accessed

4. Policy Statements

4.1 All workforce members shall report suspected or confirmed security incidents to the Privacy Officer and IT Director within 1 hour of discovery. Failure to report a suspected incident is a sanctionable offense.

4.2 The agency shall classify all security incidents using a standardized severity framework:

Level 1 — Critical: Confirmed breach affecting ≥ 500 individuals; ransomware affecting PHI systems; confirmed unauthorized access to sensitive data categories

Level 2 — High: Confirmed breach affecting < 500 individuals; unauthorized disclosure of PHI to a single unauthorized recipient; lost or stolen device containing unencrypted PHI

Level 3 — Moderate: Unauthorized access to PHI without evidence of acquisition or viewing; misdirected fax or email to wrong recipient (PHI not accessed)

Level 4 — Low: Near-miss events; policy violations without PHI exposure; unsuccessful phishing attempts

4.3 For every incident involving PHI, the Privacy Officer shall conduct the HIPAA 4-factor risk assessment per 45 CFR § 164.402(2) to determine whether a breach has occurred:

Factor 1: Nature and extent of PHI involved (types of identifiers, likelihood of re-identification)

Factor 2: Unauthorized person who used the PHI or to whom the disclosure was made

Factor 3: Whether the PHI was actually acquired or viewed

Factor 4: Extent to which risk to the PHI has been mitigated

4.4 If the 4-factor risk assessment demonstrates greater than a low probability that PHI has been compromised, the incident is a reportable breach under HIPAA. All three exceptions must also be evaluated: (a) unintentional acquisition by workforce member acting under authority (45 CFR § 164.402(1)(i)); (b) inadvertent disclosure between authorized persons (45 CFR § 164.402(1)(ii)); (c) good faith belief that unauthorized person could not retain information (45 CFR § 164.402(1)(iii)).

4.5 Notification timelines:

Individual notification (HIPAA): Without unreasonable delay, not later than 60 calendar days from the date of discovery of the breach (45 CFR § 164.404(b))

HHS notification (≥ 500 individuals): Within 60 calendar days via HHS breach portal (45 CFR § 164.408(b))

HHS notification (< 500 individuals): Within 60 calendar days after the end of the calendar year in which the breach was discovered (45 CFR § 164.408(c))

Media notification (≥ 500 individuals in a single state/jurisdiction): Within 60 calendar days (45 CFR § 164.406)

California notification (Cal. Civ. Code § 1798.82): In the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay. If breach involves ≥ 500 California residents, notify the California Attorney General

CMIA notification: If breach of medical information, notify per Cal. Civ. Code § 56.36

4.6 All investigations shall be completed within 30 calendar days of incident discovery. The Privacy Officer and IT Director shall co-lead investigations involving ePHI. The Compliance Officer shall be notified of all Level 1 and Level 2 incidents within 2 hours.

4.7 The agency shall conduct a post-incident review for all Level 1 and Level 2 incidents, including root cause analysis per QA-AE-002 and corrective action development per QA-AE-003.

POLICY CO-DG-101 — Data Governance & Minimum Necessary Enforcement

Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes the data governance program for Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. governing the lifecycle of protected health information (PHI) and electronic protected health information (ePHI) from creation through destruction. This policy operationalizes the HIPAA minimum necessary standard (45 CFR § 164.502(b)), defines access control validation requirements, mandates data minimization practices, enforces export and download controls, prevents unauthorized shadow systems, and ensures alignment with record retention requirements. This policy serves as the enterprise-level governance instrument for PHI lifecycle management and data stewardship.

3. Scope

This policy applies to all PHI and ePHI created, received, maintained, or transmitted by the agency in any medium, and to all workforce members, business associates, and systems that access, process, or store such data.

4. Policy Statements

4.1 The agency shall implement a PHI lifecycle management framework governing data creation, collection, use, disclosure, storage, transmission, archival, and destruction. Each phase shall have defined controls, responsible parties, and audit mechanisms.

4.2 The minimum necessary standard (45 CFR § 164.502(b)) shall be operationalized through role-based access profiles. Each job function shall have a defined PHI access profile specifying: (a) which categories of PHI the role may access; (b) the volume of records accessible; (c) the permitted uses; (d) the permitted disclosure recipients. Access profiles shall be reviewed annually.

4.3 Data minimization shall be enforced at every point of PHI handling: (a) collect only the PHI required for the identified purpose; (b) use only the PHI required to perform the task; (c) disclose only the PHI required by the recipient; (d) retain only the PHI required by the retention schedule.

4.4 All PHI exports, downloads, bulk queries, and external transmissions shall require documented justification and shall be logged. Bulk exports (≥ 50 records) shall require supervisory approval prior to execution.

4.5 Shadow systems — any unauthorized repository of PHI outside agency-approved systems (including personal drives, personal email, USB storage, personal cloud accounts, spreadsheets, and non-approved applications) — are prohibited. Violation constitutes a sanctionable offense.

4.6 PHI retention and destruction shall comply with CO-HP-007 (Record Retention & Destruction). Data subject to litigation hold shall be preserved per RM-ER-006 regardless of retention schedule.

4.7 The IT Director / CISO shall implement technical controls supporting data governance including: (a) role-based access control in the EHR and all PHI systems; (b) automatic session timeouts; (c) export/download logging; (d) DLP (Data Loss Prevention) monitoring where technically feasible; (e) encryption per IT-SC-003.

POLICY CO-FW-101 — Fraud, Waste & Abuse Prevention

Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes the comprehensive fraud, waste, and abuse (FWA) prevention, detection, investigation, and remediation program for Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. This policy ensures compliance with the federal False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. §§ 3729–3733), the Anti-Kickback Statute (42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b(b)), the Stark Law (42 U.S.C. § 1395nn), the Deficit Reduction Act of 2005 (§ 6032), OIG Compliance Program Guidance for Home Health Agencies, and CMS program integrity requirements.

3. Scope

This policy applies to all workforce members, Governing Body members, contractors, subcontractors, agents, and business associates of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. in connection with all federal and state healthcare program participation.

4. Policy Statements

4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. maintains a zero-tolerance policy for fraud, waste, and abuse. All workforce members are responsible for preventing, detecting, and reporting suspected FWA.

4.2 The agency shall maintain the following definitions:

Fraud: Knowingly and willfully executing or attempting to execute a scheme to defraud any healthcare program or to obtain money or property by means of false pretenses, representations, or promises.

Waste: Overutilization of services or practices that directly or indirectly result in unnecessary costs to federal healthcare programs, whether due to mismanagement, deficient practices, or abusive practices.

Abuse: Actions inconsistent with sound fiscal, business, or medical practices that result in unnecessary cost or in reimbursement for services not medically necessary or that fail to meet professionally recognized standards.

4.3 The agency shall provide accessible reporting mechanisms for suspected FWA including: (a) the compliance hotline per CO-CP-006; (b) direct report to the Compliance Officer; (c) written report via the compliance mailbox; (d) anonymous reporting option. Reports may be made anonymously and shall be protected under the agency's non-retaliation policy (CO-CP-005).

4.4 No workforce member shall be retaliated against for reporting suspected FWA in good faith. Retaliation is itself a sanctionable offense subject to immediate disciplinary action up to and including termination and referral to legal counsel.

4.5 All reported or detected FWA concerns shall be investigated per CO-CP-007 (Compliance Investigation Process). The Compliance Officer shall lead or oversee all investigations with appropriate independence.

4.6 Upon confirmation of FWA, the agency shall: (a) cease the fraudulent, wasteful, or abusive practice immediately; (b) quantify overpayment if applicable; (c) refund overpayments within 60 calendar days of identification per 42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7k(d) and the CMS 60-day repayment rule; (d) report to appropriate federal/state authorities as required by law; (e) implement corrective action per QA-AE-003; (f) apply workforce sanctions per HR-ER-002.

4.7 Disciplinary actions for confirmed FWA involvement include but are not limited to: (a) mandatory retraining; (b) written reprimand; (c) suspension; (d) termination; (e) referral to law enforcement; (f) referral to professional licensing boards.

4.8 The agency shall conduct proactive FWA monitoring including: (a) billing pattern analysis per FN-FP-007; (b) claims audit sampling per CO-RA-002; (c) visit documentation vs. claims reconciliation per CO-DC-004; (d) referral pattern monitoring per CO-FA-001.

POLICY CO-AI-101 — AI & Automated Systems Governance

Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes governance requirements for the use of artificial intelligence (AI), machine learning (ML), large language models (LLMs), and automated systems in the operations of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. This policy ensures that AI-assisted tools are used responsibly, transparently, and in compliance with HIPAA, the CMIA, CMS Conditions of Participation, and applicable professional standards. No AI or automated system shall make autonomous clinical decisions, generate fabricated documentation, or process PHI without authorization and safeguards.

3. Scope

This policy applies to all AI, ML, LLM, robotic process automation (RPA), and other automated systems used by the agency for any purpose including but not limited to: clinical decision support, documentation assistance, coding/billing support, scheduling optimization, quality analysis, predictive analytics, and administrative automation. This policy applies to all workforce members, contractors, and vendors who develop, deploy, or use such systems in connection with agency operations.

4. Policy Statements

4.1 No AI or automated system shall make autonomous clinical decisions. All clinical decisions — including diagnosis, treatment planning, medication management, care plan modifications, and patient assessments — require human clinical judgment by a licensed professional. AI may provide decision support inputs, but the licensed clinician retains full accountability for the clinical decision.

4.2 All AI-generated content used in clinical documentation, OASIS assessments, or care planning must be reviewed, validated, and authenticated by the responsible licensed clinician before becoming part of the clinical record. The clinician's signature on an AI-assisted document constitutes attestation that the content is accurate and reflects the clinician's independent professional judgment.

4.3 No AI system shall fabricate, hallucinate, or generate clinical observations, patient assessments, vital signs, interview responses, or any other clinical data. All clinical documentation must reflect actual patient encounters and direct clinical observations. Use of AI to generate fictitious clinical content constitutes fraud and is subject to immediate termination and potential legal referral.

4.4 PHI shall not be entered into, processed by, or transmitted to any AI system unless: (a) the system has been approved by the agency's IT Director / CISO and Compliance Officer; (b) a BAA is in place if the system is operated by a third party; (c) the system meets HIPAA Security Rule technical safeguard requirements; (d) data minimization principles are applied (only the minimum necessary PHI is used).

4.5 All AI systems used in agency operations shall be classified and approved:

Category A — Approved for PHI: Systems that have undergone full security assessment, BAA execution (if external), and privacy impact analysis. Listed on the Approved AI Systems Registry.

Category B — Approved, No PHI: Systems approved for use with de-identified data or non-PHI operational data only.

Category C — Prohibited: Systems that have not been assessed or that have failed assessment. Use of Category C systems with agency data is a sanctionable offense.

4.6 All AI system outputs affecting patient care, coding, billing, or compliance decisions shall be auditable. The agency shall maintain the ability to trace any AI-assisted decision or output to: (a) the input data; (b) the AI system used; (c) the human who reviewed and approved the output; (d) the date/time of review.

4.7 The agency shall maintain an AI Use Register documenting all AI systems in use, their category, purpose, data inputs, PHI exposure, approval status, responsible department, and review date.

3. Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, per diem staff, students, interns, and volunteers of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. who perform work activities in any location including the agency's office, patient homes, community settings, and while traveling for agency business.

4. Policy Statements

4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain an Injury and Illness Prevention Program (IIPP) as the foundation of its occupational safety program, per Cal. Code Regs., tit. 8, § 3203. The IIPP includes all seven required elements: (a) responsibility; (b) compliance; (c) communication; (d) hazard assessment; (e) accident/exposure investigation; (f) hazard correction; (g) training and instruction.

4.2 The agency designates the Risk Manager (or Administrator if no dedicated Risk Manager exists) as the person with authority and responsibility for implementing and maintaining the IIPP, per § 3203(a)(1).

4.3 Workplace Violence Prevention. The agency shall maintain a Workplace Violence Prevention Plan (WVPP) per Cal. Lab. Code § 6401.9 (SB 553), effective July 1, 2024. The WVPP shall: (a) identify the plan administrator; (b) include procedures for employee involvement in plan development; (c) define methods for accepting and responding to workplace violence reports; (d) establish communication procedures; (e) include emergency response protocols; (f) document workplace violence hazard assessments; (g) establish post-incident response and investigation procedures; (h) include training requirements. The WVPP shall be maintained as a written document accessible to all employees and Cal/OSHA.

4.4 Bloodborne Pathogens. The agency shall maintain an Exposure Control Plan (ECP) per 8 CCR § 5193, updated at least annually, identifying all job classifications with occupational exposure to blood and other potentially infectious materials (OPIM), the exposure determination, the schedule and method of implementing standard precautions, engineering controls, work practice controls, hepatitis B vaccination program, post-exposure evaluation and follow-up, training, and recordkeeping.

4.5 Aerosol Transmissible Diseases (ATD). The agency shall maintain an ATD Exposure Control Plan per 8 CCR § 5199, applicable to all employees who provide services in settings where occupational exposure to aerosol transmissible diseases may occur. The plan shall include: source identification, referral and transfer procedures, respiratory protection (N95 or higher), medical surveillance, training, exposure incident management, and surge capacity.

4.6 PPE. The agency shall provide, at no cost to employees, all personal protective equipment required for their job duties per 8 CCR § 3380 et seq. PPE selection shall be based on job hazard analysis. Training on proper use, maintenance, and limitations shall be provided at hire and when new hazards or PPE are introduced.

4.7 Hazard Communication. The agency shall maintain a Hazard Communication Program per 8 CCR § 5194, including: (a) written program; (b) Safety Data Sheets (SDS) for all hazardous chemicals present; (c) container labeling per GHS; (d) employee training on chemical hazards, SDS interpretation, and protective measures.

4.8 Incident Reporting and Investigation. All work-related injuries, illnesses, exposures, and near-misses shall be reported to the Risk Manager within 24 hours. The Risk Manager shall investigate all incidents to determine root cause and implement corrective actions per QA-AE-002 and QA-AE-003.

4.9 Ergonomics / Patient Handling. Clinical staff performing patient handling (lifting, transferring, repositioning) shall be trained in safe patient handling techniques and the use of available assistive equipment. The agency shall evaluate ergonomic hazards in patient care activities and implement controls.

4.10 Heat Illness Prevention. For employees who work outdoors or in environments without adequate climate control (including traveling between patient homes in extreme heat), the agency shall implement heat illness prevention measures per 8 CCR § 3395, including: (a) provision of water; (b) access to shade/cool-down areas; (c) high heat procedures; (d) emergency response.

4.11 Driving / Field Safety. All employees who drive for agency business shall comply with motor vehicle safety requirements per RM-SS-003. Additional field safety measures include: (a) pre-visit safety assessment; (b) check-in/check-out procedures; (c) high-risk visit protocols per RM-SS-001; (d) prohibition on distracted driving.

POLICY HR-EH-101 — Employee Health, Exposure & Occupational Clearance

Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes the employee health, occupational exposure management, and fitness-for-duty program for Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. This policy ensures compliance with California TB screening requirements (Cal. Health & Safety Code § 121525; Cal. Code Regs., tit. 17, § 2593), Cal/OSHA bloodborne pathogen exposure requirements (8 CCR § 5193), Cal/OSHA aerosol transmissible disease requirements (8 CCR § 5199), hepatitis B vaccination mandates, occupational exposure incident management, fitness-for-duty evaluation, and return-to-work clearance. This policy protects both the workforce and the patients served by ensuring that employees are medically screened, vaccinated as required, and fit to perform their duties safely.

3. Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, per diem staff, students, interns, and volunteers who perform direct patient care or have occupational exposure to blood, OPIM, or aerosol transmissible pathogens.

4. Policy Statements

4.1 TB Screening. All employees shall undergo tuberculosis risk assessment and screening per California law (Cal. Health & Safety Code § 121525; 17 CCR § 2593):

Pre-employment: TB risk assessment and, if indicated, TB test (TST or IGRA) prior to patient contact. Employees with a positive TB test shall have a chest X-ray and medical evaluation to rule out active disease.

Annual: Annual TB risk assessment. Repeat TB testing as indicated by risk assessment or upon known exposure.

Post-exposure: TB testing within 8–10 weeks of a significant exposure to a person with suspected or confirmed active TB.

4.2 Vaccination Tracking. The agency shall maintain vaccination records for all employees with occupational exposure:

Hepatitis B: Offered within 10 working days of initial assignment per 8 CCR § 5193. Three-dose series tracked; declination documented.

Influenza: Annual vaccination recommended; declination documented.

COVID-19: Per current CDPH and CMS requirements.

Other: As required by public health order or facility-specific requirements.

4.3 Exposure Response. For all occupational exposure incidents (bloodborne, airborne, or other hazardous material):

Immediate first aid and decontamination

Medical evaluation within 24 hours (BBP) or as clinically indicated (ATD/chemical)

Source patient testing (with consent) if applicable

Employee testing per post-exposure protocol

Follow-up testing at intervals defined by the agency's Exposure Control Plan and medical provider

Workers' compensation claim initiation per HR-WM-004

4.4 Fit-for-Duty. Employees must be medically fit to perform the essential functions of their position. Fit-for-duty evaluation may be required: (a) prior to initial assignment; (b) upon return from medical leave exceeding 5 consecutive calendar days; (c) when a supervisor has documented concerns about an employee's ability to perform essential functions safely; (d) per Cal/OSHA medical surveillance requirements (e.g., respirator use medical clearance per 8 CCR § 5144).

4.5 Return-to-Work Clearance. Employees returning to duty after: (a) work-related injury or illness; (b) medical leave exceeding 5 calendar days; (c) occupational exposure requiring treatment; or (d) communicable disease must provide written medical clearance from a licensed healthcare provider specifying: (i) fitness to return to full or modified duty; (ii) any work restrictions; (iii) duration of restrictions (if any). Modified duty accommodations shall be evaluated per ADA and FEHA requirements.
