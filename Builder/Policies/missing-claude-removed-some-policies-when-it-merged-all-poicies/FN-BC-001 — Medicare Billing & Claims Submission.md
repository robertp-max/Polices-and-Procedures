FN-BC-001 — Medicare Billing & Claims Submission

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for the accurate, timely, and compliant submission of Medicare claims for home health services provided by Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Proper billing practices are a fundamental requirement of Medicare participation and a core component of the agency's corporate compliance program. Every claim submitted to Medicare must reflect only services that were actually rendered, are clinically supported by complete contemporaneous documentation, were physician-ordered, and meet all Medicare coverage requirements under 42 CFR Part 484 and the Patient-Driven Groupings Model (PDGM). Inaccurate or fraudulent billing exposes the agency to False Claims Act liability, Civil Monetary Penalties, Medicare exclusion, and criminal prosecution. This policy ensures the agency satisfies the requirements of 42 CFR Part 424 (Conditions for Medicare Payment), the CMS Medicare Claims Processing Manual, and the OIG Compliance Program Guidance for Home Health Agencies.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

CFO / Revenue Cycle Director

All billing coordinators and billing specialists

All certified coders performing diagnosis coding or PDGM classification

Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager (for clinical documentation completeness and clinical billing correlation)

Compliance Officer (for audit, monitoring, and investigation functions)

Administrator

QAPI Coordinator (for billing quality metrics integration)

Any contracted billing service, clearinghouse, or third-party entity performing billing functions on behalf of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.

This policy does not apply to:

Direct patient care delivery staff except to the extent that their documentation directly supports the billing process. Clinical documentation requirements supporting billing are governed by CL-CD-001 through CL-CD-004, CL-OA-001 through CL-OA-019, and CL-CA-001 through CL-CA-007.

4. Policy Statements

4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall submit Medicare claims only for services that: (a) were actually and completely rendered by a qualified clinician; (b) are supported by complete, contemporaneous, authenticated clinical documentation in the medical record; (c) were ordered by an eligible physician or allowed practitioner through a signed Plan of Care; (d) meet all Medicare coverage criteria including homebound status, skilled care need, and medical necessity; and (e) are coded accurately per current ICD-10-CM guidelines and PDGM classification rules.

4.2 No claim shall be submitted for any service where the required physician certification, face-to-face encounter documentation, OASIS transmission acceptance, or clinical visit documentation is incomplete, expired, or not on file at the time of claim submission.

4.3 All claims shall be submitted within the CMS timely filing deadline (currently 12 months from the date of service) and shall comply with all HIPAA electronic transaction standards (837I institutional claim format).

4.4 The Revenue Cycle Director shall maintain a claims submission calendar, tracking log, and pre-billing verification system for all open episodes to prevent missed filing deadlines and ensure complete documentation support before any claim is released.

4.5 Any claim identified as potentially inaccurate, unsupported by documentation, or otherwise questionable shall be placed on billing hold immediately. Held claims shall not be submitted until the deficiency is resolved, documented, and cleared by the Revenue Cycle Director.

4.6 The agency shall never upcode, unbundle, duplicate bill, bill for services not rendered, or manipulate coding to maximize reimbursement beyond what documentation supports. Any such conduct constitutes grounds for immediate termination and referral to the Compliance Officer for investigation under policies CO-FA-003 and CO-CP-007.

4.7 All billing personnel shall complete initial and annual training on Medicare billing requirements, PDGM payment methodology, False Claims Act obligations, and agency billing policies, with documented competency validation, per policy CO-CP-008.

4.8 Claims submission processes shall be integrated with the agency's corporate compliance program (CO-CP-001), documentation audit program (CO-DC-002), and triangulation audit process (CO-DC-004) to ensure continuous alignment between clinical care delivery, clinical documentation, and billed claims.

4.9 Only the most current approved version of this policy shall be considered valid. Superseded versions must not be used for any operational, billing, or compliance purpose. Any revision requires re-acknowledgment by all personnel within scope within 14 calendar days.

CMS surveyors and MAC auditors reviewing the agency's billing practices will specifically verify:

Evidence that the agency has a billing quality control process. Surveyors will request evidence of pre-billing verification procedures. Agencies that cannot demonstrate a structured verification process risk Standard-level deficiency under 42 CFR § 484.100 (compliance with federal laws).

FN-BC-002 — Claims Denial Management & Appeals

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes a systematic, comprehensive process for tracking, analyzing, categorizing, appealing, and preventing Medicare claims denials at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Unmanaged denials result in revenue loss, cash flow disruption, and undetected systemic billing or documentation deficiencies. Every denial — regardless of amount — must be investigated for root cause because even small denials may indicate patterns of non-compliance that, if uncorrected, constitute systemic billing errors or False Claims Act exposure. This policy ensures that every denial is reviewed within defined timeframes, appropriately appealed when meritorious, written off only when justified and authorized, and analyzed for root cause to drive continuous improvement through the agency's QAPI program.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

CFO / Revenue Cycle Director

All billing coordinators and billing specialists

All certified coders

Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager (for clinical denial review)

Compliance Officer (for denial pattern analysis and compliance correlation)

Administrator

QAPI Coordinator (for denial trending integration with quality program)

Any contracted billing service performing denial management functions

This policy does not apply to:

Non-Medicare denials from commercial payers or Medicare Advantage plans are managed using similar principles but may have different appeal timelines and processes. Revenue Cycle Director shall maintain payer-specific appeal protocols for non-Medicare payers.

4. Policy Statements

4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a formal denial management program that tracks 100% of Medicare claim denials from initial receipt through final resolution (paid on appeal, written off with authorization, or all appeal levels exhausted).

4.2 Every denial shall be reviewed within 5 business days of receipt to determine: (a) the denial reason code and category; (b) whether the denial is technically or clinically valid; (c) whether the denial is appealable; (d) the appropriate level and strategy of appeal; (e) whether the denial reflects a systemic documentation, coding, or billing deficiency requiring broader corrective action.

4.3 All clinical denials (medical necessity, homebound status, documentation insufficiency, coverage criteria) shall be reviewed jointly by the Revenue Cycle Director and Director of Nursing before any appeal decision or write-off is authorized. The Director of Nursing's clinical assessment of appeal merit shall be documented.

4.4 The agency shall pursue all meritorious appeals through the full Medicare administrative appeals process. No claim with a legitimate clinical and documentation basis shall be written off without exhausting at least the Redetermination (Level 1) level of appeal.

4.5 No clinical denial shall be written off without the countersignature of the CFO and a written rationale documenting why the denial is valid or why appeal would be non-meritorious. The Write-Off Authorization Form (Appendix C) is required.

4.6 Denial trending data shall be reported to the CFO monthly, included in the quarterly compliance report to the Governing Body per GV-GB-001 §6.2.3, and integrated with QAPI performance monitoring per QA-PI-002.

4.7 Any single denial category accounting for more than 3% of total claims submitted in any 90-day period shall trigger a formal root cause analysis and corrective action plan within 30 calendar days per QA-AE-002 and QA-AE-003.

4.8 The Revenue Cycle Director shall maintain current knowledge of all Medicare appeal deadlines, appeal level requirements, and amount-in-controversy thresholds, updated annually.

4.9 ADR (Additional Documentation Request) responses shall be treated as the highest priority billing activity. All ADR deadlines shall be tracked on a separate ADR log with escalation protocol.

FN-BC-003 — Patient Billing & Financial Responsibility

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes comprehensive standards for patient billing, financial responsibility notification, Advance Beneficiary Notice of Noncoverage (ABN) issuance, billing statement management, and collection of patient financial obligations at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Under 42 CFR § 484.50(a)(3), patients must be informed of expected payment sources and any charges for which the patient may be responsible. Patients have a fundamental right to understand their financial responsibility before and during the receipt of services. The agency has a legal, ethical, and regulatory obligation to bill patients accurately, transparently, and in compliance with all applicable federal, state, and consumer protection laws.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

CFO / Revenue Cycle Director

All billing coordinators and billing specialists

All intake coordinators (financial screening at admission)

Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager (ABN clinical determinations)

Compliance Officer

Administrator

Any contracted billing or collection service acting on behalf of the agency

4. Policy Statements

4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall provide every patient (or authorized representative) with a clear, written explanation of their financial responsibility — including applicable insurance benefits, cost-sharing obligations, and any anticipated non-covered services — prior to or at the time of admission, before services begin.

4.2 The agency shall inform each patient of the toll-free CMS Home Health Hotline number and the agency's own billing inquiry contact information at admission per 42 CFR § 484.50(a)(3).

4.3 Advance Beneficiary Notices of Noncoverage (ABNs) shall be issued to Medicare fee-for-service beneficiaries using the current CMS-mandated form (CMS-R-131) whenever the agency has reason to believe a specific item or service will not be covered by Medicare. ABNs shall be issued before the non-covered service is provided and shall never be used as routine pre-admission documents or blanket waivers.

4.4 Patient billing statements shall be accurate, itemized, clearly readable, and include the agency's billing inquiry contact information. Statements shall be issued monthly for all open patient balances.

4.5 The agency shall not discriminate in billing practices based on race, color, national origin, disability, age, sex, religion, or any other protected characteristic under federal or state law.

4.6 Patient billing complaints and disputes shall be received, investigated, and resolved within 14 calendar days of receipt, documented per policy OP-PA-001, and shall not result in any interruption of clinically necessary services during the dispute period.

4.7 Collection activities shall be conducted in compliance with the Fair Debt Collection Practices Act (FDCPA) and applicable California state debt collection laws. No collection activity shall be initiated while a valid billing dispute is pending or a charity care application is under review.

4.8 No patient shall be denied medically necessary services due to inability to pay without first being screened for charity care or financial assistance eligibility per policy FN-FP-004.

4.9 All financial responsibility documentation shall be maintained in the patient's billing record and retained per CO-HP-007.

FN-BC-004 — Overpayment Identification & Refund

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

Federal law — specifically Section 1128J(d) of the Social Security Act, as added by Section 6402(a) of the Affordable Care Act, and codified at 42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7k(d) and implementing regulation 42 CFR § 401.305 — requires Medicare providers to report and return identified overpayments within 60 calendar days of the date the overpayment is identified. Failure to report and return an identified overpayment within this 60-day window creates a "reverse false claim" under the False Claims Act, exposing the agency to treble damages and per-claim penalties of up to $27,894 per violation (2025 adjusted amount). This obligation extends 6 years from the date the overpayment was received.

This policy establishes the mandatory process for identifying, investigating, quantifying, reporting, and returning all Medicare and Medicaid overpayments at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. within full compliance with the 60-day rule. There are no exceptions to this requirement. No amount is too small to report. No business justification exists for retaining a known overpayment.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

CFO / Revenue Cycle Director

All billing coordinators, billing specialists, and certified coders

Compliance Officer (overpayment investigation, reporting, 60-day tracking)

Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager (clinical review of clinically-driven overpayments)

Administrator

Governing Body (notification of material overpayments per GV-GB-001)

Any contractor performing billing, coding, or compliance functions

4. Policy Statements

4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall report and return any identified overpayment to Medicare or Medicaid within 60 calendar days of the date the overpayment is identified, without exception, as required by 42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7k(d) and 42 CFR § 401.305.

4.2 An overpayment is "identified" when the agency has, or should have through the exercise of reasonable diligence, determined that it has received funds to which it is not entitled under applicable payment rules. The identification date is the date the agency first had credible information creating an obligation to investigate — not the date quantification is completed.

4.3 Upon receipt of any credible information suggesting a potential overpayment, the agency shall commence a reasonable diligence investigation. Reasonable diligence shall include: (a) a timely, good-faith investigation; (b) quantification of the overpayment; and (c) a determination of the cause. Reasonable diligence shall be completed within a maximum of 6 months from receipt of credible information, except in extraordinary circumstances documented by the Compliance Officer.

4.4 The 60-day refund clock begins on the date the agency first received credible information triggering an investigation obligation — even if quantification is not yet complete. The agency shall exercise reasonable diligence within this 60-day window whenever possible, or shall report and refund the estimated amount within 60 days with a final reconciliation to follow.

4.5 No identified overpayment shall be retained, offset against future claims, delayed in refund, or concealed. Deliberate delay, concealment, or failure to return an identified overpayment is a violation of the False Claims Act and this policy, constituting grounds for immediate termination and referral for legal action.

4.6 All identified or suspected overpayments shall be reported to the Compliance Officer immediately upon identification. The Compliance Officer shall maintain a centralized Overpayment Tracking Log (Appendix B) and oversee the investigation and refund process.

4.7 Overpayments exceeding $5,000 individually, or $10,000 in aggregate within any 12-month period, shall be reported to the Governing Body at the next regular meeting. Any overpayment associated with potential fraud shall be reported to the Governing Body within 48 hours.

4.8 The agency shall consider voluntary self-disclosure to the OIG Self-Disclosure Protocol for any overpayment resulting from potential fraud, kickback, or Stark Law violation. The decision to self-disclose shall be made by the Compliance Officer, CFO, and Administrator in consultation with legal counsel.

4.9 Overpayment identification processes shall be integrated into: (a) monthly payment reconciliation per FN-BC-007; (b) the internal documentation audit program per CO-DC-002; (c) the triangulation audit per CO-DC-004; (d) coding accuracy audits per FN-CM-001/002; (e) denial pattern analysis per FN-BC-002; and (f) the financial compliance monitoring program per FN-FP-007.

FN-BC-006 — Request for Anticipated Payment (RAP) / Notice of Admission (NOA) Management

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for timely and accurate submission of the Notice of Admission (NOA) to Medicare for all home health episodes at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Effective January 1, 2022, CMS replaced the Request for Anticipated Payment (RAP) with the NOA under the Patient-Driven Groupings Model (PDGM). The NOA is a one-time electronic notification submitted to the Medicare Administrative Contractor (MAC) within 5 calendar days of the start of care (SOC) date that notifies Medicare of the patient's admission to home health services. Unlike the former RAP, the NOA does not generate a payment — it establishes the episode in the Medicare payment system and is a prerequisite for final claim submission. Failure to submit a timely NOA results in a reduction of the final payment for the affected 30-day period. This policy ensures 100% compliance with NOA submission requirements to prevent payment reductions and ensure uninterrupted revenue cycle operations.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

CFO / Revenue Cycle Director

All billing coordinators and billing specialists

Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager (SOC date coordination)

Compliance Officer

Intake Coordinators (admission date tracking)

4. Policy Statements

4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall submit a Notice of Admission (NOA) to the MAC within 5 calendar days of the start of care date for every new Medicare home health episode (SOC) and every Resumption of Care (ROC) episode, as required by CMS.

4.2 The NOA is a one-time submission per beneficiary per period of care. It is NOT required for subsequent 30-day periods within the same period of care (recertifications within a continuous episode) unless the patient is readmitted after a discharge.

4.3 If the NOA is submitted after the 5-calendar-day window but within 30 calendar days of the SOC/ROC date, the final payment for the first 30-day period is reduced by a percentage amount established by CMS (currently 1/30th of the full payment amount for each day the NOA is late).

4.4 If the NOA is not submitted within 30 calendar days of the SOC/ROC date, the episode is not established in the Medicare system and no payment can be made for the first 30-day period. This constitutes a permanent revenue loss.

4.7 NOA submission requires the following minimum data elements: (a) Medicare beneficiary number; (b) provider NPI and CCN; (c) SOC or ROC date; (d) type of admission (SOC vs. ROC); (e) physician NPI. The agency shall ensure all required data elements are accurate at the time of submission.

4.8 The NOA submission process shall be tightly integrated with the intake/admission workflow so that the NOA can be submitted within 24 hours of SOC whenever possible, with a maximum of 5 calendar days.

This policy establishes the mandatory process for reconciling expected Medicare and other payer reimbursements against actual payments received by Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. FN-CM-001 — PDGM Classification & Coding Accuracy

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes standards for accurate Patient-Driven Groupings Model (PDGM) classification of all Medicare home health 30-day payment periods at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. PDGM determines payment through five classification elements: admission source and timing, clinical grouping (based on the principal diagnosis), functional impairment level (based on OASIS functional items), comorbidity adjustment (based on secondary diagnoses), and episode timing (early vs. late periods). Accurate PDGM classification is essential because: (a) it determines the agency's reimbursement — incorrect classification results in either overpayment (creating 60-day rule liability) or underpayment (revenue loss); (b) it is directly tied to the accuracy of ICD-10 coding and OASIS assessment data; (c) CMS auditors, RACs, and OIG specifically target PDGM classification accuracy as a key audit area; (d) systematic misclassification constitutes False Claims Act exposure. This policy ensures every PDGM element is verified, documented, and traceable to the clinical record.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

CFO / Revenue Cycle Director

All certified coders performing diagnosis coding or PDGM classification

Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager (OASIS accuracy affecting functional level)

All OASIS-authorized clinicians (functional scoring)

Compliance Officer (coding audit oversight)

Billing Coordinators (pre-billing verification of PDGM)

QAPI Coordinator (coding quality integration)

4. Policy Statements

4.1 Every 30-day payment period billed to Medicare by Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall be classified under PDGM using accurate, documentation-supported data for all five classification elements.

4.2 PDGM classification shall be performed or verified by a certified coder (CCS, CCS-P, CPC, CPC-H, HCS-D, or equivalent nationally recognized coding credential) for every 30-day period before the final claim is submitted per FN-BC-001 §6.1.2.10.

4.3 The principal diagnosis code driving the clinical grouping shall be the primary reason for the home health episode and must be supported by physician documentation in the Plan of Care and the clinical record per FN-CM-002.

4.4 The functional impairment level shall be derived from OASIS functional assessment items completed per CL-OA policies. OASIS functional data used for PDGM classification must be accurate, contemporaneous, and unmodified for payment purposes.

4.5 The comorbidity adjustment shall be based on secondary diagnosis codes that are: (a) actively addressed during the episode; (b) documented by the physician; (c) coded to the highest specificity per ICD-10-CM guidelines; and (d) verified by a certified coder.

4.6 Admission source (institutional vs. community) and timing (early vs. late) shall be verified against the patient's actual admission history and prior claims data.

4.7 The agency shall NEVER: (a) select a principal diagnosis primarily to maximize payment rather than to reflect the primary reason for care; (b) manipulate OASIS functional scores to increase the functional impairment level for payment purposes; (c) add secondary diagnoses not supported by physician documentation to trigger a higher comorbidity adjustment; (d) misrepresent admission source or timing. Any such conduct constitutes upcoding and is prohibited per FN-BC-001 §4.6 and CO-FA-003.

4.8 Quarterly PDGM classification audits shall be conducted per §6.4 to verify accuracy across all five classification elements. Audit results shall be reported to the CFO and Compliance Officer.

FN-CM-002 — ICD-10 Coding Standards & Accuracy

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes comprehensive standards for ICD-10-CM diagnosis coding at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. to ensure that all diagnosis codes assigned to home health episodes are accurate, supported by physician documentation in the medical record, coded to the highest specificity per the ICD-10-CM Official Guidelines, and compliant with all applicable federal coding regulations. Accurate diagnosis coding is critical because: (a) the principal diagnosis determines the PDGM clinical grouping and directly affects payment per FN-CM-001; (b) secondary diagnoses determine comorbidity adjustments; (c) inaccurate coding constitutes potential False Claims Act liability; (d) coding accuracy is a primary OIG and RAC audit target for home health agencies; (e) diagnosis codes drive clinical quality measures reported through OASIS.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

All certified coders performing diagnosis coding

CFO / Revenue Cycle Director

Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager (physician documentation adequacy)

All OASIS-authorized clinicians (diagnosis selection during assessment)

Compliance Officer (coding audit oversight)

All physicians ordering home health services (documentation of diagnoses)

4. Policy Statements

4.1 All diagnosis codes assigned to home health episodes at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall comply with the current ICD-10-CM Official Guidelines for Coding and Reporting as published by NCHS and CMS, and the HIPAA code set standards (45 CFR § 162.1002).

4.2 Every diagnosis code — principal and secondary — must be supported by physician documentation in the medical record. Coders shall not assign diagnosis codes based solely on clinical assessment by non-physician clinicians unless the physician has acknowledged and documented the diagnosis.

4.3 The principal diagnosis shall represent the primary reason the patient is receiving home health services. Selection of the principal diagnosis shall follow the CMS OASIS Guidance Manual and ICD-10-CM Official Guidelines for sequencing in home health.

4.4 All diagnosis codes shall be assigned to the highest level of specificity available in the current ICD-10-CM code set. Unspecified codes shall be used only when clinical documentation does not provide sufficient detail for a more specific code, and a physician query has been attempted.

Clinicians may select preliminary diagnosis codes during OASIS completion, but final coding for billing shall be verified by a certified coder.

4.6 Coders shall query the physician when documentation is ambiguous, incomplete, or insufficient to assign an accurate code. Queries shall be documented in the coding record. Coders shall not assign codes based on assumptions or clinical inference not documented by the physician.

4.7 Quarterly coding accuracy audits shall be conducted per §6.4 with a minimum accuracy standard of 95%.

4.8 The agency shall NEVER: (a) assign diagnosis codes not documented by the physician; (b) assign codes to maximize reimbursement rather than reflect the documented clinical picture; (c) use unspecified codes when more specific codes are available and documentation supports them; (d) assign resolved or inactive diagnoses as current conditions; (e) change physician-documented diagnoses without physician authorization. FN-CM-003 — Medical Necessity Documentation

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes documentation standards to ensure that the medical necessity of all home health services provided by Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. is clearly, completely, and contemporaneously documented in the clinical record to support Medicare billing. Under 42 CFR § 409.42, Medicare covers home health services only when they are reasonable and necessary for the diagnosis and treatment of illness or injury or to improve the functioning of a malformed body member. Every service billed to Medicare must be supported by documentation that demonstrates: (a) the patient has a qualifying condition requiring skilled care; (b) the specific service is reasonable and necessary for the condition; (c) the patient meets homebound criteria; (d) the service is provided under a physician-ordered Plan of Care; (e) the expected outcome cannot be achieved by the patient or caregiver without skilled intervention. Insufficient medical necessity documentation is the single most common reason for Medicare home health claim denials and is the primary focus of RAC, CERT, and OIG audits.

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager (clinical documentation oversight)

All clinical staff providing direct patient care (documentation of medical necessity in visit notes)

All OASIS-authorized clinicians

All certified coders (coding alignment with documented necessity)

CFO / Revenue Cycle Director (billing alignment)

Compliance Officer (audit oversight)

All physicians ordering home health services

4. Policy Statements

4.1 Every visit note, assessment, and Plan of Care shall document the medical necessity of the services provided with sufficient specificity to demonstrate to an auditor or reviewer why the service was required for this specific patient at this specific time.

4.2 Medical necessity documentation shall address all four Medicare coverage criteria for each episode: (a) the patient is under the care of a physician who has established a Plan of Care; (b) the patient needs skilled nursing, physical therapy, speech-language pathology, or continued occupational therapy services; (c) the patient is homebound; (d) the services are reasonable and necessary for the treatment of the patient's condition.

4.3 Documentation of skilled care need shall articulate: (a) the specific skilled service(s) required; (b) the clinical rationale for why the service requires a skilled clinician (as opposed to a non-skilled caregiver); (c) the patient-specific treatment goals; (d) the expected duration and frequency of services; (e) the patient's progress toward goals or documented clinical justification for continued services when progress is limited.

4.4 Homebound status shall be documented with patient-specific narrative (not generic/templated language) in every visit note per CL-CA-005. The narrative shall describe the specific condition(s) that restrict the patient from leaving home and the taxing effort or assistance required.

4.5 For patients with chronic, stable conditions, documentation shall clearly articulate why the patient continues to require skilled services and what specific skilled interventions are being provided that a non-skilled caregiver could not perform.

4.6 The Director of Nursing shall conduct targeted medical necessity documentation reviews monthly per §6.3 to identify documentation deficiencies before claims are submitted.

4.7 All clinical staff shall receive initial and annual training on medical necessity documentation standards per §10.

FN-CM-005 — LUPA Prevention & Monitoring

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes the agency's program for monitoring visit utilization within each 30-day payment period to identify episodes at risk of Low Utilization Payment Adjustment (LUPA) and to ensure that all physician-ordered visits are delivered in accordance with the Plan of Care. Under PDGM, when the number of visits in a 30-day period falls below the CMS-established LUPA threshold for the assigned payment group, the agency receives a significantly reduced per-visit payment instead of the full PDGM case-mix adjusted payment. LUPA episodes typically reimburse at approximately 15–30% of the full payment amount, resulting in substantial revenue loss. While the agency must never add visits solely to avoid LUPA (which would constitute fraud), the agency has a clinical and operational obligation to ensure that all physician-ordered visits are actually delivered as ordered. This policy integrates clinical visit compliance (CL-SD-025), scheduling operations (OP-SL-001), and revenue cycle monitoring (FN-FP-003) into a unified LUPA prevention program managed through QAPI (QA-PI-006).

3. Scope

This policy applies to:

CFO / Revenue Cycle Director

Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager

All clinical scheduling coordinators

All billing coordinators

QAPI Coordinator

Compliance Officer

4. Policy Statements

4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall monitor visit utilization for every open 30-day payment period against the applicable PDGM LUPA threshold.

4.2 The LUPA threshold for each 30-day period is determined by the PDGM payment group assigned to that period. The Revenue Cycle Director shall maintain the current CMS LUPA threshold table and update it annually when CMS publishes updated thresholds.

4.3 Episodes identified as at risk of LUPA (projected visits below threshold) shall be reviewed by the Director of Nursing within 48 hours to determine: (a) whether all physician-ordered visits have been scheduled and delivered; (b) whether any missed, cancelled, or rescheduled visits have reduced the count below threshold; (c) whether the patient's clinical condition supports the ordered visit frequency.

4.4 The agency shall NEVER add visits that are not clinically necessary or not physician-ordered solely to exceed the LUPA threshold. Adding visits solely for payment purposes constitutes fraud. Any LUPA prevention intervention must be grounded in the physician-ordered Plan of Care and clinical need.

4.5 Legitimate LUPA prevention actions include: (a) ensuring all physician-ordered visits are scheduled and delivered; (b) resolving scheduling barriers (patient cancellations, staff availability); (c) communicating with the physician regarding appropriate visit frequency; (d) identifying missed visits and rescheduling per CL-SD-024.

4.6 LUPA rate shall be monitored as a Key Performance Indicator (KPI) and reported monthly to the CFO, quarterly to the QAPI Committee, and quarterly to the Governing Body.

4.7 A LUPA rate exceeding 10% of total 30-day periods in any calendar month shall trigger a root cause analysis per QA-AE-002.

FN-FP-007 — Financial Compliance & Fraud Monitoring Controls

1. Policy Header

2. Purpose

This policy establishes the agency's financial monitoring controls for detecting, preventing, and reporting billing anomalies, fraudulent patterns, and aberrant financial activity. Effective financial fraud monitoring is a core requirement of the OIG Compliance Program Guidance for Home Health Agencies, which mandates that agencies implement internal controls to detect billing irregularities. This policy integrates billing pattern analysis, outlier detection, internal financial audits, and escalation procedures to ensure the agency identifies potential fraud, waste, and abuse at the earliest possible stage.

3. Scope

CFO / Revenue Cycle Director, Compliance Officer, Administrator, billing coordinators, certified coders, QAPI Coordinator.
