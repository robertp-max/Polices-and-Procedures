## 1. Purpose
This policy establishes the comprehensive Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) program for Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. The ERM program provides a systematic, agency-wide approach to identifying, assessing, mitigating, monitoring, and reporting risks across all operational domains. This policy ensures that risk management is integrated into the agency's governance structure, strategic planning, and daily operations, supporting compliance with 42 CFR Part 484, OSHA requirements, state licensure standards, and OIG Compliance Program Guidance.
## 2. Scope
This policy applies to:
All members of the Governing Body of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.
The Agency Administrator
The Risk Manager
The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager
The Compliance Officer
All department directors and supervisory personnel
All clinical and administrative staff responsible for identifying and reporting risks
All contracted entities performing services on behalf of the agency
This policy does not replace domain-specific risk management activities (e.g., clinical risk managed under CL domain, financial risk under FN domain) but provides the overarching governance framework within which all domain-specific risk activities operate.
## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Enterprise Risk Management (ERM) | A structured, agency-wide process for identifying, assessing, prioritizing, mitigating, and monitoring risks that may affect the agency's ability to achieve its mission, maintain regulatory compliance, ensure patient safety, or sustain financial viability. |
| Risk | Any event, condition, or circumstance that, if it occurs, could have a negative impact on patient safety, staff safety, regulatory compliance, financial performance, or organizational reputation. |
| Risk Register | The centralized repository documenting all identified risks, their assessed severity and likelihood, assigned mitigation strategies, responsible parties, status, and review dates. |
| Risk Appetite | The level and type of risk the Governing Body is willing to accept in pursuit of the agency's mission and strategic objectives. |
| Risk Tolerance | The acceptable variation in outcomes related to specific risks, defined by measurable thresholds. |
| Mitigation | Actions taken to reduce the likelihood or impact of an identified risk. |
| Residual Risk | The level of risk remaining after mitigation strategies have been implemented. |
| Risk Owner | The individual assigned accountability for monitoring and managing a specific risk, including implementation of mitigation strategies and reporting on status. |
| Sentinel Event | An unexpected occurrence involving death or serious physical or psychological injury, or the risk thereof. |
| Near Miss | An event or situation that did not produce patient or staff injury but had the potential to do so. |

## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a formal Enterprise Risk Management program that provides a systematic approach to identifying, assessing, mitigating, and monitoring risks across all agency operations.
4.2 The Governing Body shall have ultimate oversight of the ERM program and shall receive quarterly risk reports from the Risk Manager, per policy GV-GB-001 §6.2.
4.3 The Risk Manager shall be designated as the agency's ERM program leader, with authority to access all operational domains for risk identification and assessment purposes.
4.4 A centralized Risk Register shall be maintained as the single authoritative source for all identified enterprise risks. The Risk Register shall be reviewed and updated no less than quarterly.
4.5 All agency personnel shall be responsible for identifying and reporting potential risks through the agency's incident reporting system per policy RM-ER-002.
4.6 The ERM program shall be integrated with the agency's QAPI program (QA-PG-001), compliance program (CO-CP-001), and financial oversight processes (FN-FP-007) to ensure coordinated risk governance.
4.7 Risk assessments shall be conducted annually at minimum, and additionally upon any significant operational change, regulatory change, adverse event, or external threat.
4.8 The Governing Body shall formally define the agency's risk appetite and risk tolerance thresholds. Risk tolerance thresholds shall be documented and reviewed annually.
4.9 Only the most current approved version of this policy shall be considered valid. Superseded versions must not be used for any operational or compliance purpose.
## 5. Procedures
### 5.1 ERM Program Structure and Governance

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1.1 | Governing Body | Approve the ERM program charter, including the program's scope, objectives, governance structure, and reporting requirements. | Prior to initial implementation; reviewed annually at Q1 meeting. |
| 5.1.2 | Administrator | Designate a qualified Risk Manager with defined authority, responsibilities, and reporting relationships. Document designation in writing with copy to Governing Body. | Prior to program operation; within 30 calendar days of any vacancy. |
| 5.1.3 | Risk Manager | Develop and maintain the ERM Program Manual documenting: (a) program structure and governance; (b) risk identification methodology; (c) risk assessment criteria (likelihood and impact scales); (d) risk categorization taxonomy; (e) mitigation planning standards; (f) monitoring and reporting schedules; (g) escalation protocols. | Within 60 calendar days of designation; updated annually. |
| 5.1.4 | Risk Manager | Establish a Risk Management Committee comprising, at minimum: Risk Manager (chair), Administrator, Director of Nursing, Compliance Officer, and QAPI Coordinator. | Within 30 calendar days of program establishment. |
| 5.1.5 | Risk Management Committee | Convene meetings no fewer than quarterly. Additional meetings shall be convened within 48 hours of any sentinel event or significant risk emergence. | Quarterly; ad hoc as required. |
| 5.1.6 | Risk Manager | Prepare and distribute meeting agendas no fewer than 5 calendar days before each scheduled Risk Management Committee meeting. | 5 calendar days before each meeting. |
| 5.1.7 | Risk Manager | Record formal minutes for all Risk Management Committee meetings documenting: (a) attendees; (b) risks reviewed; (c) risk register updates; (d) mitigation status; (e) new risks identified; (f) escalation decisions; (g) action items with responsible parties and deadlines. | Draft within 7 calendar days of meeting. |

### 5.2 Risk Identification

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.2.1 | Risk Manager | Implement a multi-source risk identification process that incorporates: (a) incident and near-miss reports (RM-ER-002); (b) complaint and grievance data (OP-PA-001); (c) QAPI findings (QA-PG-001); (d) compliance audit results (CO-RA-002); (e) financial variance analysis (FN-FP-003); (f) staff safety reports (RM-SS-001); (g) patient safety data (QA-AE-004); (h) regulatory change monitoring (CO-RA-001); (i) environmental safety assessments (RM-PS-001); (j) external benchmarking data. | Continuous; formally compiled quarterly. |
| 5.2.2 | All Staff | Report identified or suspected risks through the agency's incident reporting system within 24 hours of identification, per RM-ER-002. | Within 24 hours of identification. |
| 5.2.3 | Department Directors | Submit a departmental risk identification summary to the Risk Manager at the close of each quarter, documenting all risks identified within their domain during the period. | Within 10 calendar days of quarter end. |
| 5.2.4 | Risk Manager | Conduct an annual comprehensive risk assessment (enterprise-wide risk survey) across all 10 policy domains, engaging department directors and key personnel. | Annually; completed within 60 calendar days of fiscal year start. |
| 5.2.5 | Risk Manager | Conduct an ad hoc risk assessment within 72 hours of any of the following triggering events: (a) sentinel event; (b) CMS survey deficiency at Condition level; (c) significant regulatory change; (d) natural disaster or public health emergency; (e) data breach or security incident; (f) key leadership vacancy; (g) significant financial variance. | Within 72 hours of triggering event. |

### 5.3 Risk Assessment and Prioritization

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.3.1 | Risk Manager | Assess each identified risk using the agency's standardized Risk Assessment Matrix (Appendix A) evaluating: (a) Likelihood of occurrence (1–5 scale); (b) Severity of impact (1–5 scale); (c) Risk Score = Likelihood × Severity. | Within 14 calendar days of risk identification. |
| 5.3.2 | Risk Manager | Categorize each assessed risk by domain: Clinical, Compliance/Regulatory, Financial, Operational, Technology, Human Resources, Reputational, Legal/Litigation, Environmental/Safety. | Concurrent with assessment. |
| 5.3.3 | Risk Manager | Classify each risk by priority level per the Risk Assessment Matrix: Critical (20–25), High (12–19), Moderate (6–11), Low (1–5). | Concurrent with assessment. |
| 5.3.4 | Risk Management Committee | Review and validate all Critical and High priority risk assessments. Validate that risk scores, categorizations, and priority classifications are accurate and consistent. | At each quarterly meeting; within 48 hours for Critical risks. |
| 5.3.5 | Risk Manager | Escalate all Critical priority risks to the Administrator immediately upon identification and to the Governing Body within 72 hours, with a preliminary mitigation recommendation. | Immediately to Administrator; within 72 hours to Governing Body. |

### 5.4 Risk Mitigation Planning

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.4.1 | Risk Manager | Develop a Risk Mitigation Plan (Appendix B) for each Critical and High priority risk, documenting: (a) risk description and root cause; (b) current controls in place; (c) proposed mitigation actions; (d) responsible party (Risk Owner); (e) implementation timeline; (f) success metrics; (g) residual risk assessment; (h) monitoring frequency. | Within 7 calendar days for Critical; within 14 calendar days for High. |
| 5.4.2 | Risk Owner (Assigned) | Implement approved mitigation actions per the plan timeline and report progress to the Risk Manager at defined intervals. | Per plan timeline; progress reports at minimum monthly. |
| 5.4.3 | Risk Manager | Develop mitigation recommendations for Moderate priority risks. Low priority risks shall be documented in the Risk Register with accepted status and monitored for escalation. | Within 30 calendar days of assessment for Moderate risks. |
| 5.4.4 | Risk Management Committee | Review and approve all Critical and High priority mitigation plans. Approve or modify Risk Owner assignments. | At next scheduled meeting; within 48 hours for Critical risks. |
| 5.4.5 | Governing Body | Review and approve mitigation plans for Critical priority risks. Confirm risk appetite alignment. | At next quarterly meeting; within 7 calendar days for imminent patient safety risks. |

### 5.5 Risk Monitoring and Reporting

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.5.1 | Risk Manager | Maintain the centralized Risk Register (Appendix C) with current status for all identified risks. The Risk Register must document: (a) Risk ID; (b) date identified; (c) risk description; (d) category; (e) likelihood score; (f) severity score; (g) risk score; (h) priority; (i) Risk Owner; (j) mitigation status; (k) residual risk; (l) last review date; (m) next review date. | Updated continuously; formally reviewed quarterly. |
| 5.5.2 | Risk Manager | Prepare and submit a quarterly Enterprise Risk Report (Appendix D) to the Risk Management Committee and Administrator, documenting: (a) Risk Register summary with trending; (b) new risks identified; (c) risks closed/resolved; (d) mitigation plan status for all Critical and High risks; (e) risk score trending analysis; (f) domain-level risk heat map; (g) recommendations. | 7 calendar days before each quarterly Risk Management Committee meeting. |
| 5.5.3 | Risk Manager | Prepare and submit an annual Enterprise Risk Report to the Governing Body including: (a) year-in-review risk summary; (b) risk trending analysis; (c) mitigation effectiveness evaluation; (d) risk appetite/tolerance review recommendations; (e) ERM program effectiveness assessment; (f) next-year risk priorities. | Annually; presented at Q4 Governing Body meeting. |
| 5.5.4 | Administrator | Present the quarterly risk summary to the Governing Body as a standing agenda item per GV-GB-001 §6.2. | At each quarterly Governing Body meeting. |
| 5.5.5 | Risk Manager | Monitor all open mitigation plans for adherence to timelines. Escalate overdue mitigation actions to the Administrator within 7 calendar days of missed deadline. | Ongoing; escalation within 7 calendar days. |
| 5.5.6 | Risk Manager | Conduct an annual ERM program effectiveness review evaluating: (a) risk identification completeness; (b) assessment timeliness; (c) mitigation plan closure rates; (d) incident trending correlation; (e) staff reporting compliance; (f) Governing Body engagement. Document findings and improvement recommendations. | Annually; completed within 30 calendar days of fiscal year end. |

### 7.2 Surveyor Expectations
CMS surveyors conducting a standard survey will verify:
Evidence of a structured risk management program. Surveyors will request the ERM charter, program manual, and Risk Register.
Evidence of Governing Body oversight of risk. Surveyors will review Governing Body minutes for documented risk report presentation and action.
Evidence that risks are identified, assessed, and mitigated. Surveyors will select specific risks from the Register and trace through identification, assessment, mitigation plan, and resolution.
Integration with QAPI. Surveyors will verify that risk data informs the QAPI program and that adverse event data flows into the risk management process.
Incident reporting processes. Surveyors will verify the existence and functioning of the incident reporting system per RM-ER-002.
Staff awareness. Surveyors may interview staff regarding their understanding of how to report risks and incidents.
### 7.3 Common Failure Points

| Failure Point | Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No formal ERM program exists; risk management is ad hoc. | Survey deficiency; unidentified risks accumulate without mitigation. | Implement this policy; maintain charter, manual, and Risk Register. |
| Risk Register exists but is not updated or reviewed. | Stale data provides false assurance; emerging risks missed. | Enforce quarterly review; assign Risk Manager accountability. |
| Governing Body does not receive or act on risk reports. | Failure of governance oversight; per GV-GB-001. | Enforce quarterly reporting as standing Governing Body agenda item. |
| Risks identified but no mitigation plans developed. | Known risks remain unmitigated; liability exposure increases. | Enforce mitigation plan timelines per §5.4. |
| No integration between risk management and QAPI. | Siloed programs; duplicated efforts; gaps in patient safety oversight. | Enforce §5.6 coordination requirements. |
| Staff do not report risks or incidents. | Underreporting leads to incomplete Risk Register. | Enforce training requirements; maintain anonymous reporting option per RM-ER-002. |

## 1. Purpose
This policy establishes the requirements for reporting, documenting, and investigating all incidents, near-misses, and adverse events occurring in connection with the operations of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. A robust incident reporting system is essential for identifying risks, preventing recurrence, improving patient and staff safety, and supporting compliance with 42 CFR Part 484, OSHA requirements, and state mandatory reporting obligations.
## 2. Scope
This policy applies to:
All employees of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. (clinical and administrative)
All contracted and per diem staff providing services on behalf of the agency
All supervisory and management personnel
All volunteers performing activities under agency direction
The Risk Manager, Administrator, Director of Nursing, and Compliance Officer
## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Incident | Any event or occurrence that results in, or has the potential to result in, harm to a patient, staff member, visitor, or third party, or damage to property, or disruption to agency operations. |
| Adverse Event | An incident that results in actual harm or injury to a patient or staff member, including physical injury, psychological harm, or death. |
| Near Miss | An event or situation that did not result in harm but, under slightly different circumstances, could have resulted in an adverse event. |
| Sentinel Event | An unexpected occurrence involving death or serious physical or psychological injury, or the risk thereof. Sentinel events signal the need for immediate investigation and response. |
| Incident Report | The standardized document (Appendix A) used to record the facts, circumstances, and initial assessment of an incident. |
| Reportable Event | An incident that meets criteria for mandatory external reporting to state agencies, CMS, law enforcement, or other regulatory bodies. |
| Contributing Factor | A condition, action, or inaction that, while not the primary cause, increased the likelihood or severity of an incident. |
| Root Cause | The fundamental underlying reason(s) an incident occurred, which, if corrected, would prevent recurrence. |

## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 All employees, contractors, and volunteers of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall immediately report any incident, adverse event, near miss, or unsafe condition through the agency's incident reporting system.
4.2 The agency shall maintain a no-retaliation policy for good-faith incident reporting. No employee shall face disciplinary action for reporting an incident, per policy CO-CP-005.
4.3 Incident reports are confidential risk management documents. They shall not be filed in the patient's clinical record and shall be maintained in the risk management file.
4.4 All incidents shall be classified by severity level and investigated according to a tiered investigation protocol proportional to severity.
4.5 Mandatory external reporting obligations (to state agencies, CMS, law enforcement, adult protective services) shall be fulfilled within the timeframes required by applicable law, regardless of internal investigation status.
4.6 All sentinel events shall trigger an immediate investigation and root cause analysis per policy QA-AE-002.
4.7 Incident data shall be aggregated, trended, and reported to the Risk Management Committee quarterly per RM-ER-005, and to the QAPI Committee per QA-PG-003.
## 5. Procedures
### 5.1 Immediate Response

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1.1 | Reporting Staff Member | Ensure immediate safety of all persons involved. Provide first aid or emergency medical care as needed. Call 911 if life-threatening emergency exists. | Immediately upon incident occurrence. |
| 5.1.2 | Reporting Staff Member | Notify the immediate supervisor verbally of the incident. | Within 1 hour of incident occurrence. |
| 5.1.3 | Supervisor | For sentinel events or serious adverse events: Notify the Risk Manager, Director of Nursing, and Administrator immediately by phone. | Within 1 hour of notification. |
| 5.1.4 | Director of Nursing | For patient-related incidents: Assess the patient's current condition and ensure appropriate clinical intervention is initiated. Notify the attending physician as clinically indicated. | Within 2 hours of notification. |

### 5.2 Incident Report Completion

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.2.1 | Reporting Staff Member | Complete the Incident Report Form (Appendix A) documenting: (a) date, time, and location of incident; (b) persons involved (patient, staff, visitor); (c) factual description of what occurred (objective, non-judgmental language); (d) witnesses; (e) immediate actions taken; (f) injuries or harm observed; (g) equipment involved (if applicable); (h) environmental conditions. | Within 24 hours of incident occurrence. |
| 5.2.2 | Reporting Staff Member | Submit the completed Incident Report to the immediate supervisor for review. | Concurrent with completion (within 24 hours). |
| 5.2.3 | Supervisor | Review the Incident Report for completeness and accuracy. Obtain additional information from witnesses if needed. Forward to the Risk Manager. | Within 48 hours of incident occurrence. |
| 5.2.4 | Risk Manager | Log the incident in the Incident Tracking Log (Appendix B). Assign an Incident ID number. | Within 72 hours of incident occurrence. |

### 5.3 Incident Classification

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.3.1 | Risk Manager | Classify the incident severity using the Incident Severity Classification Scale (Appendix C): Level 1 — No Harm/Near Miss: Event occurred but no injury or harm resulted. Level 2 — Minor Harm: Temporary, minor injury requiring minimal intervention (e.g., first aid). Level 3 — Moderate Harm: Injury requiring medical treatment or resulting in temporary impairment. Level 4 — Serious Harm: Significant injury requiring hospitalization, surgery, or resulting in permanent impairment. Level 5 — Sentinel Event: Death, or serious physical or psychological injury, or risk thereof. | Within 72 hours of report receipt. |
| 5.3.2 | Risk Manager | Classify the incident by type: ☐ Patient fall ☐ Medication error ☐ Infection ☐ Equipment malfunction ☐ Staff injury ☐ Vehicle accident ☐ Verbal/physical threat ☐ Property damage ☐ HIPAA breach ☐ Documentation error ☐ Missed visit impacting care ☐ Other | Concurrent with severity classification. |
| 5.3.3 | Risk Manager | Determine investigation tier: Level 1–2: Manager-level review and documentation. Level 3: Risk Manager investigation with written report. Level 4–5: Full investigation with root cause analysis (per QA-AE-002). | Concurrent with classification. |

### 5.4 Investigation Process

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.4.1 | Risk Manager | For Level 3–5 incidents, initiate a formal investigation. Preserve all relevant evidence including: (a) clinical records; (b) staff schedules; (c) equipment involved; (d) photographs (if applicable); (e) witness statements; (f) electronic system logs. | Investigation initiated within 24 hours for Level 4–5; within 72 hours for Level 3. |
| 5.4.2 | Risk Manager | Interview all involved parties and witnesses. Document interviews using the Witness Statement Form (Appendix D). Interviews shall be conducted individually in a private setting. | Within 7 calendar days of incident for Level 3; within 48 hours for Level 4–5. |
| 5.4.3 | Risk Manager | For Level 4–5 incidents, convene an Investigation Team comprising: Risk Manager (lead), Director of Nursing (for clinical incidents), department director of involved area, and Compliance Officer (if regulatory implications). | Within 48 hours of incident. |
| 5.4.4 | Investigation Team | Conduct root cause analysis per QA-AE-002 for all Level 4–5 incidents. Document findings using the Root Cause Analysis template. | Completed within 30 calendar days of incident. |
| 5.4.5 | Risk Manager | Complete the Investigation Summary Report (Appendix E) for all Level 3–5 incidents, documenting: (a) incident summary; (b) investigation methodology; (c) findings of fact; (d) root cause(s) identified; (e) contributing factors; (f) recommendations; (g) corrective actions required. | Within 14 calendar days for Level 3; within 30 calendar days for Level 4–5. |
| 5.4.6 | Risk Manager | For Level 1–2 incidents, the supervisor completes a brief investigation summary on the Incident Report Form (Appendix A, Section 3) and submits to Risk Manager for trending. | Within 7 calendar days of incident. |

### 5.5 Mandatory External Reporting

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.5.1 | Risk Manager | Determine whether the incident triggers mandatory external reporting obligations including: (a) State health department notification; (b) Adult/Child Protective Services report (per CL-PR-006); (c) OSHA injury/illness report (per 29 CFR § 1904); (d) CMS adverse event reporting; (e) Law enforcement notification; (f) HIPAA breach notification (per CO-HP-003); (g) Coroner/medical examiner notification (if applicable). | Within 24 hours of incident classification. |
| 5.5.2 | Risk Manager / Compliance Officer | Complete and submit all mandatory external reports within required timeframes. Document the report, recipient, date, and method of submission. | Per applicable regulatory timeframes (see Appendix F). |
| 5.5.3 | Risk Manager | Notify the Administrator and Compliance Officer of all mandatory external reports filed. | Within 24 hours of filing. |
| 5.5.4 | Administrator | Notify the Governing Body of all Level 4–5 incidents and all mandatory external reports filed, per GV-GB-001 §6.5. | Within 72 hours; at next Governing Body meeting for full report. |

### 5.6 Corrective Action and Follow-Up

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.6.1 | Risk Manager | For all Level 3–5 incidents, develop corrective action recommendations based on investigation findings. Submit to Risk Management Committee for approval. | Within 7 calendar days of investigation completion. |
| 5.6.2 | Risk Management Committee | Review and approve corrective action plans per QA-AE-003. Assign responsible parties and deadlines. | At next scheduled meeting; within 48 hours for Level 4–5. |
| 5.6.3 | Assigned Responsible Party | Implement corrective actions per approved plan. Document implementation evidence. | Per plan timeline. |
| 5.6.4 | Risk Manager | Monitor corrective action implementation. Verify effectiveness through follow-up audit or review. | At 30, 60, and 90 days post-implementation. |
| 5.6.5 | Risk Manager | Close the incident in the Incident Tracking Log when: (a) investigation is complete; (b) corrective actions are implemented and verified; (c) any external reporting obligations have been fulfilled. | Upon completion of all required actions. |

### 5.7 Trending and Analysis

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.7.1 | Risk Manager | Aggregate incident data monthly for trending analysis. Track: (a) incident volume by type; (b) incident volume by severity; (c) incident volume by department/service; (d) staff involved (de-identified for trending); (e) time of day/day of week patterns; (f) corrective action effectiveness. | Monthly. |
| 5.7.2 | Risk Manager | Present quarterly incident trending report to Risk Management Committee and QAPI Committee per RM-ER-005 and QA-SM-001. | Quarterly. |
| 5.7.3 | Risk Manager | Escalate any identified patterns or clusters (3 or more similar incidents within 30 days) to the Administrator and Director of Nursing for immediate review. | Within 48 hours of pattern identification. |

### Common Failure Points

| Failure Point | Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Staff do not report incidents or near misses due to fear of blame. | Underreporting; unknown risks accumulate. | Enforce no-retaliation policy; provide anonymous reporting option; emphasize culture of safety in training. |
| Incident reports are placed in patient clinical records. | Legal discovery exposure; breach of risk management privilege. | Train all staff that incident reports go ONLY to Risk Management file. |
| Investigation is not conducted or is superficial. | Root causes not identified; recurrence likely. | Enforce tiered investigation protocol with defined timelines. |
| Mandatory external reporting deadlines missed. | Regulatory violations; fines; survey deficiencies. | Maintain Appendix F (External Reporting Reference); Risk Manager tracks all deadlines. |
| Incident data not trended or analyzed. | Patterns missed; systemic issues persist. | Enforce monthly aggregation and quarterly reporting per §5.7. |

## 1. Purpose
This policy mandates the conduct of regular, systematic risk assessments across all operational domains of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Risk assessments provide the foundation for informed decision-making, resource allocation, and risk mitigation by identifying, scoring, and prioritizing risks based on likelihood and severity of impact.
## 2. Scope
This policy applies to the Risk Manager, all department directors, the Administrator, the Risk Management Committee, and all personnel responsible for conducting or contributing to risk assessments.
## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Risk Assessment | The systematic process of identifying potential risks and evaluating their likelihood of occurrence and potential severity of impact. |
| Likelihood | The probability that a risk event will occur, scored on a standardized scale. |
| Severity | The potential impact of a risk event if it occurs, scored on a standardized scale. |
| Risk Score | The product of Likelihood × Severity, used to prioritize risks. |
| Risk Prioritization | The process of ranking risks by their composite risk score to determine the order and intensity of response. |
| Comprehensive Risk Assessment | An enterprise-wide assessment conducted annually across all operational domains. |
| Targeted Risk Assessment | A focused assessment of a specific domain, process, or event conducted in response to a trigger. |

## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall conduct a comprehensive enterprise-wide risk assessment annually.
4.2 Targeted risk assessments shall be conducted within 72 hours of any triggering event as defined in RM-ER-001 §5.2.5.
4.3 All risk assessments shall use the agency's standardized Risk Assessment Matrix (RM-ER-001, Appendix A) to ensure consistency.
4.4 Department directors shall participate in the annual comprehensive risk assessment by identifying and submitting domain-specific risks.
4.5 All assessed risks shall be documented in the centralized Risk Register per RM-ER-001.
4.6 Risk prioritization shall directly inform the agency's mitigation planning, resource allocation, and strategic planning processes.
## 5. Procedures
### 5.1 Annual Comprehensive Risk Assessment

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1.1 | Risk Manager | Develop the annual risk assessment plan, identifying: (a) domains to be assessed; (b) assessment methodology; (c) data sources; (d) timeline; (e) participants required. | Within 30 calendar days of fiscal year start. |
| 5.1.2 | Risk Manager | Distribute the Departmental Risk Identification Worksheet (Appendix A) to all department directors with instructions. | Within 30 calendar days of fiscal year start. |
| 5.1.3 | Department Directors | Complete the Departmental Risk Identification Worksheet by identifying the top 5 risks within their domain, with preliminary likelihood/severity estimates and supporting data. | Within 30 calendar days of receipt. |
| 5.1.4 | Risk Manager | Compile all departmental worksheets. Conduct independent risk identification review using: (a) prior year Risk Register; (b) incident data; (c) QAPI findings; (d) compliance audit results; (e) financial data; (f) regulatory changes; (g) industry benchmarking; (h) staff surveys. | Within 15 calendar days of worksheet compilation. |
| 5.1.5 | Risk Manager | Score each identified risk using the standardized Risk Assessment Matrix (RM-ER-001, Appendix A). Document assessment rationale. | Within 15 calendar days of compilation. |
| 5.1.6 | Risk Management Committee | Convene the annual risk assessment review meeting. Validate all risk scores, prioritize the enterprise risk portfolio, and identify the top 10 enterprise risks. | Within 60 calendar days of fiscal year start. |
| 5.1.7 | Risk Manager | Update the Risk Register with all new and re-assessed risks. Prepare the Annual Enterprise Risk Assessment Summary (RM-ER-001, Appendix F). | Within 7 calendar days of Committee meeting. |
| 5.1.8 | Risk Manager | Present the Annual Risk Assessment Summary to the Administrator and Governing Body per RM-ER-001 §5.5.3. | At next quarterly Governing Body meeting. |

### 5.2 Targeted Risk Assessments

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.2.1 | Risk Manager | Upon identification of a triggering event (sentinel event, regulatory change, survey deficiency, data breach, key leadership vacancy, significant financial variance, or public health emergency), initiate a targeted risk assessment. | Within 72 hours of triggering event. |
| 5.2.2 | Risk Manager | Scope the targeted assessment: (a) affected domain(s); (b) affected populations; (c) data sources; (d) participants required. | Within 72 hours. |
| 5.2.3 | Risk Manager | Conduct the targeted assessment using the Targeted Risk Assessment Form (Appendix B). Score all identified risks using the standardized matrix. | Completed within 14 calendar days for non-emergency triggers; within 7 calendar days for sentinel events. |
| 5.2.4 | Risk Manager | Present targeted assessment findings and recommendations to the Risk Management Committee (or Administrator if Committee cannot convene in time). | Within 48 hours of assessment completion. |

### 5.3 Quarterly Risk Portfolio Review

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.3.1 | Risk Manager | Reassess all Critical and High priority risks in the Risk Register at each quarterly review, updating likelihood and severity scores based on current data. | Quarterly. |
| 5.3.2 | Risk Manager | Identify any risks that have changed priority classification (escalated or de-escalated) and document the rationale. | Quarterly. |
| 5.3.3 | Risk Management Committee | Review and validate quarterly reassessments. Confirm continued appropriateness of mitigation plans. | At each quarterly meeting. |

## 1. Purpose
This policy establishes requirements for maintaining adequate insurance coverage as a core risk transfer strategy within the ERM framework of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Appropriate insurance coverage protects the agency, its patients, staff, and governing body from financial loss resulting from liability claims, property damage, and other insurable risks.
## 2. Scope
This policy applies to the Governing Body, Administrator, Risk Manager, CFO/Revenue Cycle Director, and any personnel involved in insurance procurement, claims management, or risk transfer decisions.
## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Professional Liability Insurance | Coverage protecting the agency and its clinical staff against claims arising from professional services, including malpractice, negligence, and errors/omissions. |
| General Liability Insurance | Coverage for bodily injury, property damage, and personal/advertising injury claims arising from agency operations. |
| Workers' Compensation Insurance | State-mandated coverage for employee work-related injuries and illnesses. |
| Directors & Officers (D&O) Insurance | Coverage protecting Governing Body members and officers against claims arising from their management decisions. |
| Cyber Liability Insurance | Coverage for losses arising from data breaches, cyberattacks, and privacy violations. |
| Certificate of Insurance (COI) | A document issued by an insurance carrier confirming the existence and terms of an insurance policy. |
| Risk Transfer | The strategy of shifting financial responsibility for a risk to a third party (typically an insurance carrier). |

## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain current and adequate insurance coverage as a fundamental component of its enterprise risk management strategy.
4.2 The following insurance coverage types shall be maintained at minimum:
(a) Professional liability (malpractice) insurance
(b) General liability insurance
(c) Workers' compensation insurance (per California state law)
(d) Commercial auto insurance (for agency-owned or leased vehicles, if applicable)
(e) Cyber liability insurance
(f) Directors & Officers liability insurance (if the Governing Body includes a board of directors)
4.3 Coverage levels shall be reviewed annually by the Risk Manager and Administrator to ensure adequacy based on current risk exposure, agency size, service volume, and industry benchmarks.
4.4 The Governing Body shall approve insurance coverage levels annually as part of the budget approval process per FN-FP-005.
4.5 No lapse in required insurance coverage shall occur. The Risk Manager shall monitor all policy expiration dates and initiate renewal no later than 90 calendar days before expiration.
4.6 All contracted and per diem staff shall provide evidence of current professional liability insurance coverage prior to providing services per HR-WM-002.

## 1. Purpose
This policy mandates systematic analysis of risk, incident, and adverse event data to identify trends, patterns, and systemic issues requiring proactive intervention. Trending and pattern analysis transforms raw data into actionable intelligence that supports evidence-based risk mitigation and quality improvement.
## 2. Scope
This policy applies to the Risk Manager, QAPI Coordinator, Risk Management Committee, and all personnel who generate, collect, or analyze risk-related data.
## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Trending | The process of tracking data points over time to identify directional changes (increases, decreases, stability). |
| Pattern Analysis | The process of examining data to identify recurring themes, clusters, or correlations that may indicate systemic issues. |
| Cluster | Three or more similar incidents occurring within a 30-day period, or a statistically significant increase above baseline. |
| Baseline | The established rate or frequency of events against which changes are measured. |
| Leading Indicator | A predictive metric that signals increased risk before adverse events occur. |
| Lagging Indicator | A retrospective metric that measures outcomes after events have occurred. |

## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 The Risk Manager shall conduct monthly trending analysis of all incident and risk data.
4.2 Quarterly trending reports shall be presented to the Risk Management Committee, QAPI Committee, and Governing Body per RM-ER-001.
4.3 Any identified cluster (3+ similar events within 30 days) shall trigger an immediate investigation and escalation per §5.3.
4.4 Trending data shall directly inform risk assessment priorities, mitigation planning, training needs identification, and QAPI project selection.
4.5 Both leading and lagging indicators shall be monitored to enable proactive risk mitigation.
## 5. Procedures
### 5.1 Data Collection and Aggregation

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1.1 | Risk Manager | Aggregate data monthly from all risk data sources: (a) incident reports (RM-ER-002); (b) Risk Register (RM-ER-001); (c) QAPI adverse event data (QA-AE-001); (d) complaint and grievance data (OP-PA-001); (e) workers' compensation claims (HR-WM-004); (f) infection surveillance data (QA-SM-002); (g) claims and litigation data (RM-ER-006); (h) staff safety reports (RM-SS-001); (i) compliance audit findings (CO-RA-002). | Monthly, by the 10th of the following month. |
| 5.1.2 | Risk Manager | Maintain the Risk Trending Dashboard (Appendix A) tracking monthly and quarterly metrics across all risk categories. | Updated monthly. |

### 5.2 Trending Analysis

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.2.1 | Risk Manager | Conduct monthly trending analysis of incident data by: (a) incident type; (b) severity level; (c) location/setting; (d) time of day/day of week; (e) staff role; (f) patient demographics (de-identified); (g) contributing factors. | Monthly. |
| 5.2.2 | Risk Manager | Compare current period data to: (a) prior month; (b) same month prior year; (c) rolling 12-month average (baseline). Identify statistically significant increases or decreases. | Monthly. |
| 5.2.3 | Risk Manager | Analyze leading indicators monthly: (a) staffing vacancy rates; (b) training compliance rates; (c) documentation deficiency rates; (d) complaint volume; (e) equipment maintenance overdue items; (f) policy acknowledgment compliance. | Monthly. |

## 1. Purpose
This policy establishes procedures for managing liability claims, coordinating with legal counsel, preserving evidence, and supporting litigation defense for Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Effective claims management minimizes financial exposure, protects the agency's reputation, and ensures compliance with legal and insurance requirements.
## 2. Scope
This policy applies to the Risk Manager, Administrator, Compliance Officer, Governing Body, legal counsel, insurance carriers, and any personnel involved in claims-related activities or litigation support.
## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Claim | A demand for compensation or other relief arising from alleged injury, harm, or loss attributed to the agency's services or operations. |
| Demand Letter | A written communication from a claimant or attorney asserting a claim and demanding resolution. |
| Litigation Hold | A directive to preserve all documents, records, electronic data, and communications related to anticipated or pending litigation. |
| Reservation of Rights | A notice from an insurance carrier that it will investigate and defend a claim while reserving the right to deny coverage. |
| Settlement | Resolution of a claim through negotiated agreement without trial. |
| Discovery | The legal process of exchanging relevant information between parties in litigation. |

## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 All claims, demand letters, lawsuits, subpoenas, and notices of potential claims received by any agency employee shall be immediately forwarded to the Risk Manager.
4.2 The Risk Manager shall notify the applicable insurance carrier(s) within 24 hours of receiving any claim or notice of potential claim per RM-ER-004.
4.3 A litigation hold shall be issued within 24 hours of receiving any lawsuit, demand letter, or credible notice of potential litigation.
4.4 No agency employee shall discuss claims, litigation, or potential litigation with any external party (including claimants, their attorneys, or media) without authorization from the Risk Manager or legal counsel.
4.5 The Governing Body shall be notified of all claims and lawsuits per GV-GB-001 §6.5.
4.6 All claims management activities shall be coordinated with the agency's legal counsel per GV-EA-003.
## 5. Procedures
### 5.1 Claims Receipt and Initial Response

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1.1 | Any Employee | Upon receiving any claim, demand letter, lawsuit, subpoena, or notice of potential claim, immediately forward to the Risk Manager. Do not respond to the claimant or their attorney. | Immediately upon receipt. |
| 5.1.2 | Risk Manager | Log the claim in the Claims Tracking Log (RM-ER-004, Appendix C). Assign a claim number. | Within 4 hours of receipt. |
| 5.1.3 | Risk Manager | Notify the applicable insurance carrier(s) per policy terms. Document notification date, time, and carrier representative. | Within 24 hours. |
| 5.1.4 | Risk Manager | Notify the Administrator and Compliance Officer. | Within 24 hours. |
| 5.1.5 | Administrator | Notify the Governing Body per GV-GB-001 §6.5. | Within 72 hours. |
| 5.1.6 | Risk Manager | Engage legal counsel per GV-EA-003 for any claim involving: (a) formal lawsuit; (b) demand exceeding $25,000; (c) allegations of abuse, neglect, or fraud; (d) regulatory enforcement action; (e) wrongful death. | Within 48 hours. |

### 5.2 Litigation Hold and Evidence Preservation

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.2.1 | Risk Manager | Issue a Litigation Hold Notice (Appendix A) to all relevant custodians of records and information. | 5.2.5 | Risk Manager | Maintain the Litigation Hold until written release is provided by legal counsel. | Until released by legal counsel. |

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

## 1. Purpose
This policy defines protocols for staff safety during home visits, including threat assessment, communication check-ins, and high-risk visit procedures for Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. The safety of staff providing services in uncontrolled home environments is paramount and requires proactive safety planning, clear communication protocols, and immediate response capabilities.
## 2. Scope
This policy applies to all employees, contracted staff, and per diem personnel of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. who provide services in patient homes or travel for agency business, as well as supervisory personnel responsible for staff deployment and scheduling.
## 3. Definitions

| --- | --- |
| Home Visit Safety Assessment | An evaluation of the safety conditions of a patient's home environment as they relate to staff safety, including neighborhood conditions, household members, pets, and environmental hazards. |
| High-Risk Visit | A visit to a home or neighborhood where identified safety concerns exist, including history of violence, substance abuse in the home, aggressive persons, unsafe animals, weapons present, or high-crime area. |
| Check-In Protocol | The communication procedure requiring field staff to contact the agency at defined intervals to confirm their safety and location. |
| Buddy System | A safety measure requiring two staff members to attend a home visit jointly when elevated safety concerns exist. |
| Duress Code | A pre-established verbal code or signal that allows a staff member to communicate distress without alerting a threatening person. |
| Safe Zone | A predetermined, accessible, and secure location near the patient's home to which staff can retreat in the event of a safety threat. |

## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a comprehensive staff safety program that protects all personnel during home health visits and agency travel.
4.2 A Home Visit Safety Assessment shall be conducted for every new patient prior to or at the time of the first visit, and updated whenever safety conditions change.
4.3 All field staff shall follow the agency's Check-In Protocol during patient visits. No staff member shall make a home visit without notifying the agency of their location and expected return.
4.4 Visits shall not be conducted when the staff member's safety is in imminent danger. Staff have the right and obligation to leave a home immediately if they feel unsafe.
4.5 High-risk visits shall require enhanced safety measures including the buddy system, specific check-in intervals, and supervisor notification.
4.6 All staff shall receive initial and annual training on personal safety, de-escalation techniques, and emergency response procedures.
4.7 All safety incidents involving staff shall be reported per RM-ER-002 and investigated.
## 5. Procedures
### 5.1 Home Visit Safety Assessment

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1.1 | Intake Coordinator / Scheduling | At the time of referral intake, screen for known safety concerns: (a) history of violence or aggressive behavior; (b) substance abuse in the home; (c) weapons in the home; (d) unsafe animals; (e) neighborhood/area safety concerns; (f) communicable disease risk; (g) environmental hazards. Document findings on the Home Visit Safety Assessment Form (Appendix A). | During intake, prior to first visit. |
| 5.1.2 | Clinician (First Visit) | Complete the Home Visit Safety Assessment during the first visit, documenting: (a) neighborhood conditions; (b) home exterior (lighting, access, visibility); (c) home interior (clutter, hazards, cleanliness); (d) household members present; (e) animals; (f) weapons observed; (g) signs of substance abuse; (h) patient/caregiver behavior; (i) cell phone reception; (j) parking/egress accessibility. | At first visit; documented within 24 hours. |
| 5.1.3 | Clinician | If safety concerns are identified, notify the supervisor immediately. Assign a safety risk level: ☐ Standard (no concerns) ☐ Elevated (manageable concerns with precautions) ☐ High Risk (requires enhanced safety measures). | At first visit or upon identification of new concerns. |
| 5.1.4 | Director of Nursing / Supervisor | For Elevated and High-Risk classifications: (a) develop a Safety Plan (Appendix B); (b) notify all staff assigned to the patient; (c) implement enhanced safety measures per §5.3. | Within 24 hours of classification. |
| 5.1.5 | All Visiting Staff | Reassess safety conditions at each visit. Update the Safety Assessment if conditions change. Report new concerns to supervisor immediately. | Every visit. |

### 5.2 Check-In Protocol

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.2.1 | All Field Staff | Before each home visit, notify the agency office (or on-call supervisor if after hours) of: (a) patient name and address; (b) expected arrival time; (c) expected visit duration. | Before each visit. |
| 5.2.2 | All Field Staff | Upon arrival at the patient's home, conduct a brief external safety scan (observe vehicles, persons, conditions). If anything appears unsafe, do not enter. Retreat and contact supervisor. | At arrival. |
| 5.2.3 | All Field Staff | Upon completion of the visit, notify the agency office (or on-call supervisor) that the visit is complete and they have safely departed. | Within 15 minutes of departure. |
| 5.2.4 | Scheduling / Office Staff | If a check-in is not received within 30 minutes of the expected visit completion time, attempt to contact the staff member by phone. | Within 30 minutes of expected check-in. |
| 5.2.5 | Supervisor | If the staff member cannot be reached after 2 phone attempts (within 15 minutes of first attempt), escalate to the Administrator and initiate the Missing Staff Protocol: (a) contact the patient's home phone; (b) contact the staff member's emergency contact; (c) dispatch another staff member to the location; (d) contact law enforcement if no contact is made within 60 minutes. | Per protocol; law enforcement within 60 minutes. |
| 5.2.6 | All Field Staff (High-Risk Visits) | For High-Risk classified visits, check in with the office at 15-minute intervals during the visit. | Every 15 minutes during High-Risk visits. |

### 5.3 High-Risk Visit Procedures

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.3.1 | Supervisor | Assign High-Risk visits only during daylight hours unless clinically urgent. | Ongoing. |
| 5.3.2 | Supervisor | Implement the buddy system for all High-Risk visits. Two staff members shall attend jointly. | For all High-Risk visits. |
| 5.3.3 | Supervisor | Ensure all staff assigned to High-Risk visits are briefed on: (a) specific safety concerns; (b) Safety Plan provisions; (c) duress code and safe zone locations; (d) 15-minute check-in requirement. | Before each High-Risk visit. |
| 5.3.4 | Field Staff | For High-Risk visits: (a) park vehicle facing outward for rapid departure; (b) keep car keys accessible at all times; (c) carry only essential equipment; (d) identify exits upon entering the home; (e) keep path to exit clear; (f) carry fully charged cell phone. | At each High-Risk visit. |
| 5.3.5 | Field Staff | If at any point during any visit the staff member feels unsafe: (a) immediately end the visit with a calm, non-confrontational statement; (b) leave the home; (c) proceed to the nearest safe zone; (d) contact the supervisor; (e) file an incident report per RM-ER-002. Staff shall NEVER compromise their safety to complete a visit. | Immediately upon feeling unsafe. |

### 5.4 Duress Communication

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.4.1 | Risk Manager | Establish and communicate an agency-wide duress code — a specific word or phrase that staff can use during a phone call to indicate they are in danger without alerting a threatening person. | At program establishment; refreshed annually. |
| 5.4.2 | All Staff | If unable to leave safely, use the duress code during any phone communication. | As needed. |
| 5.4.3 | Office/On-Call Staff | Upon hearing the duress code: (a) do NOT acknowledge the code verbally; (b) keep the caller on the line; (c) immediately contact law enforcement and provide the staff member's location; (d) notify the Administrator. | Immediately upon hearing duress code. |

### 5.5 Post-Incident Response

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.5.1 | Staff Member | Report any safety incident (threat, assault, unsafe condition, near miss) per RM-ER-002. | Within 24 hours. |
| 5.5.2 | Supervisor | Ensure the affected staff member receives appropriate support: (a) medical attention if injured; (b) employee assistance program referral; (c) temporary reassignment from the patient if requested. | Immediately; ongoing as needed. |
| 5.5.3 | Risk Manager | Investigate the incident. Determine whether criminal reporting is required. Update the patient's Safety Assessment and safety risk level. | Within 72 hours. |
| 5.5.4 | Director of Nursing | Determine whether services to the patient can safely continue. If the safety risk cannot be mitigated, consider discharge per CL-CP-006. | Within 72 hours. |

## 1. Purpose
This policy establishes Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.'s workplace violence prevention program, including risk assessment, reporting protocols, response procedures, and staff protection measures. Home health workers face elevated violence risk due to the uncontrolled home environment, and this policy provides a comprehensive prevention and response framework in compliance with Cal/OSHA workplace violence prevention standards (SB 553).
## 2. Scope
This policy applies to all employees, contractors, per diem staff, and volunteers of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. It covers all work settings including patient homes, the agency office, community locations, and while in transit.
## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Workplace Violence | Any act or threat of physical violence, harassment, intimidation, bullying, or other threatening disruptive behavior that occurs at any work location. |
| Type I Violence | Violence by persons with no relationship to the agency (e.g., stranger, intruder during a home visit). |
| Type II Violence | Violence by patients, family members, or caregivers. |
| Type III Violence | Violence by current or former employees. |
| Type IV Violence | Violence by persons with a personal relationship with an employee (e.g., domestic violence that follows employee to work). |
| Threat Assessment | A structured evaluation of a reported threat to determine credibility, immediacy, and appropriate response. |
| Workplace Violence Incident Log | The record of all workplace violence incidents as required by Cal/OSHA. |

## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. maintains a zero-tolerance policy for workplace violence. No acts of violence, threats, intimidation, harassment, or bullying will be tolerated from any person — employee, patient, family member, visitor, or third party.
4.2 All staff shall immediately report any incident of workplace violence, threat, or intimidation per RM-ER-002 and this policy.
4.3 No employee shall be retaliated against for reporting workplace violence in good faith, per CO-CP-005.
4.4 The agency shall maintain a written Workplace Violence Prevention Plan (WVPP) as required by California SB 553, effective July 1, 2024.
4.5 Threat assessments shall be conducted for all reported credible threats by the Risk Manager within 24 hours.
4.6 All staff shall receive initial and annual workplace violence prevention training.

### 1. Purpose
This policy establishes standards for motor vehicle safety, driving eligibility, accident reporting, and investigation procedures for all personnel of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. who operate motor vehicles for agency business. Home health services require extensive driving in variable conditions, and motor vehicle accidents represent a significant risk to staff safety, patient service continuity, agency liability, and insurance costs.
### 2. Scope
This policy applies to:
All employees of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. who use personal vehicles or agency-owned/leased vehicles for agency business
All contracted and per diem staff who drive to patient homes or other locations for agency services
All supervisory and management personnel responsible for staff deployment
The Risk Manager, HR Director, and Administrator
### 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Agency Business | Any driving performed in connection with employment duties including travel to/from patient homes, agency office, meetings, training, or supply pick-up. Does not include normal commute to/from the primary work location. |
| Motor Vehicle Record (MVR) | The official driving record maintained by the Department of Motor Vehicles documenting an individual's driving history, violations, and license status. |
| Preventable Accident | A motor vehicle accident in which the driver failed to exercise every reasonable precaution to prevent the accident, regardless of fault assigned by law enforcement. |
| Non-Preventable Accident | A motor vehicle accident in which the driver exercised all reasonable precautions but could not avoid the accident due to the actions of another party or unavoidable conditions. |
| Distracted Driving | Operating a motor vehicle while engaged in any activity that diverts attention from driving, including but not limited to cell phone use, texting, eating, adjusting navigation, or attending to materials. |
| Agency Vehicle | Any motor vehicle owned, leased, or rented by Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. for use in agency operations. |

### 4. Policy Statements
4.1 All personnel who drive for agency business shall maintain a valid driver's license appropriate for the vehicle operated and carry minimum automobile insurance coverage as required by California state law.
4.2 The agency shall verify the motor vehicle record (MVR) of all personnel who drive for agency business at the time of hire and annually thereafter.
4.3 All motor vehicle accidents occurring during agency business shall be reported immediately per the procedures in this policy and documented per RM-ER-002.
4.4 The use of handheld electronic devices while operating a motor vehicle for agency business is strictly prohibited, consistent with California Vehicle Code § 23123 and § 23123.5.
4.5 All personnel shall comply with all applicable traffic laws, wear seatbelts at all times, and operate vehicles in a safe and defensive manner.
4.6 Personnel who fail to maintain driving eligibility or who demonstrate unsafe driving practices shall be restricted from driving for agency business.
4.7 Agency-owned or leased vehicles (if applicable) shall be maintained in safe operating condition with documented maintenance records.
### 5. Procedures
#### 5.1 Driving Eligibility and Verification

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1.1 | HR Director | At the time of hire, verify that all personnel who will drive for agency business possess a valid California driver's license (or applicable state license). Obtain a copy for the personnel file. | At hire; prior to first driving assignment. |
| 5.1.2 | HR Director | Obtain and review the Motor Vehicle Record (MVR) from the Department of Motor Vehicles for all personnel who drive for agency business. | At hire; annually thereafter. |
| 5.1.3 | HR Director | Evaluate the MVR using the Driving Eligibility Criteria (Appendix A). Disqualifying criteria include: (a) suspended or revoked license; (b) 3 or more moving violations in the past 36 months; (c) DUI/DWI conviction in the past 60 months; (d) reckless driving conviction in the past 36 months; (e) any felony involving a motor vehicle. | At hire; annually. |
| 5.1.4 | HR Director | Verify that the employee maintains automobile liability insurance at or above California minimum requirements ($15,000/$30,000/$5,000). Obtain proof of insurance and maintain in personnel file. | At hire; annually; upon each renewal. |
| 5.1.5 | HR Director | Notify the Risk Manager of any disqualifying MVR finding or insurance lapse. Suspend the employee's driving authorization immediately. | Within 24 hours of discovery. |
| 5.1.6 | Risk Manager | Determine whether alternative non-driving duties can be assigned. If driving is an essential function of the position and the employee cannot be reassigned, initiate appropriate HR action per HR-ER-002. | Within 7 calendar days. |
| 5.1.7 | All Driving Staff | Report any change in driver's license status (suspension, revocation, restriction), any moving violation, any DUI/DWI arrest, or any lapse in automobile insurance to the supervisor and HR Director within 24 hours. | Within 24 hours of occurrence. |

#### 5.2 Safe Driving Standards

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.2.1 | All Driving Staff | Comply with all applicable traffic laws at all times when driving for agency business. | At all times. |
| 5.2.2 | All Driving Staff | Wear a seatbelt at all times while the vehicle is in motion. Ensure all passengers wear seatbelts. | At all times. |
| 5.2.3 | All Driving Staff | Do not operate a handheld electronic device while driving. If a call must be made or answered, pull over safely and stop the vehicle before using the device. Hands-free use is permitted only if the device is mounted and requires only a single tap to activate; no texting or emailing by any method is permitted while driving. | At all times. |
| 5.2.4 | All Driving Staff | Do not drive while under the influence of alcohol, illegal drugs, or any prescription/over-the-counter medication that impairs driving ability. | At all times. |
| 5.2.5 | All Driving Staff | Conduct a brief pre-trip vehicle inspection before the first driving assignment of each workday: (a) tires (visual condition); (b) lights and signals; (c) windshield wipers; (d) mirrors; (e) fuel level adequate for planned routes. | Before first trip each day. |
| 5.2.6 | All Driving Staff | Exercise increased caution during adverse driving conditions including rain, fog, darkness, construction zones, and unfamiliar areas. Delay or reschedule non-urgent visits if conditions create significant danger, with supervisor notification. | As conditions warrant. |
| 5.2.7 | Supervisors | Do not require staff to drive in conditions that present a clear and significant danger to safety (e.g., severe weather, road closures). Follow OP-SL-007 for inclement weather decisions. | As conditions warrant. |

#### 5.3 Motor Vehicle Accident Response

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.3.1 | Involved Driver | If involved in a motor vehicle accident while on agency business: (a) Stop the vehicle immediately. Do not leave the scene. (b) Check for injuries to self and others. (c) Call 911 if there are injuries, significant property damage, or road hazard. (d) Move vehicles out of traffic if safe to do so and no injuries. (e) Exchange information with other parties: name, insurance, license, vehicle registration. (f) Obtain names and contact information of witnesses. (g) Take photographs of the scene, vehicle damage, and road conditions if safely possible. (h) Do not admit fault or make statements beyond exchanging required information. | Immediately at scene. |
| 5.3.2 | Involved Driver | Notify the immediate supervisor by phone as soon as safely possible after the accident. | As soon as safely possible; within 1 hour. |
| 5.3.3 | Involved Driver | Complete the Motor Vehicle Accident Report Form (Appendix B) documenting all details of the accident. | Within 24 hours. |
| 5.3.4 | Supervisor | Notify the Risk Manager of the accident. | Within 2 hours of being notified. |
| 5.3.5 | Risk Manager | Log the accident in the Incident Tracking Log per RM-ER-002. Obtain the police report number. | Within 24 hours. |
| 5.3.6 | Risk Manager | Notify the agency's automobile insurance carrier per RM-ER-004 policy terms. | Within 24 hours. |
| 5.3.7 | Risk Manager | If the driver or any other person was injured, ensure appropriate medical treatment and complete workers' compensation reporting per HR-WM-004 and OSHA requirements per 29 CFR § 1904. | Within 24 hours; OSHA reporting per regulatory timeframes. |
| 5.3.8 | Risk Manager | Determine if the patient visit schedule requires adjustment due to vehicle availability, driver injury, or driver distress. Coordinate with scheduling per OP-SL-001. | Within 24 hours. |

#### 5.4 Accident Investigation

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.4.1 | Risk Manager | Investigate all motor vehicle accidents occurring during agency business. Obtain: (a) completed Accident Report Form (Appendix B); (b) police report; (c) photographs; (d) witness statements; (e) driver's statement; (f) MVR of involved driver; (g) vehicle inspection report (if applicable). | Investigation initiated within 72 hours; completed within 14 calendar days. |
| 5.4.2 | Risk Manager | Classify the accident as: ☐ Preventable — driver failed to exercise reasonable precaution. ☐ Non-Preventable — driver exercised all reasonable precautions. Document the classification rationale. | Within 14 calendar days. |
| 5.4.3 | Risk Manager | For preventable accidents, recommend corrective action which may include: (a) additional driver safety training; (b) driving privileges suspension pending re-evaluation; (c) disciplinary action per HR-ER-002; (d) assignment modification. | Within 7 calendar days of investigation completion. |
| 5.4.4 | Risk Manager | For accidents involving injury, significant property damage (>$5,000), or fatality, complete a full investigation report (per RM-ER-002, Appendix E) and present findings to the Risk Management Committee. | Per RM-ER-002 timelines. |
| 5.4.5 | Risk Manager | Trend motor vehicle accident data monthly and report as part of the quarterly Risk Trending Report per RM-ER-005. | Monthly trending; quarterly reporting. |

### 1. Purpose
This policy establishes requirements for assessing and documenting the home environment for safety hazards that may affect patient health, safety, and clinical outcomes for Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Home health services are delivered in uncontrolled environments where hazards—including structural deficiencies, fire risks, infection risks, fall hazards, and environmental contaminants—may compromise patient and staff safety. Systematic environmental assessment enables identification, documentation, patient/caregiver education, and mitigation of hazards to the extent possible.
### 2. Scope
This policy applies to all clinical staff of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. who provide services in patient homes, including registered nurses, licensed vocational nurses, physical therapists, occupational therapists, speech-language pathologists, medical social workers, home health aides, and their supervisors.
### 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Environmental Safety Assessment | A systematic evaluation of the patient's home environment to identify hazards that may affect patient safety, staff safety, or the ability to deliver safe and effective care. |
| Home Environment Hazard | Any condition within or surrounding the patient's home that could cause injury, infection, illness, or impede safe care delivery. |
| Mitigable Hazard | A hazard that can be corrected or reduced through patient/caregiver education, equipment provision, environmental modification, or community resource referral. |
| Non-Mitigable Hazard | A hazard that cannot be corrected and that creates a significant and ongoing risk to patient or staff safety. |
| Environmental Safety Plan | A documented plan addressing identified hazards, interventions, patient/caregiver education provided, and follow-up actions. |

### 4. Policy Statements
4.1 An Environmental Safety Assessment shall be completed for every patient at the time of admission (SOC/ROC) and documented in the clinical record.
4.2 Environmental safety shall be reassessed at each recertification, upon any reported change in the home environment, upon any safety incident, and as clinically indicated.
4.3 All identified hazards shall be documented, discussed with the patient/caregiver, and addressed through education, intervention, or referral to the extent possible.
4.4 If a hazard cannot be mitigated and creates an imminent risk to patient safety, the clinician shall notify the supervisor immediately and the agency shall determine whether services can safely continue.
4.5 Environmental Safety Assessment findings shall be communicated to all disciplines providing care in the home.
4.6 Environmental safety data shall be aggregated and reported to the QAPI Committee and Risk Management Committee quarterly for trending analysis.
### 5. Procedures
#### 5.1 Initial Environmental Safety Assessment

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1.1 | Admitting Clinician | Complete the Home Environmental Safety Assessment Form (Appendix A) during the initial visit (SOC or ROC), evaluating the following domains: (a) Structural Safety: stairs, handrails, flooring, door widths, bathroom accessibility; (b) Fire Safety: smoke detectors, fire extinguisher, cooking safety, heating sources, oxygen in use, smoking in home; (c) Fall Hazards: rugs, clutter, cords, lighting, wet surfaces, uneven flooring; (d) Infection Risk: cleanliness, pest infestation, mold, water quality, waste disposal; (e) Electrical Safety: exposed wiring, overloaded outlets, extension cords; (f) Temperature/Ventilation: adequate heating/cooling, ventilation, extreme temperatures; (g) Medication Storage: secure, appropriate temperature, out of reach of children; (h) Medical Equipment: safe placement, clean, functioning properly; (i) Neighborhood/Access: access to home, lighting, security, barriers to emergency egress; (j) Emergency Preparedness: posted emergency numbers, accessible phone, patient emergency plan. | At SOC/ROC visit; documented within 24 hours. |
| 5.1.2 | Admitting Clinician | For each identified hazard, document: (a) the specific hazard; (b) severity (Low/Moderate/High); (c) intervention provided (education, modification, referral); (d) patient/caregiver response; (e) follow-up plan. | Concurrent with assessment. |
| 5.1.3 | Admitting Clinician | Educate the patient and caregiver on identified hazards and recommended corrective actions. Document education provided and learner response per CL-SD-017. | At assessment visit. |
| 5.1.4 | Admitting Clinician | For HIGH severity hazards that cannot be immediately mitigated, notify the supervisor by phone within 2 hours and document the notification. | Within 2 hours of identification. |
| 5.1.5 | Director of Nursing / Supervisor | For hazards creating imminent risk: (a) determine whether services can safely continue with modifications; (b) if services cannot safely continue, initiate discussion with physician and patient/caregiver per CL-CP-006; (c) document the decision and rationale. | Within 24 hours. |
| 5.1.6 | Admitting Clinician | Communicate environmental safety findings to all disciplines providing care in the home via the patient's clinical record and verbal handoff at case conference. | Within 48 hours of assessment; at next case conference. |

#### 5.2 Ongoing Environmental Safety Monitoring

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.2.1 | All Visiting Clinicians | Observe the home environment at each visit for new or changed hazards. Document any new hazard identified. | Every visit. |
| 5.2.2 | All Visiting Clinicians | If a new hazard is identified: (a) document on the Environmental Safety Update Form (Appendix B); (b) provide patient/caregiver education; (c) notify the primary nurse/case manager; (d) notify the supervisor if HIGH severity. | At time of identification. |
| 5.2.3 | Primary Nurse / Case Manager | Complete a formal reassessment of the home environment using Appendix A at each recertification period. | At each recertification. |
| 5.2.4 | Primary Nurse / Case Manager | Complete a formal reassessment within 7 calendar days of any of the following: (a) patient fall in the home; (b) report of injury in the home; (c) change in patient mobility or functional status; (d) change in medical equipment; (e) change in household members; (f) report from any clinician of new hazard. | Within 7 calendar days of trigger. |

#### 5.3 Environmental Safety Referrals

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.3.1 | Clinician / MSW | For hazards requiring resources beyond the patient's capacity (e.g., structural repairs, pest control, heating/cooling, assistive devices), refer to appropriate community resources per CL-SD-005 and document the referral. | Within 7 calendar days of identification. |
| 5.3.2 | Clinician | For fire safety concerns (no smoke detector, oxygen use with smoking, heating hazard), provide fire safety education and contact the local fire department for smoke detector installation if the patient is willing. | Within 7 calendar days. |
| 5.3.3 | Clinician / OT | For fall hazard concerns, coordinate with occupational therapy (if ordered) for home safety modification recommendations and assistive device assessment per CL-SD-003. | Within 7 calendar days; at next OT visit. |

| 5.4.2 | Risk Manager | Present environmental safety trending data to the Risk Management Committee and QAPI Committee per RM-ER-005 and QA-SM-001. | Quarterly. |

### 1. Purpose
This policy defines procedures for the safe handling, storage, transport, and disposal of biohazardous materials, sharps, pharmaceutical waste, and other regulated medical waste generated during home health service delivery by Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Compliance with OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens Standard, California Medical Waste Management Act, and EPA regulations is required to protect staff, patients, and the community.
### 2. Scope
This policy applies to all clinical staff, home health aides, and any personnel who handle, transport, or dispose of medical waste, sharps, biohazardous materials, or pharmaceutical waste during agency operations.
### 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Biohazardous Waste | Waste containing blood, body fluids, or other potentially infectious materials (OPIM) that may pose a risk of infection. |
| Sharps | Any object that can penetrate the skin, including needles, syringes with needles, lancets, scalpel blades, and broken glass contaminated with blood or OPIM. |
| Sharps Container | A puncture-resistant, leak-proof, closable container labeled with the biohazard symbol, used for the disposal of sharps. |
| Pharmaceutical Waste | Expired, unused, or contaminated medications requiring special disposal to prevent environmental contamination or misuse. |
| Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) | Gloves, gowns, masks, eye protection, and other equipment used to create a barrier between the worker and infectious material. |
| Standard Precautions | Infection control practices applied to all patients regardless of known infection status, including hand hygiene, PPE use, and safe injection practices. |
| Exposure Incident | A specific eye, mouth, mucous membrane, non-intact skin, or parenteral contact with blood or OPIM resulting from the performance of an employee's duties. |

### 4. Policy Statements
4.1 All staff shall follow Standard Precautions when handling blood, body fluids, or other potentially infectious materials per CL-SD-016 and OSHA 29 CFR § 1910.1030.
4.2 Sharps shall be disposed of immediately after use in an approved sharps container at the point of use. Recapping of needles is prohibited.
4.3 Biohazardous waste generated in the patient's home shall be managed and disposed of in compliance with California Medical Waste Management Act (Health & Safety Code § 117600 et seq.) and agency procedures.
4.4 All staff shall have access to appropriate PPE and shall be trained on its proper use, removal, and disposal.
4.5 Exposure incidents shall be reported and managed per the agency's Exposure Control Plan and RM-ER-002.
4.6 Pharmaceutical waste shall be disposed of per FDA and EPA guidelines, with controlled substances disposed of per DEA requirements.
### 5. Procedures
#### 5.1 Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1.1 | Agency (Risk Manager / Operations Director) | Provide all clinical staff with appropriate PPE including: (a) gloves (non-latex available); (b) gowns; (c) face masks; (d) eye protection/face shields; (e) CPR pocket masks. Ensure sufficient supply for all visits. | Continuous; supply checked monthly. |
| 5.1.2 | All Clinical Staff | Use appropriate PPE when exposure to blood, body fluids, or OPIM is anticipated. Select PPE based on the anticipated exposure per Standard Precautions. | At each applicable patient encounter. |
| 5.1.3 | All Clinical Staff | Remove and dispose of contaminated PPE before leaving the patient's home. Dispose in a plastic bag that is sealed and placed in the patient's household waste unless visibly saturated with blood (in which case, treat as biohazardous waste per §5.3). | At completion of each encounter. |

#### 5.2 Sharps Management

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.2.1 | All Clinical Staff | Carry an approved, labeled sharps container to every visit where sharps may be used. | At each applicable visit. |
| 5.2.2 | All Clinical Staff | Dispose of sharps immediately after use directly into the sharps container at the point of use. Do NOT recap, bend, break, or remove needles from syringes. | Immediately after use. |
| 5.2.3 | All Clinical Staff | When sharps container is ¾ full, close and seal it. Do not overfill. Replace with a new container. | When ¾ full. |
| 5.2.4 | All Clinical Staff | Transport sealed sharps containers to the agency office for proper disposal through the agency's contracted medical waste hauler. Transport in a secondary leak-proof container in the vehicle. | At next return to office; at least weekly. |
| 5.2.5 | All Clinical Staff | Educate patients and caregivers who self-administer injections on proper sharps disposal. Provide or arrange for a sharps container for the home. Instruct on community sharps disposal options. | At SOC and as needed. |
| 5.2.6 | Operations Director | Contract with a licensed medical waste hauler for regular sharps and medical waste pick-up from the agency office. Maintain the Waste Hauler Agreement (Appendix A) and disposal manifests. | Continuous; contract reviewed annually. |

#### 5.3 Biohazardous Waste Management

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.3.1 | All Clinical Staff | Items saturated or dripping with blood or OPIM (dressings, gauze, wound packing) shall be placed in a red biohazard bag or bag marked with the biohazard symbol. | At time of generation. |
| 5.3.2 | All Clinical Staff | In the patient's home, if a biohazard bag is not available, place contaminated items in a sealed plastic bag labeled "BIOHAZARD" and transport to the agency office for proper disposal. | At each applicable visit. |
| 5.3.3 | All Clinical Staff | Small amounts of blood or body fluid on surfaces may be cleaned using a 1:10 bleach solution or EPA-registered disinfectant. Staff shall wear gloves during cleanup. | At time of spill. |
| 5.3.4 | Operations Director | Ensure the agency office maintains a designated, secure area for temporary storage of biohazardous waste awaiting hauler pick-up. Area must be labeled with biohazard symbol and inaccessible to unauthorized persons. | Continuous. |

#### 5.4 Pharmaceutical Waste Disposal

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.4.1 | All Clinical Staff | Educate patients and caregivers on safe disposal of unused or expired medications per FDA guidelines: (a) medication take-back programs; (b) DEA-authorized collection sites; (c) FDA flush list (select medications); (d) household trash method (mix with coffee grounds/kitty litter, seal in container). | At SOC; at discharge; as needed. |
| 5.4.2 | All Clinical Staff | Do not accept or transport patient medications. If medication disposal is necessary during a visit (e.g., contaminated medication), document the disposal, amount, and method. | As needed. |
| 5.4.3 | Director of Nursing | For controlled substance disposal witnessed in the home, ensure two staff members are present and both sign the disposal documentation. | As applicable. |

#### 5.5 Exposure Incident Response

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.5.1 | Exposed Employee | Immediately following an exposure incident: (a) wash the affected area with soap and water (needlestick: allow to bleed, then wash; splash: flush eyes/mucous membranes with water for 15 minutes); (b) notify the supervisor. | Immediately. |
| 5.5.2 | Supervisor | Direct the employee to seek post-exposure medical evaluation from a designated healthcare provider or emergency department. | Within 2 hours. |
| 5.5.3 | Exposed Employee | Complete an Incident Report per RM-ER-002. | Within 24 hours. |
| 5.5.4 | Risk Manager | Ensure post-exposure evaluation and follow-up per OSHA Bloodborne Pathogens Standard (29 CFR § 1910.1030(f)) including: (a) documentation of route and circumstances of exposure; (b) identification of source individual (if possible and with consent); (c) blood testing of exposed employee (with consent); (d) post-exposure prophylaxis as recommended by healthcare provider; (e) counseling. | Within 24 hours; follow-up per medical provider. |
| 5.5.5 | Risk Manager | Investigate the incident to determine if controls or work practices can be improved to prevent recurrence. | Within 14 calendar days. |
| 5.5.6 | Risk Manager | Maintain the Sharps Injury Log (Appendix B) as required by OSHA, recording: (a) type and brand of device; (b) department/work area; (c) description of incident. Employee identity is kept confidential. | Within 14 days of incident; log retained 5 years. |

### 1. Purpose
This policy defines procedures for monitoring, responding to, and documenting medical product, medical device, and equipment safety recalls and alerts affecting patients or operations of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Timely identification and response to recalls protects patients from harm due to defective products and ensures regulatory compliance.
### 2. Scope
This policy applies to the Risk Manager, Operations Director, Director of Nursing, all clinical staff, and all personnel responsible for procuring, distributing, or using medical products, devices, or equipment on behalf of the agency.
### 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Product Recall | A notification from a manufacturer, the FDA, or other regulatory body that a medical product, device, or equipment has been found to be defective, dangerous, or non-compliant and must be removed from use, returned, repaired, or replaced. |
| FDA Safety Alert | A notification issued by the FDA regarding safety concerns with a specific product or device that may not require recall but warrants monitoring and precautions. |
| Class I Recall | A recall where there is a reasonable probability that the use of or exposure to a product will cause serious adverse health consequences or death. |
| Class II Recall | A recall where the use of or exposure to a product may cause temporary or medically reversible adverse health consequences. |
| Class III Recall | A recall where the use of or exposure to a product is not likely to cause adverse health consequences. |
| Affected Product | Any product, device, or equipment in the agency's possession or in use by agency patients that is subject to a recall or safety alert. |

### 4. Policy Statements
4.1 The Risk Manager shall monitor FDA recall and safety alert databases weekly to identify recalls that may affect agency operations or patients.
4.2 Upon identification of a recall affecting agency products or patient-in-use products, the agency shall respond according to the recall classification and urgency per the procedures in this policy.
4.3 All Class I recalls affecting products in agency possession or patient use shall be treated as emergent and responded to within 24 hours.
4.4 All recall actions shall be documented in the Recall Tracking Log (Appendix A) and retained for a minimum of 7 years.
4.5 Products subject to recall shall be immediately removed from use, segregated, and labeled "DO NOT USE — RECALL" pending disposition per manufacturer/FDA instructions.
### 5. Procedures
#### 5.1 Recall Monitoring

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1.1 | Risk Manager | Subscribe to and monitor the following recall/alert sources: (a) FDA MedWatch Safety Alerts; (b) FDA Medical Device Recalls; (c) CDC Health Alert Network; (d) manufacturer notifications; (e) DME supplier notifications. | Weekly monitoring; immediate review of direct notifications. |
| 5.1.2 | Risk Manager | Maintain a list of all medical products, devices, and equipment commonly used or prescribed by the agency (Product Inventory List). | Maintained continuously; updated quarterly. |
| 5.1.3 | All Staff | Upon receiving any recall notice, product safety alert, or product complaint from a patient, immediately notify the Risk Manager. | Within 24 hours of receipt. |

#### 5.2 Recall Response — Agency-Held Products

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.2.1 | Risk Manager | Upon identification of a recall affecting products in agency inventory: (a) determine the recall classification (Class I, II, or III); (b) identify all affected product by lot number, serial number, or model; (c) log the recall in the Recall Tracking Log (Appendix A). | Within 24 hours for Class I; within 72 hours for Class II/III. |
| 5.2.2 | Risk Manager / Operations Director | Immediately remove all affected products from active inventory. Segregate and label "DO NOT USE — RECALL." Secure in a designated area. | Within 24 hours for Class I; within 72 hours for Class II/III. |
| 5.2.3 | Operations Director | Follow manufacturer/FDA instructions for disposition: (a) return to manufacturer; (b) repair/replacement; (c) destroy per instructions. Document disposition. | Per manufacturer/FDA instructions. |
| 5.2.4 | Risk Manager | Notify the Director of Nursing and Administrator of the recall and actions taken. | Within 24 hours for Class I; within 72 hours for Class II/III. |

#### 5.3 Recall Response — Patient-In-Use Products

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.3.1 | Risk Manager | Identify all patients currently using the affected product by cross-referencing the Product Inventory List, patient care records, and DME supplier records. | Within 24 hours for Class I; within 72 hours for Class II/III. |
| 5.3.2 | Director of Nursing | For Class I recalls: Contact each affected patient by phone immediately. Instruct the patient/caregiver to discontinue use of the product (unless discontinuation poses a greater risk). Arrange for replacement or clinical intervention as needed. | Within 24 hours. |
| 5.3.3 | Director of Nursing | For Class II/III recalls: Notify each affected patient at the next scheduled visit or by phone within 7 calendar days. Provide instructions per manufacturer/FDA guidance. | Within 7 calendar days. |
| 5.3.4 | Visiting Clinician | Document the recall notification in the patient's clinical record including: (a) product identified; (b) recall classification; (c) instructions provided; (d) patient response; (e) replacement/alternative arranged. | At time of notification. |
| 5.3.5 | Risk Manager | Coordinate with DME suppliers to arrange product replacement for affected patients. | Within 72 hours for Class I; within 14 calendar days for Class II/III. |
| 5.3.6 | Risk Manager | If any patient has experienced adverse effects from the recalled product, complete an incident report per RM-ER-002 and file an FDA MedWatch report. | Within 24 hours of identification. |

#### 5.4 Recall Closure

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.4.1 | Risk Manager | Close the recall in the Tracking Log when: (a) all agency-held affected products are disposed of per instructions; (b) all affected patients have been notified and product replaced; (c) all documentation is complete. | Upon completion of all actions. |
| 5.4.2 | Risk Manager | Report all Class I and Class II recalls and response actions to the Risk Management Committee at the next quarterly meeting. | Quarterly. |

### 1. Purpose
This policy defines risk assessment and intervention protocols for patients at risk of elopement or unsafe wandering served by Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Patients with cognitive impairment, dementia, delirium, behavioral health conditions, or developmental disabilities may be at risk of leaving their home environment unsafely, resulting in injury, exposure, or death. Systematic identification, documentation, and care planning reduce this risk.
### 2. Scope
This policy applies to all clinical staff who assess, plan, and deliver care to patients with identified or suspected elopement/wandering risk, their supervisors, and the Director of Nursing.
### 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Elopement | A patient's unauthorized or unsafe departure from their place of residence, particularly when the patient lacks the cognitive capacity to navigate safely or is at risk of harm. |
| Wandering | Aimless or repetitive locomotion, often associated with cognitive impairment, that may lead to elopement if not monitored. |
| Elopement Risk Assessment | A clinical evaluation of a patient's likelihood of engaging in elopement or unsafe wandering, considering cognitive status, behavioral history, environment, and supervision. |

### 4. Policy Statements
4.1 All patients shall be screened for elopement/wandering risk at SOC/ROC as part of the comprehensive assessment.
4.2 Patients identified as at risk shall have an individualized Wandering/Elopement Safety Plan documented in the plan of care.
4.3 Caregivers of at-risk patients shall be educated on elopement prevention strategies, monitoring techniques, and emergency response.
4.4 Elopement events shall be reported per RM-ER-002 and investigated.
4.5 The agency shall not physically restrain patients per CL-PR-004 but shall implement environmental, educational, and monitoring interventions.
### 5. Procedures
#### 5.1 Elopement/Wandering Risk Screening

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1.1 | Admitting Clinician | Screen all patients for elopement/wandering risk at SOC/ROC using the Elopement Risk Screening Tool (Appendix A), evaluating: (a) cognitive status (BIMS, orientation, diagnosis of dementia/Alzheimer's); (b) history of wandering or elopement; (c) behavioral agitation or restlessness; (d) exit-seeking behavior; (e) level of supervision in the home; (f) home security features (locked doors, alarms, fencing); (g) proximity to hazards (roads, water, extreme weather exposure). | At SOC/ROC; documented within 24 hours. |
| 5.1.2 | Admitting Clinician | Classify patient risk: ☐ Low Risk — No indicators present. ☐ Moderate Risk — 1–2 risk factors; adequate supervision typically present. ☐ High Risk — 3+ risk factors; history of elopement; inadequate supervision. | Concurrent with screening. |
| 5.1.3 | Admitting Clinician | For Moderate and High Risk patients, complete the Elopement/Wandering Safety Plan (Appendix B) and incorporate interventions into the plan of care. | Within 48 hours of screening. |

#### 5.2 Safety Plan Development

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.2.1 | Primary Nurse / Case Manager | Develop the Elopement/Wandering Safety Plan (Appendix B) addressing: (a) specific risk factors identified; (b) environmental modifications recommended (door alarms, locks, secure fencing, identification bracelet); (c) supervision recommendations (level of supervision needed; caregiver schedule); (d) patient/caregiver education provided; (e) referrals (community resources, Alzheimer's Association Safe Return, GPS device); (f) emergency response plan if elopement occurs; (g) physician notification; (h) monitoring frequency. | Within 48 hours of risk identification. |
| 5.2.2 | Primary Nurse / Case Manager | Educate the patient (to the extent of comprehension) and caregiver on: (a) wandering/elopement risk and potential consequences; (b) environmental safety modifications; (c) monitoring strategies; (d) what to do if the patient elopes (call 911, provide description, contact police Silver Alert if applicable); (e) available community resources. Document education per CL-SD-017. | At plan development; reinforced at each visit. |
| 5.2.3 | Primary Nurse / Case Manager | Notify the physician of the identified elopement risk and safety plan. Obtain orders for any recommended interventions as applicable. | Within 48 hours. |
| 5.2.4 | Primary Nurse / Case Manager | Communicate the elopement risk and safety plan to all disciplines providing care in the home. | Within 48 hours; at next case conference. |

#### 5.3 Ongoing Monitoring

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.3.1 | All Visiting Clinicians | Monitor for signs of wandering behavior or elopement risk at each visit. | 5.3.2 | Primary Nurse / Case Manager | Reassess elopement risk using Appendix A at each recertification and upon any change in cognitive status, behavioral status, living situation, or caregiver availability. | At recertification; upon change. |
| 5.3.3 | All Visiting Clinicians | If a new wandering episode or elopement is reported by the caregiver, immediately notify the primary nurse/case manager and complete an incident report per RM-ER-002. | Within 24 hours. |

#### 5.4 Elopement Event Response

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.4.1 | Any Staff / Caregiver | If a staff member discovers or is informed that a patient has eloped: (a) immediately search the immediate vicinity; (b) call 911 if the patient cannot be located within 15 minutes; (c) provide 911 with patient description, cognitive status, medical conditions, and last known location; (d) request Silver Alert activation if applicable; (e) notify the supervisor/on-call immediately. | Immediately. |
| 5.4.2 | Supervisor / Director of Nursing | Coordinate with law enforcement. Notify the patient's emergency contact and physician. | Within 1 hour. |
| 5.4.3 | Risk Manager | Complete investigation per RM-ER-002. Review and update the safety plan. Determine if additional interventions or caregiver support is needed. | Within 72 hours. |
| 5.4.4 | Primary Nurse / Case Manager | Reassess the patient after any elopement event. Update the Safety Plan. Determine if the current level of home supervision is adequate or if a higher level of care is needed. | Within 48 hours of patient's return. |

### 1. Purpose
This policy establishes enhanced safety protocols for the management of high-risk medications in the home health setting by Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. High-risk medications, including anticoagulants, opioids, insulin, and other agents with narrow therapeutic indices, carry an elevated potential for serious harm if not properly managed. This policy ensures patient safety through structured assessment, monitoring, education, and coordination.
### 2. Scope
This policy applies to all clinical staff who assess, administer, manage, or educate patients regarding high-risk medications, including registered nurses, licensed vocational nurses, therapists contributing to medication management, and their supervisors.
### 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| High-Risk Medication | A medication or class of medications with an elevated potential for causing significant patient harm when used in error, including but not limited to the ISMP List of High-Alert Medications in Community/Ambulatory Settings. |
| High-Alert Medication Classes | Anticoagulants (warfarin, DOACs, heparin); Opioids; Insulin and hypoglycemic agents; Chemotherapy/antineoplastic agents; Immunosuppressants; Digoxin; Sedatives/hypnotics; Antiarrhythmics; Methotrexate (non-oncologic use). |
| Narrow Therapeutic Index (NTI) | A medication where small changes in dose or blood concentration can lead to significant therapeutic failure or adverse effects. |
| Medication Adverse Event | Any injury resulting from the use of a medication, whether from error, adverse drug reaction, or drug interaction. |

### 4. Policy Statements
4.1 The agency shall maintain a list of designated high-risk/high-alert medications based on the ISMP List of High-Alert Medications in Community/Ambulatory Settings, updated annually.
4.2 Enhanced safety protocols shall be applied to all patients receiving high-risk medications, including increased assessment frequency, structured monitoring parameters, patient/caregiver education, and documented safety checks.
4.3 All medication adverse events involving high-risk medications shall be reported per RM-ER-002 and QA-AE-001.
4.4 The agency shall maintain competency requirements for all clinical staff involved in high-risk medication management.
4.5 High-risk medication safety data shall be monitored and reported to the QAPI Committee quarterly.
### 5. Procedures
#### 5.1 High-Risk Medication Identification

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1.1 | Director of Nursing | Maintain the agency's High-Risk Medication List (Appendix A) based on the current ISMP List of High-Alert Medications in Community/Ambulatory Settings. Review and update annually or upon ISMP list revision. | Annually; upon ISMP update. |
| 5.1.2 | All Clinical Staff | At SOC/ROC and at each medication reconciliation, identify whether the patient is receiving any medication on the agency's High-Risk Medication List. Flag the patient's record accordingly. | At SOC/ROC; at each medication reconciliation. |

#### 5.2 Enhanced Assessment and Monitoring

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.2.1 | Admitting Clinician / Primary Nurse | For patients receiving high-risk medications, complete the High-Risk Medication Safety Assessment (Appendix B) at SOC/ROC, documenting: (a) specific high-risk medications; (b) indication for use; (c) current dose, frequency, and route; (d) prescribing physician; (e) relevant laboratory values (INR for anticoagulants, blood glucose for insulin/hypoglycemics, drug levels for digoxin/antiarrhythmics); (f) patient's knowledge of the medication; (g) ability to self-manage (cognition, dexterity, vision); (h) identified risk factors for adverse events; (i) drug interactions with current medication regimen; (j) monitoring plan. | At SOC/ROC; documented within 24 hours. |
| 5.2.2 | Primary Nurse / Case Manager | Establish medication-specific monitoring parameters for each high-risk medication in the plan of care, including: (a) clinical assessment parameters (vital signs, bleeding signs, hypoglycemia signs, etc.); (b) laboratory monitoring schedule; (c) assessment frequency; (d) escalation thresholds. | Within 48 hours of SOC. |
| 5.2.3 | All Visiting Clinicians | At each visit to a patient receiving high-risk medications, assess for: (a) adherence to prescribed regimen; (b) signs/symptoms of adverse effects per medication class; (c) relevant vital signs or point-of-care testing results; (d) patient's understanding and questions; (e) changes in other medications that could affect interactions; (f) safe storage of medication. | Every visit. |
| 5.2.4 | Primary Nurse | Monitor laboratory values per the medication-specific monitoring plan. Notify the physician of any out-of-range results. Document notification and physician response. | Per monitoring plan; physician notification within 4 hours of receiving results. |

#### 5.3 Medication-Specific Protocols

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.3.1 | Primary Nurse | Anticoagulants (Warfarin, DOACs, Heparin): (a) Assess for signs of bleeding at each visit (bruising, petechiae, dark stools, hematuria, gum bleeding); (b) Monitor INR per physician orders (warfarin); (c) Educate on dietary considerations (warfarin/Vitamin K); (d) Educate on drug interactions; (e) Educate on signs requiring emergency care; (f) Document on High-Risk Medication Monitoring Flow Sheet (Appendix C). | Every visit; INR per orders. |
| 5.3.2 | Primary Nurse | Opioids: (a) Assess pain level using standardized tool per CL-SD-014; (b) Assess for respiratory depression (respiratory rate <12 — notify physician immediately); (c) Assess for sedation level; (d) Assess for constipation and bowel function; (e) Assess for signs of misuse or diversion; (f) Educate on safe storage and disposal; (g) Assess for fall risk (sedation contributing to falls per CL-SD-015); (h) Document on Appendix C. | Every visit. |
| 5.3.3 | Primary Nurse | Insulin and Hypoglycemic Agents: (a) Monitor blood glucose per physician orders; (b) Assess for signs of hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia; (c) Verify correct insulin type, dose, and administration technique; (d) Assess injection sites for lipodystrophy; (e) Assess storage (refrigeration for unopened insulin); (f) Educate on hypoglycemia recognition and treatment; (g) Document on Appendix C. | Every visit; glucose per orders. |
| 5.3.4 | Primary Nurse | Digoxin: (a) Assess apical pulse for 60 seconds before administration (hold and notify physician if <60 bpm); (b) Assess for signs of toxicity (nausea, vomiting, visual disturbances, bradycardia, arrhythmia); (c) Monitor digoxin levels and electrolytes (potassium) per physician orders; (d) Document on Appendix C. | Every visit. |

#### 5.4 Patient/Caregiver Education

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.4.1 | Primary Nurse | Provide structured, individualized education to the patient and caregiver for each high-risk medication, covering: (a) purpose of the medication; (b) correct dose, frequency, and route; (c) expected therapeutic effects; (d) potential side effects and adverse effects; (e) signs and symptoms requiring immediate medical attention; (f) food and drug interactions; (g) safe storage and disposal; (h) importance of not modifying dose without physician direction; (i) laboratory monitoring requirements. | At SOC; reinforced at each visit; documented per CL-SD-017. |
| 5.4.2 | Primary Nurse | Assess patient/caregiver understanding using teach-back method. If comprehension is inadequate, modify educational approach and repeat. | At each education session. |
| 5.4.3 | Primary Nurse | For patients on opioids, provide education on safe storage (locked, out of reach of others), disposal of unused medications per FDA guidelines, and naloxone availability (if ordered). | At SOC; reinforced as indicated. |

#### 5.5 Adverse Event Response

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.5.1 | Any Clinical Staff | If a medication adverse event involving a high-risk medication is identified: (a) assess the patient's current condition; (b) provide immediate clinical intervention as indicated; (c) notify the physician; (d) file an incident report per RM-ER-002 and QA-AE-001. | Immediately upon identification. |
| 5.5.2 | Risk Manager | Investigate all medication adverse events involving high-risk medications. Determine whether the event was preventable. | Within 14 calendar days. |
| 5.5.3 | Risk Manager | Report high-risk medication adverse event data to the QAPI Committee and Risk Management Committee quarterly per RM-ER-005. | Quarterly. |

### 1. Purpose
This policy establishes the agency's response framework for pandemic and infectious disease outbreaks including staff protection, patient triage, service modification protocols, and coordination with public health authorities for Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Pandemics and infectious disease outbreaks require rapid, coordinated, and sustained response to protect staff and patients while maintaining essential services.
### 2. Scope
This policy applies to all employees, contractors, per diem staff, and volunteers of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. It is activated by the Administrator upon declaration of a public health emergency by federal, state, or local authorities, or upon determination by the agency that an infectious disease outbreak poses a significant risk to staff or patients.
### 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Pandemic | A global outbreak of an infectious disease affecting a large number of people across multiple countries or regions, as declared by the WHO or national health authorities. |
| Infectious Disease Outbreak | An increase in the occurrence of an infectious disease above what is normally expected in a defined area or population, as determined by public health authorities. |
| Public Health Emergency | A declaration by federal (HHS Secretary), state (Governor), or local health authorities that an infectious disease or other condition poses a significant risk to public health. |
| Pandemic Response Team | The agency's designated team responsible for coordinating the agency's response to a pandemic or infectious disease outbreak. |
| Essential Services | Patient care services that must be maintained during a pandemic to prevent imminent harm, hospitalization, or death. |
| Modified Operations | Adjusted service delivery protocols implemented during a pandemic, which may include telehealth substitution, modified visit frequency, or prioritized patient triage. |
| Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) | Equipment worn to minimize exposure to infectious agents including masks, respirators, gowns, gloves, eye protection, and face shields. |

### 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a Pandemic Response Plan that addresses preparation, response, and recovery phases.
4.2 The agency shall maintain a minimum 30-day supply of critical PPE at all times.
4.3 Staff protection shall be prioritized, including PPE provision, exposure protocols, and accommodation for high-risk staff.
4.4 Service delivery shall be modified as necessary during a pandemic to balance patient safety, staff safety, and essential service continuity.
4.5 The agency shall coordinate with local, state, and federal public health authorities during any outbreak or pandemic.
4.6 All pandemic response activities shall be documented and debriefed following event resolution.
### 5. Procedures
#### 5.1 Preparedness

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1.1 | Risk Manager | Maintain the Pandemic Response Plan (Appendix A) including: (a) activation criteria and authority; (b) Pandemic Response Team roster; (c) communication plan; (d) PPE procurement and inventory; (e) staff screening protocols; (f) patient triage criteria; (g) service modification protocols; (h) telehealth implementation plan; (i) infection control procedures; (j) mental health support; (k) deactivation criteria; (l) after-action review process. | Reviewed annually; updated upon any activation or significant public health change. |
| 5.1.2 | Operations Director | Maintain a minimum 30-day PPE stockpile including: (a) surgical masks; (b) N95 respirators; (c) gloves; (d) gowns; (e) face shields/eye protection; (f) hand sanitizer; (g) disinfecting supplies. Inventory checked monthly. | Maintained continuously; inventory monthly. |
| 5.1.3 | Risk Manager | Establish the Pandemic Response Team comprising: Risk Manager (lead), Administrator, Director of Nursing, HR Director, Operations Director, and Compliance Officer. | Established at policy effective date; updated with any personnel change. |
| 5.1.4 | HR Director | Ensure all staff are current on infection control training per CL-SD-016, including Standard Precautions, Transmission-Based Precautions, and PPE donning/doffing. | Annually; additional training upon pandemic activation. |
| 5.1.5 | Risk Manager | Establish relationships with local public health departments and maintain current contact information for: (a) county health department; (b) state health department; (c) CDC information line; (d) local emergency management. | Updated annually. |

#### 5.2 Activation and Response

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.2.1 | Administrator | Activate the Pandemic Response Plan when: (a) a public health emergency is declared by any level of government; (b) the local health department issues guidance affecting home health operations; (c) agency staff or patients are affected by an infectious disease outbreak; (d) CDC issues travel or operational alerts affecting agency operations. | Within 24 hours of trigger. |
| 5.2.2 | Risk Manager | Convene the Pandemic Response Team within 24 hours of activation. Establish meeting frequency (minimum daily during initial response; weekly during sustained response). | Within 24 hours. |
| 5.2.3 | Risk Manager | Implement the Staff Screening Protocol: (a) daily symptom screening for all staff before patient contact; (b) temperature checks (if applicable per pathogen); (c) exposure questioning; (d) exclusion criteria for symptomatic or exposed staff. | At activation; daily during response. |
| 5.2.4 | Director of Nursing | Implement Patient Triage using the Patient Priority Classification (Appendix B): Priority 1 — Essential: Patients whose care cannot be deferred without imminent risk of hospitalization, serious decline, or death. Priority 2 — Important: Patients who require continued services but can tolerate brief modification or telehealth substitution. Priority 3 — Deferrable: Patients whose services can be safely suspended for a defined period with monitoring. | Within 48 hours of activation. |
| 5.2.5 | Director of Nursing | Implement service modifications as indicated: (a) convert eligible visits to telehealth per CL-SD-009; (b) extend visit intervals for stable patients (with physician authorization); (c) prioritize in-person visits for Priority 1 patients; (d) group geographic visits to minimize travel and exposure; (e) assign dedicated staff to confirmed-positive patient visits (cohorting). | Within 48 hours; ongoing during response. |
| 5.2.6 | Operations Director | Implement PPE distribution: (a) issue PPE to all field staff per CDC/OSHA guidance for the specific pathogen; (b) train staff on proper donning/doffing; (c) monitor daily PPE consumption; (d) initiate emergency procurement if stockpile drops below 14-day supply. | At activation; daily monitoring. |
| 5.2.7 | HR Director | Identify and accommodate high-risk staff (immunocompromised, pregnant, >65, chronic conditions) per HR-ER-008 and applicable law. Options include: (a) remote/telehealth assignment; (b) administrative duties; (c) leave accommodations. | Within 72 hours of activation. |
| 5.2.8 | Administrator | Communicate with patients, caregivers, referral sources, and community partners regarding service modifications using the Patient Emergency Communication Plan per RM-EP-003. | Within 48 hours; ongoing. |

#### 5.3 Infection Control During Response

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.3.1 | All Clinical Staff | For all patient visits during pandemic/outbreak: (a) perform hand hygiene before and after each patient contact; (b) use PPE per CDC/OSHA guidance for the specific pathogen; (c) screen the patient and household for symptoms upon arrival; (d) maintain appropriate distance when not performing direct care; (e) follow respiratory hygiene/cough etiquette; (f) disinfect equipment between patients. | Every visit. |
| 5.3.2 | Director of Nursing | For visits to confirmed or suspected positive patients: (a) assign trained staff with appropriate PPE; (b) implement Transmission-Based Precautions per CDC guidance; (c) notify all disciplines scheduled to visit the patient; (d) schedule positive patients last in the day when possible; (e) ensure proper PPE doffing and disposal. | At each applicable visit. |
| 5.3.3 | Risk Manager / Director of Nursing | Report confirmed cases (staff or patients) to the local health department as required. Cooperate with contact tracing. Notify potentially exposed staff within 24 hours. | Within 24 hours of confirmation. |
| 5.3.4 | HR Director | Manage staff exposures and positive cases: (a) implement isolation/quarantine per CDC guidance; (b) ensure access to testing; (c) maintain confidentiality; (d) manage return-to-work criteria per CDC guidance; (e) provide EAP and mental health support. | Per CDC guidance timeframes. |

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.4.1 | Administrator | Deactivate the Pandemic Response Plan when: (a) public health emergency is declared over; (b) local transmission has declined to baseline; (c) agency operations can safely return to normal. | When criteria met; documented decision. |
| 5.4.2 | Pandemic Response Team | Conduct an After-Action Review within 30 calendar days of deactivation documenting: (a) timeline of events and response; (b) effectiveness of preparedness measures; (c) gaps identified; (d) PPE adequacy; (e) staff impact; (f) patient impact; (g) financial impact; (h) lessons learned; (i) corrective actions for future preparedness. | Within 30 calendar days of deactivation. |
| 5.4.3 | Risk Manager | Update the Pandemic Response Plan based on After-Action Review findings. | Within 60 calendar days of deactivation. |
| 5.4.4 | Director of Nursing | Restore normal service delivery. Reassess all patients whose services were modified during the response. | Within 14 calendar days of deactivation. |
| 5.4.5 | Risk Manager | Present the After-Action Review to the Governing Body. | At next quarterly meeting. |

## 1. Purpose
This policy establishes requirements for emergency preparedness training, testing, and exercise activities for all Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. employees in compliance with 42 CFR § 484.102(d), the CMS Emergency Preparedness Rule (81 FR 63860), and California Department of Public Health emergency preparedness requirements. Training and testing ensure that all staff can effectively execute the agency's Emergency Operations Plan (RM-EP-001) during actual emergencies, protecting patients, staff, and the community.

## 2. Scope
All employees of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. regardless of position or employment status (full-time, part-time, per diem). Contracted clinical staff shall receive equivalent training from the agency.

## 3. Policy Statements
3.1 Initial Training: All new employees shall complete emergency preparedness orientation training during the onboarding process before beginning independent patient care assignments per HR-TA-005. Initial training shall cover at minimum:
(a) The agency's Emergency Operations Plan (RM-EP-001) and each employee's specific role;
(b) Chain of command and communication procedures during emergencies;
(c) Incident Command System (ICS) basics;
(d) Patient population-specific emergency risks (electrical-dependent patients, mobility-limited patients);
(e) Personal protective equipment (PPE) use per RM-SS-002;
(f) Evacuation and shelter-in-place procedures.

3.2 Annual Training: All employees shall complete at minimum annual emergency preparedness training covering updates to the Emergency Operations Plan and any lessons learned from prior exercises.

3.3 Exercises: The agency shall conduct at least two (2) emergency preparedness exercises per year as required by 42 CFR § 484.102(d)(2):
(a) Exercise 1: A community-based full-scale exercise OR, if a community exercise is not available, a facility-based full-scale exercise.
(b) Exercise 2: If Exercise 1 was a full-scale exercise, Exercise 2 may be a tabletop exercise conducted at the executive/management level. If Exercise 1 was a community exercise, Exercise 2 must be a facility-based full-scale or functional exercise.

3.4 After-Action Review (AAR): Each exercise shall be followed by a formal AAR within 30 days. The AAR shall: (a) identify performance strengths; (b) identify gaps and deficiencies; (c) develop corrective action items with responsible parties and due dates; (d) be documented and retained per CO-HP-007; (e) be reported to the Governing Body.

3.5 Documentation: All training completions and exercise participation shall be documented by HR in the agency's training records system. Documentation shall include: employee name, date, training/exercise type, duration, and competency assessment result if applicable.

3.6 Corrective Actions: Deficiencies identified in exercises shall be incorporated into updates to the Emergency Operations Plan (RM-EP-001) within 60 days of the AAR.

## 1. Purpose
This policy establishes the agency's plan for communicating with and providing care to patients during and following emergencies, disasters, and other disruptions that affect the delivery of home health services. Per 42 CFR § 484.102(c), Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. must maintain an all-hazards communication plan that addresses patients' needs in the event of a disruption to normal operations. Patients who depend on home health services — particularly those with electrical-dependent equipment, mobility limitations, or limited social support — are among the most vulnerable populations during any emergency.

## 2. Scope
All patients currently receiving home health services from Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. and all clinical and administrative staff responsible for patient communication and care coordination.

## 3. Policy Statements
3.1 Patient Risk Assessment: At admission, the admitting RN shall complete a Patient Emergency Risk Assessment as part of the standard intake process. The assessment shall document:
(a) Electrical-dependent equipment in use (oxygen concentrator, ventilator, suction machine, hospital bed, lifts);
(b) Mobility status: ambulatory, wheelchair-dependent, bed-bound, ability to self-evacuate;
(c) Communication limitations: language, hearing, vision, cognitive impairment;
(d) Social support: emergency contact name, relationship, phone number; availability of caregiver;
(e) Geographic risk: location in flood zone, wildfire risk area, or mobile home/manufactured housing;
(f) Utility dependency: whether equipment or medications require refrigeration.

3.2 Patient Emergency Communication System: The agency shall maintain a current patient census in electronic format that enables rapid communication to all active patients during an emergency, including:
(a) A robocall/automated notification capability OR a manual call tree for all active patients;
(b) The ability to triage and prioritize outreach to the highest-risk patients (electrical-dependent, isolated, high-acuity) within the first 2 hours of an emergency.

3.3 Communication Procedures During Emergency:
(a) Within 2 hours of activating the Emergency Operations Plan (RM-EP-001), the Administrator or designee shall initiate patient notification to advise patients of any service disruption, estimated restoration, and emergency contact procedures.
(b) High-risk patients (electrical-dependent, bed-bound) shall receive direct personal contact (phone call) within the first 2 hours; remaining patients may be reached by automated notification.
(c) Patients who cannot be reached shall be flagged for emergency welfare check within 4 hours.

3.4 Utility-Dependent Patients: Patients using electrical medical equipment shall be: (a) registered with their local utility company as a Medical Baseline or Life Support customer; (b) advised to register with county/city emergency management for welfare check programs; (c) included in the highest-priority tier for emergency outreach.

3.5 Coordination with Local Emergency Management:
The agency shall maintain working relationships with: (a) Local Health Department emergency preparedness program; (b) County Office of Emergency Services; (c) Local utility companies' medical baseline programs; (d) Community Partners for Emergency Response (CPER) and local evacuation resources.

3.6 Continuity of Care: When a patient must be evacuated or cannot be reached during an emergency, the DON shall coordinate care continuity including: (a) transfer of care documentation to any receiving facility; (b) medication bridge supply arrangements; (c) coordination with treating physician regarding service interruption; (d) documentation of all emergency-related care actions in the clinical record per CL-CD-001.

3.7 Patients Who Relocate During Emergency: A patient who relocates during a declared emergency (e.g., evacuation to shelter or family home) shall be contacted within 24 hours. If the relocation places them outside the agency's service area, the agency shall assist in transferring their care to an appropriate local provider.

3.8 Post-Emergency Review: Within 30 days of any significant emergency event requiring activation of this plan, the Administrator shall conduct a post-event review documenting: patients contacted, patients unreachable, services disrupted, care continuity measures taken, and lessons learned.
