## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes the Corporate Compliance Program ("Program") for Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. The Program is the agency's formal, systematic commitment to conducting all operations with integrity, in full conformity with applicable federal and state laws, regulations, and the standards issued by the Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services (CMS), the Office of Inspector General (OIG), and all other regulatory bodies having jurisdiction over the agency's operations. The Program satisfies the requirements set forth in the OIG Compliance Program Guidance for Home Health Agencies and supports compliance with 42 CFR § 484.100 (Condition of Participation: Compliance with Federal, State, and Local Laws) and the agency's obligation to maintain Medicare certification. The Program provides the structural foundation upon which all other compliance-related policies in the CO domain operate.
## 3. Scope
This policy applies to:
All Governing Body members of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.
The Agency Administrator
The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager
The Compliance Officer and Compliance Committee members
All full-time, part-time, per diem, and contracted employees
All volunteers performing services on behalf of the agency
All third-party vendors, contractors, and business associates who interact with agency operations, billing, or patient care
Note: This policy does not supersede any applicable federal or state law. Where this policy conflicts with a law or regulation, the more stringent requirement governs.
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a fully operational Corporate Compliance Program that satisfies the seven foundational elements of an effective compliance program as defined by the OIG Compliance Program Guidance for Home Health Agencies.
4.2 The seven required elements of the Program are:
Written policies, procedures, and standards of conduct
Designation of a Compliance Officer and Compliance Committee
Effective training and education
Effective lines of communication
Internal monitoring and auditing
Enforcement of standards and well-publicized disciplinary guidelines
Prompt response to detected offenses and development of corrective action
4.3 The Governing Body shall maintain ultimate accountability for the effectiveness of the Program and shall receive compliance reports at no less than quarterly intervals per policy GV-GB-001.
4.4 The Program shall be operationally independent. The Compliance Officer shall have direct reporting access to the Governing Body and shall not be subject to retaliation, pressure, or interference in performing compliance functions.
4.5 Participation in compliance training and adherence to the Code of Conduct (CO-CP-004) are conditions of continued employment and/or contract with Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.
4.6 The agency shall not employ or contract with any individual or entity that appears on the OIG List of Excluded Individuals/Entities (LEIE) or the System for Award Management (SAM) exclusion database, per policy HR-TA-003.
4.7 All known or suspected violations of law, regulation, agency policy, or the Code of Conduct shall be reported promptly through mechanisms established in policy CO-CP-006 and investigated per policy CO-CP-007.
4.8 Only the most current approved version of this policy shall be considered valid. Any revision requires re-acknowledgment by all workforce members within scope within 14 calendar days.

## 8. Compliance Measurement
### 8.1 How Compliance Is Measured

| Compliance Indicator | Measurement Method | Acceptable Standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Program is formally adopted by Governing Body. | Review of Governing Body minutes for adoption resolution. | Documented adoption on file at all times. |
| Compliance Officer is designated and functioning. | Review of designation documentation; Governing Body minutes. | Current designation documented; no vacancy exceeds 30 days without interim designee. |
| Annual audit work plan is approved. | Review of Compliance Committee minutes; work plan document. | Approved at Q1 Compliance Committee meeting; 100% of planned audits initiated. |
| Quarterly compliance reports submitted to Governing Body. | Review of report submission dates vs. quarterly meeting dates. | 100% of reports submitted at least 7 days before each meeting. |
| All workforce members complete annual compliance training. | Review of training completion logs. | 95% completion rate within 30 days of deadline; 100% within 45 days. |
| Investigations initiated within required timeframes. | Review of investigation log with initiation dates. | 100% initiated within 7 calendar days of report receipt. |
| Confirmed overpayments refunded within 60 days. | Review of refund documentation and identification dates. | 100% compliance with 60-day repayment rule. |

### 8.2 Common Failure Points and Mitigation

| Failure Point | Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Program exists on paper but is not operationally active. | OIG and CMS will treat a non-functional Program as equivalent to having no Program. | Compliance Officer must demonstrate active operations through documented audits, meeting minutes, and training records. |
| Compliance Officer reports to Administrator rather than directly to Governing Body. | Independence is compromised; conflicts of interest may be suppressed. | Governing Body must establish direct reporting line in the designation document. |
| Training completion rates fall below 95%. | OIG views low completion as evidence of a non-effective Program. | Compliance Officer must track and escalate to Governing Body Chair within 7 days of missed deadline. |
| Investigations are not documented in writing. | Undocumented investigations are treated as not having occurred in any subsequent audit or survey. | All investigations must be opened, tracked, and closed in writing per CO-CP-007. |

## 2. Purpose
This policy defines the qualifications, designation process, authority, independence, reporting structure, and responsibilities of the Compliance Officer of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. The Compliance Officer is the individual designated by the Governing Body who holds primary accountability for the day-to-day operation of the Corporate Compliance Program established under policy CO-CP-001. This policy ensures that the agency satisfies the OIG requirement that an effective compliance program include a designated Compliance Officer with sufficient authority and operational independence to fulfill all compliance functions without conflict or interference.
## 3. Scope
This policy applies to:
The Governing Body (as the designating authority)
The individual designated as Compliance Officer
The Administrator
Any individual serving in an interim Compliance Officer capacity
The Compliance Committee
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall designate a qualified Compliance Officer prior to agency operation and maintain that designation continuously thereafter.
4.2 The Compliance Officer shall be designated by the Governing Body and the designation shall be documented in Governing Body meeting minutes with the full Designation and Authority Letter (Appendix A).
4.3 The Compliance Officer shall have the authority, independence, and resources necessary to fulfill all compliance functions. No member of agency leadership — including the Administrator — shall have the authority to override, impede, or retaliate against the Compliance Officer's performance of their duties.
4.4 The Compliance Officer shall have a direct reporting line to the Governing Body. The Compliance Officer may also have an administrative reporting relationship to the Administrator for operational purposes, provided that this reporting relationship does not compromise the independence of the Compliance Officer.
4.5 Any vacancy in the Compliance Officer role shall be filled within 30 calendar days. An interim Compliance Officer shall be designated within 14 calendar days of a vacancy and documented in writing.
4.6 The Compliance Officer role shall not be combined with the role of Agency Administrator or any clinical role that would create a conflict of interest in performing compliance oversight functions.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Compliance Officer | The individual designated by the Governing Body to operate the Corporate Compliance Program, investigate compliance concerns, coordinate auditing and monitoring, and report directly to the Governing Body on all compliance matters. |
| Independence | The ability to conduct compliance oversight functions, investigations, and reporting without interference, pressure, or retaliation from any member of agency management or the Governing Body. |
| Designation | The formal act of the Governing Body appointing an individual to serve as Compliance Officer, documented in meeting minutes and the Designation and Authority Letter. |
| Interim Compliance Officer | An individual temporarily fulfilling Compliance Officer responsibilities during a vacancy, with full authority granted by the Governing Body. |
| Conflict of Interest | Any situation in which the Compliance Officer's personal, financial, or professional interests could reasonably be perceived to compromise the independent performance of compliance functions. |

| Requirement | Standard |

| Experience | Minimum 2 years of experience in healthcare compliance, regulatory affairs, or health care law; home health experience strongly preferred. |

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes the structure, composition, responsibilities, meeting requirements, and documentation standards for the Compliance Committee of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. The Compliance Committee is the multi-disciplinary operational body that supports the Compliance Officer in implementing, monitoring, and evaluating the Corporate Compliance Program (CO-CP-001). The Committee provides the mechanism by which compliance risk, audit findings, regulatory changes, and corrective actions are reviewed at a cross-functional level and escalated to the Governing Body as appropriate.
## 3. Scope
This policy applies to all Compliance Committee members, all departments represented on the Committee, and all senior leadership personnel whose operational decisions affect the agency's compliance posture.
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a functioning Compliance Committee, chaired by the Compliance Officer.

4.4 The Compliance Committee shall not have authority to override Governing Body directives or the Compliance Officer's independent compliance determinations.
4.5 Attendance at Compliance Committee meetings is a professional obligation for all designated members. Absence without advance notice and a designated alternate is not acceptable.

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes the Code of Conduct and Ethics ("Code") of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. The Code defines the ethical standards, behavioral expectations, legal obligations, and prohibited conduct applicable to every individual who works for or on behalf of the agency. The Code is a foundational element of the Corporate Compliance Program (CO-CP-001) and reflects the agency's commitment to conducting all operations with honesty, integrity, and respect for patients, employees, referral partners, and payers.
## 3. Scope
This Code applies without exception to:
All Governing Body members
The Administrator and all senior leadership
All full-time, part-time, per diem, and contracted employees
All volunteers
All vendors, business associates, and contractors acting on behalf of the agency
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 Every individual within the scope of this policy shall conduct themselves in accordance with the standards of this Code at all times, whether on or off agency premises, when representing the agency in any capacity.
4.2 Adherence to this Code is a condition of employment or contract with Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Violations are subject to disciplinary action up to and including termination or contract cancellation.
4.3 No one in a position of authority — including the Administrator or Governing Body members — may authorize conduct that violates this Code, applicable law, or regulation.
4.4 All workforce members have the affirmative obligation to report known or suspected violations of this Code through the mechanisms established in policy CO-CP-006.
4.5 No individual shall be retaliated against for good-faith reporting of a Code violation, per policy CO-CP-005.
## 5. Core Standards of Conduct
### 5.1 Patient-Centered Care and Dignity
All workforce members shall:
Treat every patient with dignity, respect, and compassion regardless of diagnosis, age, race, color, national origin, sex, disability, religion, or ability to pay.
Protect patient privacy and confidentiality at all times per policies CO-HP-001 and CO-HP-002.
Honor patient rights as defined in policy CL-PR-001.
Never exploit, abuse, neglect, or mistreat a patient. All suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation shall be reported immediately per policies CL-PR-006 and HR-ER-009.
Provide only services that are ordered, medically necessary, and within their scope of practice.
### 5.2 Integrity in Billing and Documentation
All workforce members shall:
Document clinical services accurately, completely, and truthfully.
Never document services not actually rendered, alter clinical records to support billing, or fail to document care that was provided.
Never submit or participate in the submission of a false claim to Medicare, Medicaid, or any other payer.
Report suspected billing irregularities or documentation falsification through CO-CP-006.
### 5.3 Conflict of Interest
All workforce members shall:
Disclose any personal, financial, or professional interest that could conflict with the agency's interests or their professional obligations.
Never allow a conflict of interest to influence a patient care decision, business decision, or compliance determination.
Governing Body members shall comply with the full conflict of interest management requirements of policy GV-GB-003.
### 5.4 Gifts, Gratuities, and Kickbacks
All workforce members shall:
Never accept or offer gifts, meals, entertainment, or gratuities that exceed $25 per occurrence or $50 annually from any patient, vendor, referral source, or competitor.
Never offer, pay, solicit, or receive anything of value in exchange for patient referrals, in violation of the Anti-Kickback Statute (42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b).
Report any solicitation of a kickback or improper referral arrangement immediately to the Compliance Officer.
### 5.5 Confidentiality and Privacy
All workforce members shall:
Never access, use, or disclose patient health information beyond the minimum necessary for their assigned duties.
Never discuss patient information in non-private settings (hallways, vehicles, social settings).
Never share login credentials, access patient records on behalf of another, or circumvent system access controls.
Report any known or suspected breach of patient information per policy CO-HP-003.
### 5.6 Workplace Respect and Anti-Harassment
All workforce members shall:
Treat colleagues, supervisors, and subordinates with respect and professionalism at all times.
Never engage in harassment, discrimination, or hostile conduct based on any protected characteristic.
Report harassment or discrimination per policy HR-ER-004.
### 5.7 Compliance with Laws and Regulations
All workforce members shall:
Comply with all applicable federal and state laws, regulations, and agency policies relevant to their role.
Never perform, authorize, or conceal any act that they know or reasonably believe violates applicable law.
Cooperate fully with all internal compliance audits and investigations.
### 5.8 Stewardship of Agency Resources
All workforce members shall:
Use agency resources (equipment, supplies, technology, time) only for legitimate agency business purposes.
Never misappropriate, steal, or misuse agency property.
Report waste, misuse, or misappropriation of agency resources through CO-CP-006.
### 5.9 Social Media and Public Communications
All workforce members shall:
Never post patient information, clinical photographs, or identifiable patient details on any social media platform.
Never make public statements that misrepresent the agency's services, credentials, or compliance status.
Comply with policy IT-UP-003 for all social media and public communications.
## 6. Reporting Violations
6.1 All workforce members have an affirmative obligation to report suspected violations of this Code, applicable law, or agency policy.
6.2 Reports may be made through any of the following mechanisms (per policy CO-CP-006):
Direct contact with the Compliance Officer
Agency compliance hotline (anonymous option available)
Direct contact with the Governing Body Chair (for matters involving the Compliance Officer or Administrator)
6.3 Reports may be made anonymously. Anonymous reports will be investigated to the extent practicable given the information provided.
6.4 No individual will be retaliated against for making a good-faith compliance report, per policy CO-CP-005.
## 7. Consequences of Violations

| Severity Level | Examples | Potential Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Level 1 — Minor | Unintentional policy deviation; minor documentation error (without billing impact); failure to attend required training by deadline. | Documented counseling; mandatory retraining; corrective action plan. |
| Level 2 — Moderate | Repeated policy violations; unauthorized PHI disclosure; gift acceptance above threshold; failure to report a known violation. | Written warning; performance improvement plan; suspension; retraining. |
| Level 3 — Serious | Fraudulent billing; falsification of records; patient abuse or neglect; kickback participation; retaliation against a reporter; intentional HIPAA violation. | Immediate suspension pending investigation; termination; regulatory reporting; referral for civil or criminal prosecution. |

Discipline shall be applied consistently regardless of seniority, role, or tenure. The Administrator shall be notified of all Level 2 and Level 3 violations within 24 hours. The Governing Body shall be notified of all Level 3 violations within 24 hours.

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes the protections afforded to individuals who, in good faith, report suspected violations of law, regulation, agency policy, or the Code of Conduct (CO-CP-004), and prohibits retaliation of any kind against such individuals. Effective compliance programs depend on a workforce that feels safe reporting concerns. This policy provides the legal and operational framework for non-retaliation consistent with the False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. § 3730), applicable California whistleblower statutes (California Labor Code § 1102.5), and OIG Compliance Program Guidance.
## 3. Scope
This policy applies to all workforce members of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc., including Governing Body members, the Administrator, all employees, contractors, volunteers, and business associates. It applies to all reports of suspected violations, regardless of whether the report ultimately results in a finding of wrongdoing.
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. strictly prohibits retaliation of any form against any individual who, in good faith, reports a suspected compliance violation, cooperates with a compliance investigation, or refuses to participate in conduct they reasonably believe violates law or agency policy.
4.2 Retaliation is defined broadly and includes but is not limited to: termination, demotion, suspension, reduction in hours or pay, reassignment, harassment, intimidation, exclusion from meetings, negative performance evaluations influenced by the report, or any other adverse employment or contractual action.
4.3 The protection of this policy applies to all good-faith reporters regardless of whether the reported concern is ultimately substantiated.
4.4 An individual who knowingly makes a false or malicious report with the intent to harm another individual or the agency is not protected by this policy and is subject to disciplinary action per CO-CP-004.
4.5 The Governing Body shall be directly notified of any alleged retaliation against a reporter within 24 hours of the allegation. The Compliance Officer may not serve as the sole investigator if they are the subject of the retaliation allegation.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Whistleblower | Any individual who reports, in good faith, a suspected violation of law, regulation, or agency policy, including suspected fraud, waste, abuse, patient harm, privacy breach, or Code of Conduct violation. |
| Good Faith | A sincere belief, based on reasonable information available to the reporter at the time, that a violation may have occurred or is occurring. |
| Retaliation | Any adverse action taken against an individual because they made a good-faith compliance report, participated in an investigation, or refused to participate in unlawful conduct. |
| False Claims Act (FCA) qui tam provision | The federal law provision (31 U.S.C. § 3730(h)) that specifically prohibits retaliation against employees who take action in furtherance of a False Claims Act lawsuit. |
| California Labor Code § 1102.5 | California state law prohibiting employer retaliation against employees who disclose information about suspected legal violations to government agencies or internal compliance authorities. |

## 3. Scope

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes the standardized process for initiating, conducting, documenting, and closing compliance investigations at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. A structured, consistent investigation process is essential to ensure that reported concerns are addressed promptly, that evidence is properly preserved and assessed, that confidentiality is maintained, that corrective action is implemented where warranted, and that the agency fulfills its obligations under the False Claims Act, OIG guidance, and applicable federal and state law.
## 3. Scope
This policy applies to:
All compliance investigations initiated under the reporting mechanisms of CO-CP-006
All self-identified compliance concerns identified through internal auditing (CO-RA-002)
All retaliation investigations under CO-CP-005
All investigations directed by the Governing Body
All investigations of potential fraud, waste, or abuse under CO-FA-003
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 Every compliance report or self-identified concern shall be evaluated for investigation within 5 business days of receipt.
4.2 Formal investigations shall be initiated within 7 calendar days of receipt of a compliance report that, upon triage, warrants investigation.
4.3 All compliance investigations shall be documented in writing from initiation through closure.
4.4 All investigation records are confidential compliance documents. Access shall be restricted to the Compliance Officer, legal counsel (when engaged), and the Governing Body.
4.5 The Compliance Officer shall preserve investigation records under a litigation hold protocol if there is any indication that civil or criminal proceedings may arise from the subject matter.
4.6 The agency shall cooperate fully and promptly with any government investigation. All document requests or inquiries from government agencies shall be immediately routed to the Compliance Officer and legal counsel.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Investigation | A structured, documented inquiry into a reported or self-identified compliance concern to determine whether a violation occurred, its scope, its cause, and the appropriate remedial response. |
| Investigator | The Compliance Officer or designee appointed to conduct the investigation. Must be independent of the subject matter and individuals under review. |
| Investigation File | The complete documentation package for an investigation including the opening memo, evidence collected, interview notes, findings memo, and closure documentation. |
| Litigation Hold | A directive to preserve all records, communications, and data that may be relevant to anticipated or active legal proceedings. |
| Voluntary Disclosure | The proactive disclosure of a confirmed compliance violation to a federal or state regulatory agency. May mitigate penalties if conducted promptly and in good faith. |
| Overpayment | Any funds received from Medicare, Medicaid, or another payer to which the agency is not entitled. Must be reported and refunded within 60 days of identification per the False Claims Act. |

## 2. Purpose
This policy mandates initial and ongoing compliance training and education for all workforce members of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Effective compliance training is a foundational element of the Corporate Compliance Program (CO-CP-001) and is required by OIG Compliance Program Guidance for Home Health Agencies. Training ensures that all workforce members understand their individual compliance obligations, the agency's standards of conduct, applicable laws and regulations, reporting mechanisms, and the consequences of non-compliance.
## 3. Scope
This policy applies to all workforce members of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. including Governing Body members, the Administrator, all clinical and administrative employees, contractors, per diem staff, and volunteers. Role-specific training requirements are defined in Section 6.3.
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 All workforce members shall complete compliance orientation training within 14 calendar days of hire or contract commencement.
4.2 All workforce members shall complete annual compliance refresher training within 30 calendar days of the start of each calendar year.
4.3 Completion of required compliance training is a condition of continued employment or contract. Failure to complete training within the required timeframe shall result in escalation per Section 6.5.
4.4 Training shall be delivered in a manner and language accessible to all workforce members. The agency shall provide interpreter assistance for workforce members with limited English proficiency upon request.
4.5 All training completions shall be documented with the workforce member's name, role, training module, completion date, and assessment score (if applicable).

| Role-Specific Training | Compliance training modules targeted to specific workforce roles addressing regulatory requirements most relevant to that role's functions. |
| Competency Assessment | A post-training evaluation (quiz, skills demonstration, or attestation) confirming the workforce member's comprehension of training content. |

## 2. Purpose
This policy mandates the systematic monitoring of federal, state, and local regulatory changes affecting Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. and establishes a defined process for assessing the impact of regulatory changes on agency operations, policies, and training, and implementing required updates within defined timeframes. Regulatory non-compliance resulting from undetected regulatory changes constitutes a significant survey and financial risk. This policy ensures the agency maintains continuous compliance with 42 CFR Part 484, HIPAA, California home health regulations, OIG guidance, and all other applicable regulatory frameworks.
## 3. Scope
This policy applies to the Compliance Officer, Administrator, Director of Nursing, Revenue Cycle Director, HR Director, IT Director, and all department heads whose operational areas are subject to regulatory oversight.
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a systematic regulatory monitoring program that tracks all regulatory changes affecting the agency's operations from CMS, OIG, DHHS, California HCAI, California DHCS, and other applicable bodies.
4.2 All identified regulatory changes with an effective date within 90 days shall be triaged for impact within 10 business days of identification.
4.3 Policy revisions required by regulatory changes shall be completed and approved within 30 calendar days of the regulatory change effective date, or earlier if the change imposes immediate compliance obligations.
4.4 Workforce notification and training on regulatory changes shall be completed within 30 calendar days of policy revision.
4.5 The Compliance Officer shall present a regulatory change summary to the Governing Body at each quarterly meeting.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Regulatory Change | Any new, amended, or rescinded federal or state law, regulation, rule, guidance document, or official interpretive policy that affects the agency's operations, billing, clinical practices, or compliance obligations. |
| Impact Assessment | A documented analysis of how a regulatory change affects the agency's current policies, procedures, workflows, staff training requirements, and billing practices. |
| Final Rule | A CMS regulation published in the Federal Register that has completed the notice-and-comment rulemaking process and carries the force of law. |
| Proposed Rule | A regulation published for public comment that has not yet been finalized. Monitoring for proposed rules allows the agency to prepare for anticipated changes. |
| CMS Transmittal | A CMS-issued update to the State Operations Manual, Claims Processing Manual, or other operational guidance. |
| Monitoring Sources | The authoritative regulatory sources the Compliance Officer monitors on a defined frequency (see Appendix A). |

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes the internal compliance auditing program of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Internal auditing is a required element of the Corporate Compliance Program (CO-CP-001) and provides the agency's primary mechanism for proactively identifying compliance deficiencies, billing errors, documentation gaps, and operational non-conformities before they result in government action, survey deficiencies, or financial penalties. This policy defines the scope, methodology, frequency, reporting, and corrective action requirements for all internal compliance audits.
## 3. Scope
This policy applies to all audit activities conducted by or on behalf of the Compliance Officer including billing audits, clinical documentation audits, OASIS accuracy audits, HIPAA compliance audits, exclusion screening audits, and policy acknowledgment audits. It does not govern external audits by CMS, OIG, or other regulatory bodies (see CO-RA-003).
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain an active internal compliance auditing program with an annual risk-based audit work plan approved by the Compliance Committee.
4.2 All audit findings shall be documented in writing and reported to the Compliance Committee and Governing Body per the escalation standards of this policy.
4.3 All deficiencies identified through auditing shall be addressed through corrective action plans per policy QA-AE-003 within 14 calendar days of audit report finalization.
4.4 Corrective action effectiveness shall be validated through re-audit within 90 calendar days of CAP implementation.
4.5 All audit records are compliance documents and shall be maintained for a minimum of 7 years per policy CO-HP-007.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Compliance Audit | A structured, systematic review of records, processes, or transactions against a defined regulatory, policy, or billing standard to identify deviations, deficiencies, or risk areas. |
| Billing Audit | An audit focused on the accuracy, completeness, and regulatory conformity of claims submitted to Medicare or other payers. |
| Clinical Documentation Audit | An audit focused on the accuracy, timeliness, completeness, and authentication of clinical records. |
| OASIS Audit | An audit focused on the accuracy, completeness, and timeliness of OASIS data collection and transmission. |
| Triangulation Audit | An audit that cross-references physician orders, visit delivery documentation, and billed claims to verify alignment across all three sources. |
| Sample Size | The number of records selected for review in a given audit; must be statistically sufficient to support conclusions. |
| Extrapolation | The statistical process of projecting audit findings from a sample to the universe of transactions to estimate total financial exposure. |
| CAP (Corrective Action Plan) | A documented plan with specific actions, responsible parties, and deadlines to remediate identified audit deficiencies per QA-AE-003. |

### 6.2 Audit Execution — Standard Protocol

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.2.1 | Compliance Officer / Auditor | Prepare an Audit Initiation Notice (Appendix A) for each audit specifying: audit type, scope, time period reviewed, sample size, and methodology. | 5 business days before audit initiation. |
| 6.2.2 | Compliance Officer / Auditor | Conduct the audit per the defined scope and methodology. | 6.2.3 | Compliance Officer / Auditor | Document all findings contemporaneously using the Audit Findings Worksheet (Appendix B). | 6.2.4 | Compliance Officer | Finalize the Audit Report (Appendix C) within 14 calendar days of audit completion. The report must include: (a) executive summary; (b) methodology; (c) sample description; (d) findings by category; (e) deficiency rate; (f) financial exposure estimate (if applicable); (g) recommendations; (h) prior audit comparison (if repeat audit). | Within 14 calendar days of audit completion. |

### 8.2 Common Failure Points and Mitigation

| Failure Point | Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |

## 10. Training Requirements
The Compliance Officer and all individuals who conduct or assist with internal compliance audits shall complete training on audit methodology, documentation standards, and regulatory requirements applicable to each audit area prior to conducting their first audit.

Annual refresher training on the audit program shall be included in the compliance training curriculum per CO-CP-008.

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes the continuous survey readiness program of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Survey readiness is the state of operational preparedness in which the agency can demonstrate compliance with all applicable Conditions of Participation (42 CFR Part 484), state licensure requirements, and accreditation standards at any point in time — including unannounced CMS surveys. This policy ensures the agency maintains documented evidence of compliance, conducts mock surveys, prepares staff for surveyor interactions, and responds promptly and effectively to external audit and survey findings.
## 3. Scope
This policy applies to the Compliance Officer, Administrator, Director of Nursing, all department heads, all clinical and administrative staff who may interact with external surveyors, and all personnel responsible for maintaining documentation that may be reviewed during external audits or surveys.
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain continuous survey readiness at all times. Survey readiness is not an event-driven activity; it is a continuous operational standard.
4.2 The agency shall conduct at minimum one comprehensive mock survey annually and one focused mock survey per quarter, targeting areas identified as high-risk through internal audit findings, OIG Work Plan priorities, or prior survey deficiencies.
4.3 All staff shall be trained on surveyor interaction protocols including: (a) greeting and badge verification; (b) immediate notification of the Administrator and Compliance Officer; (c) answering only questions within their scope of knowledge; (d) providing only requested documentation; (e) never volunteering information beyond the specific question asked.
4.4 All external survey findings shall be treated with the same urgency as internal audit findings. Plans of Correction shall be developed, implemented, and monitored within CMS-required timeframes.
4.5 The agency shall maintain a Survey Readiness Binder (physical and/or electronic) accessible at the main office at all times containing all documents surveyors are likely to request.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Survey Readiness | The continuous state of documented compliance with all applicable regulatory standards enabling the agency to demonstrate compliance during any unannounced survey. |
| Mock Survey | An internally conducted or externally commissioned simulation of a CMS or state licensure survey using the applicable survey protocol (SOM Appendix B for CMS). |
| Plan of Correction (POC) | The agency's formal, written response to survey deficiencies that identifies root cause, corrective actions, responsible parties, completion dates, and ongoing monitoring. |
| Condition-Level Deficiency | A survey finding indicating that the agency fails to meet a Condition of Participation in its entirety — the most serious survey finding that may result in termination of Medicare certification. |
| Standard-Level Deficiency | A survey finding indicating non-compliance with a specific standard within a Condition of Participation — serious but less severe than Condition-level. |
| Survey Readiness Binder | A compiled set of documents maintained continuously for immediate surveyor access. |
| Immediate Jeopardy | A situation in which the agency's non-compliance has caused, or is likely to cause, serious injury, harm, impairment, or death to a patient. |

## 2. Purpose
This policy mandates continuous compliance with all applicable Medicare Conditions of Participation (CoP) for Home Health Agencies as codified in 42 CFR Part 484. Compliance with CoPs is the foundational legal requirement for Medicare certification and the agency's authority to provide and be reimbursed for home health services. This policy establishes the monitoring, accountability, and reporting framework that ensures every Condition and Standard is met at all times — not solely during survey periods.
## 3. Scope
This policy applies to the Governing Body, Administrator, Director of Nursing, Compliance Officer, all department heads, and all workforce members whose functions relate to any Condition of Participation.
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain continuous compliance with all Conditions of Participation in 42 CFR Part 484 as a condition of Medicare certification.
4.2 The agency shall maintain a CoP Compliance Matrix (Appendix A) mapping each Condition and Standard to the responsible party, the implementing agency policy, and the monitoring mechanism.
4.3 The Compliance Officer shall conduct an annual CoP compliance assessment and present findings to the Governing Body at the Q1 quarterly meeting.
4.4 Any identified gap in CoP compliance shall be treated as a High-priority finding requiring corrective action within 14 calendar days per QA-AE-003.
4.5 The Governing Body shall receive a CoP compliance status report at each quarterly meeting as part of the compliance report per CO-CP-001.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Conditions of Participation (CoP) | The federal regulatory requirements in 42 CFR Part 484 that a home health agency must meet to participate in the Medicare program. |
| Condition-Level Requirement | A broad regulatory requirement (e.g., 42 CFR § 484.60 — Care Planning). Failure to meet constitutes a Condition-level deficiency. |
| Standard-Level Requirement | A specific sub-requirement within a Condition. Failure to meet one Standard does not necessarily constitute a Condition-level deficiency. |
| CoP Compliance Matrix | A comprehensive document mapping each Condition and Standard to implementing policies, responsible parties, and monitoring mechanisms. |
| Annual CoP Assessment | A structured annual review verifying the agency's compliance status against every applicable CoP Condition and Standard. |

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes the processes for maintaining continuous compliance with all applicable California state home health licensure requirements under the California Health and Safety Code (§§ 1725–1742) and the regulations of the California Health Care Access and Information (HCAI) department. State licensure is a prerequisite for Medicare certification and agency operation.
## 3. Scope
This policy applies to the Administrator, Compliance Officer, HR Director, and all personnel responsible for maintaining documents, credentials, or operational standards required by state licensure.
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. (HCAI License No. 406412878) shall maintain current, valid California home health agency licensure at all times.
4.2 The Administrator shall ensure all state licensure renewal requirements are completed no fewer than 90 calendar days before license expiration.
4.3 The agency shall comply with all California state regulations governing home health agency operations including staffing requirements, supervision standards, patient rights, and reporting obligations.
4.4 Any state licensure deficiency, citation, or enforcement action shall be reported to the Governing Body within 24 hours and addressed per CO-RA-007.

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes the agency's response protocol for regulatory sanctions, citations, enforcement actions, civil money penalties, or other adverse regulatory actions imposed by CMS, OIG, state regulatory bodies, or accrediting organizations. Prompt, structured response to enforcement actions is essential to preserve the agency's Medicare certification, state licensure, and operational viability.
## 3. Scope
Applies to the Governing Body, Administrator, Compliance Officer, Director of Nursing, legal counsel, and all personnel involved in responding to enforcement actions.
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 Any regulatory sanction, citation, enforcement action, civil money penalty, or notice of termination received by Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall be treated as the highest-priority agency matter requiring immediate executive response.
4.2 The Governing Body shall be notified within 24 hours of receipt of any enforcement action.
4.3 Legal counsel shall be engaged within 48 hours for any enforcement action that may affect Medicare certification, state licensure, or impose financial penalties exceeding $10,000.
4.4 The Compliance Officer shall maintain an Enforcement Action Response Log (Appendix A) documenting all enforcement actions, response activities, and outcomes.
4.5 All enforcement action response documentation is privileged and confidential.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Enforcement Action | Any adverse regulatory action including survey deficiency citations, civil money penalties, payment suspension, termination proceedings, exclusion, or revocation of licensure. |
| Civil Money Penalty (CMP) | A monetary penalty imposed by CMS or OIG for regulatory violations. |
| Termination | The revocation of Medicare certification ending the agency's participation in the Medicare program. |
| Informal Dispute Resolution (IDR) | A process allowing agencies to dispute specific survey findings before the official POC deadline. |
| Immediate Jeopardy (IJ) | A situation in which non-compliance has caused or is likely to cause serious injury, harm, impairment, or death. Requires removal within 23 calendar days. |

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes safeguards to prevent violations of the federal Anti-Kickback Statute (AKS — 42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b(b)) and the Physician Self-Referral Law (Stark Law — 42 U.S.C. § 1395nn). Both statutes prohibit certain financial arrangements between healthcare providers that could influence patient referrals for services reimbursed by federal healthcare programs. Violations carry severe civil, criminal, and exclusion penalties.
## 3. Scope
Applies to all Governing Body members, the Administrator, all employees, contractors, vendors, referral sources, and any individual or entity that provides, receives, or facilitates patient referrals to or from Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall not offer, pay, solicit, or receive any remuneration — directly or indirectly, in cash or in kind — to induce or reward patient referrals for services covered by Medicare, Medicaid, or any federal healthcare program.
4.2 The agency shall not enter into any financial arrangement with a referring physician or physician group that does not satisfy a recognized AKS safe harbor or Stark Law exception.
4.3 All referral source relationships shall be reviewed by the Compliance Officer for AKS/Stark compliance prior to formalization and at least annually thereafter.
4.4 All marketing and referral development activities shall comply with this policy and shall not involve: (a) payments or gifts to referral sources in exchange for referrals; (b) free or below-market-value services to referral sources; (c) entertainment or meals exceeding $25 per occurrence or $50 per year per referral source individual; (d) any arrangement where compensation varies based on the volume or value of referrals.
4.5 Any suspected AKS or Stark violation shall be reported immediately through CO-CP-006 and investigated per CO-CP-007.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Anti-Kickback Statute (AKS) | Federal criminal statute (42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7b(b)) prohibiting the knowing and willful offer, payment, solicitation, or receipt of remuneration to induce or reward referrals for items or services reimbursed by federal healthcare programs. |
| Stark Law | Federal physician self-referral law (42 U.S.C. § 1395nn) prohibiting a physician from referring Medicare patients for designated health services to an entity with which the physician (or immediate family member) has a financial relationship, unless an exception applies. |
| Safe Harbor | Regulatory provisions under the AKS that protect certain payment and business practices from prosecution if all conditions of the safe harbor are met. |
| Stark Law Exception | Specific regulatory exceptions under the Stark Law that permit financial relationships between physicians and entities if all conditions are satisfied. |
| Remuneration | Anything of value — cash, gifts, entertainment, free or below-market services, employment, or any other direct or indirect benefit. |
| Referral Source | Any individual, physician, physician group, hospital, skilled nursing facility, or other entity that refers or directs patients to Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. |
| Fair Market Value (FMV) | The value in arm's-length transactions, consistent with the general market value of the subject transaction. |

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes the agency's education and prevention program for compliance with the Federal False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. §§ 3729–3733), the California False Claims Act (California Government Code §§ 12650–12656), and the 60-day overpayment refund obligation under 42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7k(d). ## 3. Scope
Applies to all workforce members, with particular emphasis on clinical staff who document services, billing staff who submit claims, and leadership who oversee operations.
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall not knowingly present, or cause to be presented, a false or fraudulent claim for payment to Medicare, Medicaid, or any other federal or state healthcare program.
4.2 The agency shall not knowingly make, use, or cause to be made or used a false record or statement material to a false or fraudulent claim.
4.3 The agency shall identify and refund overpayments to CMS within 60 calendar days of identification, as required by 42 U.S.C. § 1320a-7k(d).

## 5. Definitions

| Knowingly | Having actual knowledge, acting in deliberate ignorance, or acting in reckless disregard of the truth. Specific intent to defraud is NOT required. |
| Qui Tam Provision | The FCA provision allowing private citizens (relators) to file lawsuits on behalf of the government and receive a portion of any recovery. |
| Reverse False Claim | Retention of an overpayment beyond the 60-day identification period constitutes a "reverse" false claim. |
| 60-Day Rule | The obligation to report and refund identified overpayments to CMS within 60 calendar days of identification. |
| Overpayment | Any funds received from a federal healthcare program to which the agency was not entitled. |

## 2. Purpose
Establishes the agency's comprehensive HIPAA Privacy Program in compliance with the HIPAA Privacy Rule (45 CFR Parts 160 and 164, Subparts A and E). The Privacy Program ensures the confidentiality of protected health information (PHI) while enabling appropriate use and disclosure for treatment, payment, and healthcare operations.
## 3. Scope
All workforce members, business associates, vendors, and any individual who creates, receives, maintains, or transmits PHI on behalf of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 The agency shall maintain a HIPAA Privacy Program that protects all PHI in any form (oral, written, electronic).
4.2 The Compliance Officer shall serve as the Privacy Officer (or designate a qualified individual).
4.3 The agency shall maintain a current Notice of Privacy Practices (NPP) and provide it to all patients at the start of care.
4.4 PHI shall be used and disclosed only as permitted by the HIPAA Privacy Rule, with the minimum necessary standard applied to all uses and disclosures.
4.5 Patient rights under HIPAA shall be honored including: access, amendment, accounting of disclosures, restrictions, and confidential communications.
4.6 All workforce members shall receive HIPAA privacy training at orientation and annually.

# CO-DC-001 — Assessment Audit Trail and Data Integrity
## Policy Header

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Policy ID | CO-DC-001 |
| Policy Title | Assessment Audit Trail and Data Integrity |
| Classification Tier | REQUIRED |
| Policy Owner/Steward | Compliance Officer |
| Status | ACTIVE |

## 2. Purpose
Requires all assessment entries, modifications, and coding decisions to be captured in a timestamped, version-controlled audit trail to support data integrity, regulatory compliance, and fraud prevention.
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 All clinical assessment entries and OASIS coding decisions shall be captured in a system-generated, immutable audit trail.
4.2 Every modification to an assessment after initial entry shall be documented with: original value, modified value, modifying user, timestamp, and reason for change.
4.3 No assessment data shall be deleted from the audit trail. Corrections shall follow CO-DC-003 standards.
4.4 The Compliance Officer shall have access to all audit trails for compliance monitoring purposes.
## 6. Procedures

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.1 | IT Director | Configure EHR system to capture and retain a complete audit trail for all assessment data including: user ID, date/time of entry, date/time of modification, original values, modified values, and reason for change. | Before agency operation; maintained continuously. |
| 6.2 | IT Director | Ensure audit trail data is immutable — no user (including system administrators) may alter or delete audit trail entries. | Continuously. |
| 6.3 | Compliance Officer | Review audit trail data as part of documentation compliance audits per CO-DC-002. | Per audit schedule. |
| 6.4 | Compliance Officer | Investigate any audit trail anomalies (e.g., patterns of late modifications, unauthorized access, systematic coding changes) per CO-CP-007. | Within 7 calendar days of identification. |

# CO-DC-003 — Late Entry, Correction & Amendment Standards
## Policy Header

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Policy ID | CO-DC-003 |
| Policy Title | Late Entry, Correction & Amendment Standards |
| Classification Tier | REQUIRED |
| Policy Owner/Steward | Compliance Officer |
| Status | ACTIVE |

## 2. Purpose
Defines standards for late entries, error corrections, and amendments to clinical and administrative records to maintain documentation integrity and legal defensibility.
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 Original documentation shall never be deleted, obliterated, or rendered unreadable.
4.2 Late entries shall be clearly labeled as "LATE ENTRY" with the current date/time and the date of the event being documented.
4.3 Corrections shall use single-line strikethrough (for paper) or system correction function (for electronic), with the original entry preserved, the correction clearly marked, the correcting author identified, and the reason for correction documented.
4.4 Amendments shall be clearly labeled, reference the original entry, and include the amending author and date.
4.5 No correction or amendment shall be made under pressure from billing, coding, or administrative personnel to alter clinical findings.
## 6. Procedures
### 6.1 Late Entries

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.1.1 | Documenting clinician | Enter the late entry using the current date and time. Label "LATE ENTRY — documenting events of [original date]." | As soon as practicable; no later than end of next business day. |
| 6.1.2 | Documenting clinician | Document the reason for the late entry. | With the late entry. |
| 6.1.3 | Supervisor | Review late entries during supervisory review. Excessive late entries by a clinician shall be addressed through counseling and, if persistent, progressive discipline per HR-ER-002. | During supervisory review. |

### 6.2 Corrections

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.2.1 | Correcting author | Electronic: use the EHR correction function. Original entry must remain visible. Paper: single-line strikethrough; write "error," initial, date. | As soon as error is identified. |
| 6.2.2 | Correcting author | Document the reason for correction. | With the correction. |
| 6.2.3 | Compliance Officer | Monitor correction patterns through audit trail review per CO-DC-001. Patterns of corrections that change clinical acuity, coding, or billing shall be investigated per CO-CP-007. | Per audit schedule. |

### 6.3 Amendments

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.3.1 | Amending author | Label amendment clearly: "AMENDMENT to entry dated [date]." Document the additional or clarifying information. Sign and date. | As needed. |
| 6.3.2 | Amending author | Never delete or overwrite the original entry. | N/A — prohibition. |

# CO-DC-004 — Clinical, Documentation, and Billing Alignment Audit
## Policy Header

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Policy ID | CO-DC-004 |
| Policy Title | Clinical, Documentation, and Billing Alignment Audit |
| Classification Tier | REQUIRED |
| Policy Owner/Steward | Compliance Officer |
| Status | ACTIVE |

## 2. Purpose
Establishes a triangulation audit process verifying alignment between physician orders, visit delivery documentation, and billed claims. Misalignment among these three sources is a primary indicator of billing fraud, documentation falsification, or systemic operational failure.
## 4. Policy Statement
4.1 The agency shall conduct quarterly triangulation audits comparing: (a) physician orders (services ordered); (b) clinical visit documentation (services documented as delivered); (c) billed claims (services billed to payer).
4.2 All three sources must be aligned. Any mismatch constitutes a deficiency requiring investigation.
4.3 Variance types include: (a) services billed but not documented; (b) services documented but not ordered; (c) services ordered but not delivered; (d) coding discrepancies between documentation and billing.
## 6. Procedures

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.1 | Compliance Officer | Conduct quarterly triangulation audits per CO-RA-002 standards. Minimum sample: 20 episodes per quarter. | Quarterly. |
| 6.2 | Auditor | For each episode, verify: (a) every billed service has corresponding visit documentation; (b) every documented visit has a corresponding physician order; (c) billed codes match documented services; (d) visit dates on claims match visit dates in documentation. | During audit. |
| 6.3 | Auditor | Document all variances using the Triangulation Audit Worksheet (Appendix A). Classify each variance by type and severity. | During audit. |
| 6.4 | Compliance Officer | For Critical variances (services billed but not documented — potential false claim): initiate investigation per CO-CP-007. For Major variances: develop CAP per QA-AE-003. For Minor variances: address through education. | Critical: 7 days. Major: 14 days. Minor: 30 days. |
| 6.5 | Compliance Officer | Report triangulation audit results to Compliance Committee and Governing Body. | Quarterly. |

### Appendix A — Triangulation Audit Worksheet

| Episode # | Patient MRN | Certification Period | Service Date | Service Type | Physician Order Present? | Visit Documentation Present? | Claim Submitted? | Codes Match? | Dates Match? | Variance Type | Severity |

| 1 |  |  |  |  | ☐ Yes ☐ No | ☐ Yes ☐ No | ☐ Yes ☐ No | ☐ Yes ☐ No | ☐ Yes ☐ No | ☐ None ☐ Billed/Not Doc'd ☐ Doc'd/Not Ordered ☐ Ordered/Not Delivered ☐ Code Mismatch | ☐ Critical ☐ Major ☐ Minor |

| Type | Definition | Severity |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Billed but Not Documented | Claim submitted for service with no corresponding visit note | Critical |
| Documented but Not Ordered | Visit note exists but no physician order authorizes the service | Major |
| Ordered but Not Delivered | Physician order exists but no visit documentation or claim | Major |
| Code Mismatch | Documentation supports different code than billed | Major |
| Date Mismatch | Claim date differs from visit documentation date | Major |
