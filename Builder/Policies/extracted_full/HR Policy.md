## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes standardized requirements for the recruitment, screening, and hiring of all employees and contracted staff at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. The policy ensures that only qualified, eligible, and competent individuals are employed by the agency in compliance with 42 CFR § 484.105 (organization and administration), 42 CFR § 484.80 (home health aide qualifications), applicable California home health licensing requirements, OIG Compliance Program Guidance for Home Health Agencies, and all federal and state equal employment opportunity laws. A structured, documented, non-discriminatory hiring process protects the agency from regulatory liability, ensures patient safety, and promotes workforce quality.
## 3. Scope
This policy applies to:
The HR Director and all HR department staff involved in recruitment and hiring
The Administrator
The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager (for clinical position hiring decisions)
All hiring managers and supervisors who participate in candidate evaluation or selection
All contracted staffing agencies providing personnel to Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.
All applicants for employment or contracted engagement with the agency
This policy does not apply to: Governing Body member appointments, which are governed by policy GV-GB-001 and the agency's bylaws or operating agreement. Volunteer recruitment is governed by HR-WM-006.
## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a standardized, documented recruitment and hiring process that ensures all candidates are evaluated based on objective, job-related criteria as defined in current job descriptions (HR-JD series) and applicable regulatory requirements.
4.2 All hiring decisions shall comply with federal and state equal employment opportunity laws, including Title VII of the Civil Rights Act, the Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA), the Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA), and the California Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA). No applicant shall be discriminated against based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, disability, genetic information, sexual orientation, gender identity, marital status, veteran status, or any other characteristic protected by law.
4.3 No individual shall be employed or contracted by the agency without completion of all pre-employment screening requirements defined in this policy, including but not limited to: identity verification, licensure/certification verification (HR-TA-004), criminal background check (HR-TA-002), OIG/SAM exclusion screening (HR-TA-003), and reference verification.
4.4 All positions shall have a current, approved job description (HR-TA-006; HR-JD series) on file prior to posting or recruitment for that position. No position shall be filled without a job description that defines minimum qualifications, essential functions, and regulatory requirements.
4.5 The agency shall not employ or contract with any individual who is excluded from participation in any federal healthcare program, as verified through OIG LEIE and SAM database screening per policy HR-TA-003.
4.6 All recruitment and hiring documentation shall be maintained in accordance with record retention policy CO-HP-007, with personnel file standards per HR-WM-005 and HR-WM-007.
4.7 The agency shall maintain a log of all recruitment activity sufficient to demonstrate non-discriminatory hiring practices in the event of audit, complaint, or litigation.
4.8 Only the most current approved version of this policy shall be considered valid. Superseded versions must not be used for any operational or compliance purpose.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Applicant | Any individual who submits a formal application for employment or contracted engagement with the agency through an authorized recruitment channel. |
| Pre-Employment Screening | The set of verifications, checks, and assessments required before an individual may begin work, including background checks, exclusion screening, licensure verification, reference checks, health screening, and I-9 verification. |
| Conditional Offer | A written offer of employment contingent upon successful completion of all pre-employment screening requirements. An individual may not perform any work duties under a conditional offer. |
| Essential Functions | The fundamental job duties of a position that an individual must be able to perform, with or without reasonable accommodation, as defined in the position's job description. |
| Hiring Manager | The supervisor or department head responsible for evaluating candidates and making the hiring recommendation for a specific position. |
| I-9 Verification | The Employment Eligibility Verification process required by U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) to confirm an individual's identity and authorization to work in the United States. |
| Reasonable Accommodation | A modification or adjustment to a job, work environment, or hiring process that enables a qualified individual with a disability to participate in the application process or perform essential functions, provided such accommodation does not impose an undue hardship on the agency. |
| Disqualifying Result | A pre-employment screening outcome that renders the candidate ineligible for employment, including but not limited to: federal program exclusion, invalid or expired licensure for a licensed position, or criminal history incompatible with position duties and patient safety per applicable law. |

## 6. Procedures
### 6.1 Position Authorization and Job Description Verification

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.1.1 | Hiring Manager | Submit a written Position Authorization Request (Appendix A) to the HR Director identifying the position title, justification for the hire (new position, replacement, or expansion), reporting relationship, FTE status, and budget impact. | Prior to any recruitment activity. |
| 6.1.2 | HR Director | Verify that a current, approved job description exists for the position per HR-TA-006 and HR-JD series. If no current job description exists or the existing description is more than 24 months past its last review, coordinate with the hiring manager and applicable discipline lead to develop or update the job description before proceeding. | Within 3 business days of receiving the authorization request. |
| 6.1.3 | Administrator | Review and approve or deny the Position Authorization Request. For all clinical positions, the Director of Nursing must co-approve. Document approval with signature and date on the authorization form. | Within 5 business days of submission. |
| 6.1.4 | HR Director | Upon approval, create a recruitment file for the position containing: (a) approved Position Authorization Request; (b) current job description; (c) posting plan; (d) blank Structured Interview Evaluation Forms; (e) Application Screening Log. Assign a unique Recruitment Tracking Number. | Within 2 business days of Administrator approval. |

### 6.2 Job Posting and Candidate Sourcing

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.2.1 | HR Director | Develop a job posting that accurately reflects the approved job description including: (a) position title and Recruitment Tracking Number; (b) essential functions summary; (c) minimum qualifications and preferred qualifications; (d) required licensure/certifications; (e) physical requirements; (f) EEO statement; (g) application instructions including required documents (resume, application, licensure copy). | Within 3 business days of position authorization approval. |
| 6.2.2 | HR Director | Post the position through approved recruitment channels which may include: agency website, job boards (Indeed, LinkedIn, etc.), professional associations, educational institutions, California EDD, social media (per IT-UP-003), and contracted staffing agencies. Internal candidates shall be notified of open positions simultaneously with external posting unless the position is designated internal-only by the Administrator. | Posting duration: minimum 7 calendar days for non-urgent positions. Urgent clinical vacancies (defined as positions where a vacancy creates an immediate patient care coverage gap) may be expedited to a 3-day minimum with documented Administrator approval. |
| 6.2.3 | HR Director | Screen all applications against minimum qualifications stated in the job description. Document screening results for every applicant on the Application Screening Log (Appendix B), indicating whether the applicant meets minimum qualifications and the reason for any disqualification. Applications that do not meet minimum qualifications shall be retained in the recruitment file per CO-HP-007. | Within 5 business days of posting close date, or on a rolling basis for positions approved for rolling review. |

### 6.3 Interview and Evaluation Process

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.3.1 | HR Director / Hiring Manager | Select qualified candidates for interview from the screened applicant pool. Interview panels shall consist of a minimum of 2 evaluators. For clinical positions, the panel must include at least one licensed clinician of the same or supervising discipline. All interviewers shall be provided the Structured Interview Evaluation Form (Appendix C) and instructed on prohibited inquiries prior to interviewing. | Interviews scheduled within 7 business days of screening completion. |
| 6.3.2 | Interview Panel | Conduct interviews using the Structured Interview Evaluation Form (Appendix C), which contains standardized, job-related questions aligned to the position's essential functions, competency requirements, and behavioral indicators. All candidates for the same position must be asked the same core questions. Prohibited inquiries include questions about age, marital/family status, pregnancy, disability (except ability to perform essential functions with or without accommodation), religion, national origin, arrest record (unless permitted by applicable California law), or other protected characteristics unless directly related to a bona fide occupational qualification (BFOQ). | Per scheduled interview dates. |
| 6.3.3 | Each Interviewer | Complete the Structured Interview Evaluation Form (Appendix C) independently immediately following each interview. Each evaluator assigns scores independently before any group discussion. Scores must be based on documented, job-related criteria only. | Completed same day as interview. |
| 6.3.4 | Hiring Manager | Compile evaluation scores and submit a written Hiring Recommendation (Appendix D) to the HR Director identifying the recommended candidate, composite evaluation scores, and written justification for the selection. For clinical positions, the Director of Nursing must co-sign the recommendation. If the recommended candidate is not the highest-scoring candidate, a written justification of the deviation is required. | Within 3 business days of final interview. |
| 6.3.5 | HR Director | Review the hiring recommendation for completeness, consistency with evaluation scores, EEO compliance, and budget authorization. Approve or return for additional justification. | Within 2 business days of receiving the recommendation. |

### 6.4 Conditional Offer and Pre-Employment Screening

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.4.1 | HR Director | Issue a Conditional Offer Letter (Appendix E) to the selected candidate. The letter must clearly state that: (a) the offer is contingent upon successful completion of all pre-employment screening requirements; (b) the candidate may not perform any work duties until all screenings are completed and cleared; (c) a disqualifying result will result in rescission of the offer. | Within 2 business days of approved hiring recommendation. |
| 6.4.2 | Candidate | Sign and return the Conditional Offer Letter acknowledging acceptance and providing written consent for all pre-employment screening activities. | Within 5 calendar days of offer issuance. If not returned within 5 days, the HR Director shall follow up; if not returned within 10 calendar days, the offer is automatically rescinded. |
| 6.4.3 | HR Director | Initiate and coordinate completion of all required pre-employment screening: (a) Criminal background check per HR-TA-002; (b) OIG LEIE and SAM exclusion screening per HR-TA-003; (c) Professional licensure and certification verification per HR-TA-004; (d) Employment reference verification — minimum 2 professional references contacted and documented; (e) I-9 Employment Eligibility Verification — completed on or before the first day of employment per USCIS requirements; (f) Pre-employment health screening and immunization verification per HR-WM-003; (g) Drug screening per HR-ER-005 (if applicable under current agency policy); (h) Verification of education credentials as required by the job description; (i) Driving record check for any position requiring driving for agency business per RM-SS-003. | All screening initiated within 2 business days of signed conditional offer; all results received and documented prior to start date. |
| 6.4.4 | HR Director | Document all screening results on the Pre-Employment Screening Checklist (Appendix F). Every screening item must show: (a) date initiated; (b) date result received; (c) result (pass/fail/pending); (d) reviewer initials. All results must be verified and documented before the candidate is cleared to begin work. No exceptions. | Completed and signed off before employee's first day of work. |
| 6.4.5 | HR Director | If any screening result is disqualifying: (a) notify the candidate in writing per applicable fair chance/ban-the-box requirements (California Fair Chance Act); (b) provide required individualized assessment for criminal history results, considering the nature of the offense, time elapsed, and nature of the job; (c) allow the candidate the required response period before making a final adverse decision; (d) document the decision and rationale in the recruitment file. | Notification within 2 business days of disqualifying result; final decision after legally required response period. |
| 6.4.6 | HR Director | For candidates not selected, send a professional declination notification. Retain all application materials and screening records in the recruitment file per CO-HP-007 (minimum 3 years or as required by applicable state law, whichever is longer). | Declination sent within 5 business days of position fill. |

### 6.5 Final Offer and Onboarding Handoff

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.5.1 | HR Director | Upon successful completion of all pre-employment screening, issue a Final Offer Confirmation (Appendix G) confirming: (a) start date; (b) position title; (c) compensation and benefits summary; (d) reporting relationship; (e) orientation schedule per HR-TA-005; (f) documents to bring on Day 1 (I-9 documents, licensure originals, etc.). | Within 2 business days of final screening clearance. |
| 6.5.2 | HR Director | Establish the employee's personnel file per HR-WM-005 and HR-WM-007 containing all recruitment documentation, screening results, signed offer letters, job description acknowledgment, and any pre-employment correspondence. | Completed on or before employee's first day. |
| 6.5.3 | HR Director | Notify the hiring manager and, for clinical staff, the Director of Nursing of the confirmed start date, orientation schedule, and any special onboarding requirements. Provide the hiring manager with a copy of the new hire's job description for Day 1 review. | At least 3 business days prior to start date. |
| 6.5.4 | HR Director | Close the recruitment file by completing the Recruitment Closure Summary (Appendix H), documenting: (a) position filled; (b) total applicants; (c) total interviewed; (d) candidate selected; (e) total days to fill; (f) recruitment source that produced the hire. File closed recruitment records per CO-HP-007. | Within 5 business days of confirmed start date. |

### 6.6 Staffing Agency / Contract Staff Hiring

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.6.1 | HR Director | When using contracted staffing agencies, verify that the agency has a current, signed contract that requires the staffing agency to perform all pre-employment screening equivalent to this policy's requirements and to provide documentation of the same. A Business Associate Agreement (BAA) must be in place per CO-HP-005 if the contract staff will access PHI. | Prior to any contract staff assignment. |
| 6.6.2 | HR Director | Before any contract staff member begins work, obtain and verify: (a) written confirmation of completed background check; (b) OIG/SAM screening clearance; (c) current licensure/certification verification; (d) competency documentation appropriate to the assignment. Document on the Contract Staff Verification Form (Appendix I). | Before the contract staff member's first shift. |
| 6.6.3 | HR Director | Independently verify OIG/SAM exclusion status for all contract staff per HR-TA-003, regardless of staffing agency representations. | Before first shift and monthly thereafter. |

### 6.7 Escalation and Exception Handling

| Condition | Escalation Path | Corrective Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Critical clinical vacancy unfilled for more than 30 calendar days | Hiring Manager escalates to Administrator in writing. | Administrator directs expanded recruitment strategy (increased posting channels, sign-on incentive consideration, staffing agency engagement). Status reported to HR Director weekly until filled. | Expanded strategy initiated within 5 business days of escalation. |
| Pre-employment screening reveals OIG/SAM exclusion | HR Director notifies Compliance Officer immediately. | Conditional offer rescinded immediately. Compliance Officer documents per CO-FA-003. If individual was inadvertently employed, immediate separation and self-disclosure per OIG guidance. | Immediate upon discovery. |
| Candidate alleges discrimination in the hiring process | HR Director notifies Administrator and legal counsel. | Investigation per HR-ER-004 and applicable agency grievance process. All recruitment documentation preserved. | Investigation initiated within 7 calendar days. |
| Hiring manager attempts to bypass pre-employment screening | HR Director denies clearance and escalates to Administrator. | Administrator issues written directive reinforcing policy. If pattern recurs, disciplinary action per HR-ER-002. | Immediate escalation; corrective action within 7 business days. |

## 8. Compliance Monitoring
### 8.1 How Compliance Is Measured

| Compliance Indicator | Measurement Method | Acceptable Standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All positions have current job descriptions on file prior to recruitment | Audit of recruitment files for job description presence and currency | 100% compliance. |
| All pre-employment screening items completed and documented before first day | Audit of personnel files against Appendix F checklist | 100% compliance; zero employees cleared without complete screening. |
| OIG/SAM screening completed for all new hires prior to start | Cross-reference hire dates against OIG/SAM screening dates in personnel files | 100% compliance. |
| Structured interview forms used for all interviewed candidates | Audit of recruitment files for completed Appendix C forms | 100% of interviews documented on standardized form. |
| EEO compliance in job postings and interview process | Review of postings for EEO language; audit of interview forms for prohibited inquiries | 100% of postings include EEO statement; zero prohibited inquiries documented. |
| Time-to-fill tracked and reported | Recruitment Closure Summary (Appendix H) data | Average time-to-fill ≤ 45 calendar days for non-clinical; ≤ 30 days for clinical. |
| Applicant screening log maintained for all recruitments | Audit of recruitment files | 100% of recruitment files contain completed Appendix B. |

### 8.2 Surveyor Expectations
CMS surveyors and state licensing surveyors will specifically verify:
Personnel files contain evidence of pre-employment screening. Surveyors will select employee files and look for background check results, licensure verification, OIG/SAM screening, and reference checks completed prior to or on the date of hire.
Qualified staff. Surveyors will verify that employees meet the minimum qualifications defined in their job descriptions and required by regulation (e.g., RN licensure, HHA certification, therapist credentials).
No excluded individuals. Surveyors and OIG auditors will verify that no employee or contractor appears on the OIG LEIE or SAM exclusion databases.
Non-discriminatory practices. EEOC or state DFEH investigators, if triggered by a complaint, will request recruitment records including job postings, interview evaluations, and hiring rationale.
### 8.3 Common Failure Points

| Failure Point | Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employee begins work before background check results are received | Survey finding; potential patient safety risk; liability exposure if incident occurs | Enforce zero-exception policy: no work begins until Appendix F is complete and signed. |
| OIG/SAM screening not completed before start date | CMS may impose civil monetary penalties; potential program exclusion for the agency | Automate OIG/SAM screening as part of pre-employment checklist; HR Director verifies before clearing start date. |
| Interview questions include prohibited inquiries | EEO complaint; DFEH investigation; litigation risk | Use standardized Appendix C form for all interviews; train all interviewers annually on prohibited inquiries. |
| No job description on file for a filled position | Surveyor may cite inability to verify qualifications match requirements | Enforce Section 6.1.2: no recruitment proceeds without current job description. |
| Reference checks not documented | Surveyor finding for incomplete personnel file | Require minimum 2 documented reference contacts with notes per Appendix F before clearing. |
| Contract staff screening documentation not verified independently | Agency liable for excluded or unqualified contract staff | Enforce Section 6.6: independent OIG/SAM verification regardless of staffing agency attestations. |

## 10. Training Requirements
10.1 All HR staff involved in recruitment and hiring shall receive training on this policy within 14 calendar days of hire or assignment to recruitment duties. Training shall cover: (a) the complete recruitment process from authorization through onboarding handoff; (b) pre-employment screening requirements and zero-exception policy; (c) EEO compliance and prohibited interview inquiries; (d) California Fair Chance Act individualized assessment requirements; (e) OIG/SAM screening procedures; (f) documentation standards and use of all appendix forms.
10.2 All hiring managers and supervisors who participate in candidate evaluation shall receive training on: (a) use of the Structured Interview Evaluation Form (Appendix C); (b) prohibited interview inquiries; (c) EEO obligations; (d) scoring standards and documentation requirements. Training must be completed before the individual participates in any interview. Refresher training shall be provided annually.
10.3 All personnel within scope of this policy shall sign the Policy Acknowledgment Form (Appendix J) within 14 calendar days of the policy effective date, any revision, or new assignment to a role within scope.
10.4 The HR Director shall maintain a training completion log and report any non-compliance to the Administrator within 7 calendar days of the acknowledgment/training deadline.

### Appendix C — Structured Interview Evaluation Form

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes mandatory requirements for conducting criminal background checks on all prospective and current employees, contractors, and volunteers of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Criminal background screening is essential to: (a) protect the safety and welfare of patients receiving home health services in their residences; (b) comply with California Health and Safety Code requirements for home health agency personnel; (c) satisfy CMS Conditions of Participation requirements for employing qualified and suitable staff; (d) fulfill the agency's duty of care and fiduciary obligations; and (e) mitigate risk of liability arising from negligent hiring. This policy ensures that background screening is conducted consistently, documented completely, and applied in compliance with the California Fair Chance Act and all applicable federal and state fair employment laws.
## 3. Scope
This policy applies to:
All applicants for employment (full-time, part-time, per diem) at any stage after conditional offer
All contracted/staffing agency personnel assigned to work at or on behalf of the agency
All current employees subject to periodic rescreening as defined in this policy
All volunteers per HR-WM-006
The HR Director and all personnel responsible for initiating, reviewing, or acting upon background check results
This policy does not apply to: Governing Body members (screened per GV-GB-001 and HR-TA-003 for exclusion only, unless a Governing Body member also serves in an employment capacity). OIG/SAM exclusion screening is governed separately by HR-TA-003 but is coordinated with this policy.
## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall conduct a criminal background check on every individual prior to their performing any work duties for the agency, including orientation, patient contact, or access to patient information.
4.2 Background checks shall be conducted through an agency-approved, FCRA-compliant (Fair Credit Reporting Act) background screening vendor and shall include, at minimum: (a) Social Security Number trace; (b) multi-state criminal records search; (c) county criminal records search for all counties of residence within the past 7 years; (d) National Sex Offender Registry search; (e) California Department of Justice (DOJ) LiveScan fingerprint check where required by California law.
4.3 The agency shall not make any adverse employment decision based solely on a criminal history finding without first conducting an individualized assessment per the California Fair Chance Act (AB 1008) and EEOC enforcement guidance, considering: (a) the nature and gravity of the offense; (b) the time elapsed since the offense or completion of sentence; (c) the nature of the job sought and its relationship to the offense.
4.4 Convictions that are automatically disqualifying regardless of individualized assessment include: (a) any offense requiring sex offender registration; (b) any conviction for abuse, neglect, or exploitation of a minor, elderly, or dependent adult; (c) any conviction for healthcare fraud; (d) any conviction currently resulting in exclusion from federal healthcare program participation.
4.5 All current employees shall be subject to periodic rescreening every 3 years or immediately upon credible report of criminal conduct, whichever occurs first.
4.6 All background check records shall be maintained in a separate, secured confidential file — not in the general personnel file — accessible only to the HR Director, Administrator, and Compliance Officer, per CO-HP-007 and applicable privacy laws.
4.7 Only the most current approved version of this policy shall be considered valid.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| FCRA | Fair Credit Reporting Act — federal law governing the collection, use, and disclosure of consumer information including background checks conducted by third-party vendors. |
| Individualized Assessment | The process required by the California Fair Chance Act and EEOC guidance in which the agency evaluates a criminal history finding in context before making an adverse employment decision, considering the nature of the offense, time elapsed, and relevance to the job. |
| Adverse Action | A decision not to hire, promote, or continue employment of an individual based in whole or in part on information obtained through a background check. |
| Pre-Adverse Action Notice | Written notice required by the FCRA before taking adverse action based on a background check, providing the individual with a copy of the report and a summary of their rights. |
| LiveScan | California's electronic fingerprint submission system used for state and federal criminal history checks through the California Department of Justice (DOJ) and FBI. |
| Automatic Disqualifier | A criminal conviction category that disqualifies an individual from employment regardless of individualized assessment, based on the nature of the offense and its direct relevance to patient safety. |
| Rescreening | A subsequent background check conducted on a current employee at a defined interval or upon triggering event. |

## 6. Procedures
### 6.1 Pre-Employment Background Check Initiation

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.1.1 | HR Director | Upon receipt of the signed Conditional Offer Letter (HR-TA-001, Appendix E) which includes consent to background screening, initiate the background check through the agency's approved FCRA-compliant vendor. | Within 2 business days of receiving signed conditional offer. |
| 6.1.2 | HR Director | For positions requiring California DOJ LiveScan fingerprinting (per California Health and Safety Code), schedule the applicant for LiveScan and provide the Request for LiveScan Service form with the agency's ORI number. | Scheduled within 3 business days of conditional offer acceptance. |
| 6.1.3 | HR Director | Document the initiation of the background check on the Pre-Employment Screening Checklist (HR-TA-001, Appendix F) and on the Background Check Tracking Log (Appendix A of this policy). | Same day as initiation. |

### 6.2 Background Check Scope

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.2.1 | HR Director / Vendor | Ensure the background check includes all of the following components: (a) Social Security Number verification and trace; (b) Multi-state criminal records search (all 50 states); (c) County-level criminal records search for all counties of residence in the past 7 years; (d) National Sex Offender Registry (NSOR) search; (e) California DOJ LiveScan (where required by state law); (f) Federal criminal records search; (g) Driving record / motor vehicle report (for positions requiring driving per RM-SS-003). | All components initiated simultaneously. |
| 6.2.2 | HR Director | If the applicant has resided outside California within the past 7 years, ensure county-level searches are conducted in all applicable jurisdictions. If the applicant has resided outside the United States, document the limitation and conduct all available domestic checks. | Concurrent with initial background check order. |

### 6.3 Review of Background Check Results

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.3.1 | HR Director | Upon receipt of background check results, review for: (a) criminal convictions (felony or misdemeanor); (b) pending charges; (c) sex offender registry matches; (d) discrepancies between applicant-provided information and screening results. Document the review on the Background Check Review and Determination Form (Appendix B). | Within 2 business days of receiving results. |
| 6.3.2 | HR Director | If the background check is CLEAR (no convictions, no pending charges, no registry matches, no disqualifying discrepancies): mark the Pre-Employment Screening Checklist as PASS and proceed with remaining screening items. | Same day as review. |
| 6.3.3 | HR Director | If the background check reveals a criminal history finding, proceed to Section 6.4 (Individualized Assessment) before making any employment decision. Do NOT automatically rescind the offer (except for automatic disqualifiers per Section 6.3.4). | Individualized assessment initiated within 3 business days. |
| 6.3.4 | HR Director | If the background check reveals an automatic disqualifier (sex offender registration, abuse/neglect/exploitation of a minor, elderly, or dependent adult, healthcare fraud, or current federal program exclusion): the conditional offer is rescinded immediately. Issue a Pre-Adverse Action Notice, allow the required response period (minimum 5 business days per FCRA), then issue a Final Adverse Action Notice. Document on Appendix B. | Pre-Adverse Action Notice within 2 business days; Final Adverse Action after response period. |

### 6.4 Individualized Assessment (Non-Automatic Disqualifiers)

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.4.1 | HR Director | For criminal history findings that are NOT automatic disqualifiers, conduct an individualized assessment considering: (a) Nature and gravity of the offense(s); (b) Time elapsed since the offense or completion of sentence (including probation/parole); (c) Nature of the position sought and its relationship to the offense (e.g., access to vulnerable adults, financial responsibilities, driving requirements); (d) Evidence of rehabilitation (employment history, education, references, certifications obtained since offense); (e) Number and pattern of offenses; (f) Age of the applicant at the time of the offense. Document the assessment on the Individualized Assessment Worksheet (Appendix C). | Completed within 5 business days of receiving results. |
| 6.4.2 | HR Director | If the individualized assessment results in a preliminary decision to rescind the offer: (a) Issue a Pre-Adverse Action Notice to the applicant providing a copy of the background check report and a written explanation of the preliminary decision per California Fair Chance Act requirements; (b) Provide the applicant at least 5 business days to respond with additional information, context, or evidence of rehabilitation. | Pre-Adverse Action Notice within 2 business days of preliminary decision. |
| 6.4.3 | HR Director | If the applicant responds during the response period, review the additional information and update the Individualized Assessment Worksheet. If the additional information changes the determination, document the revised decision. | Updated assessment within 3 business days of receiving response. |
| 6.4.4 | HR Director | If the final determination is to rescind the offer: issue a Final Adverse Action Notice informing the applicant of the decision, the specific conviction(s) that are the basis for the decision, and the applicant's right to file a complaint with the California Civil Rights Department (CRD). Document on Appendix B. | Final notice within 2 business days of final determination. |
| 6.4.5 | HR Director | If the final determination is that the criminal history does not disqualify the applicant: document the rationale on Appendix C, mark the screening as PASS with conditions (if applicable), and proceed with remaining screening items. Retain the individualized assessment permanently in the confidential background check file. | Same day as determination. |
| 6.4.6 | HR Director | For all adverse action decisions, consult with the Administrator and, if the case involves complex legal considerations, legal counsel per GV-EA-003, before issuing the Final Adverse Action Notice. | Prior to issuing Final Adverse Action Notice. |

### 6.5 Periodic Rescreening of Current Employees

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.5.1 | HR Director | Maintain a Rescreening Schedule tracking the date of each employee's most recent background check and the next required rescreening date (every 3 years from the date of the most recent check). | Ongoing; reviewed monthly. |
| 6.5.2 | HR Director | Initiate rescreening for current employees at least 60 calendar days before the 3-year anniversary of their most recent background check. Notify the employee in writing that rescreening will be conducted. | 60 days before rescreening due date. |
| 6.5.3 | HR Director | Immediately initiate an unscheduled background check if: (a) the agency receives credible information that an employee has been arrested or convicted of a crime; (b) a complaint alleging criminal conduct is received from a patient, family member, or colleague; (c) the Compliance Officer or Risk Manager identifies a concern. | Initiated within 2 business days of triggering event. |
| 6.5.4 | HR Director | If rescreening reveals a new conviction, apply the same review process (Section 6.3 and 6.4) as for pre-employment screening. If the determination is adverse, coordinate with the Administrator and HR-ER-002 (Disciplinary Action) for employment action. | Assessment completed within 10 business days of receiving results. |

### 6.6 Record Security and Confidentiality

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.6.1 | HR Director | Maintain all background check reports, individualized assessments, and related correspondence in a separate, locked confidential file distinct from the employee's general personnel file. Electronic records must be stored with restricted access controls per IT-SC-002. | Ongoing; maintained for the duration of employment plus 7 years per CO-HP-007. |
| 6.6.2 | HR Director | Access to background check records is limited to: HR Director, Administrator, Compliance Officer, and legal counsel. No other individual may access background check records without written authorization from the Administrator. | Ongoing. |
| 6.6.3 | HR Director | Background check results shall never be shared verbally or in writing with hiring managers, supervisors, or coworkers. Hiring managers receive only a PASS/FAIL determination. | Ongoing. |

### 6.7 Escalation and Exception Handling

| Condition | Escalation Path | Corrective Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Background check results delayed beyond 15 business days | HR Director contacts vendor; escalates to Administrator if unresolved. | Employee may NOT begin work. Alternative vendor may be engaged with Administrator approval. | Contact vendor at Day 10; escalate at Day 15. |
| Employee begins work before background check completion | Immediate escalation to Administrator and Compliance Officer. | Employee placed on administrative leave pending results. ## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes mandatory requirements for screening all employees, contractors, vendors, volunteers, and Governing Body members of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. against the Office of Inspector General (OIG) List of Excluded Individuals/Entities (LEIE) and the System for Award Management (SAM) exclusion database. Federal law (42 USC § 1320a-7 and § 1320a-7a) prohibits any entity participating in federal healthcare programs from employing or contracting with excluded individuals. Employing an excluded individual exposes the agency to civil monetary penalties of up to $100,000 per instance, treble damages, program exclusion, and criminal prosecution. This policy ensures that the agency maintains continuous compliance through pre-employment screening, monthly ongoing screening, and immediate response protocols when an exclusion is identified.
## 3. Scope
This policy applies to:
All current and prospective employees (full-time, part-time, per diem)
All contracted staff, staffing agency personnel, and independent contractors
All Governing Body members (per GV-GB-001 § 6.1.4)
All vendors and suppliers with direct or indirect access to patients, patient information, or federal healthcare program funds
All volunteers per HR-WM-006
The HR Director, Compliance Officer, and all personnel responsible for conducting or overseeing exclusion screening
This policy applies regardless of whether the individual has direct patient contact. Any individual who is employed by, contracted with, or providing services on behalf of a Medicare-certified home health agency must be screened.
## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall screen every individual identified in Section 3 against both the OIG LEIE and SAM exclusion databases prior to employment, engagement, or appointment, and monthly thereafter without exception.
4.2 No individual shall perform any work, provide any services, or conduct any business on behalf of the agency until an initial exclusion screening has been completed and documented as CLEAR.
4.3 If an individual is found to be excluded from participation in federal healthcare programs at any point — during pre-employment screening, monthly ongoing screening, or through any other means — the agency shall immediately remove the individual from all duties and initiate the response protocol defined in this policy.
4.4 The Compliance Officer shall be notified within 24 hours of any exclusion match. The Compliance Officer shall oversee the investigation and, if the exclusion is confirmed, ensure self-disclosure to the OIG per applicable self-disclosure protocols.
4.5 Monthly screening shall be conducted between the 1st and 15th of each calendar month. Results shall be documented and retained per this policy.
4.6 The agency shall screen not only by name but also by any known aliases, and shall use NPI (National Provider Identifier) where available, to reduce false negatives and false positives.
4.7 Only the most current approved version of this policy shall be considered valid.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| OIG LEIE | Office of Inspector General List of Excluded Individuals/Entities — the federal database of individuals and entities excluded from participation in Medicare, Medicaid, and all federal healthcare programs. |
| SAM | System for Award Management — the federal database that includes entities debarred, suspended, or excluded from receiving federal contracts and benefits, including healthcare program participation. |
| Exclusion | A determination by the OIG or another federal agency that an individual or entity is prohibited from participating in any federal healthcare program. Items and services furnished by an excluded individual are not reimbursable by federal healthcare programs. |
| CMP | Civil Monetary Penalty — financial penalties imposed by the OIG on entities that employ or contract with excluded individuals, currently up to $100,000 per item or service furnished by the excluded individual, plus treble damages (3x the amount claimed). |
| Self-Disclosure | The voluntary reporting by a healthcare entity to the OIG when it discovers that it has employed or contracted with an excluded individual, per the OIG Self-Disclosure Protocol. |
| False Positive | A screening result that incorrectly matches an individual to an exclusion record (e.g., due to common name). Requires secondary verification before action. |

## 6. Procedures
### 6.1 Pre-Employment / Pre-Engagement Screening

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.1.1 | HR Director (or designee) | For every applicant who has accepted a conditional offer (HR-TA-001): Search the OIG LEIE database (https://exclusions.oig.hhs.gov/) using the individual's: (a) full legal name; (b) any known aliases or maiden names; (c) date of birth (for verification); (d) NPI (if applicable). | Within 2 business days of signed conditional offer. |
| 6.1.2 | HR Director (or designee) | Search the SAM exclusion database (https://sam.gov/) using the same identifiers as Step 6.1.1. | Simultaneously with LEIE search. |
| 6.1.3 | HR Director | Document results on the OIG/SAM Screening Result Form (Appendix A): (a) name searched; (b) databases searched; (c) date of search; (d) result (CLEAR or POTENTIAL MATCH); (e) screenshot or printout of search results. If CLEAR, file in personnel file and mark the Pre-Employment Screening Checklist (HR-TA-001, Appendix F) as PASS. | Same day as search. |
| 6.1.4 | HR Director | If a POTENTIAL MATCH is identified: (a) do not clear the individual to begin work; (b) verify the match using date of birth, SSN (last 4), NPI, address, or other identifying information; (c) contact the OIG hotline (1-800-HHS-TIPS) if verification is inconclusive; (d) document all verification steps on Appendix A. | Verification initiated same day; completed within 3 business days. |
| 6.1.5 | HR Director | If the match is CONFIRMED (the individual is excluded): notify the Compliance Officer within 24 hours. Rescind the conditional offer immediately in writing. Document the finding and actions taken. | Within 24 hours of confirmation. |
| 6.1.6 | HR Director | If the match is a FALSE POSITIVE (verified that the individual is not the excluded party): document the false positive verification on Appendix A, mark as CLEAR, and proceed with remaining screening. | Upon verification completion. |

### 6.3 Exclusion Response Protocol

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6.3.1 | HR Director | Upon confirming that a current employee, contractor, or Governing Body member is excluded: (a) immediately remove the individual from all duties — the individual may not provide any services, access any systems, or be present at any patient encounter; (b) notify the Compliance Officer in writing within 24 hours; (c) notify the Administrator within 24 hours. | Immediate removal; notification within 24 hours. |
| 6.3.2 | Compliance Officer | Conduct a preliminary investigation to determine: (a) the effective date of the exclusion; (b) how long the individual has been working while excluded; (c) the scope of items and services furnished by the excluded individual during the exclusion period; (d) the total amount of any claims submitted to federal healthcare programs for items/services furnished by the excluded individual. Document on the Exclusion Incident Report (Appendix D). | Investigation initiated within 48 hours; completed within 14 calendar days. |
| 6.3.3 | Compliance Officer | Calculate the agency's potential CMP exposure: up to $100,000 per item/service furnished by the excluded individual, plus treble damages on amounts claimed. Document the exposure calculation on Appendix D. | Concurrent with investigation. |
| 6.3.4 | Compliance Officer | Report the finding and investigation results to the Governing Body Chair and Administrator in writing. Recommend whether self-disclosure to the OIG is warranted (generally recommended for any period exceeding a single pay period). | Within 14 calendar days of confirmed exclusion. |
| 6.3.5 | Administrator / Governing Body | Direct the appropriate employment action. If the individual is excluded, employment must be terminated. Coordinate with legal counsel per GV-EA-003 regarding self-disclosure to the OIG under the OIG Self-Disclosure Protocol. | Employment action within 48 hours of confirmation. Self-disclosure decision within 30 calendar days per OIG guidance. |
| 6.3.6 | HR Director | Terminate the excluded individual's employment effective immediately. Process separation per HR-ER-006. Revoke all system access per IT-SC-002. Document in personnel file. | Immediate upon direction from Administrator. |
| 6.3.7 | Compliance Officer | If self-disclosure is directed, prepare and submit the self-disclosure to the OIG per the OIG Provider Self-Disclosure Protocol. Retain all documentation. Report status to the Governing Body at each quarterly meeting until resolution. | Self-disclosure submitted within 60 calendar days of discovery per OIG guidance. |
| 6.3.8 | Compliance Officer | Conduct a root cause analysis to determine how the excluded individual was hired or how the exclusion was not detected earlier. Implement corrective actions per QA-AE-002 and QA-AE-003. | Root cause analysis completed within 30 calendar days. |

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes mandatory requirements for verifying and continuously monitoring the professional licensure, certification, and registration status of all clinical and licensed personnel employed by or contracted with Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Verification of licensure is required by 42 CFR § 484.105 and § 484.115 (Personnel Qualifications), California Health and Safety Code home health agency licensing requirements, and applicable professional practice acts. Employing individuals who do not hold current, valid, unrestricted licensure appropriate to their role exposes the agency to regulatory sanctions, survey deficiencies, malpractice liability, and criminal prosecution for unauthorized practice. This policy ensures that every licensed professional practicing under the agency's authority holds a valid, verified license at all times.
## 3. Scope
This policy applies to:
All Registered Nurses (RN), Licensed Vocational Nurses (LVN), Physical Therapists (PT), Physical Therapist Assistants (PTA), Occupational Therapists (OT), Certified Occupational Therapy Assistants (COTA), Speech-Language Pathologists (SLP), Medical Social Workers (MSW), and any other licensed/certified clinical professional employed by or contracted with the agency
Home Health Aides (HHA) — certification verification per 42 CFR § 484.80
All contracted/staffing agency clinical personnel
The HR Director, Director of Nursing, and all personnel responsible for licensure verification
The Administrator
## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall verify the current, valid, unrestricted status of every professional license, certification, and registration required for each clinical position prior to the individual performing any clinical duties, and shall continuously monitor licensure status throughout employment.
4.2 Verification shall be conducted through primary source verification — direct confirmation with the issuing state licensing board, certification body, or their authorized electronic verification system. Copies of licenses provided by the applicant or employee are not sufficient as the sole means of verification.
4.3 No individual shall provide clinical services, conduct patient assessments, or perform any task requiring professional licensure until primary source verification is completed and documented as CURRENT and UNRESTRICTED.
4.4 The agency shall maintain a Licensure Expiration Tracking System (Appendix A) to proactively monitor all license expiration dates and initiate renewal verification at least 60 calendar days before expiration.
4.5 Any clinician whose license expires, is suspended, revoked, or subject to restriction shall be immediately removed from clinical duties until the licensure issue is fully resolved. Practice on an expired or restricted license is a violation of California law and agency policy.
4.6 All licensure verification records shall be maintained in the employee's personnel file per HR-WM-005 and HR-WM-007.
4.7 Only the most current approved version of this policy shall be considered valid.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Primary Source Verification | Confirmation of licensure status directly from the issuing licensing board, certifying body, or their authorized electronic verification system (e.g., California BRN online license lookup, APTA verification, NBCOT verification). |
| Unrestricted License | A license that is current, active, and not subject to any disciplinary limitations, probation, supervision requirements, or practice restrictions imposed by the licensing board. |
| Restricted License | A license that is current but subject to conditions, limitations, probation, or practice restrictions imposed by the licensing board as a result of disciplinary action. |
| License Encumbrance | Any adverse action taken against a license including probation, suspension, revocation, voluntary surrender, or practice restriction. |
| Compact License | A multistate license issued under a nurse licensure compact or therapy compact that authorizes practice in multiple member states. |
| Certification | A credential issued by a professional certifying body (e.g., HHA certification, BLS certification, specialty certification) that validates competency in a defined scope of practice. |

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes mandatory requirements for the orientation and onboarding of all new employees and contracted staff at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. A comprehensive, standardized orientation ensures that every individual entering the agency workforce understands the agency's mission, regulatory obligations, clinical standards, compliance requirements, safety protocols, and their role-specific responsibilities before providing any services. This policy ensures compliance with 42 CFR § 484.105 (organization and administration), 42 CFR § 484.80 (home health aide training), CMS State Operations Manual surveyor expectations, OSHA requirements, and California home health licensure standards.
## 3. Scope
This policy applies to:
All newly hired employees (full-time, part-time, per diem) regardless of position
All contracted/staffing agency clinical personnel prior to their first patient assignment
All individuals transitioning to a new role or department within the agency (role-specific orientation component only)
The HR Director, Director of Nursing, department supervisors, and all personnel responsible for conducting or coordinating orientation
The Administrator
This policy does not apply to: Governing Body member orientation, which is addressed in GV-GB-001 § 10. Volunteer orientation is addressed in HR-WM-006.
## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall provide a mandatory, documented orientation program to every new employee before the employee independently performs any job duties, provides any patient care, or accesses protected health information.
4.2 Orientation shall consist of two components: (a) General Agency Orientation (required for all staff); and (b) Role-Specific/Clinical Orientation (required for clinical staff and position-specific for all other roles).
4.3 General Agency Orientation must be completed within the first 5 business days of employment. Role-Specific/Clinical Orientation must be completed within the first 30 calendar days of employment. Clinical staff may not conduct unsupervised patient visits until both components are satisfactorily completed and documented.
4.4 Orientation content shall be reviewed and updated at least annually by the HR Director (general orientation) and Director of Nursing (clinical orientation) to reflect current regulations, policies, and agency operations.
4.5 Orientation completion shall be documented on the Orientation Completion Checklist (Appendix A for General, Appendix B for Clinical) and retained in the employee's personnel file per HR-WM-005 and HR-WM-007.
4.6 Contracted/staffing agency personnel shall complete an abbreviated orientation (Appendix C) covering patient safety, infection control, HIPAA, emergency procedures, and agency-specific clinical documentation requirements before their first patient assignment.
4.7 Only the most current approved version of this policy shall be considered valid.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| General Agency Orientation | The standardized orientation program covering agency-wide topics applicable to all employees regardless of position, including mission, compliance, HIPAA, safety, infection control, emergency preparedness, and workplace standards. |
| Role-Specific Orientation | The orientation component tailored to the specific duties, clinical competencies, documentation systems, and supervision requirements of the employee's assigned position. |
| Clinical Orientation | The role-specific orientation for clinical staff including EHR/OASIS training, clinical documentation standards, supervised patient visits, and competency validation. |
| Preceptor | An experienced, competent employee designated to mentor and supervise a new employee during the role-specific orientation period. |
| Supervised Visit | A patient care visit conducted by a new clinical employee under the direct or indirect supervision of a preceptor or clinical supervisor, prior to being cleared for independent practice. |
| Orientation Completion | The point at which all required orientation components have been satisfactorily completed, documented, and signed off by the appropriate supervisor, allowing the employee to perform duties independently. |

## 8. Compliance Monitoring
### 8.1 How Compliance Is Measured

| Compliance Indicator | Measurement Method | Acceptable Standard |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Failure Point | Risk | Mitigation |

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes requirements for the development, approval, maintenance, and distribution of written job descriptions for all positions at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Job descriptions serve as the foundational document defining the qualifications, scope of authority, essential functions, reporting relationships, and regulatory requirements for each position. CMS surveyors verify that employees meet the qualifications defined in job descriptions per 42 CFR § 484.105 and § 484.115. Written job descriptions also support ADA compliance, performance management, recruitment, disciplinary processes, and workforce planning.
## 3. Scope
This policy applies to:
All positions within Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. including clinical, administrative, and support roles
All hiring managers, supervisors, and department heads responsible for defining or maintaining job descriptions
The HR Director and Director of Nursing
The Administrator
## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Every position at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall have a current, written, approved job description on file with the HR Director.
4.2 Job descriptions shall be developed and approved before any individual is recruited for or appointed to a position per HR-TA-001 § 6.1.2.
4.3 Each job description must contain, at minimum, the following elements: (a) position title; (b) department/discipline; (c) FLSA classification (exempt/non-exempt); (d) reporting relationship (reports to); (e) supervisory responsibilities; (f) position summary; (g) essential functions (ADA-compliant language); (h) minimum qualifications (education, experience, licensure/certification); (i) preferred qualifications; (j) physical requirements and working conditions; (k) regulatory requirements specific to the position; (l) approval signatures and date.
4.4 Job descriptions shall be reviewed at least biennially (every 24 months) or upon: (a) regulatory change affecting the position; (b) significant change in duties; (c) vacancy requiring recruitment; (d) organizational restructuring.
4.5 Each employee shall receive a copy of their current job description at hire, upon any revision, and at each annual performance evaluation, and shall sign an acknowledgment.
4.6 Job descriptions for clinical positions must align with the HR-JD series policies (HR-JD-000 through HR-JD-011) and applicable professional practice acts.
4.7 Only the most current approved version shall be considered valid.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Essential Functions | The fundamental duties of a position that an employee must be able to perform, with or without reasonable accommodation, as distinct from marginal functions. Essential functions are determined based on: the reason the position exists, the number of employees available to perform the function, and the degree of specialization required. |
| FLSA Classification | The determination of whether a position is exempt or non-exempt under the Fair Labor Standards Act, affecting overtime eligibility. |
| Minimum Qualifications | The education, experience, licensure, certification, and competency requirements that a candidate must possess to be considered for the position. |
| Preferred Qualifications | Additional qualifications that enhance a candidate's suitability but are not required for consideration. |
| Physical Requirements | The physical demands of the position including lifting, standing, walking, driving, and environmental exposures, documented in ADA-compliant language. |

## 2. Purpose
This policy defines the agency's annual mandatory training requirements for all employees to ensure continuous compliance with CMS Conditions of Participation, OSHA standards, HIPAA, OIG compliance guidance, California home health licensing requirements, and agency operational standards. Annual training reinforces critical knowledge, addresses emerging risks, fulfills regulatory mandates, and supports the QAPI program's commitment to continuous improvement per 42 CFR § 484.65.
## 3. Scope
This policy applies to: All employees of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. (full-time, part-time, per diem) regardless of position. Contracted staff training requirements are addressed through staffing agency contractual obligations and verified per HR-TA-001 § 6.6.
## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain an Annual Mandatory Training Program that requires every employee to complete defined training topics each calendar year.
4.2 The Annual Mandatory Training Calendar (Appendix A) shall be established by the HR Director in collaboration with the Director of Nursing, Compliance Officer, and Risk Manager by December 15 of each year for the upcoming year.
4.3 All mandatory training must be completed by each employee within the calendar year (January 1 – December 31). New hires who start after July 1 must complete all remaining mandatory training by December 31 of their hire year or within 6 months of hire, whichever is later.
4.4 Training may be delivered through in-person classroom, online/e-learning, competency fairs, simulations, or a combination, provided that: (a) content meets regulatory requirements; (b) completion is individually documented; (c) competency is assessed where required.
4.5 The HR Director shall maintain an Annual Training Compliance Dashboard (Appendix B) tracking completion rates by employee, department, and topic.
4.6 Failure to complete mandatory training within the required timeframe shall result in progressive action: (a) written reminder at 30 days overdue; (b) supervisor notification at 45 days overdue; (c) administrative suspension from clinical duties (clinical staff) or disciplinary action per HR-ER-002 at 60 days overdue.
4.7 Only the most current approved version shall be valid.

| Competency Assessment | An evaluation method (test, return demonstration, simulation, case study) that measures whether the employee can apply the training content to their job duties. |
| Training Compliance Rate | The percentage of employees who have completed all required training within the defined timeframe. |

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes standards for supporting, tracking, and documenting continuing education (CE) and professional development activities for all professional staff at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Continuing education ensures that clinical and professional employees maintain the knowledge and competencies required by their licensing boards, advances evidence-based practice, and supports the agency's commitment to quality patient care and regulatory compliance.
## 3. Scope
All licensed/certified professional staff (RN, LVN, PT, PTA, OT, COTA, SLP, MSW) and any other credentialed staff whose licensure or certification requires continuing education. Administrative staff professional development is encouraged but not mandated by this policy.
## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 All licensed/certified professional employees are responsible for meeting the CE requirements of their respective licensing boards and maintaining proof of compliance.
4.2 The agency shall support continuing education by: (a) providing paid time for approved CE activities up to 16 hours per year for full-time employees (prorated for part-time); (b) maintaining a CE resource library; (c) offering in-house CE opportunities where feasible.
4.3 The agency shall maintain a CE Tracking System (Appendix A) to monitor each employee's CE status relative to their license renewal requirements.
4.4 Employees shall submit documentation of completed CE activities to the HR Director within 30 calendar days of completion using the CE Activity Report Form (Appendix B__
4.5 CE activities must be relevant to the employee's professional role and obtained from providers approved or accredited by the applicable licensing board.
4.6 Failure to maintain CE compliance as required by the licensing board places the employee's license at risk. The agency will coordinate with HR-TA-004 to address any licensure issues arising from CE non-compliance.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Continuing Education (CE) | Structured educational activities designed to maintain, develop, or enhance the knowledge, skills, and professional competence of licensed professionals, as required by their licensing boards. |
| CE Unit / Contact Hour | The standard measurement of continuing education. One contact hour typically equals 50-60 minutes of educational activity. CE requirements vary by profession and state. |
| Approved Provider | An organization or institution accredited or approved by the applicable licensing board or recognized accrediting body to offer continuing education. |

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes requirements for initial and ongoing competency evaluation for all clinical staff at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Competency evaluation ensures that clinical personnel possess and maintain the knowledge, skills, and abilities necessary to provide safe, effective patient care in compliance with 42 CFR § 484.105, § 484.80 (HHA competency), § 484.115 (personnel qualifications), and applicable California professional practice standards. Competency results are linked to the QAPI program per QA-PI-005 and QA-PI-007 to drive targeted education and performance improvement.
## 3. Scope
All clinical staff: RN, LVN, PT, PTA, OT, COTA, SLP, MSW, HHA, and any other clinical personnel providing direct or indirect patient care. Contracted clinical staff competency is verified per HR-TA-001 § 6.6.
## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Every clinical employee shall undergo an initial competency evaluation during orientation (HR-TA-005) and an annual competency evaluation thereafter.
4.2 Competency evaluation shall assess both general clinical competencies (applicable to all clinical staff) and discipline-specific competencies (applicable to each profession's scope of practice).
4.3 Competency shall be evaluated using multiple methods: direct observation, skills demonstration (return demonstration), written assessment, case study analysis, clinical record review, and/or simulation.
4.4 Home health aides shall be evaluated per 42 CFR § 484.80 requirements including competency in all required skill areas with supervised performance at least every 12 months.
4.5 Competency deficits shall trigger a documented remediation plan with defined milestones and reassessment timeframe. An employee who fails to achieve competency after remediation shall be referred for employment action per HR-ER-002.
4.6 Competency evaluation results shall be reported to the QAPI program per QA-PI-005 and QA-PI-007 to identify training needs and measure the impact of education on patient outcomes.
4.7 Only the most current approved version shall be valid.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Competency | The demonstrated ability to apply knowledge, skills, and judgment to perform clinical duties safely and effectively in alignment with established standards of practice and agency protocols. |
| Competency Evaluation | A structured assessment process using defined criteria and tools to determine whether a clinical employee meets the required standard of performance. |
| Remediation Plan | A documented plan specifying the competency deficit, targeted education/training interventions, practice opportunities, reassessment method, timeline, and responsible supervisor. |
| Skills Check-Off | A direct observation evaluation in which the employee demonstrates a specific clinical skill to a qualified evaluator who documents the performance against a defined checklist of required steps. |

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes requirements for accepting, supervising, and evaluating students and interns completing clinical or administrative fieldwork at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Proper supervision protects patients, maintains regulatory compliance, supports workforce pipeline development, and ensures that student activities are conducted under appropriate professional oversight in compliance with applicable professional practice acts, educational program requirements, and agency standards.
## 3. Scope
All students and interns from accredited educational programs placed at the agency for clinical rotations, fieldwork, or administrative internships. All staff serving as preceptors or supervisors for students/interns. The Director of Nursing, HR Director, and Administrator.
## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 The agency shall only accept students/interns from accredited educational programs with which the agency has a current, signed Affiliation Agreement (Appendix A).
4.2 Students/interns shall not provide any patient care services independently. All patient care activities must be under the direct supervision of a licensed preceptor.
4.3 Students/interns shall complete an abbreviated orientation (per HR-TA-005, Appendix C standards) and sign confidentiality/HIPAA agreements before any patient contact.
4.4 Preceptors must meet minimum qualifications: (a) current unrestricted license in the applicable discipline; (b) minimum 2 years of clinical experience; (c) minimum 1 year with the agency; (d) no active disciplinary actions; (e) completion of preceptor training.
4.5 The agency shall conduct OIG/SAM screening (HR-TA-003) and verify student identity before placement begins.
4.6 Students/interns are not employees and shall not be used to replace or supplement staff for patient care coverage.
## 5. Definitions

| Affiliation Agreement | A written contract between the agency and an accredited educational institution defining the terms, responsibilities, liability, and insurance requirements for student placements. |
| Preceptor | A licensed agency employee designated to provide direct supervision, mentorship, and clinical instruction to an assigned student/intern. |
| Direct Supervision | The preceptor is physically present during all patient care activities performed by the student. |

## 2. Purpose
This policy mandates initial and annual emergency preparedness training and drill participation for all staff at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. per 42 CFR § 484.102 (Emergency Preparedness), CMS Emergency Preparedness Rule requirements, and California state emergency preparedness standards. Training ensures all employees understand their roles during emergencies, can execute the agency's Emergency Operations and Business Continuity Plan (OP-FM-005), and can protect patients and themselves during disasters, pandemics, and other emergencies.
## 3. Scope
All employees regardless of position. Coordination with OP-FM-005, CL-PR-005, RM-EP-001, RM-EP-002, and RM-EP-003.
## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 All new employees shall complete emergency preparedness training during orientation per HR-TA-005.
4.2 All employees shall participate in at least 2 emergency preparedness exercises per year: (a) one community-based exercise (if available) or a tabletop exercise; and (b) one agency-based drill (tabletop, functional, or full-scale). Per CMS requirements, if the agency participates in a community-based exercise, the second exercise may be a tabletop. If not, both must be exercises (at least one being a full-scale or functional exercise).
4.3 Each drill/exercise shall be followed by a documented after-action review (AAR) identifying strengths, gaps, and corrective actions.
4.4 Training and drill completion shall be documented and retained per CO-HP-007.
4.5 Drill results shall be reported to the Governing Body per GV-GB-001 § 6.2.6.2 and to the QAPI program.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Tabletop Exercise | A discussion-based exercise where participants walk through a simulated emergency scenario verbally, testing plans, policies, and communication without actual deployment of resources. |
| Functional Exercise | An exercise that tests specific operations or functions of the emergency plan in a simulated environment with actual deployment of some resources. |
| Full-Scale Exercise | A comprehensive exercise that tests the entire emergency plan with actual resource deployment and real-time coordination. |
| After-Action Review (AAR) | A structured debrief following an exercise or actual emergency that evaluates performance, identifies gaps, and generates corrective action items. |

## 6. Procedures

## 1. Policy Header

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes the process, frequency, and documentation requirements for employee performance evaluations at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Performance evaluations provide a structured framework for assessing job performance, identifying strengths and development needs, linking individual performance to competency evaluation results (HR-TD-003) and QAPI outcomes (QA-PI-005), and supporting workforce management decisions including retention, promotion, and corrective action.
## 3. Scope
All employees after completion of the introductory/probationary period (90 calendar days). The Administrator's evaluation is conducted by the Governing Body per GV-GB-001 § 6.2.2.4.
## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Every employee shall receive a formal written performance evaluation at least annually, within 30 calendar days following the employee's anniversary date.
4.2 New employees shall receive an evaluation at the end of the 90-day introductory period.
4.3 Performance evaluations shall be based on objective, documented criteria derived from the employee's current job description (HR-TA-006), competency evaluation results (HR-TD-003), attendance records, training compliance, and documented performance observations throughout the evaluation period.
4.4 Evaluations shall use the standardized Performance Evaluation Form (Appendix A) to ensure consistency and reduce bias.
4.5 The evaluation process shall include a face-to-face meeting between the supervisor and employee, employee self-assessment opportunity, and written acknowledgment of receipt.
4.6 Performance evaluation results shall be considered in decisions regarding compensation adjustments, promotions, additional training assignments, and corrective action.
4.7 Performance evaluation records are confidential per Tier 3 and accessible only to the employee, their direct supervisor, HR Director, and Administrator.
## 5. Definitions

| Term | Definition |

## 3. Scope
All employees. Does not apply to Governing Body members (governed by GV-GB-001).

(d) Involuntary Layoff / Reduction in Force — agency-initiated for business or economic reasons;
(e) End of Assignment — for temporary, per diem, or contract employees;
(f) Death or Incapacitation.

4.2 Notice Requirements: Employees in clinical or patient-facing roles are requested to provide a minimum of two (2) weeks written notice. The Administrator, DON, or department managers are requested to provide four (4) weeks notice. The agency may choose to accept or waive the notice period.

4.3 Final Wage Payment: California Labor Code requirements shall be strictly observed:
(a) Involuntary termination: Final wages including all accrued, unused vacation shall be paid on the last day of employment (Labor Code § 201);
(b) Voluntary resignation with 72+ hours notice: Final wages due on the employee's last day;
(c) Voluntary resignation with less than 72 hours notice: Final wages due within 72 hours of resignation;
(d) Failure to comply may result in waiting time penalties of up to 30 days of wages.

4.4 Separation Checklist: HR shall use a standardized Separation Checklist to ensure completion of:
(a) Return of all agency property: keys, access cards, uniforms, mobile devices, laptops, identification badges;
(b) Revocation of all system access: EHR, email, agency intranet, payroll system, telehealth platforms — within 24 hours of last day;
(c) Completion of COBRA/Cal-COBRA notification for benefits-eligible employees within the federal 14-day notice deadline;
(d) Provision of EDD Notice to Employee as to Change in Relationship (DE 2320) per California EDD requirements;
(e) Final timesheet review and processing;
(f) Collection of signed separation documentation.

4.5 Exit Interview: HR shall offer an exit interview to all voluntarily separating employees to gather constructive feedback about working conditions, management practices, and agency operations. Exit interview data shall be aggregated and reviewed by the Administrator quarterly.

4.6 Patient Care Continuity: The DON shall be notified of any clinical staff separation at least 1 business day before the last working day to arrange patient reassignment, ensure no patients are left without a case manager, and transfer care documentation. Abrupt clinical separations are a patient safety issue and shall be escalated immediately.

4.7 Reference Policy: The agency shall provide employment verification (dates of employment, positions held, and confirmation of eligibility for rehire) to authorized inquiries. Detailed reference information beyond employment verification requires a signed Release of Information from the former employee.

4.8 Rehire Eligibility: Employees separated in good standing (non-disciplinary) may be considered for rehire. Employees terminated for cause involving patient abuse, fraud, or OIG exclusion criteria are permanently ineligible for rehire and shall be flagged in the HR system.

4.9 Documentation: All separation documentation shall be retained in the personnel file per HR-WM-005 and CO-HP-007.

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes mandatory reporting obligations for all Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. employees regarding suspected or known abuse, neglect, abandonment, or exploitation of patients (elder adults, dependent adults, and minors) receiving home health services. All employees of the agency are mandatory reporters under the California Elder Abuse and Dependent Adult Civil Protection Act (EADACPA, Welfare & Institutions Code § 15600 et seq.), the Child Abuse and Neglect Reporting Act (CANRA, Penal Code § 11164 et seq.), and the In-Home Supportive Services (IHSS) reporting requirements. Failure to report known or suspected abuse is a criminal misdemeanor under California law, punishable by up to 6 months imprisonment and/or a $1,000 fine. This policy is non-negotiable.

## 3. Scope
All employees, contractors, volunteers, and students of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. without exception, including clinical staff, administrative staff, and management.

## 4. Who Must Report
4.1 Every employee of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. is a mandatory reporter for suspected elder/dependent adult abuse and child abuse when they observe or have reasonable suspicion of abuse in their professional capacity.

4.2 The reporting obligation is personal — supervisors may NOT instruct employees to withhold or delay a report. Supervisors who interfere with mandatory reporting may be subject to criminal charges.

4.3 "Reasonable suspicion" does not require certainty or proof. If the reporter believes abuse might be occurring based on observed signs, symptoms, or disclosures, they are legally required to report.

## 5. Types of Reportable Abuse
5.1 Elder/Dependent Adult Abuse (WIC § 15610):
(a) Physical abuse — non-accidental injury, unreasonable restraint, sexual assault;
(b) Neglect — failure to provide food, shelter, health care, or protection from physical harm;
(c) Abandonment — desertion without arrangements for care;
(d) Financial abuse — taking or misappropriating money or property;
(e) Isolation — preventing contacts with others by threat or deception;
(f) Psychological/mental abuse — intimidation, humiliation, threatening behavior.

5.2 Child Abuse (PC § 11165):
(a) Physical abuse; (b) Sexual abuse; (c) Emotional abuse; (d) Neglect; (e) Willful cruelty or unjustifiable punishment.

## 6. Reporting Procedures
6.1 Immediate telephone report: Any employee who observes or suspects abuse shall make an immediate telephone report — within 2 hours of becoming aware — to:
(a) Adult Protective Services (APS): for elder/dependent adults not residing in a care facility;
(b) Long-Term Care Ombudsman: for residents of residential care or skilled nursing facilities;
(c) CDPH: for abuse in licensed healthcare facilities;
(d) Law enforcement (911 or local police): for any situation involving imminent danger, sexual assault, or criminal activity.

6.2 Written report: A written report on the appropriate standardized form (SOC 341 for elder/dependent adult abuse; SS 8572 for child abuse) shall be submitted within 2 business days of the oral report.

6.3 Internal notification: In addition to the legal report, the employee shall immediately notify their supervising clinician and the Administrator. The Administrator shall document the report and any agency action in the patient's clinical record and in the agency's incident log per QA-AE-004.

6.4 Confidentiality of the reporter: Reports made in good faith are confidential and protected from civil and criminal liability under California law. The identity of the reporter is protected per WIC § 15633.

6.5 Non-retaliation: Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. prohibits retaliation against any employee who makes a good-faith mandatory abuse report. Retaliation is a violation of California law and agency policy, and will result in disciplinary action up to and including termination.

6.6 Interaction with patients: When reporting abuse by a family member or caregiver, agency staff shall NOT confront the alleged abuser. Staff shall ensure patient safety (call 911 if imminent danger) and leave the interaction to APS and law enforcement.

## 7. Documentation
7.1 The reporter shall document in the clinical record: (a) observed signs/symptoms leading to suspicion; (b) patient statements, if any; (c) date, time, and agency contacted for oral report; (d) name of receiving official, if available; (e) follow-up actions taken.

7.2 A copy of the written report shall be filed in the patient's clinical record and in the agency's incident file. The report and clinical record are confidential and shall be protected per CO-HP-001.

## 8. Training
All employees shall complete mandatory abuse recognition and reporting training at hire and annually thereafter per HR-TD-001. Training shall cover: abuse definitions, signs and symptoms, reporting procedures, and protections for reporters.

## 2. Position Summary
The Medical Social Worker (MSW) provides medical social services to patients receiving home health care to assist in resolving social, emotional, and environmental factors that affect or impair the patient's ability to benefit from medical treatment. Per 42 CFR § 484.115(e), the MSW must have at minimum a baccalaureate degree in social work (BSW) or a related behavioral science field. Medical social services are a separately billable Medicare Part A skilled service when medically necessary.

## 3. Minimum Qualifications

| Requirement | Specification |
| --- | --- |
| Education | Bachelor of Social Work (BSW) from CSWE-accredited program required; Master of Social Work (MSW) preferred. |
| Licensure | California LCSW (Licensed Clinical Social Worker) or ACSW (Associate Clinical Social Worker) preferred; BSW acceptable per 42 CFR § 484.115(e). |
| Experience | Minimum 1 year in medical social work or community case management preferred. |
| Background | OIG/SAM, background check per HR-TA-002 and HR-TA-003. |

## 4. Essential Job Functions
4.1 Completes social work assessments for referred patients to identify social, emotional, financial, environmental, and cultural factors affecting the patient's health and recovery.
4.2 Develops individualized social service plans as part of the interdisciplinary care plan per CL-CP-003.
4.3 Assists patients and families in accessing community resources: housing, food assistance, transportation, home modification, caregiver support, financial assistance programs.
4.4 Provides counseling and supportive therapy to patients and families coping with chronic illness, disability, and end-of-life issues.
4.5 Facilitates advance directive discussions and assists patients in understanding their options per CL-PR-002.
4.6 Coordinates care transitions: discharge planning, facility placement, community referrals.
4.7 Identifies and reports suspected elder/dependent adult abuse per HR-ER-009.
4.8 Documents all patient contacts and interventions per CL-CD-001 within 24 hours per CL-CD-004.
4.9 Participates in interdisciplinary case conferences per CL-CP-003.

## 5. Scope of Practice
The MSW provides social, not medical, services. MSWs may not perform clinical nursing or therapy procedures. All recommendations for changes to the medical plan of care shall be communicated to the DON or treating physician.

## 6. Regulatory References
42 CFR § 484.115(e) — Medical Social Worker qualifications; 42 CFR § 484.60(b) — Social services in care planning; Title 22 CCR § 74715.

## 2. Purpose
This policy establishes requirements for maintaining adequate staffing levels to provide safe, high-quality, continuous home health services to all patients of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. in compliance with 42 CFR § 484.105 (Organization and Administration) and California Title 22 CCR § 74731. Unsafe staffing levels are a patient safety risk and a Conditions of Participation deficiency. The agency must demonstrate at all times that sufficient qualified staff are available to meet patient care needs.

## 3. Scope
All clinical and administrative positions. HR Director and DON are co-responsible for staffing management.

## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 The agency shall at all times maintain sufficient qualified staff to provide all services required by the plans of care of currently admitted patients.
4.2 Minimum staffing requirements:
(a) At least one RN shall be on-call 24 hours per day, 7 days per week per 42 CFR § 484.30(c);
(b) The DON position shall not remain vacant for more than 30 days without an approved interim designee plan;
(c) At least one Medicare-qualified RN shall be on the agency's payroll or under a written contractual arrangement at all times.
4.3 The DON shall maintain a staffing plan that projects patient census, visit volume, and required clinical FTE for 30, 60, and 90 days rolling.
4.4 When census or acuity increases require additional staff, the DON shall notify the Administrator and initiate recruitment per HR-TA-001 before the deficit becomes a patient care risk.
4.5 Per diem and contracted staff shall be used to supplement (not replace) regular staff. Contracted clinical staff shall meet the same qualifications and screening requirements as employees per HR-WM-002.
4.6 Clinician caseloads shall be monitored to ensure: (a) no RN carries more than a reasonably manageable caseload (typically 25-30 active patients per case manager, depending on acuity); (b) overtime shall not be regularly used to compensate for understaffing; (c) high-acuity patients receive priority scheduling.
4.7 Staffing data shall be reported to the QAPI committee and Governing Body quarterly as part of the operational report.
