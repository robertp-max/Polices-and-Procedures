## 1. Purpose
This policy establishes standards for receiving, screening, processing, and managing patient referrals and intake activities at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. The referral and intake process is the gateway to all home health services and must ensure timely, accurate, and compliant processing of every referral to support patient access, regulatory compliance, and continuity of care. This policy ensures the agency satisfies applicable requirements of 42 CFR § 484.105 (Organization and Administration), 42 CFR § 484.50 (Patient Rights — timely acceptance/notification), and 42 CFR § 484.55 (Comprehensive Assessment).
## 2. Scope
This policy applies to:
All intake coordinators, intake nurses, and intake staff
The Operations Director
The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager
The Administrator
All clinical staff involved in referral screening or clinical eligibility determination
All contracted or per diem staff performing intake functions
All referral sources communicating with the agency
This policy does not govern clinical assessment content after admission (see CL-CA-001) or billing/authorization processes (see FN-CM-004).
## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Referral | A communication from a physician, hospital, skilled nursing facility, patient/family, or other source requesting home health services for a patient. |
| Intake | The administrative and clinical screening process that evaluates a referral for appropriateness, eligibility, and agency capacity prior to acceptance or denial. |
| Referral Source | Any individual or entity that initiates a referral, including physicians, discharge planners, case managers, patients, family members, and community organizations. |
| Clinical Eligibility Screening | The clinical review of referral information to determine whether the patient meets criteria for home health services including homebound status, skilled need, and medical necessity. |
| Intake Coordinator | The designated staff member responsible for receiving, logging, screening, and processing referrals per this policy. |
| Payer Verification | The process of confirming the patient's insurance coverage, benefit eligibility, and authorization requirements prior to admission. |
| Service Area | The geographic boundaries within which the agency is authorized and operationally equipped to provide services, as defined in OP-IM-003. |
| Referral Disposition | The final outcome of intake processing: Accepted, Declined (with reason), or Pending. |

## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a structured referral and intake process that ensures every referral is received, logged, screened, and dispositioned in a timely, accurate, and non-discriminatory manner.
4.2 All referrals shall be processed within defined timeframes to support timely access to care. Urgent referrals shall be triaged within 2 hours of receipt; routine referrals shall be processed within 24 hours of receipt.
4.3 No referral shall be declined on the basis of race, color, national origin, sex, age, disability, religion, sexual orientation, gender identity, or diagnosis except where the agency lacks the clinical competency, licensure, or staffing to safely provide the required services.
4.4 Every referral shall be logged in the agency's referral tracking system with a unique referral number, date and time of receipt, referral source, and patient identifying information at the time of initial contact.
4.5 Clinical eligibility screening shall be performed by a licensed clinician (RN or therapist) for all referrals prior to acceptance.
4.6 Payer verification and authorization (where required) shall be completed prior to or concurrent with the first billable visit.
4.7 Referral sources and patients/families shall be notified of the referral disposition (acceptance or declination) within defined timeframes with documentation of all notifications.
4.8 Declined referrals shall include documentation of the reason for declination and evidence that the referral source was offered alternative provider information where available.
4.9 The intake process shall capture all information necessary to support the Start of Care assessment, initial plan of care, and first visit scheduling.
## 5. Procedures
### 5.1 Referral Receipt and Logging

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1.1 | Intake Coordinator | Receive referral via any approved channel (fax, electronic referral, phone, secure portal, walk-in). Log immediately in the referral tracking system with: (a) unique referral number; (b) date and time of receipt; (c) referral source name, title, organization, and contact information; (d) patient name, date of birth, and contact information; (e) referring physician name and NPI; (f) primary diagnosis and reason for referral; (g) urgency level (Urgent / Routine). | At time of receipt. |
| 5.1.2 | Intake Coordinator | Assign urgency classification: URGENT — patient has immediate clinical need, same-day or next-day SOC required (e.g., post-surgical, wound vac, IV therapy, fall risk); ROUTINE — standard intake processing timeline applies. | Immediately upon logging. |
| 5.1.3 | Intake Coordinator | Acknowledge receipt to the referral source: URGENT referrals — verbal or written acknowledgment within 1 hour; ROUTINE referrals — written acknowledgment within 4 business hours. | Per urgency classification. |

### 5.2 Intake Screening — Administrative

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.2.1 | Intake Coordinator | Verify patient resides within the agency's defined service area per OP-IM-003. If outside the service area, proceed to Section 5.6 (Declination). | Within 2 hours (Urgent) / 8 hours (Routine). |
| 5.2.2 | Intake Coordinator | Verify insurance/payer information: (a) confirm active coverage; (b) verify home health benefit eligibility; (c) identify prior authorization requirements; (d) confirm Medicare beneficiary status if applicable; (e) document verification results in the referral record. | Within 4 hours (Urgent) / 24 hours (Routine). |
| 5.2.3 | Intake Coordinator | Collect and verify all required referral documentation: (a) physician face-to-face encounter documentation (if available); (b) hospital discharge summary (if applicable); (c) current medication list; (d) relevant medical history and diagnoses; (e) any existing orders or plan of care; (f) advance directive status; (g) emergency contact information. Document any missing items and initiate retrieval. | Within 24 hours of receipt. |
| 5.2.4 | Intake Coordinator | Verify that the patient does not have an active episode with another Medicare-certified home health agency. If dual certification is identified, coordinate with the other agency and document resolution. | Within 24 hours. |

### 5.3 Intake Screening — Clinical Eligibility

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.3.1 | Intake RN / Clinical Manager | Perform clinical eligibility screening based on referral information. Evaluate: (a) homebound status indicators; (b) skilled nursing or therapy need; (c) medical necessity for home health services; (d) physician willingness to certify/order services; (e) patient/caregiver willingness to participate; (f) safety of the home environment (based on available information). | Within 4 hours (Urgent) / 24 hours (Routine). |
| 5.3.2 | Intake RN / Clinical Manager | If clinical eligibility cannot be determined from referral documents alone, contact the referral source or physician office to obtain additional clinical information. Document all outreach attempts and outcomes. | Within 24 hours; minimum 2 attempts before escalation. |
| 5.3.3 | Intake RN / Clinical Manager | Document the clinical eligibility determination on the Intake Clinical Screening Form (Appendix A) with rationale. Sign and date the form. | Upon completion of screening. |
| 5.3.4 | Clinical Manager | Review and approve all clinical eligibility determinations for complex or borderline cases including: (a) patients with behavioral health diagnoses as primary condition; (b) patients with homebound status concerns; (c) patients requiring services the agency has limited experience delivering; (d) patients with active wound vac, TPN, or complex infusion needs. | Within 4 hours of escalation. |

### 5.4 Referral Acceptance and Admission Initiation

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.4.1 | Intake Coordinator | Upon clinical eligibility confirmation and payer verification, update referral status to ACCEPTED. | Immediately upon approval. |
| 5.4.2 | Intake Coordinator | Notify the referral source of acceptance verbally and in writing. Provide: (a) estimated SOC date; (b) agency contact information; (c) after-hours contact number. | Within 2 hours of acceptance (Urgent) / within 24 hours (Routine). |
| 5.4.3 | Intake Coordinator | Notify the patient and/or family/caregiver of acceptance: (a) introduce the agency; (b) provide the estimated SOC visit date and time window; (c) advise of patient rights notification (to be delivered at SOC); (d) collect any additional information needed for the SOC visit. | Within 4 hours of acceptance (Urgent) / within 24 hours (Routine). |
| 5.4.4 | Intake Coordinator | Prepare and transmit the complete intake packet to the assigned SOC clinician: (a) referral documents; (b) clinical screening results; (c) payer/authorization information; (d) patient demographics and contact information; (e) advance directive status; (f) medication list; (g) any special instructions (interpreter needs, access instructions, safety concerns). | Prior to SOC visit; minimum 12 hours before scheduled visit. |
| 5.4.5 | Operations Director / Scheduling | Schedule the SOC visit per CMS requirements: (a) URGENT — within 24 hours of acceptance or as medically indicated; (b) ROUTINE — within 48 hours of physician order date. Coordinate with the assigned clinician and confirm with the patient. | Per urgency and CMS requirements. |

### 5.5 Referral Pending Management

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.5.1 | Intake Coordinator | If a referral cannot be accepted or declined within the standard timeframe due to missing information, pending physician orders, or payer verification delays, update status to PENDING with documented reason. | At the point the standard timeframe is exceeded. |
| 5.5.2 | Intake Coordinator | Follow up on all pending items daily. Document each follow-up attempt including date, time, method, and outcome. | Daily until resolved; minimum 1 documented attempt per business day. |
| 5.5.3 | Operations Director | Review all referrals in PENDING status exceeding 72 hours. Escalate to the Administrator if resolution is not achieved within 5 business days. | Every 72 hours; escalation at 5 business days. |
| 5.5.4 | Intake Coordinator | Notify the referral source of pending status and expected resolution timeline. | Within 24 hours of status change to PENDING; updates every 48 hours thereafter. |

### 5.6 Referral Declination

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.6.1 | Intake RN / Clinical Manager | Document the specific reason for declining the referral on the Referral Declination Form (Appendix B). Acceptable reasons include: (a) patient outside service area; (b) agency lacks clinical competency or licensure for required services; (c) insufficient staffing to safely provide services; (d) patient does not meet home health eligibility criteria; (e) no valid physician order obtainable; (f) patient/family declines services. Non-acceptable reasons: diagnosis type, payer source (except non-contracted Medicaid managed care with documented non-participation), race, ethnicity, gender, age, disability, or socioeconomic status. | At the time of declination decision. |
| 5.6.2 | Intake Coordinator | Notify the referral source of the declination: (a) provide the specific reason; (b) offer alternative provider information where available; (c) document notification including date, time, method, person notified, and alternative information provided. | Within 24 hours of declination decision. |
| 5.6.3 | Intake Coordinator | Notify the patient and/or family of the declination and provide information about how to access services from other providers or contact the State Home Health Hotline. | Within 24 hours of declination decision. |
| 5.6.4 | Operations Director | Review all declination reports weekly. Analyze declination trends monthly for patterns indicating systemic issues (staffing shortages, service area gaps, training needs). Report findings to the Administrator monthly and to QAPI quarterly. | Weekly review; monthly analysis; quarterly QAPI reporting. |

### 5.7 Escalation and Exception Handling

| Condition | Escalation Path | Corrective Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Urgent referral not processed within 2 hours | Intake Coordinator → Operations Director immediately | Operations Director assumes direct management; assigns clinical screener; documents delay and root cause. | Resolution within 4 hours. |
| Routine referral not processed within 24 hours | Intake Coordinator → Operations Director | Operations Director reviews cause, reassigns if needed, notifies referral source of delay. | Resolution within 48 hours. |
| Referral pending > 5 business days without resolution | Operations Director → Administrator | Administrator contacts referral source/physician directly; determination made to accept, decline, or extend with documented justification. | Resolution within 7 business days. |
| Pattern of declinations exceeding 10% of referrals received in any 30-day period | Operations Director → Administrator → QAPI Committee | Root cause analysis initiated; staffing, competency, or service area expansion evaluated; corrective action plan within 30 days. | Analysis within 14 days; corrective plan within 30 days. |
| Allegation of discriminatory referral practices | Any staff → Compliance Officer | Compliance investigation per CO-CP-007; report to Governing Body if substantiated. | Investigation within 7 days of report. |

## 7. Compliance Monitoring
### 7.1 How Compliance Is Measured

| Compliance Indicator | Measurement Method | Acceptable Standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All referrals logged at time of receipt with complete data | Monthly audit of referral log (10% random sample or minimum 20 referrals) | ≥ 95% compliance |
| Urgent referrals processed within 2 hours | Referral tracking system timestamps | ≥ 98% compliance |
| Routine referrals processed within 24 hours | Referral tracking system timestamps | ≥ 95% compliance |
| Clinical eligibility screening completed and documented for all referrals | Audit of Appendix A forms | 100% compliance |
| Declination reasons documented and compliant with non-discrimination requirements | Monthly review of Appendix B forms | 100% compliance |
| Referral source and patient notification completed within defined timeframes | Audit of notification documentation | ≥ 95% compliance |
| Pending referrals followed up daily | Referral tracking system documentation | 100% compliance |
| Declination rate monitored and reported to QAPI | Quarterly QAPI reports | Reporting compliance 100% |

### 7.2 Common Failure Points

| Failure Point | Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Referrals received but not logged immediately | Lost referrals; patient safety risk; CMS citation for access issues | Mandate real-time logging; daily reconciliation of all referral channels |
| Clinical screening not performed or not documented | Inappropriate admissions or missed eligibility issues; survey finding | Require Appendix A completion for every referral; weekly audit |
| Declination without documented reason | Appearance of discriminatory practices; complaint investigation | Require Appendix B for every declination; Operations Director weekly review |
| SOC visit not scheduled within required timeframe | Delayed care; patient harm; CMS citation | Daily scheduling review; escalation protocol for unscheduled SOCs |
| Intake packet incomplete or not transmitted to SOC clinician | Clinician unprepared; assessment quality compromised | Checklist verification before transmission; SOC clinician confirmation |

## 10. Training Requirements
10.1 All intake staff shall receive training on this policy within 14 calendar days of hire or assignment to intake functions. Training shall cover: (a) referral logging requirements; (b) urgency classification; (c) clinical screening process; (d) payer verification procedures; (e) non-discrimination requirements; (f) declination documentation and notification requirements; (g) escalation protocols.
10.2 Annual refresher training shall be conducted for all intake staff and documented per HR-TD-001.
10.3 All staff completing this training shall sign the Policy Acknowledgment Form (Appendix C) within 14 calendar days of training completion or policy revision.

## 1. Purpose
This policy defines the clinical, operational, and regulatory criteria that must be met for a patient to be accepted for admission to Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. This policy ensures that every admission decision is based on objective, non-discriminatory criteria and that the agency only accepts patients for whom it can provide safe, effective, and compliant care per 42 CFR § 484.50 (Patient Rights), 42 CFR § 484.55 (Comprehensive Assessment), and 42 CFR § 484.105 (Organization and Administration).
## 2. Scope
This policy applies to all intake staff, clinical screeners, the Operations Director, the Director of Nursing/Clinical Manager, the Administrator, and any individual involved in patient acceptance decisions.
## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Acceptance Criteria | The objective clinical, operational, and regulatory requirements that must be satisfied before a patient is admitted to home health services. |
| Medical Necessity | The requirement that services ordered are reasonable and necessary for the treatment of the patient's illness or injury, consistent with accepted standards of medical practice. |
| Homebound Status | A patient qualifies as homebound if leaving the home requires a considerable and taxing effort due to illness or injury, as defined by CMS and 42 CFR § 484.55. |
| Skilled Need | The requirement for services that can only be safely and effectively provided by or under the supervision of a licensed professional. |

## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall accept patients for admission only when all of the following criteria are met: (a) the patient has a qualifying skilled need; (b) the patient is homebound or expected to meet homebound criteria; (c) a physician is available to certify and order home health services; (d) the services required are within the agency's scope, licensure, and competency; (e) the agency has adequate staffing to deliver services safely; (f) the patient's residence is within the agency's service area; (g) payer coverage has been verified or the patient has agreed to financial responsibility.
4.2 The agency shall not discriminate in patient acceptance decisions based on race, color, national origin, sex, age, disability, religion, sexual orientation, gender identity, diagnosis, or source of payment except where the agency is not a participating provider in the patient's insurance plan.
4.3 The agency shall not refuse admission based solely on the complexity of the patient's condition if the agency has the clinical competency and staffing to provide the required services safely.
4.4 All acceptance decisions shall be documented with the specific criteria evaluated and the rationale for the decision.
4.5 Patients who do not meet acceptance criteria at the time of referral but may qualify in the future (e.g., pending physician orders, pending insurance enrollment) shall be managed under the pending referral process per OP-IM-001.
## 5. Procedures
### 5.1 Acceptance Criteria Evaluation

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |

| 5.1.1 | Intake RN | Evaluate the patient against each acceptance criterion using the Patient Acceptance Criteria Checklist (Appendix A). All criteria must be evaluated; no criterion may be omitted. | During clinical eligibility screening per OP-IM-001. |
| 5.1.2 | Intake RN | For each criterion, document one of three findings: MET, NOT MET (with explanation), or PENDING (with action plan). | At time of evaluation. |
| 5.1.3 | Intake RN | If all criteria are MET, recommend acceptance and proceed to admission per OP-IM-001 §5.4. | Immediately upon determination. |
| 5.1.4 | Clinical Manager | If any criterion is NOT MET or if the case involves clinical complexity, the Clinical Manager shall review the determination and make the final acceptance/declination decision. | Within 4 hours of referral. |
| 5.1.5 | Operations Director | If acceptance requires staffing redistribution or service area exception, obtain approval from the Operations Director prior to confirming acceptance. | Within 24 hours. |

## 1. Purpose
This policy defines the geographic boundaries within which Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. provides home health services and establishes standards for ensuring adequate coverage throughout the defined service area. CMS requires that a home health agency serve patients within a defined geographic area and that the agency be operationally capable of providing timely, consistent services throughout that area per 42 CFR § 484.105.
## 2. Scope
This policy applies to the Governing Body, Administrator, Operations Director, all intake staff, scheduling staff, and clinical staff.
## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Service Area | The geographic region, defined by counties, ZIP codes, or other boundaries, within which the agency provides home health services as identified in its Medicare enrollment and state licensure. |
| Primary Service Area | The core geographic zone where the agency maintains consistent staffing and can meet all visit frequency and timeliness requirements. |
| Extended Service Area | Geographic zones outside the primary area that the agency may serve on a case-by-case basis subject to staffing availability and Operational Director approval. |
| Service Area Exception | An approved deviation from the standard service area for a specific patient, documented and time-limited. |

## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a clearly defined service area that is: (a) documented and approved by the Governing Body; (b) consistent with the agency's Medicare enrollment application (CMS-855A); (c) consistent with the agency's California state home health license; (d) supported by adequate staffing to serve the entire area.
4.2 The service area shall be reviewed annually by the Operations Director and approved by the Administrator. Any changes to the service area require Governing Body notification.
4.3 The agency shall not accept patients residing outside the defined service area unless a service area exception is approved by the Operations Director.
4.4 The agency shall monitor service delivery performance across the service area to identify gaps in coverage and address them through staffing adjustments, scheduling optimization, or service area modification.

## 1. Purpose
This policy establishes standards for scheduling, managing, tracking, and documenting patient visits at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Effective scheduling is essential for ensuring physician-ordered visit frequencies are met, patient care is timely, LUPA risk is minimized, and CMS visit compliance is maintained per 42 CFR § 484.60 (Care Planning) and 42 CFR § 484.105 (Organization and Administration__
## 2. Scope
This policy applies to all scheduling staff, the Operations Director, the Director of Nursing/Clinical Manager, all clinical staff assigned patient visits, and any contracted or per diem staff delivering visits.
## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Visit Frequency | The number, type, and schedule of visits ordered by the physician in the plan of care. |
| LUPA | Low Utilization Payment Adjustment — a reduced payment applied when the number of visits in a 30-day period falls below the CMS threshold.__ |
| Missed Visit | A scheduled visit that was not completed on the scheduled date due to any cause (clinician, patient, or operational). |
| Visit Compliance Rate | The percentage of physician-ordered visits that are actually delivered within the ordered timeframe. |
| SOC Window | The CMS-defined timeframe within which the Start of Care assessment must be completed (within 5 calendar days of the referral date or within 5 calendar days of the patient's return home). |

## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 All patient visits shall be scheduled in accordance with the physician-ordered visit frequency and disciplines specified in the plan of care.
4.2 The agency shall maintain a centralized scheduling system that provides real-time visibility into all scheduled, completed, and missed visits.
4.3 SOC visits shall be scheduled within 48 hours of the physician order date (routine) or within 24 hours (urgent) to comply with CMS timely initiation requirements.
4.4 All visits shall be scheduled with patient notification at least 24 hours in advance (except for same-day urgent visits). The patient shall be informed of the expected visit date and approximate time window.
4.5 Visit compliance shall be monitored daily. Any variance from the ordered frequency shall be identified, documented, and corrected within 24 hours.
4.6 The scheduling process shall incorporate LUPA risk monitoring per FN-CM-005 and QA-PI-006.
4.7 Clinician assignments shall consider: (a) clinical competency matched to patient needs; (b) geographic proximity; (c) continuity of care (same clinician where feasible); (d) caseload balance.

## 1. Purpose
This policy establishes the agency's requirement to provide 24-hour, 7-day-a-week availability of services as required by 42 CFR § 484.50(a) and 42 CFR § 484.105. Patients have the right to receive care and services 24 hours a day, 7 days a week. The agency must ensure after-hours clinical response capability for all patients on active service.
## 2. Scope
This policy applies to the Operations Director, the Director of Nursing/Clinical Manager, all clinical staff participating in on-call rotations, the scheduling coordinator, and the Administrator.
## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| After-Hours | Any time outside the agency's standard business hours, including weekends and holidays. |
| On-Call | A designated period during which a qualified clinician is available by telephone and, when necessary, available to make home visits within defined response timeframes. |
| On-Call Clinician | A licensed registered nurse or therapist designated to provide after-hours triage, telephonic guidance, and emergency home visits. |
| Clinical Triage | The process of evaluating a patient's symptoms and needs via telephone to determine appropriate response (telephonic guidance, scheduled visit, urgent visit, or emergency referral). |

## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall provide 24-hour, 7-day-a-week availability of clinical services to all patients on active service, including weekends and holidays, per 42 CFR § 484.50(a).
4.2 After-hours availability shall include: (a) a live-answer or prompt call-back telephone response (call-back within 30 minutes maximum); (b) telephonic clinical triage by a licensed clinician; (c) the ability to dispatch a clinician for an in-person visit when clinically indicated.
4.3 All patients and caregivers shall be informed of the agency's after-hours contact number at the time of admission and at each subsequent visit. The after-hours number shall be prominently documented in the patient's home health folder.
4.4 On-call clinicians shall have access to patient clinical records sufficient to provide safe, informed clinical guidance and care.
4.5 All after-hours contacts, triage decisions, and interventions shall be documented in the patient's clinical record.

## 1. Purpose
This policy defines the clinical and operational protocols for maintaining home health services during declared public health emergencies including pandemics, infectious disease outbreaks, and other public health crises. ## 2. Scope
All agency staff, the Operations Director, the Director of Nursing/Clinical Manager, the Administrator, and the Governing Body.
## 3. Policy Statements
3.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain the ability to deliver essential home health services during public health emergencies, adapting service delivery methods as necessary while maintaining patient safety and regulatory compliance.
3.2 The agency shall prioritize patient care based on acuity, safety, and medical necessity during periods of reduced capacity.
3.3 Telehealth and remote monitoring may be utilized as adjuncts to in-person visits during public health emergencies when authorized by CMS and consistent with CL-SD-009.
3.4 Staff safety protocols, including PPE requirements, shall be enhanced during public health emergencies per RM-EP-001 and CL-SD-016.

## 1. Purpose
This policy defines the process for receiving, documenting, investigating, and resolving patient complaints and grievances at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. per 42 CFR § 484.50(a)(3) which requires that patients be informed of and have access to a complaint process and that the agency investigate and resolve complaints.
## 2. Scope
All agency staff, the Operations Director, the Director of Nursing/Clinical Manager, the Administrator, the Compliance Officer, and the QAPI Coordinator.
## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Complaint | Any expression of dissatisfaction by a patient, family member, caregiver, or patient representative regarding any aspect of care or services provided by the agency. |
| Grievance | A formal complaint that has been documented and requires investigation and formal resolution. All written complaints and any verbal complaint not resolved at the time of the initial contact are classified as grievances. |
| Complainant | The individual filing the complaint or grievance (patient, family member, caregiver, or authorized representative). |

## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a formal complaint and grievance resolution process that is accessible to all patients and their representatives.
4.2 All patients shall be informed of their right to file complaints at the time of admission and provided with: (a) the agency's internal complaint process; (b) the State Home Health Hotline number; (c) the CMS 1-800-MEDICARE number; (d) contact information for the California Department of Public Health.
4.3 No patient shall experience retaliation, reduction in services, or negative consequences for filing a complaint or grievance.
4.4 All complaints shall be acknowledged within 24 hours. All grievances shall be investigated and resolved within 30 calendar days. If resolution requires more than 30 days, the complainant shall receive written notification of the status and expected completion date.
4.5 Complaint and grievance data shall be trended, analyzed, and reported to QAPI quarterly per QA-SM-003.
## 5. Procedures
### 5.1 Complaint Receipt and Logging

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1.1 | Any Staff Member | Upon receiving a complaint from any source, immediately document: (a) complainant name and contact; (b) patient name; (c) date and time; (d) nature of complaint; (e) desired resolution. Forward to the Operations Director. | At time of receipt. |
| 5.1.2 | Operations Director | Log the complaint in the Complaint & Grievance Tracking Log (Appendix A). Assign a unique tracking number. Classify as: (a) INFORMAL COMPLAINT — resolved at the point of initial contact; (b) FORMAL GRIEVANCE — requires investigation. | Within 4 hours of receipt. |
| 5.1.3 | Operations Director | Acknowledge receipt to the complainant: INFORMAL — verbal acknowledgment at the time of resolution; FORMAL — written acknowledgment within 24 hours (via mail, email, or hand-delivery). | Within 24 hours. |

### 5.2 Investigation

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.2.1 | Operations Director (or designee) | Investigate all formal grievances. Investigation shall include: (a) review of the patient's clinical record; (b) interviews with involved staff; (c) review of relevant policies and procedures; (d) consultation with the Clinical Manager for clinical concerns; (e) consultation with the Compliance Officer for compliance concerns. | Investigation initiated within 72 hours; completed within 21 calendar days. |
| 5.2.2 | Operations Director | Document investigation findings on the Complaint Investigation & Resolution Form (Appendix B). | Upon completion of investigation. |
| 5.2.3 | Operations Director | If the investigation reveals a substantiated clinical concern, notify the Clinical Manager and initiate corrective action per applicable policies. If the investigation reveals potential compliance violation, notify the Compliance Officer per CO-CP-007. If the investigation reveals potential abuse, neglect, or exploitation, report per CL-PR-006. | Immediately upon identification. |

### 5.3 Resolution and Notification

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.3.1 | Operations Director | Determine the resolution. Options include: (a) corrective action with involved staff; (b) process improvement implementation; (c) additional staff training; (d) service modification; (e) formal apology; (f) finding unsubstantiated (with explanation). | Within 30 calendar days of initial complaint. |

## 1. Purpose
This policy establishes standards for verifying patient identity at each home health visit to prevent service delivery errors, medication errors, and misidentification. Patient identification is a National Patient Safety Goal (NPSG) and a fundamental requirement of safe healthcare delivery.
## 2. Policy Statements
2.1 All clinical staff shall verify the identity of the patient using at least two patient identifiers before providing any care, treatment, or services at each visit.
2.2 Acceptable identifiers include: (a) patient's full legal name; (b) date of birth; (c) medical record number; (d) home address. Room number, bed number, or physical location are NOT acceptable identifiers.
2.3 If identity cannot be verified, the clinician shall not proceed with care delivery until verification is achieved.
## 3. Procedures

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3.1 | All Clinical Staff | At the start of each visit, ask the patient (or caregiver if patient cannot respond) to state their full name and date of birth. Compare responses to the patient record. | At each visit, before care delivery. |
| 3.2 | All Clinical Staff | Document patient identity verification in the visit note. | At each visit. |
| 3.3 | All Clinical Staff | If the patient or caregiver cannot verify identity, use alternative methods: (a) check identification documents; (b) verify against photograph (if available in record); (c) contact the agency office for verification. | At point of concern. |
| 3.4 | All Clinical Staff | If identity cannot be confirmed, do not deliver services. Notify the Clinical Manager immediately. Document the incident per RM-ER-002. | Immediately. |

| 3.3 | All Clinical Staff | For LEP patients, arrange interpreter services BEFORE the visit. Do not attempt to deliver clinical care, obtain consent, or provide patient education without qualified interpretation. | Before each visit with LEP patient. |
| 3.4 | All Clinical Staff | Document interpreter use in the visit note including: (a) language interpreted; (b) interpreter name and ID number; (c) method (in-person, phone, video). | At each visit using interpreter. |

## 1. Purpose
This policy establishes standards for the selection, qualification, contracting, monitoring, and performance evaluation of vendors and suppliers providing goods, services, or contracted functions to Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Effective vendor management ensures the agency receives quality products and services, maintains regulatory compliance (including anti-kickback and Stark Law requirements per CO-FA-001), protects patient safety, controls costs, and satisfies applicable requirements of 42 CFR § 484.105 (Organization and Administration), 42 CFR § 484.100 (Compliance with Federal, State, and Local Laws), and OIG Compliance Program Guidance for Home Health Agencies.
## 2. Scope
This policy applies to:
The Operations Director
The Administrator
The Compliance Officer
The CFO / Finance Director
All department heads or staff authorized to initiate vendor relationships or procurement
All current and prospective vendors, suppliers, and contracted service providers
This policy does not govern employment relationships (see HR-WM-002 for contractors and per diem staff) or Business Associate Agreements for HIPAA purposes (see CO-HP-005), though vendor qualification under this policy may be concurrent with BAA execution.
## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Vendor | Any external individual, company, or organization that provides goods, services, or contracted functions to the agency in exchange for compensation. |
| Supplier | A vendor that provides physical products including medical supplies, equipment, office supplies, or technology hardware. |
| Contracted Service Provider | A vendor that provides professional or operational services including staffing agencies, interpreter services, answering services, IT managed services, consulting, or waste management. |
| Vendor Qualification | The process of evaluating a vendor's credentials, capabilities, compliance status, and suitability before entering into a business relationship. |
| Approved Vendor List (AVL) | The agency's maintained list of vendors that have been qualified, approved, and authorized for use. |
| Vendor Performance Evaluation | A periodic assessment of a vendor's adherence to contractual requirements, quality standards, timeliness, and regulatory compliance. |
| Exclusion Screening | Verification that a vendor (and its principals) does not appear on the OIG List of Excluded Individuals/Entities (LEIE) or the System for Award Management (SAM) exclusion database. |

## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain an Approved Vendor List (AVL) of all vendors and suppliers authorized to provide goods or services to the agency.
4.2 No vendor shall be engaged, and no purchase order or contract shall be executed, until the vendor has been qualified through the agency's vendor qualification process, except for emergency procurements under Section 5.6.
4.3 All vendor relationships shall be documented through written contracts or purchase agreements that define: (a) scope of goods or services; (b) pricing and payment terms; (c) quality and performance standards; (d) compliance requirements; (e) termination provisions; (f) insurance and liability requirements; (g) Business Associate Agreement (if vendor will access PHI).
4.4 The agency shall not enter into any vendor relationship that could constitute a violation of the Anti-Kickback Statute or Stark Law. All vendor relationships involving referral sources, physicians, or healthcare entities shall be reviewed by the Compliance Officer before execution per CO-FA-001.
4.5 All vendors providing goods or services paid directly or indirectly by federal healthcare programs shall be screened against the OIG LEIE and SAM exclusion databases prior to engagement and monthly thereafter per HR-TA-003.
4.6 Vendor performance shall be evaluated at least annually. Vendors failing to meet performance standards shall be placed on corrective action or removed from the AVL.
4.7 The agency shall maintain vendor records for a minimum of 7 years per CO-HP-007.
## 5. Procedures
### 5.1 Vendor Identification and Selection

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1.1 | Requesting Department Head | Identify the need for a vendor or supplier. Submit a Vendor Request Form (Appendix A) to the Operations Director including: (a) description of goods or services needed; (b) estimated annual spend; (c) any specific vendor candidates; (d) clinical or patient safety implications; (e) urgency level. | At time of identified need. |
| 5.1.2 | Operations Director | Review the request. If estimated annual spend exceeds $5,000, obtain at least 2 competitive quotes or proposals. If spend exceeds $25,000, obtain at least 3 competitive quotes and involve the CFO in evaluation. | Within 7 business days of request receipt. |
| 5.1.3 | Operations Director | Evaluate vendor candidates based on: (a) product/service quality; (b) pricing competitiveness; (c) references and reputation; (d) regulatory compliance history; (e) geographic service capability; (f) financial stability; (g) insurance coverage adequacy. | Within 14 business days. |
| 5.1.4 | Operations Director | Select the preferred vendor and proceed to qualification (Section 5.2). Document the selection rationale. | Upon completion of evaluation. |

### 5.2 Vendor Qualification

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.2.1 | Operations Director | Collect vendor qualification documentation using the Vendor Qualification Checklist (Appendix B): (a) W-9 or tax identification; (b) business license or registration; (c) professional licenses or certifications (if applicable); (d) certificate of insurance (general liability, professional liability, workers' compensation as applicable); (e) references (minimum 2 current clients); (f) compliance certifications or attestations; (g) product certifications or FDA registrations (for medical suppliers). | Within 14 business days of selection. |
| 5.2.2 | Compliance Officer | Perform exclusion screening: (a) verify vendor entity and principals against OIG LEIE; (b) verify against SAM exclusion database; (c) document results on the Vendor Qualification Checklist. If the vendor or any principal is excluded, the vendor shall not be engaged. | Within 7 business days of receiving vendor information. |
| 5.2.3 | Compliance Officer | For vendors involving referral sources, physicians, or healthcare entities: review the proposed relationship for Anti-Kickback and Stark Law compliance per CO-FA-001. Document the review and determination. | Within 14 business days; prior to contract execution. |
| 5.2.4 | Operations Director | If the vendor will access, create, receive, maintain, or transmit PHI, coordinate with the Compliance Officer to execute a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) per CO-HP-005 prior to any PHI access. | Prior to vendor access to PHI. |
| 5.2.5 | Operations Director | Upon successful qualification, add the vendor to the Approved Vendor List (Appendix C) with: (a) vendor name and contact; (b) goods/services provided; (c) contract effective date; (d) qualification date; (e) next review date; (f) exclusion screening dates. | Upon completion of qualification. |

### 5.3 Contract Execution

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.3.1 | Operations Director / CFO | Execute a written contract or purchase agreement with all qualified vendors providing recurring goods or services exceeding $2,500 annually. The contract must include: (a) detailed scope of goods or services; (b) pricing, payment terms, and invoice requirements; (c) quality and performance standards; (d) compliance with all applicable laws and regulations; (e) requirement to notify agency of any exclusion, sanction, or material change; (f) right to audit records related to services provided; (g) termination provisions (for cause and without cause); (h) insurance requirements and indemnification; (i) confidentiality and PHI protection (BAA if applicable); (j) dispute resolution mechanism. | Prior to commencement of services. |
| 5.3.2 | Administrator | Approve all contracts with an annual value exceeding $25,000. Contracts exceeding $100,000 require Governing Body notification. | Prior to contract execution. |
| 5.3.3 | Operations Director | Maintain the original executed contract in the vendor file. Provide a copy to the CFO for financial tracking. | At time of execution. |

### 5.4 Ongoing Vendor Monitoring

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.4.1 | Compliance Officer | Screen all active vendors against the OIG LEIE and SAM exclusion databases monthly. Document screening results. If a vendor is found to be excluded, immediately notify the Operations Director and Administrator. Cease all transactions with the excluded vendor within 24 hours. | Monthly; immediate action upon exclusion finding. |
| 5.4.2 | Operations Director | Verify that all vendor insurance certificates remain current. Request updated certificates at least 30 days before expiration. Suspend services from any vendor with lapsed insurance. | 30 days before expiration; suspension upon lapse. |
| 5.4.3 | Operations Director / Requesting Department | Monitor vendor performance on an ongoing basis. Document any quality issues, delivery failures, compliance concerns, or service interruptions as they occur using the Vendor Performance Issue Log (Appendix D). | Continuous; documented at time of occurrence. |

### 5.5 Annual Vendor Performance Evaluation

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.5.1 | Operations Director | Conduct an annual performance evaluation for all vendors on the AVL using the Vendor Performance Evaluation Form (Appendix E). Evaluate: (a) quality of goods/services; (b) timeliness of delivery; (c) responsiveness to issues; (d) pricing competitiveness; (e) compliance and exclusion screening results; (f) insurance currency; (g) overall satisfaction. | Annually; within 60 days of contract anniversary or fiscal year end. |
| 5.5.2 | Operations Director | Assign a performance rating: SATISFACTORY — Vendor meets or exceeds expectations. Continue relationship. NEEDS IMPROVEMENT — Vendor has documented deficiencies. Issue a Vendor Corrective Action Notice (Appendix F) with a 30-day improvement timeline. UNSATISFACTORY — Vendor has critical or repeated deficiencies. Initiate vendor replacement and removal from the AVL. | At time of evaluation. |
| 5.5.3 | Operations Director | Report vendor performance evaluation results to the Administrator annually. Include recommendations for vendor changes, cost savings opportunities, or quality improvements. | Annually; within 30 days of evaluation completion. |

### 5.6 Emergency Procurement

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.6.1 | Operations Director | In emergency situations where immediate procurement is necessary to prevent harm to patients or ensure continuity of care, the Operations Director may authorize a single-source procurement without competitive bidding, provided: (a) the emergency is documented; (b) the vendor is screened against OIG/SAM before or within 24 hours of engagement; (c) the procurement amount does not exceed $10,000; (d) the Administrator is notified within 24 hours; (e) the vendor is subsequently qualified through the standard process within 30 days if the relationship will continue. | At time of emergency; follow-up qualification within 30 days. |
| 5.6.2 | Operations Director | Document the emergency procurement on the Emergency Procurement Authorization Form (Appendix G). | Within 24 hours of procurement. |

### 5.7 Vendor Termination

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.7.1 | Operations Director | Terminate vendor relationships when: (a) the vendor receives an UNSATISFACTORY performance rating and does not improve; (b) the vendor or its principals are found on the OIG/SAM exclusion list; (c) the vendor fails to maintain required insurance; (d) the vendor violates contract terms; (e) the vendor poses a risk to patient safety; (f) the agency no longer requires the vendor's goods or services. | Per contract termination provisions; immediate for exclusion or patient safety. |
| 5.7.2 | Operations Director | Document the termination reason. Notify the vendor in writing. Remove the vendor from the AVL. Ensure a replacement vendor is identified and qualified before termination if the goods/services are essential to operations. | Written notification per contract terms. |
| 5.7.3 | Operations Director | Retain terminated vendor records for minimum 7 years per CO-HP-007. | At time of termination. |

### 7.2 Common Failure Points

| Failure Point | Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vendor engaged without qualification or exclusion screening | Federal program integrity risk; potential fraud liability; OIG sanction | Enforce AVL policy; no PO/payment without AVL status |
| Expired vendor insurance | Liability exposure for agency | Automated tracking with 30-day renewal reminders |

## 1. Purpose
This policy establishes standards for the receipt, processing, tracking, routing, and retention of all incoming and outgoing mail and official correspondence at Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. Proper mail management supports timely response to regulatory communications, physician orders, payer correspondence, patient communications, and legal documents. This policy ensures the agency meets documentation and communication requirements under 42 CFR § 484.105 (Organization and Administration) and HIPAA privacy requirements for handling correspondence containing Protected Health Information (PHI) per CO-HP-001.
## 2. Scope
This policy applies to:
All office staff responsible for mail handling
The Operations Director
The Administrator
The Compliance Officer (for regulatory and legal correspondence)
All department heads responsible for responding to correspondence within their functional area
This policy covers: (a) U.S. Postal Service mail; (b) courier/overnight deliveries (FedEx, UPS, etc.); (c) secure fax communications; (d) official agency correspondence sent externally. This policy does not govern electronic email (see OP-SL-005 and IT-UP-002) or clinical documentation transmission (see CL-CD-001).
## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Official Correspondence | Any written communication sent or received by the agency in its official capacity including regulatory notices, legal documents, payer communications, physician communications, and patient correspondence. |
| Time-Sensitive Correspondence | Mail or correspondence requiring response or action within a defined deadline, including CMS survey notifications, legal notices, payer denial letters, and physician orders. |
| PHI-Containing Correspondence | Any mail or document that contains patient-identifiable health information subject to HIPAA privacy protections. |
| Mail Log | The agency's record of all incoming and outgoing official correspondence including date, sender/recipient, type, and routing. |
| Regulatory Correspondence | Communications from or to CMS, the Medicare Administrative Contractor (MAC), California HCAI, the OIG, or any regulatory or accrediting body. |

## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain an organized system for receiving, logging, routing, and responding to all incoming mail and official correspondence.
4.2 All incoming mail shall be opened, sorted, logged, and routed to the appropriate department or individual on the day of receipt (or next business day if received on a weekend or holiday).
4.3 Time-sensitive correspondence shall be flagged, prioritized, and delivered to the responsible party immediately upon identification, with escalation if the responsible party is unavailable.
4.4 All correspondence containing PHI shall be handled, routed, and stored in compliance with HIPAA privacy requirements per CO-HP-001 and CO-HP-004.
4.5 All outgoing official correspondence shall be reviewed, approved, and documented before transmission.
4.6 The agency shall maintain a mail log for all incoming and outgoing official correspondence to support audit trails and regulatory compliance.
4.7 Fax communications containing PHI shall be transmitted only through the agency's secure fax system per OP-SL-005 and CO-HP-002.
## 5. Procedures
### 5.1 Incoming Mail Processing

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1.1 | Designated Mail Handler | Collect all mail from the agency mailbox, courier deliveries, and secure fax queue each business day. | Daily by 10:00 AM. |
| 5.1.2 | Designated Mail Handler | Open and sort all incoming mail. Classify each item: (a) REGULATORY — from CMS, MAC, HCAI, OIG, accrediting body; (b) LEGAL — from attorneys, courts, law enforcement; (c) CLINICAL — physician orders, referrals, F2F documentation, lab results; (d) FINANCIAL — payer correspondence, EOBs, denial letters, payment notices; (e) PATIENT — patient correspondence, complaints, records requests; (f) GENERAL — office supplies, vendor communications, advertising. | At time of opening. |
| 5.1.3 | Designated Mail Handler | Log all incoming mail in the Incoming Mail Log (Appendix A). For each item, record: (a) date received; (b) sender name and organization; (c) classification; (d) brief description; (e) routed to (department/individual); (f) time-sensitive flag (yes/no); (g) response deadline (if identifiable). | At time of processing. |
| 5.1.4 | Designated Mail Handler | Flag and immediately deliver all time-sensitive correspondence to the responsible party: REGULATORY — Administrator and Compliance Officer immediately; LEGAL — Administrator immediately; Compliance Officer if compliance-related; CLINICAL (physician orders) — Clinical Manager / Intake immediately; FINANCIAL (denial letters with appeal deadlines) — CFO / Billing immediately. If the responsible party is unavailable, deliver to their designated backup. | Immediately upon identification. |
| 5.1.5 | Designated Mail Handler | Route all remaining correspondence to the appropriate department by end of the business day. Place in designated departmental mailboxes or deliver directly. | By end of business day. |
| 5.1.6 | Designated Mail Handler | Handle all PHI-containing correspondence in compliance with HIPAA: (a) do not leave PHI-containing mail unattended in open areas; (b) route directly to the responsible party in a sealed envelope or folder if hand-delivering; (c) log PHI-containing items with sufficient identifying information for tracking without exposing PHI in the mail log. | At all times. |

## 1. Purpose
This policy establishes the agency's comprehensive Emergency Preparedness Plan, Emergency Operations, and Business Continuity framework for Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. as required by 42 CFR § 484.102 — Condition of Participation: Emergency Preparedness. CMS requires all Medicare- and Medicaid-participating home health agencies to develop and maintain a comprehensive emergency preparedness program based on an all-hazards approach. This policy ensures the agency can: (a) protect the safety and well-being of patients and staff during emergencies; (b) maintain continuity of essential services; (c) communicate effectively with patients, staff, external authorities, and the community; (d) recover operations following a disaster or emergency; (e) comply with all applicable federal, state, and local emergency preparedness requirements.
## 2. Scope
This policy applies to:
The Governing Body
The Administrator
The Operations Director
The Director of Nursing / Clinical Manager
The Compliance Officer
The IT Director / CISO
All clinical and administrative staff
All contracted or per diem staff
All patients and caregivers served by the agency
This policy applies to all types of emergencies including but not limited to: natural disasters (earthquakes, wildfires, floods, severe storms), technological hazards (power outages, cyberattacks, EHR system failure), public health emergencies (pandemics, infectious disease outbreaks per OP-SL-006), workplace violence, active threats, and any event that disrupts normal agency operations.
## 3. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| --- | --- |
| Emergency | Any event, actual or imminent, that threatens the safety of patients, staff, or the public, or that disrupts the agency's ability to deliver services. |
| All-Hazards Approach | An integrated approach to emergency preparedness that focuses on capacities and capabilities critical to preparedness for a full spectrum of emergencies rather than planning for specific types of events. |
| Emergency Preparedness Plan (EPP) | The agency's written comprehensive plan addressing emergency response, continuity of operations, communication, and training per 42 CFR § 484.102. |
| Emergency Management Team (EMT) | The designated group of agency leaders responsible for directing the emergency response and business continuity operations. |
| Business Continuity Plan (BCP) | The component of the EPP that addresses maintaining or restoring essential agency operations during and after an emergency. |
| Incident Commander | The individual designated to lead the agency's emergency response for a specific incident. Default: Administrator; alternate: Operations Director. |
| Essential Functions | Agency operations that must be maintained during an emergency to ensure patient safety and regulatory compliance, including: patient care services for critical patients, medication management, on-call services, and communication with patients and authorities. |
| Hazard Vulnerability Analysis (HVA) | A systematic assessment of potential hazards, their likelihood, and the agency's vulnerability to each hazard, used to prioritize preparedness activities. |
| Recovery Time Objective (RTO) | The maximum acceptable time to restore essential functions after a disruption. |

## 4. Policy Statements
4.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall develop, maintain, and implement a comprehensive, all-hazards Emergency Preparedness Plan (EPP) per 42 CFR § 484.102.
4.2 The EPP shall be reviewed and updated at least annually by the Emergency Management Team and approved by the Governing Body at the third quarterly meeting per GV-GB-001 § 6.2.6.1.
4.3 The agency shall conduct a Hazard Vulnerability Analysis (HVA) at least annually to identify and prioritize potential emergencies based on the agency's geographic location, patient population, and operational profile.
4.4 The agency shall designate an Emergency Management Team with clearly defined roles and responsibilities for emergency response, communication, and recovery.
4.5 The agency shall conduct emergency preparedness training for all staff at orientation and annually thereafter per HR-TD-005.
4.6 The agency shall conduct at least 2 emergency preparedness exercises per year — one community-based (if available) or facility-based tabletop exercise and one operations-based exercise per RM-EP-002.
4.7 The agency shall maintain a communication plan that ensures the ability to contact all patients, staff, and external authorities during an emergency.
4.8 The agency shall maintain cooperative agreements or arrangements with local emergency management agencies, other healthcare providers, and community organizations to support emergency response.
4.9 The agency shall prioritize patient safety above all other considerations during any emergency. No administrative concern shall override clinical necessity.
4.10 The agency shall maintain business continuity capabilities with defined Recovery Time Objectives for all essential functions.
## 5. Procedures
### 5.1 Emergency Preparedness Plan Development and Maintenance

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.1.1 | Operations Director | Develop and maintain the Emergency Preparedness Plan (EPP) addressing all four CMS-required elements: (a) risk assessment and planning (based on HVA); (b) policies and procedures; (c) communication plan; (d) training and testing program. | Initial development prior to Medicare certification; maintained continuously. |
| 5.1.2 | Operations Director | Conduct or update the Hazard Vulnerability Analysis (HVA) annually using the HVA Worksheet (Appendix A). Evaluate at minimum: (a) earthquakes (California-specific); (b) wildfires; (c) power outages; (d) flooding; (e) severe weather; (f) pandemic/infectious disease; (g) cyberattack/data breach; (h) workplace violence; (i) civil unrest; (j) supply chain disruption. For each hazard, assess: probability, human impact, property impact, business impact, internal preparedness, and external preparedness. | Annually; updated within 30 days of any significant new hazard identification. |
| 5.1.3 | Emergency Management Team | Review the EPP annually. Incorporate findings from: (a) the updated HVA; (b) after-action reviews from exercises and actual events; (c) regulatory changes; (d) community resource changes; (e) patient population changes. | Annually; minimum 30 days before Governing Body review. |
| 5.1.4 | Governing Body | Review and approve the EPP annually at the third quarterly meeting per GV-GB-001. | Annually at Q3 meeting. |

### 5.2 Emergency Management Team and Command Structure

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.2.1 | Administrator | Designate the Emergency Management Team (EMT). | 5.2.2 | Administrator | Ensure all EMT members understand their emergency roles and have 24/7 contact information for all team members. Maintain an Emergency Contact Card (Appendix B) for all EMT members. | At designation; updated within 7 days of any contact change. |

Distribute to all EMT members and keep copies in the agency office, supply bags, and on-call kits. | Maintained continuously; redistributed at any change. |

### 5.3 Emergency Activation and Response

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5.3.1 | Any Staff Member / Incident Commander | Activate the Emergency Preparedness Plan when any of the following occurs: (a) a federal, state, or local emergency is declared affecting the agency's service area; (b) an event occurs that threatens the immediate safety of patients or staff; (c) the agency's ability to deliver essential services is disrupted; (d) the agency office becomes inaccessible or uninhabitable; (e) the IT/EHR system is down for more than 4 hours; (f) a public health emergency is declared (also activates OP-SL-006). | Immediately upon triggering event. |
| 5.3.2 | Incident Commander | Upon activation: (a) assess the scope and severity of the event; (b) activate the EMT; (c) establish a command post (physical or virtual); (d) assign the emergency level: LEVEL 1 — WATCH: Potential threat identified; heightened awareness; no operational changes yet. LEVEL 2 — ALERT: Probable impact; modified operations initiated; patient prioritization begins. LEVEL 3 — EMERGENCY: Active impact; essential functions only; all non-essential operations suspended. | Within 1 hour of activation. |
| 5.3.3 | Clinical Operations Lead | Activate the Patient Safety Priority Protocol: (a) Triage all active patients using the Patient Acuity Triage Tool (OP-SL-006 Appendix A); (b) ensure Priority 1 and 2 patients are contacted within 4 hours; (c) ensure all patients with life-sustaining needs (IV therapy, wound vac, insulin-dependent) have a care continuity plan; (d) coordinate with on-call clinicians for emergency coverage. | Within 4 hours of LEVEL 2 or 3 activation. |
| 5.3.4 | Communications Lead | Activate the Emergency Communication Plan: (a) notify all staff of the activation and their instructions; (b) notify all patients per RM-EP-003; (c) notify the Governing Body; (d) coordinate with local emergency management if applicable; (e) notify CMS/State if required. | Within 2 hours of activation. |
| 5.3.5 | Logistics Lead | Assess and secure: (a) staff availability and safety; (b) transportation and access routes; (c) supply inventory and access; (d) communication system functionality; (e) office/facility status. Report findings to Incident Commander. | Within 2 hours of activation. |
| 5.3.6 | IT/Systems Lead | Assess IT system status: (a) EHR system availability; (b) communication systems; (c) data backup integrity; (d) remote access capability. If EHR is down, activate downtime procedures per OP-SL-005 Appendix B. If cyberattack suspected, activate IT-DR-005 Security Incident Response. | Within 1 hour of activation. |

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| 5.7.2 | Incident Commander | Upon deactivation: (a) notify all staff of return to normal operations; (b) notify all patients of service restoration; (c) notify external entities previously contacted; (d) begin recovery operations. | Within 4 hours of deactivation decision. |
| 5.7.3 | Operations Director | Coordinate recovery: (a) restore all services to normal levels; (b) reschedule all missed visits within 48 hours per OP-SL-001; (c) complete all paper documentation data entry per OP-SL-005; (d) verify all patient care needs are addressed; (e) assess and replace damaged equipment or supplies. | Within 72 hours of deactivation. |
| 5.7.4 | Operations Director | Conduct after-action review per Section 5.6.5. | Within 30 days of deactivation. |
