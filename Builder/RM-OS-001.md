## 2. Purpose

This policy constitutes the written Injury and Illness Prevention Program (IIPP) of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc., as required by California Code of Regulations (CCR), Title 8, §3203. A written IIPP is mandated for ALL California employers regardless of size, industry classification, or number of employees. The IIPP is the foundational Cal/OSHA compliance document from which all other occupational safety programs at this agency derive their authority and structure.

This program establishes:
- The identity of the person responsible for implementing and maintaining the IIPP;
- The system used to ensure compliance with safe and healthy work practice requirements;
- The system for communicating with employees about occupational health and safety;
- The procedures for identifying and evaluating workplace hazards;
- The procedures for investigating occupational injuries, illnesses, and near-misses;
- The methods for correcting identified hazards in a timely manner;
- The training and instruction program;
- The recordkeeping requirements.

This IIPP applies to the entirety of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc.'s operations, including office environments and all field operations in patient residences and community settings.

---

## 3. Scope

This program applies to:
* All employees of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. in all roles (clinical, administrative, supportive)
* All work locations: the agency's main office, any branch offices, all patient homes and community settings where employees perform services
* Per Diem, contract, and temporary employees performing services on behalf of the agency
* Supervisors, managers, and senior leadership in their obligation to enforce and model safe work practices

This IIPP does not supersede the more specific Cal/OSHA programs required by separate provisions of Title 8:
- RM-OS-002 (ATD Exposure Control Plan — 8 CCR §5199)
- RM-OS-003 (Bloodborne Pathogen Exposure Control Plan — 8 CCR §5193)
- RM-OS-004 (Heat Illness Prevention Program — 8 CCR §3395/§5141.1)
- RM-SS-002 (Workplace Violence Prevention Plan — Cal. Labor Code §6401.9 / SB 553) — note: the WVPP is an appendix to this IIPP per SB 553

Those programs are cross-referenced in Section 10 and incorporated herein by reference as required subprograms of this master IIPP.

## 4. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| :---- | :---- |
| IIPP | Injury and Illness Prevention Program — the written, comprehensive workplace health and safety program required by 8 CCR §3203 for all California employers. |
| Cal/OSHA | California Division of Occupational Safety and Health (Cal/OSHA), also referred to as DOSH — the California agency responsible for enforcing Title 8 occupational safety regulations. |
| Hazard | Any condition, practice, or behavior in the workplace that poses a risk of injury, illness, or death to employees. Includes physical, chemical, biological, ergonomic, and psychosocial hazards. |
| Corrective Action | Steps taken to eliminate or control an identified hazard, including engineering controls, administrative controls, and personal protective equipment (PPE) in the hierarchy of controls. |
| Responsible Person | The individual designated by the employer as responsible for implementing and maintaining the IIPP. At this agency, the Responsible Person is the Administrator, with the Risk Manager serving as the designated operational lead. |
| Near-Miss | Any event that could have resulted in injury or illness to an employee but did not, by chance or corrective action. Near-misses must be investigated and documented. |
| Imminent Hazard | A hazard that presents an immediate risk of death or serious physical harm to employees that cannot be eliminated through standard work practices without immediate corrective action. |
| Injury & Illness (I&I) Log | The OSHA 300 Log (Form 300) used to record all work-related injuries and illnesses throughout the year, as required by 8 CCR §14300. |

## 5. Policy Statement

5.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a written, implemented, and current Injury and Illness Prevention Program meeting all requirements of California Code of Regulations, Title 8, §3203.

5.2 The Administrator is the designated Responsible Person for this IIPP. The Risk Manager (or Administrator Designee) is the operational lead for day-to-day IIPP implementation, hazard assessments, training coordination, and recordkeeping.

5.3 All employees are required to comply with safe and healthful work practices identified in this IIPP and its associated subprograms. Employees who fail to follow safety requirements are subject to progressive disciplinary action per HR-ER-002.

5.4 The agency shall not retaliate against any employee who reports a workplace hazard, near-miss, injury, or illness in good faith. Retaliation against an employee for exercising rights under Cal/OSHA or this IIPP is prohibited and may result in termination per HR-ER-005 and HR-ER-003.

5.5 This IIPP shall be reviewed and updated at least annually, or whenever:
- A new job processes, procedures, or equipment are introduced that create new hazards;
- A workplace injury, illness, or near-miss investigation reveals a program deficiency;
- A Cal/OSHA inspection results in a citation;
- New Cal/OSHA regulations affect the agency's operations.

5.6 The agency shall provide effective safety and health training to all employees in a language and at a literacy level understandable to the employee.

5.7 The agency shall maintain records of IIPP implementation, inspections, training, incident investigations, and hazard corrections for a minimum of 5 years or as required by applicable regulation, whichever is longer.

## 6. Procedures

### 6.1 Designation of Responsible Person

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.1.1 | Administrator | Designate in writing the IIPP Responsible Person: the Administrator. Designate the Risk Manager as the operational IIPP lead. Maintain the designation in this document and in personnel assignment records. | At time of IIPP adoption; updated within 14 days of any change in role. |
| 6.1.2 | Administrator | Ensure the Risk Manager has the authority, training, and organizational resources to implement the IIPP without impediment. Provide the Risk Manager with direct access to management for decisions requiring immediate corrective action. | Continuous. |
| 6.1.3 | Risk Manager | Maintain a current IIPP implementation file including: current version of this policy, all inspection records, training logs, incident investigation reports, hazard correction logs, and all associated subprogram ECPs. | Continuous; available for Cal/OSHA inspection at any time. |

### 6.2 Compliance Assurance System

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.2.1 | Supervisors / Clinical Managers | Observe employees performing job tasks at least monthly during routine supervisory contacts. Document observations that identify safe performance, unsafe acts, or safety non-compliance. Report non-compliance to Risk Manager within 1 business day. | Monthly observations; reports within 1 business day of observed non-compliance. |
| 6.2.2 | Risk Manager | Implement a safety recognition system for employees who consistently demonstrate safe work practices. Recognition shall be communicated at staff meetings and documented in employee records. | Quarterly recognition cycle. |
| 6.2.3 | Risk Manager / HR Director | Apply progressive discipline per HR-ER-002 to employees who repeatedly violate safety requirements, refuse to follow IIPP procedures, or disable safety controls. Discipline severity shall be commensurate with the risk posed by the violation. | Per HR-ER-002 timeline. |
| 6.2.4 | Administrator | Review IIPP compliance summary metrics (inspection completion rate, training completion rate, incident rate, hazard correction timeliness) at monthly management meetings. Document review in meeting minutes. | Monthly. |

### 6.3 Employee Safety Communication System

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.3.1 | Risk Manager | Conduct safety-focused staff meetings or safety huddles with all employees at least quarterly. Topics shall include: hazard alerts, incident summaries (de-identified), regulatory updates, and safety improvement initiatives. Document attendance and topics. | Quarterly; documentation retained. |
| 6.3.2 | Risk Manager | Maintain a safety communication channel accessible to all employees (physical safety board at office + electronic communication for field staff) where: (a) safety alerts are posted within 24 hours of identification; (b) Cal/OSHA Summary (Form 300A) is posted February 1–April 30 each year; (c) IIPP summary and relevant subprograms are accessible. | Continuously; Form 300A posted annually February 1–April 30. |
| 6.3.3 | All Employees | Report any workplace hazard, near-miss, unsafe condition, or occupational injury/illness to their supervisor immediately upon discovery. Employees shall use the Hazard/Incident Report Form (Appendix A). Reports shall not be discouraged or penalized. | Immediately upon discovery. |
| 6.3.4 | Risk Manager | Respond to all employee hazard reports in writing within 5 business days, documenting: (a) acknowledgment of the report; (b) findings of the hazard assessment; (c) corrective action taken or planned; (d) estimated completion date. Provide the response to the reporting employee. | Within 5 business days of report receipt. |
| 6.3.5 | Risk Manager | For employees with limited English proficiency, provide all safety communications, training, and forms in the employee's primary language per OP-PA-003 (Language Access Services). | Concurrent with English-language materials. |

### 6.4 Workplace Hazard Identification and Evaluation

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.4.1 | Risk Manager | Conduct a comprehensive workplace hazard assessment of all work environments including: (a) main office; (b) any branch/satellite offices; (c) generic home environment hazards applicable to all patient home visits (e.g., slips, biological exposures, violence, physical demands, heat). Document findings in the Hazard Assessment Report (Appendix B). | Annually; initial assessment within 30 days of IIPP adoption. |
| 6.4.2 | Risk Manager | Conduct unannounced and periodic office safety inspections at least quarterly. Inspection scope: physical hazards (trip/slip, fire exits, ergonomics), biological hazard controls, PPE availability, emergency equipment. Use the Office Safety Inspection Checklist (Appendix C). | Quarterly; document in inspection log. |
| 6.4.3 | Clinical Staff (field employees) | Before entering any patient residence, perform a brief visual safety assessment using the Field Visit Safety Checklist (Appendix D): (a) presence of animals/pets that pose injury risk; (b) obvious structural hazards (broken stairs, inadequate lighting); (c) presence of firearms or weapons in view; (d) behavioral/mental health indicators affecting safety; (e) temperature extremes. | Before each visit. |
| 6.4.4 | Risk Manager | Review all completed Field Visit Safety Checklist reports monthly to identify trending hazards across the patient population and geographic area. Adjust hazard controls accordingly. | Monthly review; documented in IIPP log. |
| 6.4.5 | Risk Manager | When a new job task, patient population, service type, or geographic area is added to the agency's operations, conduct a targeted hazard assessment for the new activity before the first employee performs that task in that capacity. | Before introduction of new activities. |

### 6.5 Incident Investigation

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.5.1 | Injured/Exposed Employee | Report any work-related injury, illness, or near-miss to their direct supervisor immediately. If the supervisor is unavailable, report directly to the Risk Manager. | Immediately upon injury/illness/near-miss. |
| 6.5.2 | Supervisor | Ensure the employee receives appropriate medical care. For medical emergencies, call 911 immediately. For non-emergencies, direct employee to the agency's designated occupational health provider. Complete the first section of the Incident Investigation Form (Appendix E) within 24 hours of receiving the report. | Medical care: immediately. Form completion: within 24 hours. |
| 6.5.3 | Risk Manager | Conduct a thorough investigation of all reportable incidents, near-misses, and any incident involving: (a) emergency room treatment; (b) restricted duty; (c) days away from work; (d) loss of consciousness; (e) exposure to blood, body fluids, or aerosol transmissible pathogens. The investigation must identify: (a) root cause; (b) contributing factors; (c) hazard controls to prevent recurrence. | Investigation initiated within 24 hours; written report within 5 business days. |
| 6.5.4 | Risk Manager | Record all work-related injuries and illnesses on the OSHA 300 Log (Form 300) per 8 CCR §14300. Classify properly (first aid only, recordable, or reportable). | Within 7 calendar days of incident (recordable determination). |
| 6.5.5 | Risk Manager | For incidents resulting in: (a) death; (b) inpatient hospitalization of any employee; (c) amputation; (d) loss of an eye — report to Cal/OSHA by calling 1-844-522-5672 immediately. Do not wait for investigation completion. | **Immediately** upon knowledge of death; within 8 hours for hospitalization, amputation, or eye loss. |
| 6.5.6 | Administrator | Review all serious incident investigation reports. Authorize resource allocation for corrective actions that require capital or contract expenditure. | Within 7 business days of investigation report receipt. |

### 6.6 Hazard Correction

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.6.1 | Risk Manager | Upon identification of a hazard, classify by severity: (a) Imminent Hazard — requires immediate action; (b) Serious Hazard — requires correction within 24 hours; (c) Moderate Hazard — corrected within 7 calendar days; (d) Minor Hazard — corrected within 30 calendar days. Document classification in Hazard Correction Log (Appendix F). | At time of hazard identification. |
| 6.6.2 | Risk Manager | Apply the hierarchy of controls in the following order: (1) Elimination; (2) Substitution; (3) Engineering Controls; (4) Administrative Controls; (5) Personal Protective Equipment. Explain in the Hazard Correction Log why higher-order controls were not feasible if PPE is the primary control. | When selecting corrective actions. |
| 6.6.3 | Risk Manager | For hazards in patient homes that the agency cannot directly control (e.g., structural deficiencies, pet hazards), develop and communicate administrative controls: (a) notify the field employee; (b) document home hazard in patient record; (c) consider whether the hazard is incompatible with safe service delivery; (d) if incompatible, escalate to Clinical Manager for determination of continued service per OP-IM-002. | Within 24 hours of hazard identification. |
| 6.6.4 | Risk Manager | Track all open hazard correction items in the Hazard Correction Log. Report status to Administrator monthly. Close completed items only when physical verification of correction is documented. | Monthly status report; continuous tracking. |

### 6.7 Employee Safety Training

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.7.1 | Training Coordinator / Risk Manager | Provide general IIPP safety training to all new employees before they begin job duties that expose them to hazards. Training must cover: (a) employee rights under Cal/OSHA; (b) the agency's IIPP and how to report hazards/incidents; (c) hazards specific to their job classification; (d) PPE assigned to their role; (e) emergency procedures; (f) location of first aid supplies and emergency contacts. | Before first exposure to workplace hazards (first day of field duties or first day in office, whichever comes first). |
| 6.7.2 | Training Coordinator / Risk Manager | Conduct annual safety refresher training for all employees covering: (a) IIPP updates; (b) incident analysis from the prior year; (c) regulatory requirement changes; (d) job-specific safety refreshers (BBP, ATD, heat illness, workplace violence) with reference to relevant subprograms. | Annually; completed within the calendar year. |
| 6.7.3 | Risk Manager | Provide targeted safety training whenever: (a) a new hazard is introduced; (b) an incident investigation reveals a knowledge gap; (c) a Cal/OSHA citation is issued; (d) a significant change in work procedures occurs. | Within 30 calendar days of trigger event. |
| 6.7.4 | Training Coordinator | Document all safety training on the Safety Training Log (Appendix G): employee name, date, topic, trainer, duration, employee attestation. Retain records for minimum 5 years. | At time of training; retain for 5 years. |

### 6.8 Escalation and Exception Handling

| Condition | Escalation Path | Corrective Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Imminent hazard identified in any work environment | Risk Manager immediately notifies Administrator and affected supervisor. | Immediately remove affected employees from hazard area. Correct hazard before resuming work; or if correction requires third-party, implement interim administrative controls. | Immediate. |
| Employee refuses safety training | Supervisor reports to Risk Manager and HR Director. | HR-ER-002 progressive discipline applied. Employee may not perform duties creating the relevant hazard until training is completed. | Discipline initiated within 5 business days of refusal. |
| Cal/OSHA conducts inspection | Risk Manager and Administrator accompany inspector. | Cooperate fully. All citations reviewed by legal counsel. Respond within timeframe specified in citation. Corrective actions implemented and documented. | Per citation abatement dates. |
| IIPP annual review not completed by anniversary date | Risk Manager notifies Administrator. | Emergency review convened within 14 calendar days; reason for delay documented. | Within 14 days of missed deadline. |

## 8. Compliance & Audit Considerations

### 8.1 How Compliance Is Measured

| Compliance Indicator | Measurement Method | Acceptable Standard |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Written IIPP exists, is current, and contains all 8 required elements | Annual review against 8 CCR §3203 checklist | All 8 elements present; reviewed and signed within the last 12 months |
| Annual hazard assessment completed | Review of dated Hazard Assessment Report | Completed annually; no more than 12 months since last assessment |
| Quarterly office inspections conducted | Review of inspection log dates and documentation | 4 inspections documented per calendar year |
| All newly hired employees received safety training before hazard exposure | Review of Safety Training Log; verify date vs. hire date | 100% trained before first hazard exposure |
| Annual safety refresher completed | Training completion rate | 100% of employees within calendar year |
| Incidents investigated within 5 business days | Review of Incident Investigation Forms | 100% of qualifying incidents investigated within 5 days |
| Cal/OSHA reportable incidents reported within required timeframes | Review of reporting log | 100% reported within regulatory deadlines |
| Form 300 maintained and posted | Review of Form 300 log and posting evidence | Form 300 maintained; Form 300A posted Feb 1–Apr 30 annually |
| Hazard corrections completed within classified timeframes | Review of Hazard Correction Log | 100% Imminent (same-day), Serious (24 hours), Moderate (7 days), Minor (30 days) |

### 8.2 What Surveyors and Auditors Will Look For

Cal/OSHA compliance officers conducting a workplace inspection will specifically verify:

1. The written IIPP document exists, is current, and is available at the workplace. The absence of a written IIPP is a General Industry Safety Order violation with potential civil penalty.
2. The Responsible Person is clearly identified by name and title. An IIPP that identifies a generic "HR" or "Safety Officer" who is not specifically named is inadequate.
3. Records of hazard assessments, safety inspections, incident investigations, and corrective actions are maintained and accessible.
4. Employees can confirm they received safety training and know how to report hazards. Cal/OSHA may interview employees to verify IIPP effectiveness.
5. The Form 300 Log is properly maintained and all recordable incidents are entered correctly.
6. All required subprogram ECPs (BBP, ATD, Workplace Violence) are incorporated by reference and exist as separate written programs.
7. The IIPP is available in a language understandable to non-English-speaking employees.

### 8.3 Common Failure Points

| Failure Point | Risk | Mitigation |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| IIPP exists only as a template with no agency-specific customization | Cal/OSHA citation; penalty | This document is Agency-specific; all roles, contacts, and procedures reference Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. specifically |
| Responsible Person is generic (HR/Safety) not named | Citation | Administrator named as Responsible Person; Risk Manager named as operational lead in this document |
| No field-specific hazard assessment (only office hazards assessed) | Citation; unprotected field staff | Field Visit Safety Checklist (Appendix D) addresses home environment hazards specifically |
| Incident investigation form exists but not consistently used | Missed near-misses; no trend analysis | Training and supervisor compliance assurance procedures enforce form completion |
| Safety training records not maintained separately | Cannot demonstrate training in Cal/OSHA inspection | Safety Training Log (Appendix G) maintained by Training Coordinator, separate from general HR file |
| Form 300 not maintained because "we had no recordable injuries" | Form 300 must be maintained even if zero entries | Form 300 maintained with zero entries where applicable |
