## 2. Purpose

This document constitutes the written Bloodborne Pathogen (BBP) Exposure Control Plan for Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc., as required by California Code of Regulations, Title 8, §5193 — Bloodborne Pathogen Standard (substantially equivalent to, and in some respects more stringent than, OSHA 29 CFR §1910.1030). Home health agencies employ clinical staff who perform invasive procedures in uncontrolled patient residence environments, creating occupational bloodborne pathogen exposure risk among the highest across all healthcare settings.

This plan is designed to eliminate or minimize employee exposure to bloodborne pathogens including:
- Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV)
- Hepatitis B Virus (HBV)
- Hepatitis C Virus (HCV)
- Any other pathogen that is present in human blood and can cause disease in humans

This ECP establishes the agency's exposure determination, methods of compliance, hepatitis B vaccination program, post-exposure evaluation and follow-up program, communication of hazards, recordkeeping, and annual review process.

This ECP is a subprogram of RM-OS-001 (IIPP). It does not replace CL-SD-016 (Infection Prevention & Control), which governs patient-centered IPC standards. This ECP governs EMPLOYEE occupational safety for BBP exposure prevention and response.

---

## 3. Scope

This plan applies to:

* All employees and contractors of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. with reasonably anticipated occupational exposure to blood or other potentially infectious materials (OPIM) in the performance of their duties, without regard to personal protective equipment
* Job classifications with occupational exposure: Registered Nurse (RN), Licensed Vocational Nurse (LVN), Physical Therapist (PT), Occupational Therapist (OT), Speech-Language Pathologist (SLP), Medical Social Worker (MSW), Home Health Aide (HHA), Intake Coordinator (if performing blood glucose or clinical screening)
* All work settings in which clinical procedures involving potential blood/OPIM contact occur: patient residences, community settings, clinical consultation locations

## 4. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| :---- | :---- |
| Bloodborne Pathogens | Pathogenic microorganisms present in human blood that can cause disease in humans. The term also applies to any body fluid visibly contaminated with blood; all body fluids involving uncertainty about blood content; semen, vaginal secretions, cerebrospinal fluid, synovial fluid, pleural fluid, peritoneal fluid, pericardial fluid, amniotic fluid; and other OPIM. |
| OPIM | Other Potentially Infectious Materials — includes all body fluids listed in 8 CCR §5193(b)(1)(B) in addition to blood. |
| Occupational Exposure | Reasonably anticipated skin, eye, mucous membrane, or parenteral contact with blood or OPIM that may result from the performance of an employee's duties. |
| Exposure Incident | A specific eye, mouth, other mucous membrane, non-intact skin, or parenteral contact with blood or OPIM that results from the performance of an employee's duties. This is a triggering event for post-exposure evaluation. |
| Universal Precautions | An approach to infection control in which all human blood and certain OPIM are treated as if known to be infectious, regardless of patient diagnosis. (Also referred to as Standard Precautions in contemporary IPC terminology, which is the preferred term.) |
| Engineering Controls | Physical or mechanical measures that isolate or remove the BBP hazard from the workplace, including but not limited to: sharps disposal containers, self-sheathing needles, needleless IV systems, safety-engineered sharps, splash guards. |
| Work Practice Controls | Practices that reduce exposure by altering the manner in which tasks are performed, including no two-hand needle recapping, proper sharps disposal procedures, hand hygiene practices. |
| Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) | Specialized clothing or equipment worn by employees to protect against a hazard. For BBP: gloves, gowns, face shields, masks, eye protection, mouthpieces, resuscitation bags. |
| Sharps Injury Log | The electronic or paper log documenting all sharps injuries per 8 CCR §5193(h)(4), required recording: type and brand of device involved, department/work area, explanation of incident. |
| PLHCP | Physician or Licensed Health Care Professional — the occupational health professional who conducts post-exposure medical evaluation and provides the written opinion. |

## 5. Policy Statement

5.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a current, written Bloodborne Pathogen Exposure Control Plan that meets all requirements of 8 CCR §5193 and is accessible to all employees during every work shift.

5.2 The agency shall determine and document which employees have occupational exposure as part of this ECP (see Section 6.1 and Appendix A — BBP Exposure Determination Table).

5.3 The agency shall implement and maintain a schedule of methods to achieve compliance with the BBP standard, applying engineering controls, work practice controls, and PPE in the hierarchy established by 8 CCR §5193(d).

5.4 The agency shall offer the hepatitis B vaccination series at no cost to all employees with occupational exposure, within 10 working days of assignment to a position with occupational exposure, following training, and when the employee is not occupationally contraindicated.

5.5 The agency shall provide post-exposure evaluation and follow-up at no cost to any employee who sustains an exposure incident, within the timeframes specified in Section 6.6.

5.6 The agency shall maintain a sharps injury log (separate from the OSHA 300 Log) that records all sharps injuries in the manner specified in 8 CCR §5193(h)(4), and use the sharps injury log data annually to identify opportunities to implement safer sharps devices.

5.7 The agency shall provide BBP training to all employees with occupational exposure before initial assignment and annually thereafter, meeting the content requirements of 8 CCR §5193(g).

5.8 The agency shall not charge employees for any aspect of the BBP control program: training, PPE, vaccination, post-exposure evaluation, recordkeeping, or this ECP itself.

5.9 Employee BBP exposure medical records shall be maintained confidentially, separate from personnel files, per California CMIA requirements (CO-CA-001) and 8 CCR §5193(h)(1).

5.10 This ECP shall be reviewed and updated at least annually, whenever a task or procedure is modified in a way that affects BBP exposure risk, and whenever safer medical devices become commercially available for tasks performed by agency employees.

## 6. Procedures

### 6.1 BBP Exposure Determination

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.1.1 | Director of Nursing / Risk Manager | Complete and maintain the BBP Exposure Determination by job classification (Appendix A), identifying: (a) all job classifications where occupational exposure occurs without regard to protective equipment; (b) all job classifications where occupational exposure occurs in certain tasks only (List 2 — identify specific tasks). | At ECP adoption; review and confirm annually. |
| 6.1.2 | Director of Nursing | For each job classification in List 1 (exposure in all tasks), document the primary exposure pathways (e.g., blood draw, wound care, IV access, suctioning). For List 2, document only the specific tasks creating exposure. Attach as Appendix A. | At adoption; update within 30 days of new service introduction. |
| 6.1.3 | Risk Manager | When a new job task or clinical service is introduced, conduct a supplemental BBP exposure determination for the new activity before any employee performs the task. | Before task introduction. |

### 6.2 Engineering and Work Practice Controls

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.2.1 | Risk Manager / Director of Nursing | Evaluate and implement, to the extent feasible, engineering controls for all sharps procedures performed in patient homes: (a) all IV insertions shall use needleless or safety-engineered IV access systems; (b) all blood draws shall use safety-engineered phlebotomy devices; (c) finger-stick devices shall be self-retracting; (d) all agency-supplied sharps shall be safety-engineered as commercially available. | Prior to ECP effective date; update annually with sharps injury review and device availability. |
| 6.2.2 | Risk Manager | Conduct an annual review of available safety-engineered sharps devices, taking into account: sharps injury log data, frontline employee input, and commercial availability. Document that safer alternatives were considered; if not implemented, document clinical or technical infeasibility. | Annually; documented in annual ECP review. |
| 6.2.3 | All Clinical Staff | Perform all sharps procedures using work practice controls: (a) NEVER recap needles using a two-hand technique; (b) needles may be recapped using a one-hand scoop technique or a mechanical recapping device only when required for safe disposal transport; (c) needles shall never be bent, broken, or removed from disposable syringes by hand; (d) broken glassware shall be handled with mechanical device (forceps, brush), not bare hands; (e) blood or OPIM specimens shall be contained in leak-proof containers. | Every procedure. |
| 6.2.4 | All Clinical Staff | Dispose of all contaminated sharps immediately after use in an agency-supplied, OSHA-compliant sharps disposal container (rigid, puncture-resistant, leak-proof, labeled with the biohazard symbol and the word "BIOHAZARD"). Field staff shall carry a portable sharps container to each visit. | Immediately after each use. |
| 6.2.5 | All Clinical Staff | Wash hands immediately after removing gloves and after any skin contact with blood or OPIM. When handwashing is not immediately available in the field, use ABHR (alcohol-based hand rub) immediately and wash with soap and water as soon as feasible. | After each patient contact involving blood/OPIM. |
| 6.2.6 | All Clinical Staff | Perform mouth-to-mouth resuscitation using a disposable CPR barrier device (pocket mask or face shield) from the agency-issued field supply kit. Never perform unprotected mouth-to-mouth resuscitation. | Whenever CPR is performed. |

### 6.3 Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.3.1 | Risk Manager / Agency Supply | Provide all BBP-applicable PPE to employees at no cost: (a) exam gloves (non-latex option available for employees with latex sensitivity); (b) fluid-resistant gowns or aprons for procedures with splash risk; (c) face shields or safety glasses + mask for procedures with OPIM splash/spray risk; (d) surgical masks; (e) CPR barrier devices; (f) portable sharps containers for field use. | PPE available before any employee begins duties with exposure risk; replenished as needed. |
| 6.3.2 | All Clinical Staff | Wear gloves for all anticipated direct contact with blood, OPIM, mucous membranes, non-intact skin, or contaminated surfaces or items. Replace gloves that are torn, punctured, or significantly contaminated. Double-glove when performing procedures with high sharps risk (e.g., complex wound care, IV removal from agitated patient). | Every patient contact involving exposure risk. |
| 6.3.3 | All Clinical Staff | Wear appropriate face and eye protection (face shield or goggles + mask) when performing procedures that create splash or spray of blood or OPIM: suctioning, wound irrigation, IV access in high-pressure lines, bleeding control of arterial sites. | During AGP-type or splash-risk procedures. |
| 6.3.4 | All Clinical Staff | If PPE is penetrated (e.g., glove puncture) or if another mechanism creates potential exposure, report as an Exposure Incident per Section 6.6 immediately. | Immediately. |
| 6.3.5 | Director of Nursing | Evaluate the adequacy of PPE type and availability at least annually by reviewing exposure incident patterns, sharps injury log, and employee feedback. Update the PPE supply protocol accordingly. | Annually. |

### 6.4 Hepatitis B Vaccination Program

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.4.1 | HR / Risk Manager | Offer the HBV vaccine series at no cost to all employees with occupational exposure, as identified in Appendix A. The offer shall be made after BBP training and within 10 working days of initial assignment to an occupationally exposed position. | Within 10 working days of assignment. |
| 6.4.2 | HR | Document the HBV vaccination offer and employee response on the HBV Vaccine Offer/Declination Form (Appendix B). If the employee declines the vaccine, obtain a signed declination statement. Employees who decline may later request vaccination at no cost while still employed in an exposure-risk position. | At time of offer; update if employee later accepts. |
| 6.4.3 | HR | Maintain proof of HBV vaccination series completion (or documented immunity via titer) and signed declination statements in the employee's confidential medical file. | At time of vaccination or declination. |
| 6.4.4 | HR / Risk Manager | After completion of the 3-dose HBV series, offer post-vaccination serology testing (anti-HBs) to verify immune response if employee requests, or in the event of a subsequent occupational HBV exposure (non-immune employees require expedited evaluation). | Per employee request or post-exposure determination. |
| 6.4.5 | HR | Offer HBV booster or revaccination per current CDC guidance when new guidance recommends it, or if a non-immune employee is identified after a prior completed series. | Per CDC/ACIP guidance updates. |

### 6.5 Housekeeping and Decontamination

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.5.1 | Clinical Staff | After any clinical procedure in a patient home involving blood or OPIM: (a) discard all single-use contaminated sharps in the portable sharps container; (b) discard all single-use contaminated PPE in a biohazard waste bag; (c) clean any surface contaminated with blood or OPIM using an appropriate disinfectant (EPA-registered hospital-grade disinfectant active against HIV and HBV) from the agency's approved supply list; (d) seal and return biohazard waste bag from the patient home per agency waste disposal protocol. | After each procedure. |
| 6.5.2 | Risk Manager | Maintain an approved list of EPA-registered disinfectants suitable for blood/OPIM decontamination that field staff are authorized to use. Post the list in the agency office and include in field supply kits. | Maintained continuously; reviewed annually. |
| 6.5.3 | Clinical Staff | Never transport contaminated sharps in soft bags, pockets, or any container not specifically designated as a sharps container. Transport full portable sharps containers sealed per manufacturer instructions to the agency office for proper disposal per agency biomedical waste disposal agreement. | Continuous. |

### 6.6 Exposure Incident Response

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.6.1 | Exposed Employee | For any exposure incident (needlestick, splash to eye/mucous membrane, broken skin contact with blood/OPIM): (a) immediately wash the affected skin area thoroughly with soap and water; (b) for eye/mucous membrane exposure, flush with clean water for ≥15 minutes; (c) do NOT apply caustic agents, bleach, or anti-infective agents to the wound; (d) report to supervisor and Director of Nursing immediately. | Immediately upon exposure. |
| 6.6.2 | Supervisor / Director of Nursing | Upon receipt of exposure report: (a) confirm initial first aid was performed; (b) contact the agency's designated PLHCP/occupational health provider immediately to arrange same-day post-exposure evaluation; (c) document the exposure circumstances on the BBP Exposure Incident Form (Appendix C). | Within 2 hours of exposure report. |
| 6.6.3 | Risk Manager | Arrange and authorize the employee's post-exposure evaluation at no cost. The evaluation must include: (a) documentation of the route and circumstances of exposure; (b) identification of the source patient, if possible, with source patient testing (with consent per California law — CMIA requirements apply); (c) HIV baseline testing of exposed employee; (d) HBV and HCV baseline testing; (e) assessment for HIV PEP if indicated (must commence within 72 hours); (f) PLHCP written opinion within 15 days of evaluation. | Same-day evaluation arranged by supervisor. PEP assessment within 2–4 hours if HIV source risk. |
| 6.6.4 | Risk Manager | If the source patient is known or suspected to have HIV: ensure the employee is informed of the availability of HIV PEP and that PEP must begin within 72 hours for maximum effectiveness. Ensure the occupational health provider assesses PEP eligibility. | Within 2 hours of determining source HIV status. |
| 6.6.5 | Risk Manager | Receive the PLHCP written opinion within 15 business days of the evaluation and provide a copy to the employee. The opinion shall state only: (a) whether HBV vaccine is indicated and was provided; (b) that the employee was informed of results and any necessary follow-up. The written opinion shall NOT include the employee's diagnosis, specific HIV/HBV/HCV test results, or other medical information. | Within 15 days of evaluation; provide copy to employee. |
| 6.6.6 | Risk Manager | Document the exposure incident in the Sharps Injury Log (Appendix D) if it involved a sharp device, per 8 CCR §5193(h)(4). Record: type and brand of device, department/work area (patient home visit), and explanation of incident. Log shall be de-identified before any aggregate review by personnel other than the Responsible Person. | Within 7 days of incident. |
| 6.6.7 | Risk Manager | Record any work-related BBP illness on the OSHA 300 Log per RM-OS-001. Report to Cal/OSHA immediately if the exposure results in hospitalization or death. | Per RM-OS-001 recordkeeping requirements. |
| 6.6.8 | Risk Manager / Director of Nursing | Conduct a post-exposure Root Cause Analysis per QA-AE-002 to determine contributing factors, including whether engineering controls were in place and used, whether PPE was available and used, and whether a safer device could have prevented the exposure. Update ECP and device selection within 30 days of RCA completion. | RCA within 14 days; ECP update within 30 days. |

### 6.7 Escalation and Exception Handling

| Condition | Escalation Path | Corrective Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Employee refuses HBV vaccine and will not sign declination | HR Director and Risk Manager notified. | Employee may continue occupational exposure duties but risk is documented. HR documents employee's refusal of agency's safety program offering. | Within 5 business days. |
| Source patient for exposure incident refuses blood test | PLHCP and Risk Manager assess baseline exposure risk from alternative information. | Post-exposure management proceeds based on clinical risk assessment per current CDC/OSHA guidance. | Within 24 hours of refusal. |
| HIV PEP supply unavailable through standard provider | Risk Manager escalates to Administrator, contacts alternative occupational health or emergency department. | Ensure employee reaches PEP-capable provider within 72-hour window regardless of standard provider availability. | Within 2 hours of supply/access issue identification. |
| Annual ECP review identifies safer sharps device commercially available | Risk Manager documents the device, evaluates feasibility. | If feasible, transition to safer device within 90 days. If not feasible, document clinical rationale in writing. | 90 days for implementation; immediate documentation of decision. |

## 8. Compliance & Audit Considerations

### 8.1 How Compliance Is Measured

| Compliance Indicator | Measurement Method | Acceptable Standard |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Written BBP ECP exists, current, and accessible | Annual review against 8 CCR §5193 checklist | All required elements present; reviewed within 12 months |
| BBP Exposure Determination current | Review of Appendix A | All job classifications assessed; reviewed annually |
| HBV vaccine offered within 10 working days of assignment | HR file audit — verify offer date vs. assignment date | 100% within 10 working days |
| Declination statements on file for employees who declined HBV vaccine | HR medical file audit | 100% of declined employees have signed declination form |
| All exposure incidents have PLHCP written opinion within 15 days | Review of Appendix C files | 100% within 15 days |
| Sharps injury log maintained | Review of Appendix D log | All sharps injuries documented; log reviewed annually for device review |
| BBP training completed annually | Training completion rate | 100% within calendar year |
| BBP medical records in confidential medical file (not personnel file) | HR file audit | 100% separate and access-controlled |

### 8.2 What Surveyors and Auditors Will Look For

Cal/OSHA compliance officers will specifically verify:

1. The written BBP ECP is available at the workplace. The ECP must be accessible to employees throughout each work shift.
2. The Exposure Determination identifies specific job classifications and tasks with occupational exposure without regard to PPE.
3. Engineering controls are being used and the annual review of safer needle devices is documented with employee input.
4. The HBV vaccination program is being offered within 10 working days, and declination forms are on file.
5. Post-exposure evaluation and follow-up procedures were followed for any documented incidents, and PLHCP written opinions are on file.
6. The Sharps Injury Log is maintained separately from the OSHA 300 Log, and the log has been reviewed annually for safer device opportunities.
7. BBP medical records are in a separate confidential medical file, not the general personnel file.

CMS surveys may cross-check against CL-SD-016 (IPC) and HR-WM-003 (immunizations) for cross-compliance evidence.

### 8.3 Common Failure Points

| Failure Point | Risk | Mitigation |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| No annual safer sharps device review with employee input | Cal/OSHA §5193(d)(2)(B) requires annual review with frontline input | Annual device review meeting with clinical staff documented; findings in ECP file |
| HBV vaccine not offered within 10 working days | Cal/OSHA citation | HR onboarding checklist includes HBV offer as Day 1–10 action item |
| Exposure incident response not initiated same-day | Loss of PEP window (72 hours for HIV) | 24/7 supervisor reporting protocol; occupational health provider on retainer for after-hours exposure evaluation |
| BBP records filed in personnel file | CMIA violation; Cal/OSHA violation | Separate medical file per CO-CA-001; HR-WM-007 training reinforces separation |
| No sharps injury log since "no injuries occurred" | Cal/OSHA citation — log must be maintained to record any incidents | Log maintained with zero entries when applicable; available for inspection |
