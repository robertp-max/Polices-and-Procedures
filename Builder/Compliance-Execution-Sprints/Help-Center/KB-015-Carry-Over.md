# KB-015 — Carry-Over and What It Means for Your Sprint

**Audience:** Everyone. **Time to read:** 2 minutes.

A unit **carries over** when a sprint closes (Day 14) and the unit is not
in the **Completed** column.

## What carry-over does to you

- The unit retains its current state and re-appears on the next sprint's
  board on Day 1.
- It counts on the **Carry-Over Units** chart in Reports for the next
  sprint.
- It **does not** reset its `dueDate`. Carry-over does not extend the
  deadline.
- If the unit is past due, it will appear in the next sprint already
  flagged red.

## What carry-over does to your metrics

- Hurts **On-Time Completion %** (already missed the original sprint).
- Hurts the next sprint's **Carry-Over Units** chart (target: trending
  down).
- May hurt **Audit Readiness Score** if the unit is critical.

## Avoiding carry-over

1. On Day 12 of every sprint, run a personal sweep:
   - Filter Sprint Board to **My Work** + **Not Completed**.
   - Triage anything not started.
2. Use the **R3 Overdue Resolution Sweep** recurring unit (Compliance
   Officer) to catch agency-wide carry-over candidates.
3. If a unit cannot finish, **Block it with a clear reason** before
   sprint close — at least the metric attribution is honest.

## What if carry-over is unavoidable?

It happens — long signature SLAs, vendor delays, holidays. The system
does not penalize you for *one* carry-over with a documented reason. It
penalizes the *trend*.
