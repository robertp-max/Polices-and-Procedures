## 2. Purpose

This document constitutes the written Aerosol Transmissible Disease (ATD) Exposure Control Plan for Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc., as required by the California Code of Regulations, Title 8, §5199 — Aerosol Transmissible Diseases Standard. Home health agencies are explicitly designated as "ATD-susceptible workplaces" under 8 CCR §5199(a)(5) because employees routinely enter residences and provide direct patient care where aerosol transmissible pathogens may be present.

This plan establishes the specific protections, procedures, and responsibilities required for employees whose work creates a risk of exposure to aerosol transmissible pathogens including, but not limited to:
- Mycobacterium tuberculosis (TB)
- SARS-CoV-2 (COVID-19) and variants
- Measles (Rubeola)
- Chickenpox/Varicella
- Influenza (during periods of declared epidemic or outbreak)
- Monkeypox/Mpox
- Any other pathogen designated as an ATD under 8 CCR §5199(a)(1)

This ECP is an appendix to and subprogram of RM-OS-001 (IIPP). It does not replace CL-SD-016 (Infection Prevention & Control), which governs patient care IPC standards. This ECP governs EMPLOYEE occupational safety obligations specifically.

---

## 3. Scope

This plan applies to:

* All employees of Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. who may have occupational exposure to aerosol transmissible pathogens in the course of their work duties
* All work settings: patient residences, group homes, skilled nursing facilities visited for care coordination, and the agency's office if a clinical activity occurs there
* Specific job classifications with potential ATD occupational exposure: Registered Nurse (RN), Licensed Vocational Nurse (LVN), Physical Therapist (PT), Occupational Therapist (OT), Speech-Language Pathologist (SLP), Medical Social Worker (MSW), Home Health Aide (HHA), Intake Coordinator (if conducting in-home assessments)

Administrative staff with no patient-facing duties in clinical settings are covered by ATD general precaution requirements but are not classified as ATD-exposed workers under 8 CCR §5199(a)(4).

## 4. Definitions

| Term | Definition |
| :---- | :---- |
| ATD | Aerosol Transmissible Disease — an infectious disease transmitted through airborne droplet nuclei or droplets, as listed in Appendix D of 8 CCR §5199. |
| ATD Exposure Control Plan (ECP) | A written, site-specific document detailing the employer's procedures for identifying and controlling occupational ATD exposure risks, as required by 8 CCR §5199(c). |
| Airborne Infectious Isolation (AII) | Procedures and conditions required to safely care for a patient known or suspected to have an airborne transmissible ATD, including appropriate respiratory protection, patient placement, and ventilation. |
| Respiratory Protection Program | The employer's program for ensuring that employees are properly fit-tested for and provided with appropriate respiratory protective equipment (NIOSH-approved N95 or higher) per 8 CCR §5144. |
| Fit Testing | The mandatory OSHA/Cal/OSHA protocol used to verify that a specific respirator model creates an effective seal on an individual employee's face, conducted before initial use and at least annually. |
| ATD Exposure Incident | A specific eye, mouth, mucous membrane, or respiratory tract exposure to an ATD pathogen through contact that creates a potential for infection during a specific episode. |
| Aerosol-Generating Procedure (AGP) | A clinical procedure that generates airborne particles, including suctioning, nebulizer treatments, high-flow oxygen, CPR, and certain oral/respiratory care procedures. |
| ATD-Susceptible Workplace | A work location where employees, due to the nature of their duties, are at elevated risk for ATD exposure — home health agencies are explicitly classified as ATD-susceptible workplaces per 8 CCR §5199(a)(5). |

## 5. Policy Statement

5.1 Care Indeed Home Health Care, Inc. shall maintain a current, written ATD Exposure Control Plan meeting all requirements of 8 CCR §5199 for all workers with potential ATD occupational exposure.

5.2 The agency shall determine, document, and maintain an ATD Exposure Determination identifying, by job classification, all employees with potential occupational ATD exposure. All employees in clinical roles listed in Section 3 are classified as ATD-exposed workers.

5.3 The agency shall provide, at no cost to the employee, appropriate respiratory protective equipment — minimum NIOSH-approved N95 respirator — whenever an employee may encounter a patient with a known or suspected ATD. The agency shall also provide surgical/procedure masks for all field visits as a minimum standard.

5.4 The agency shall maintain a Respiratory Protection Program that meets all requirements of 8 CCR §5144, including initial medical evaluation, fit testing, and annual re-fit testing for all employees assigned N95 respirators.

5.5 The agency shall ensure employees are medically cleared for respirator use before being required to use an N95 or higher respirator, using the OSHA Medical Evaluation Questionnaire per 29 CFR §1910.134(e).

5.6 The agency shall provide ATD-specific exposure investigation, post-exposure medical evaluation, and follow-up at no cost to the employee, within timeframes specified in Section 6.5.

5.7 All employees with potential ATD occupational exposure shall receive ATD-specific training that meets the content requirements of 8 CCR §5199(g) before initial assignment and annually thereafter.

5.8 This ECP shall be reviewed and updated at least annually or whenever a new ATD is identified as a significant occupational risk (e.g., a public health emergency declaration), a change in agency operations creates new ATD exposures, or a post-exposure investigation reveals a program gap.

5.9 This ECP is a subprogram of RM-OS-001 (IIPP) and does not conflict with CL-SD-016 (Infection Prevention & Control). Where both apply, the more protective standard governs employee occupational safety.

## 6. Procedures

### 6.1 ATD Exposure Determination

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.1.1 | Director of Nursing / Risk Manager | Conduct and document an ATD Exposure Determination that identifies, by job classification, all employees with potential occupational ATD exposure without regard to personal protective equipment. Attach the Exposure Determination Table (Appendix A) to this ECP. | At ECP adoption; reviewed and confirmed annually. |
| 6.1.2 | Director of Nursing | For each identified job classification, document the specific ATD exposure pathways: (a) direct patient care involving respiratory assessment; (b) aerosol-generating procedures (suctioning, nebulizer, oxygen therapy, CPR); (c) presence in confined spaces (small rooms) with ATD patients without adequate ventilation; (d) transport of patients with ATD. | At ECP adoption; reviewed and confirmed annually. |
| 6.1.3 | Risk Manager | When a new clinical role or service line is established, conduct a supplemental Exposure Determination within 30 days of service introduction to assess ATD exposure risk for the new role. Update Appendix A accordingly. | Within 30 days of new service introduction. |

### 6.2 ATD Precaution Procedures — All Field Visits

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.2.1 | Scheduling / Intake Coordinator | During scheduling, screen for ATD risk indicators by verifying with referral source and patient/caregiver: (a) known or suspected active TB; (b) known COVID-19 positive status; (c) known varicella, measles, or other listed ATD; (d) immunocompromised household members who may be especially affected. Flag high-risk visits in the scheduling system. | At time of scheduling and at each visit authorization. |
| 6.2.2 | Field Clinical Staff | Upon arrival at every patient residence, perform a brief respiratory symptom screen before entering: ask the patient and/or caregiver if anyone in the home has respiratory symptoms (cough, fever, shortness of breath), known COVID-19, TB, or other respiratory illness in the prior 14 days. Document findings in the patient visit note. | Before each visit entry. |
| 6.2.3 | Field Clinical Staff | For ALL visits: don a surgical/procedure mask before patient contact. Agency shall supply masks to all field staff. Masks shall not be reused across multiple patient visits or across days. | Every visit. |
| 6.2.4 | Field Clinical Staff | For visits flagged as high-risk ATD per 6.2.1 OR where 6.2.2 screening identifies active symptoms: don NIOSH-approved fit-tested N95 respirator (from personal supply issued by agency) before entering the area where the patient resides. Don appropriate eye protection (goggles or face shield) if performing AGPs or mucous membrane contact is anticipated. | High-risk visits; symptomatic household visits. |
| 6.2.5 | Field Clinical Staff | If an AGP (e.g., suctioning, nebulizer treatment, CPR, high-flow oxygen) is performed: wear N95 or higher respirator regardless of patient ATD status. Position patient to maximize ventilation away from the clinician when possible. | Whenever performing an AGP, regardless of known ATD status. |
| 6.2.6 | Field Clinical Staff | Perform proper donning, doffing, and disposal of respiratory PPE per agency PPE training. Sanitize hands after doffing. If N95 is damaged, soiled, or has compromised fit, remove and replace; notify scheduler for additional supplies. | Every visit. |

### 6.3 Respiratory Protection Program

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.3.1 | Risk Manager | Maintain a current NIOSH-approved N95 respirator model(s) on the agency's approved respirator list. Select models for which medical evaluation and fit testing will be conducted. Do not use KN95 respirators as OSHA-compliant respiratory protection in occupational settings (KN95 is not NIOSH-approved). | Continuous; annual review. |
| 6.3.2 | Risk Manager / HR | Before assigning any employee to a role requiring N95 respirator use, administer the OSHA Medical Evaluation Questionnaire (29 CFR §1910.134 Appendix C) to identify any medical conditions that might interfere with respirator use. Route questionnaire responses to the designated PLHCP (Physician or Licensed Health Care Professional) for review. Employees with medical conditions identified by the PLHCP shall be medically cleared before N95 use. | Before initial assignment to N95-required duties. |
| 6.3.3 | Risk Manager | Conduct initial qualitative or quantitative fit testing for all employees assigned to N95 use using a NIOSH-approved testing protocol and the specific N95 model the employee will wear. Document fit test results on the Fit Test Record (Appendix B). Employees who fail fit testing with the agency's standard model shall be evaluated for alternative models. | Before initial N95 use; at least annually. |
| 6.3.4 | Risk Manager | Conduct annual re-fit testing for all N95-assigned employees. Re-fit testing is also required if any of the following occur: (a) employee reports the respirator no longer fits; (b) employee undergoes significant dental or facial changes; (c) employee gains or loses more than 20 lbs; (d) employee undergoes facial surgical procedures. | Annually; upon triggering conditions listed. |
| 6.3.5 | Risk Manager | Maintain an adequate supply of issued N95 respirators to field staff. Track issuance. Ensure field staff replace N95s that are damp, soiled, physically damaged, or have perceived loss of seal. Field staff shall not reuse respirators across visits unless the respirator model is rated for extended use — document such models specifically. | Continuous supply management; document issuance. |
| 6.3.6 | Risk Manager | Evaluate respirator use performance at least annually: review fit test records, supply consumption, any incidents related to respiratory exposure, and employee feedback. Update respiratory protection program annually. | Annually. |

### 6.4 TB-Specific Controls

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.4.1 | Director of Nursing / HR | Ensure TB screening of all newly hired employees per HR-WM-003 (Employee Health & Immunization Requirements). A 2-step tuberculin skin test (TST) or IGRA blood test documents baseline TB status. | At hire; before direct patient contact. |
| 6.4.2 | Director of Nursing | Assess each patient's TB risk during intake using the standard TB Risk Assessment tool (Appendix C). Patients with active or suspected TB shall be so flagged in the care management system. | At intake; update with clinical changes. |
| 6.4.3 | Field Clinical Staff | For patients with known or suspected active TB: (a) confirm fit-tested N95 before entry; (b) ensure patient wears surgical mask if tolerated; (c) open windows if available and safe; (d) minimize time in confined spaces; (e) document ATD precautions taken in visit note. | Each visit for TB-flagged patients. |
| 6.4.4 | Director of Nursing | If a field employee reports potential exposure to a patient later confirmed to have active, infectious pulmonary TB: initiate ATD exposure investigation per Section 6.5. Notify Cal/OSHA if a work-related TB infection is identified. | Within 24 hours of notification of confirmed TB source. |

### 6.5 ATD Exposure Incident Response

| Step | Responsible Party | Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| 6.5.1 | Exposed Employee | Report any potential ATD exposure incident to a supervisor and to the Director of Nursing immediately. An ATD exposure incident includes: (a) being in the same room for ≥15 minutes without appropriate respiratory protection with a person later confirmed to have an airborne ATD; (b) any unprotected AGP exposure. | Immediately upon recognition of exposure. |
| 6.5.2 | Director of Nursing / Risk Manager | Initiate an ATD Exposure Investigation within 24 hours: (a) document the date, time, location, and nature of exposure; (b) identify the source patient or individual; (c) determine whether ATD status of exposure source is known or suspect; (d) assess whether the employee's PPE was correctly donned during exposure. | Investigation initiated within 24 hours. |
| 6.5.3 | Risk Manager | Arrange post-exposure medical evaluation at the agency's designated occupational health provider at no cost to the employee. The evaluation must include: (a) assessment of exposure circumstances; (b) testing appropriate to the ATD (e.g., TB test, COVID test); (c) recommendations for prophylaxis where applicable (e.g., measles PEP); (d) work restriction assessment. | Post-exposure evaluation: within 24 hours for high-risk exposures; within 2 business days for moderate-risk. |
| 6.5.4 | Risk Manager | Document the exposure investigation and response on the ATD Exposure Incident Form (Appendix D). File in the employee's medical file (separate from personnel file per CMIA requirements — CO-CA-001, HR-WM-007). | Within 5 business days of incident; retain for duration of employment + 30 years per 8 CCR §5199(h). |
| 6.5.5 | Risk Manager | If the ATD exposure results in a work-related illness, record on the OSHA 300 Log (Form 300) per RM-OS-001 Section 6.5 and 8 CCR §14300. Report to Cal/OSHA if hospitalization or death results. | Recording: within 7 days of determination. Reporting: per RM-OS-001 thresholds. |
| 6.5.6 | Risk Manager / Director of Nursing | Conduct a root cause analysis of each ATD exposure incident (per QA-AE-002) to determine whether an ECP deficiency contributed to the exposure. Update the ECP or training within 30 days of the RCA findings. | RCA completed within 14 days; ECP/training update within 30 days. |

### 6.6 Escalation and Exception Handling

| Condition | Escalation Path | Corrective Action | Timeframe |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Public health emergency declaring a novel ATD | Risk Manager notifies Administrator; monitors Cal/OSHA and CDPH guidance. | Update ECP within 30 days to incorporate novel pathogen-specific guidance. Provide emergent training to all field staff within 7 days of identified new risk. | ECP update: 30 days. Staff training: 7 days. |
| Employee refuses to wear N95 on ATD-flagged visit | Clinical Manager and Risk Manager notified. | Do not assign employee to that visit. Conduct counseling and retraining. HR-ER-002 discipline for refusal. Document. | Same day. Retraining within 5 business days. |
| N95 supply chain shortage | Risk Manager and Administrator escalate immediately. | Secure alternative NIOSH-approved respirator supply. Use hierarchy: N95 → KN95 (emergency use only with documentation of supply shortage) → proceed only with administrative controls if no respirator available and defer visit if clinical risk is tolerable. | Contingency plan activated within 24 hours of shortage identification. |
| Annual respirator fit test not completed for an employee | Risk Manager notifies employee and supervisor. | Employee may not perform ATD-risk visits until re-fit tested. Schedule within 14 days. | Re-fit test within 14 days of missed deadline. |

## 8. Compliance & Audit Considerations

### 8.1 How Compliance Is Measured

| Compliance Indicator | Measurement Method | Acceptable Standard |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Written ATD ECP exists and is current | Annual review against 8 CCR §5199 checklist | All required elements present; reviewed within 12 months |
| Exposure Determination completed and current | Review of Appendix A | All clinical job classifications assessed; reviewed annually |
| All N95-assigned employees medically cleared | Review of PLHCP clearance forms | 100% clearance documented before initial N95 use |
| All N95-assigned employees fit-tested annually | Review of fit test records (Appendix B) | 100% fit-tested within prior 12 months |
| N95s available at all ATD-flagged visits | Random field supply audit; employee survey | Employees confirm N95 is always available for high-risk visits |
| ATD exposure incidents investigated within timeframe | Review of Appendix D forms | 100% investigated within 24 hours of report |
| ATD-specific training completed | Training completion rate | 100% at orientation; 100% annual within calendar year |

### 8.2 What Surveyors and Auditors Will Look For

Cal/OSHA compliance officers will specifically verify:

1. That the ECP is written, site-specific, and not a generic template. The ATD ECP must reference the agency's specific operations, patient populations, and job classifications.
2. That an Exposure Determination has been completed identifying all at-risk job classifications.
3. Evidence that all N95-assigned employees are medically cleared and have current fit test documentation.
4. That ATD exposure incident records are maintained in the employee's confidential medical file (not personnel file).
5. Evidence of ATD-specific training at orientation and annually.
6. That the ECP is incorporated into the master IIPP (RM-OS-001) as a subprogram.

CMS surveyors examining Appendix B (Home Health CoPs) may cross-check ECP against condition-level Infection Prevention & Control requirements (42 CFR §484.70).

### 8.3 Common Failure Points

| Failure Point | Risk | Mitigation |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| No Exposure Determination or generic "all employees" statement | Citation; does not identify actual exposure tasks | Appendix A specifies exposure by job class and by specific clinical activity |
| Staff using KN95s while claiming OSHA compliance | Cal/OSHA citation — KN95 is NOT NIOSH-approved for occupational use | Agency issues NIOSH-approved N95 only; training distinguishes N95 from KN95 |
| Fit test records not maintained or expired | Cal/OSHA citation; potential liability from exposure | Annual fit testing process tracked by Risk Manager; no ATD-flagged visits permitted for unfit-tested staff |
| ATD exposure records filed in personnel file | CMIA violation (Civil Code §56.20); Cal/OSHA violation | Medical exposure records filed in separate confidential medical file per CO-CA-001 |
| ECP not updated after public health emergency | Program gap for novel pathogens | CO-RA-001 monitoring triggers ECP review within 30 days of declared public health emergency |
