## Scene 8 — Escalation Practice: Two Decisions, One Standard: "The Bruise Alex Almost Didn't Mention" & "What Grace Chen Asked For"

### Story Beat
It's Thursday afternoon, the same week Alex has been shadowing and now taking supervised solo-style visits. Two separate moments test the same underlying skill: knowing the limit of Alex's role and acting inside it without hesitation. First, at Mrs. Linh Chen's home, her daughter Grace Chen — worried and well-meaning — asks Alex to also check her own blood pressure and adjust her mother's insulin dose "since you're already here." It's an off-scope-care request, not a crisis, and it tests a quieter, more common version of boundary-holding: decline kindly, cite the plan of care, and redirect Grace to her own physician. Second, this is the same Mr. Ray Torres visit from Scene 7, continuing a few minutes later as that visit wraps up: while finishing up in Mr. Torres's living room — the same room, the same afternoon, the wound-care refusal already handled — Alex notices new bruising on his forearm while doing a last safety check. Mr. Torres lives with his adult son, who is present in the home. This is the transfer test for the mandatory-reporting protocol Alex learned in Scene 6, now applied live, alone, in the home. Both moments share one throughline: Alex's job has edges, and knowing exactly where they are — what's in scope, what's reportable, what's not Alex's call to make alone — is what "regulatory integrity" actually looks like in practice, not just a phrase from orientation. This scene hooks backward to Scene 6 (same mandatory-reporting protocol, same Dana voice, same exact sentence) and directly to Scene 7's closing bridge line ("Alex is going to notice something on Mr. Torres's arm"), and forward to Scene 9 where Dana debriefs the week and confirms Alex "did it right" on both counts.

### Learner Role
The learner sees the scene through Alex's eyes across two independent decision points and makes the actual calls Alex must make: at Mrs. Chen's, how to respond when asked to step outside the plan of care; and at Mr. Torres's, what to notice, what to write in the field notebook, what to say (and not say) to the patient and his son, and what to do the instant the visit ends. Neither scenario offers a rewarded shortcut — agreeing to off-scope care and investigating/confronting a caregiver both exist as tempting distractors with real consequence feedback, not dead ends that punish exploration.

### Workspace & Visual Concept
**Template: DecisionBoard, two sequential branching scenarios sharing one stage shell.**

**Branch 1 — Mrs. Linh Chen's Living Room (off-scope-care decision):**
A simpler, single-frame decision scene. A static illustration (or lightweight new frame reusing existing home-interior assets) shows Alex standing near Mrs. Chen, seated, with her daughter Grace Chen beside her holding a blood pressure cuff and an insulin pen out toward Alex. No hotspots here — the whole frame is the prompt. A single decision card sits below the image with 2-3 options. This branch is intentionally lighter-weight than Branch 2: one decision, one feedback beat, no observation nodes.

**Branch 2 — Mr. Torres's Living Room, Minutes Later (suspected neglect/abuse decision):**
This is the same room and the same visit from Scene 7 — Mr. Torres's living room, a few minutes after the wound-care refusal, as the visit wraps up and Alex does a final safety check. His adult son, who lives with him, is present. The existing `scene-08-neglect-abuse/v1.png` illustration is used **as this branch's full-frame visual anchor**, not replaced. It already stages exactly what the beat needs: Alex (white coat, chart in hand) standing left, Mr. Torres seated on the couch center-right holding something in his lap with hands close together, and a small cluster of floor objects (cup, dish, framed photo/tablet) near the couch. Regions:

- **Backdrop layer**: the full v1.png image, locked aspect, `object-fit: cover` inside a 16:9 stage that never crops Mr. Torres or Alex.
- **Hotspot overlay layer** (absolutely positioned, percentage-based to survive resize): three discovery nodes pinned to real image content — Mr. Torres's forearm/hands area, the framed photo on the shelf behind him (representing his son's presence in the home), and Alex's field notebook in her own hands.
- **Choice panel**: slides up from the bottom third as a translucent card once a hotspot is tapped.
- **Field Notes drawer**: right-side panel on desktop (≥1024px), becomes a bottom sheet under `lg`. Persists across the whole scene (both branches), accumulating unlocked notes top-to-bottom.
- **Reference Notes ribbon**: thin, collapsible strip pinned under the Field Notes drawer header — citation chips only.

**Shared progress rail**: **5 pips** along the top edge of the stage — pip 1 (Chen decision), then pips 2-4 (Observe → Document → Report for Branch 2), then pip 5 (Report decision resolved). A labeled **header region directly above the pip row** reads the current branch name ("Branch 1: The Off-Scope Request" / "Branch 2: Escalation") and is the anchored location for the safe completion label once the scene resolves — this header region is the grounded UI slot referenced in Interaction Pattern step 6 below; it exists independent of the pips themselves.

No zoom modal is needed for Branch 2's image — the relevant detail (forearm area) is legible at full-bleed size; a light CSS zoom (1.15x, `transform-origin` at hotspot position, respecting `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`) previews on hover/focus before commit-click, then returns to full frame when the choice panel opens. Branch 1 has no zoom behavior — it's a single static frame.

**Continuity note:** Branch 2 is not staged as a new or different household — it is the direct continuation of Scene 7's Torres visit, seconds after that scene's closing bridge line. The header label, Field Notes, and narration all name Mr. Torres explicitly rather than treating him as a new or composite character.

### Interaction Pattern
1. **Scene opens on Branch 1**: the `gao001-s8-node-chen-decision` card is visible immediately — no locked state, no hotspots, since this is a single-frame prompt-and-choose beat. Options: "Check her blood pressure and adjust the insulin dose since you're already there" [tempting, wrong], "Tell Grace you're not able to help with that today" with no explanation [incomplete — partial credit feedback, re-offer], and the correct "Explain kindly that today's visit is scoped to her mother's plan of care, and that Grace's own blood pressure and medication questions need her own physician" [correct].
2. Selecting an option fires immediate feedback (correct: chime + Field Note reveal + node moves to **resolved**, pip 1 fills; incorrect: soft buzz, in-story consequence text explaining the risk of stepping outside plan-of-care scope, option to pick again — max 2 attempts before the correct path is highlighted per pacing rule).
3. Once `gao001-s8-node-chen-decision` resolves, the header region updates to "Branch 2: Escalation," the stage transitions to the v1.png backdrop, and the 3 observation hotspots (observe-bruise, caregiver-presence, chart-documentation) become visibly affordanced with a soft pulsing ring icon (static ring if `prefers-reduced-motion`). The report-decision node is hidden until prerequisites clear.
4. **Discovered state**: tapping a hotspot opens the choice panel with 2-3 options. Each option is a plausible action, not a random distractor (e.g., for caregiver-presence: "Ask Mr. Torres's son directly what happened" [tempting, wrong] vs. "Note who was present, without questioning them" [correct]). Same feedback/retry rules as step 2.
5. Once all 3 observation nodes are **resolved**, pips 2-4 fill and the **gao001-s8-node-report-decision** card appears below the stage. This is the second graded crux: options include "Call Adult Protective Services yourself right now," "Wait until your next visit to see if it happens again," and the correct "Document your observations, finish the visit professionally, and report to your supervisor immediately afterward." Only the correct option resolves the node; wrong picks show consequence feedback and re-offer the choice (no punitive lockout).
6. **Completion criteria**: `completionRule: { type: 'all_nodes' }` — all 5 nodes resolved across both branches. On the derived `isComplete` boolean flipping true (via `useEffect`, not `setTimeout`), scene-complete narration fires once and the safe completion label **"Escalation Practice Complete"** renders in the **header region above the progress rail** (the same labeled slot used for branch names during the scene — grounded in the Workspace & Visual Concept spec above). `onComplete()` fires exactly once to the shell; no `useLearner()` call originates from this component.
7. State persists per node (`initialState` prop) so Save & Exit mid-scene resumes with prior resolved nodes marked resolved, the correct branch/header shown, and their narration marked "already delivered."

### Field Notes
**The Off-Scope Request — What Alex Didn't Do**
Grace Chen, Mrs. Chen's daughter, asked Alex to check her own blood pressure and adjust her mother's insulin dose while already in the home. Alex said no — kindly, but clearly. Today's visit is scoped to Mrs. Chen's plan of care; anything outside it, including care for another person in the house or medication changes not ordered in that plan, isn't Alex's call to make. Alex pointed Grace to her own physician instead. That's not a brush-off — it's regulatory integrity: staying inside the plan of care under §484.60 protects the patient, Grace, and Alex.

**The Forearm — What Alex Noticed**
A few minutes after Mr. Torres's wound-care refusal, doing a last safety check in the same living room, Alex saw bruising on his forearm that wasn't there at the last visit. That alone is enough to act — reasonable suspicion, not proof, is the legal and ethical trigger for reporting. It's the same pause-observe-don't-assume instinct Alex practiced with Mr. Okafor. Alex does not need to know how it happened before taking the next step.

**The Room, Not the Family — What Alex Chose Not to Do**
Alex noted that Mr. Torres's adult son, who lives with him, was present in the home, without questioning him about the injury. Asking pointed questions or confronting a family member can compromise a later investigation and puts Alex in a role that isn't Alex's to fill — observation and reporting are the job, not detective work.

**Alex's Chart — Writing What Was Seen, Not What Was Guessed**
Alex opened the same field notebook carried since day one and wrote down the location, size, and color of the bruising, the timeline ("not present last visit"), and Mr. Torres's own words — nothing more. A note like "I think his son did this" is an opinion and does not belong in the clinical record; the suspicion gets reported through the proper channel, not written as an accusation. The notebook captures the observation in the moment; Alex transfers those same objective facts into the clinical record afterward — the notebook and the clinical record are not the same document, but they carry the same facts.

**After the Visit — The Call to Dana**
As soon as the visit ended, Alex contacted Dana and relayed the facts. Dana confirmed the next step out loud, the same way she had in orientation: "Follow agency mandatory reporting protocol immediately; do not investigate or confront — your job stops at reporting the facts." Dana added that Compliance would follow up directly with Alex once the report was filed, so Alex would know it had been received and handled — Alex's job ends at reporting, but Alex isn't left wondering what happened after.

### Reference Notes
- Reference: A home health plan of care defines the scope of services for a specific patient. 42 CFR §484.60 — Care Planning, Coordination of Services — governs what is and isn't in scope for a given visit. Informational.
- Reference: Staff may be interviewed by a surveyor about how a concern like this was handled. Reporting duties are informational context, not legal advice.
- Reference: Federal and state law require reporting of suspected abuse, neglect, or exploitation. Your state's Adult Protective Services (APS) framework governs external reporting; Compliance/supervisor initiates it. Informational.
- Reference: 42 CFR §484.50 — Conditions of Participation: Patient Rights. Applies here to the patient's dignity during the encounter, not as a substitute for the reporting duty. Informational.

### Accessibility
- Keyboard path: Tab order follows header region → Chen decision card options → (Branch 2 loads) progress rail → hotspot 1 → hotspot 2 → hotspot 3 → (after resolution) report-decision card → Field Notes drawer → Reference Notes ribbon. Each hotspot and decision option is a real `<button>` with visible focus ring, not a bare click-div.
- ARIA: hotspots use `role="button"` with `aria-label` naming the node (e.g., "Examine Mr. Torres's forearm — locked" / "...— discovered" / "...— resolved"); the Chen decision options use `aria-label` naming the choice text directly; choice panels are `role="dialog"` with `aria-modal="true"`, focus-trapped, closing on Escape only after a choice is made (no dismiss-without-deciding on either graded-crux node — Chen decision and report-decision — since both are graded; Escape on the two observation-only nodes in Branch 2 returns focus to the triggering hotspot without penalty).
- `aria-live="polite"` region announces short state changes only ("Branch 2 unlocked: Escalation" / "Node unlocked: The Forearm") — never the full narration text, which lives in the transcript panel per the accessibility spec.
- Captions/transcript: every `NarrationSegment` with `transcriptFlag: true` renders verbatim in the Field Notes drawer's transcript sub-panel, synced to currently playing segment.
- Color independence: node/branch state (locked/discovered/resolved) is conveyed by icon shape + ring/border fill + text label, never by color alone (ring becomes a filled circle icon on resolve, not just a color shift).
- Reduced motion: all pulsing, scaling, shake, and cross-fade microinteractions degrade to static states or instant opacity swaps; no parallax on either backdrop image.

### QA Risks
- **Two-branch sequencing bug**: if a learner navigates directly (deep link, browser back/forward, Save & Exit resume) to a Branch 2 state without the Chen node marked resolved, the header/progress rail could desync. Mitigation: gate Branch 2 stage rendering strictly on `nodes.chen.resolved === true`; if false, force-render Branch 1 regardless of any other state.
- **Image hotspot drift**: percentage-based hotspot coordinates on the v1.png backdrop can drift off-target if the image is later re-cropped or regenerated at a different aspect ratio. Mitigation: lock the backdrop's aspect ratio container (`aspect-ratio: 16/9`) and treat this specific v1.png file as frozen per the continuity-device rule ("do not re-shoot") — any future art revision requires re-validating all three hotspot percentages against the new file before merge.
- **Compliance wording drift**: the mandated sentence must match `CoreValuesInteractiveViewer.tsx:142` character-for-character. Mitigation: import/reference the string as a shared constant (e.g., `MANDATORY_REPORTING_SENTENCE` exported from a shared compliance-strings module) rather than retyping it in this scene's narration data and Field Notes data — eliminates transcription risk at the source.
- **Completion-gating edge case**: if a learner resolves the 3 Branch 2 observation nodes out of order or revisits a resolved node, the report-decision node must not re-lock or re-hide. Mitigation: gate visibility strictly on `nodes.filter(resolved).length >= 3` within Branch 2, never on order or last-clicked node id.
- **Forbidden-adjacent wording creep**: "Escalation Practice Complete" must not drift toward "Reported" or "Case Closed," which could imply an investigative outcome/verdict was reached; likewise the Chen branch feedback must never drift toward implying formal policy attestation occurred. Mitigation: CI string-check against the approved safe-label list; block any label variant containing "closed," "resolved case," "confirmed," "verdict," "attested," or "acknowledged."
- **Narration/Field Notes redundancy violation**: because the mandated sentence is the one intentional verbatim exception, a reviewer might flag it as a duplication defect. Mitigation: tag this specific pair in the narration data with `mandatedVerbatim: true` so the CI redundancy checker whitelists it instead of failing the build.
- **Tone risk with real subject matter**: any future localization/voice-talent pass could introduce dramatized inflection (music sting, fearful tone) inconsistent with the non-graphic/non-melodramatic mandate for Branch 2, or an overly stern tone for the comparatively low-stakes Chen branch. Mitigation: include a recording-direction note in the narration asset brief ("Branch 1: warm and matter-of-fact; Branch 2: clinical, calm, no dramatic emphasis") alongside the script text delivered to voice production.
