## Scene 2 — Our Mission — What It Demands of You: "The Mission Isn't a Slogan"

### Story Beat — what happens to Alex; 1 short paragraph; continuity hooks to previous/next scene.
It's Monday afternoon. Alex has finished the welcome-desk orientation and now sits in a quiet meeting room with Dana Whitfield, RN, for a mission briefing — a natural next step after the "What You Will Learn" / "Why it matters" framing Alex read at the welcome desk. Dana holds up the phone displaying the mission statement on-screen and reads it aloud once, verbatim, before she puts it on the table between them and walks through it phrase by phrase, translating each one into what Alex will actually do in a patient's home. Alex's field notebook is open on their knee (continuity object from Scene 1), and by the end of the conversation five phrases have become five things Alex can picture doing. This scene closes with a clear operating rule about reporting uncertainty, which sets up the fuller reporting protocol taught later in Scenes 6 and 8.

### Learner Role — what the learner does/decides as/with Alex.
The learner sits in Alex's seat during the briefing. For each of the five mission phrases Dana raises, the learner first opens the phrase to hear the explanation (discovery), then is handed a short field situation and must choose, as Alex, the response that actually matches the mission phrase — not the response that merely sounds nice. Each phrase node ends in a decision with a plausible wrong option, built specifically for Scene 2 to test recognition of the mission phrase at a foundational level; deeper procedural teaching for any topic that reappears in a later scene (for example, refusal handling in Scene 7) is deliberately left lighter here so that scene can own its own first full teaching moment.

### Workspace & Visual Concept — layout for this scene (may be any pattern; name regions and their jobs); how the existing anchor image or a new full-frame SVG is used; zoom/modal behavior.
**Template: DiscoveryScene.** The existing anchor image (`GAO-001/scene-02-mission/v2.png`) is used as a **cropped/zoomed backdrop**, not a full hotspot canvas — it depicts two women in armchairs mid-conversation, which fits the briefing setting. It is displayed full-bleed behind a **translucent "Mission Card" overlay** that docks center-left over the empty wall/window space (avoiding the two figures), so the photo reads as environmental context rather than a hotspot map. ### Interaction Pattern — step-by-step interaction loop incl. states, completion criteria, "Scene Practice Complete" requirements.
1. **Scene mounts** → scene-start narration plays automatically → all 5 chips render in `locked` visual state (dim, pulsing glow affordance per pacing rule against invisible hotspots).
2. Learner taps a chip → chip enters `discovered` state → modal opens, **Phase 1**: Dana's explanation line (node-unlock narration begins playing; transcript renders in modal) plus, for patient-centered and dignity nodes, a Reference Notes citation chip appears.
3. **Phase 2**: modal reveals the field situation with 2-3 response options, one correct.
4. Learner selects → immediate feedback (Phase 3): correct → chime, green confirmation, node becomes `complete`, Field Notes entry locks in on the drawer, progress dot fills; incorrect → shake animation (no lockout), explanatory feedback naming the risk, option to retry immediately (max 2 attempts before a direct hint per pacing rule).
5. Modal closes, learner returns to the phone surface; next chip available (order not graded — all orders permitted).
6. **Completion rule:** `all_nodes` — all 5 phrase nodes reach `complete`. This is tracked as a derived boolean (`allNodesComplete`), computed each render from node state rather than stored separately. A `useEffect` watches `allNodesComplete`; on the transition from `false` to `true`, and guarded by a `hasFiredRef` flag so it cannot re-run, it plays the scene-complete narration once, shows the "Mission Practice Complete" text on the Mission Card overlay, and calls `onComplete()`. No checkbox, no signature line — a single non-attestation confirmation strip. This training completion is separate from, and does not substitute for, any formal P&P acknowledgment workflow.
7. **Resume:** `initialState` restores which chips are already `complete`. Reopening a completed node's chip opens the modal in a distinct **review-only mode**: it renders the Phase 1/2/3 transcript text and the learner's prior choice as read-only content, with a manual "Replay narration" control, and does not re-run the Phase 3 feedback/completion animation, does not touch progress-dot state, and cannot re-trigger the `allNodesComplete` transition (which has already fired and stays fired for the session via `hasFiredRef`). Node-unlock audio does not autoplay for already-complete nodes on scene mount or on review-mode open; autoplay is gated on a `firstUnlockThisSession` flag that is distinct from `isComplete`.

### Field Notes — for each node
**Patient-Centered.** *Alex's takeaway:* Every visit decision starts with what this patient needs right now, not what fits the schedule. If a family member asks for help with something outside the plan of care, the mission-driven answer is a polite decline plus a same-day note to Dana — not improvising in the moment to be agreeable.

**Evidence-Based.** *Alex's takeaway:* Care Indeed's protocols exist so that care doesn't depend on any one clinician's habits from a previous job. When Alex isn't sure whether a new task fits the plan of care, the answer is to check before doing it, not to guess and proceed.

**Promote Independence.** *Alex's takeaway:* The goal of every visit is to help a patient do more for themselves, not to make them comfortable staying dependent. If a patient asks Alex to keep doing something they've regained the ability to do themselves, the mission-aligned response is to coach and encourage the patient to do it, not to keep doing it for them out of habit or convenience.

**Dignity & Quality of Life.** *Alex's takeaway:* A patient's home is not a clinical room — it's their space. Alex knocks, asks before touching anything, and treats an unsafe condition (a loose rug, a blocked exit) as something to document and report the same day, not something to quietly work around.

**Regulatory Integrity.** *Alex's takeaway:* Compliance isn't a separate department's job — it lives inside every note Alex writes. "Close enough" documentation gets corrected before it's submitted, not shrugged off, because the record has to reflect what actually happened.

### Reference Notes — concise citation/surveyor-facing footnotes
- **Patient-Centered:** Reference: 42 CFR §484.60 — Plan of Care. Requires services to be individualized to the patient's assessed needs. Informational, subject to separate validation; supports the "Patient-Centered" mission phrase.
- **Dignity & Quality of Life:** Reference: 42 CFR §484.50 — Patient Rights. Covers dignity, respect, and a patient's right to informed participation in their own care. Informational, subject to separate validation.
- **Regulatory Integrity:** "Report uncertainty promptly" is a Care Indeed operating principle, not a citation number — surveyors may ask any staff member how they'd handle uncertainty about a reportable concern. Full mandatory-reporting protocol language is introduced later in training.

- **§484.50 vs §484.60 mix-up in implementation:** two different citations appear in this single scene (unlike most scenes, which carry one). Mitigation: unit-test/CI string-match each citation to its exact node ID (`patient-centered` → 484.60, `dignity-quality-of-life` → 484.50) so a copy-paste error can't silently swap them; §484.110 must never appear on this scene's Patient-Centered or Dignity nodes.
- **Review-mode ambiguity re-triggering completion effects:** reopening an already-complete node's modal must not replay the Phase 3 correct/incorrect animation, must not re-fill an already-filled progress dot, and must not flip `allNodesComplete` a second time. Mitigation: modal takes an explicit `mode: 'discovery' | 'review'` prop set from node state at open time; `hasFiredRef` and per-node `complete` flags are read-only once set, and CI includes a test that opens and closes a completed node's modal twice with zero additional `onComplete` calls and zero progress-dot re-animations.
