# GAO-001 "A New Journey" — Narrative Design Bible

## 1. ALEX REYES — CHARACTER SHEET

**Background:** Alex Reyes, 34, RN with 7 years acute-care/SNF experience (med-surg, then two years in a skilled nursing facility). Just hired by Care Indeed Home Health. Competent clinician, first home health job ever. Relocated for the role; no prior home health, no prior Care Indeed history — genuinely new to solo home visits, OASIS, and this agency's escalation chain.

**Personality:** Careful, observant, quietly self-critical. Asks good questions but sometimes hesitates before asking them (fear of looking inexperienced). Dry, understated humor under pressure — never flip about patient safety. Takes notes obsessively (ties to field-notebook motif).

**Anxious about:** working without a team down the hall; whether "my gut says something's off" is enough reason to report; documentation being "good enough" vs. defensible; being the one who has to say no to a patient or family member.

**Confident about:** clinical fundamentals (vitals, assessment, wound care mechanics), genuine compassion, prior experience spotting deterioration.

**Growth arc across the week:** Day 1 — oriented but overwhelmed by *how much is different* about home health. Mid-week — starts translating acute-care instincts into home-health judgment (autonomy, documentation-as-witness). Scenarios (Day 4-5) — first real tests of escalation nerve; Alex's instinct to "handle it myself" must yield to "report through the chain, don't investigate." End of week — walks into the post-test not because Alex memorized answers, but because Alex now recognizes situations and knows the correct first move. Arc = **from competent outsider to oriented insider**, not novice-to-expert.

**Supporting cast:**
- **Preceptor/supervisor:** **Dana Whitfield, RN**, Clinical Supervisor, 11 years at Care Indeed. Warm but exacting; the "when in doubt, report" voice. Sends the Day-1 welcome email, appears via messages/voicemail-style check-ins across the week, debriefs scenarios.
- **Scene 4 (Core Values Field Practice) patient:** **Mr. Sam Okafor**, 71, composite CHF/COPD patient, home visit for core-values practice vignettes (dignity, teamwork, excellence, etc.). Fictional composite, not tied to a real record.
- **Scene 7 (bruising/observation scenario) patient:** **Mr. Ray Torres**, 78, composite home health patient, lives with his adult son. (Matches existing content's "Mr. Torres" — retained for continuity/legacy alignment.)
- **Scene 8 (scope-boundary scenario) patient + family:** **Mrs. Linh Chen**, 66, composite PT patient; her daughter **Grace Chen** makes the inappropriate request. (Retained from existing content.)
- **Recurring peer:** **Marcus Odom, PT** — fellow new-ish clinician, occasionally texts/compares notes with Alex (light peer-support texture, not a major plot driver).

## 2. WEEK STRUCTURE — 9 Scenes Mapped to Alex's First Week

| # | Day | Scene Title (existing page mapped) | Story Beat (one line) | Emotional Note |
|---|-----|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mon AM | Welcome Desk (orientation login) | Alex logs in on day one, opens Dana's welcome email, sees the scope of what's ahead | Nervous anticipation, grounded — not dread |
| 2 | Mon PM | Mission Briefing | Dana walks Alex through the mission line-by-line using real field examples | Clarity landing — "oh, this is actually my job description" |
| 3 | Tue AM | Vision Pillars | Alex tours (virtually/via materials) how the four pillars show up operationally before first field day | Growing sense of standard, not slogan |
| 4 | Tue PM | Core Values Field Practice (Mr. Okafor, shadow visit) | Alex shadows a visit with Mr. Okafor and must apply each value in the moment | First real "I have to act, not just read" pressure — earned confidence |
| 5 | Wed AM | Home Health vs. Facility Care | Alex reflects on the shadow visit — realizes how different autonomy/documentation are from SNF life | Honest disorientation resolving into insight |
| 6 | Wed PM | Regulatory Responsibility (+ mandatory reporting protocol intro) | Dana briefs Alex on personnel file, reporting duties, before Alex's first solo-adjacent visits | Weight of responsibility, steadied by clear protocol |
| 7 | Thu | Scenario: Mr. Torres — concerning observation | Alex, now doing supervised solo-style visits, notices new bruising and must decide what to do | Tension, restraint, correct instinct under uncertainty |
| 8 | Thu/Fri | Scenario: Mrs. Chen's daughter's request | Alex faces a boundary-testing ask mid-visit and must hold the scope line | Quiet firmness, no melodrama |
| 9 | Fri | Survey Readiness & Week Wrap | Alex reviews the week with Dana, preps for "a surveyor could ask you anything," heads into post-test | Earned readiness, calm competence |

This preserves the 9-scene/9-page structure and page order 1:1 — only the frame (a real week, real people, real stakes) is new.

## 3. CONTINUITY DEVICES

- **The Badge**: introduced Scene 1 (photo captured at welcome desk). Referenced visually/verbally in Scenes 6 and 9 ("you'll wear this into every home — it's the trust CI has already placed in you"). Do not re-shoot/re-capture it later — it's the same badge photo throughout.
- **The Field Notebook**: Alex's paper/note-taking habit, seeded Scene 1 (visible object at desk). Becomes the literal mechanism for Scenes 7-8 documentation decisions ("Alex opens the notebook and writes down exactly what was observed — no interpretation, just facts"). Notebook entries from Scene 7 can be glimpsed again in Scene 9's week-review beat.
- **Dana's messages**: a consistent channel (email in Scene 1, in-person briefing Scenes 2/3/6, a short check-in voicemail/text before Scenes 7 and 8, debrief in Scene 9). Dana's voice should feel like the same person throughout — same phrasing tics ("when in doubt, report" is Dana's line, used first in Scene 2's mission table and echoed by Dana again in Scene 6 and by Alex internally in Scene 7).
- **Mr. Okafor's shadow visit (Scene 4) primes Scenes 7-8**: the *skills* practiced on Okafor (dignity/autonomy/teamwork/excellence) are the exact skills Alex must apply solo-ish with Torres and Chen. Scene 7/8 narration should explicitly gesture back ("This is the same instinct you practiced with Mr. Okafor — pause, observe, don't assume") — retrieval practice, not new content.
- **Scenario scenes reuse earlier cast for retrieval, not new characters**: Dana (supervisor to report to in Scene 7), the mission/values language from Scenes 2/4 (Scene 8's "regulatory integrity" and scope-of-practice reasoning should echo Scene 2's mission breakdown verbatim-in-spirit, not reintroduce it).
- **Personnel file thread**: Scene 6 introduces the personnel-file checklist; Scene 9 closes the loop ("everything you did this week — the training, the attestation record — lives in that file").

## 4. VOICE & TONE RULES

**Chosen narration voice: close-third on Alex.** ("Alex opens the email..." not "You open the email...") Justification: this is compliance training that must be defensible as instructionally complete and TTS/accessibility-ready; close-third lets narration carry full instructional content (citations, protocol steps) inside a character's experience without forcing the learner into an artificial "you" identity that breaks when instructional density rises. The learner *watches and decides with* Alex via interactive choices, but narration describes Alex, not the learner.

**Dialogue style:** short, workplace-real sentences. Dana speaks like a supervisor, not a textbook — she explains *why*, once, clearly, then moves on. Alex's internal lines are observational, not confessional ("Alex notices the bruising wasn't there last visit" — not "Alex's heart sank"). No exclamation points in instructional dialogue. Patients speak plainly and with dignity — never as props.

**Do:**
- Keep every regulatory citation, protocol step, and enumerated list from source content intact somewhere in narration (per the narration-defect fix mandate).
- Let Dana be the primary conduit for policy/citation language — it's natural for a supervisor to cite chapter and verse.
- Show Alex's competence; the drama is in *adaptation*, not incompetence.
- Use plain, declarative sentences for protocol steps (esp. the mandated reporting sentence — verbatim, unaltered, no paraphrase).

**Don't:**
- No melodrama, no music-sting moments, no "Alex's world stopped."
- No cartoonish villains — Ray Torres's son and Grace Chen are not written as suspicious/adversarial; ambiguity is the point.
- Don't let Alex ever independently investigate, confront, or resolve a reportable concern — Alex escalates, always.
- Don't invent new policy content — only dramatize what's already in the recon inventory.
- Never use forbidden attestation phrasing; only "Scene Practice Complete" / "Mission Practice Complete" / "Core Values Practice Complete" / "Training Module Complete" / "Ready for Post-Test."

## 5. STORY-VS-INSTRUCTION SEAM

- **Scene-start narration**: pure story — orient the learner in time/place/stakes ("It's Tuesday afternoon. Alex is shadowing a visit with Mr. Okafor before taking on visits independently."). No citations here.
- **Transition sentence (mandatory bridge)**: one sentence that pivots from story to instruction using Dana or Alex's own reasoning as the vehicle — never a hard cut. E.g., "Dana had told Alex exactly what to listen for in a moment like this: [instructional content follows in Dana's voice or Alex's recalled reasoning]." This is the seam — instruction is always *attributed* to a character's knowledge or speech, never dropped in as disembodied narrator lecture.
- **Node-unlocked narration**: instructional content delivered in-character (Dana explaining, Alex recalling training, a Field Notes entry Alex is writing) — full citation/protocol detail lives here, per the redundancy-distribution rule (scene = experience, Field Notes = what-was-unlocked, Reference Notes = citations, narration = story + full coverage).
- **Feedback narration (right/wrong choice)**: stay in scene — Dana or Alex's own reflection delivers the correction, not a disembodied "Incorrect." Wrong choices should feel like a realistic near-miss Alex catches or is caught on, not a game-show buzzer.
- **Scene-complete narration**: closes the story beat first (what Alex just did/learned in-world), then states the safe completion label as a separate, clearly instructional/system line — never blend "Alex feels ready" with "Scene Practice Complete" in the same sentence; keep the systemic completion statement visually/verbally distinct from the story's emotional beat.
- **Hard rule for Scenes 6 and 8**: the mandated reporting sentence must appear verbatim, and it should be delivered as something Dana says or Alex recalls Dana saying — never paraphrased even slightly, never split across two lines with story text in between.
