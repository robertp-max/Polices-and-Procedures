# GAO-001 Learning Framework Specification

## 1. LEARNING OBJECTIVES (per scene)

**Scene 1 — Welcome/Orientation Desk**
- Identify Care Indeed's Medicare-certified, ACHC-accredited status and its implication for personal accountability
- State the module's 7 coverage areas (mission, vision, values, home-health differences, documentation, escalation, survey readiness)
- Recognize that module completion documents competency orientation in the personnel file (not policy attestation)

**Scene 2 — Mission Field Practice**
- Translate each of 5 mission phrases (patient-centered, evidence-based, promote independence, dignity/quality of life, regulatory integrity) into a field action
- Apply the mission to 5 situational triggers (refusal, out-of-scope request, unsafe condition, incomplete documentation, uncertainty) and select the compliant response
- Cite 42 CFR §484.60 correctly as the basis for individualized, patient-centered care

**Scene 3 — Vision Pillars**
- Match each of 4 vision pillars (Clinical Excellence, Workforce Growth, Regulatory Leadership, Community Trust) to its concrete daily behavior
- Explain why "survey-ready every single day" — documentation complete today, not later; training current today, not next week; a surveyor could walk in tomorrow — defines Regulatory Leadership, not periodic catch-up

**Scene 4 — Core Values Field Practice** (existing bar)
- Select the correct core value for 6 field vignettes (Integrity, Compassion, Excellence, Teamwork, Accountability, Compliance)
- Choose the compliant action once the value is identified
- Recall the PT→RN→MSW teamwork coordination example as a concrete interdisciplinary pattern

**Scene 5 — Home Health vs. Facility Care (Split Compare)**
- Contrast facility-based and home-health practice across 5 dimensions (guest-in-home, autonomy, documentation-as-defense, environment-as-workplace, coordination effort)
- Justify why documentation is the "sole record" in home health, unlike facility care

**Scene 6 — Mandatory Reporting Briefing (Dana Whitfield)**
- Enumerate the regulatory duties Dana briefs Alex on (4 regulatory-duty cards: oversight exposure, personnel file completeness, mandatory reporting, competency maintenance)
- Apply the exact reporting protocol sequence, in order: observe, document objective facts, report immediately, supervisor/Compliance assists, external reporting must not be delayed, do not investigate or confront
- Recite the exact mandated-reporting sentence as Dana states it, verbatim

**Scene 7 — Mr. Ray Torres Refusal Scenario (sequential decision nodes)**
- Execute the refusal pathway in order: respect the refusal while assuming capacity, explain the risk clearly, notify appropriately, document the response, continue the visit safely
- Tie the refusal pathway to 42 CFR §484.50 (Patient Rights) as its regulatory basis
- Recognize that respecting refusal is not the same as disengaging — safe continuation of care is still required

**Scene 8 — Two-Branch Scenario (Chen off-scope request / Torres bruising continuation)**
- Branch 1 (Mrs. Linh Chen and daughter Grace Chen): decline an off-scope request, citing plan-of-care scope under 42 CFR §484.60, and refer the family to Mr. Chen's own physician
- Branch 2 (Mr. Ray Torres, same visit, minutes later — bruising observed): apply mandatory reporting to suspected neglect — document objective facts only, report immediately, understand that reasonable suspicion requires no proof before reporting
- Apply the exact mandatory-reporting sentence a second time in a new context (spaced re-exposure, not duplication), consistent with Scene 6

**Scene 9 — Journey Map / Survey Readiness Review**
- Recall all 5 sample surveyor questions and the "answer honestly, defer to supervisor if unsure" rule
- State the remediation protocol (80% pass, 3-business-day retake window, 3-attempt cap before escalation) verbatim
- Demonstrate readiness for post-test via cumulative review of nodes unlocked in Scenes 2–8, including a final retrieval pass over the module's recurring props (Alex's ID badge, field notebook) as recap cues

**Note on Scene 7 (source page 7) knowledge checkpoints:** the original page-7 gauntlet of 3 ungraded checkpoints is not a standalone scene. Its retrieval-practice function is distributed across the module: a mission-recall retrieval moment lands in Scene 2, a "notice something concerning" retrieval moment lands in Scene 5, and a cumulative retrieval pass lands in Scene 9. Scene 7 itself is the Torres refusal decision scenario (see above) — it carries no ungraded-checkpoint content.

## 3. INTERACTION-LEARNING FIT

| Scene | Pattern | Learning type | Why it fits | Feedback design |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Discovery (desk objects) | Recognition | Low-stakes orientation; objects = ambient facts, not judgment calls | Immediate, descriptive only — no right/wrong, just "explored" confirmation chime |
| 2 | Decision board (5 mission phrases → 5 field responses) | Judgment | Mission phrases are abstract; pairing with concrete field triggers forces application, not memorization | Immediate + explanatory: wrong pick shows why the tempting answer fails, then reveals correct link |
| 3 | Pillars (4-panel match) | Recognition→Judgment hybrid | Pillars are categorical; matching behavior-to-pillar tests classification before scenario judgment starts in Scene 4+ | Immediate; incorrect match shakes and re-prompts, does not advance until resolved (non-punitive retry) |
| 4 | Values hotspots (existing bar) | Judgment (2-phase: identify value → pick correct action) | Two-phase gate mirrors real decision-making: name the principle, then act on it | Shake + replay on wrong; chime + advance on right; never locks learner out |
| 5 | Split compare (facility vs. home health) | Recognition/contrast | Side-by-side framing is the natural shape for "how is X different from Y" content | Toggle-reveal per dimension; no scoring — this scene is conceptual grounding, not a decision test |
| 6 | Flow sequence (Dana Whitfield briefs Alex: 4 regulatory-duty cards sort + observe → document → report, NOT investigate/confront) | Procedure | Mandatory reporting is a strict ordered protocol; sequencing interaction enforces order-sensitivity that multiple-choice can't | Explanatory feedback on any out-of-order or skipped step; must show the exact mandated sentence as the terminal correct-path confirmation |
| 7 | Decision board, strictly sequential nodes (Mr. Ray Torres refusal scenario) | Judgment (applied, procedural) | Refusal handling is itself an ordered pathway (respect → explain risk → notify → document → continue safely); sequential nodes enforce that order while still requiring judgment at each step | Explanatory feedback tied to *why* skipping or reordering a step creates risk; non-punitive retry, returns to the step in question |
| 8 | Branching x2 (Branch 1: Mrs. Linh Chen and daughter Grace Chen off-scope request; Branch 2: same-visit Torres bruising continuation) | Judgment (applied, multi-option) | Full narrative scenarios need branching, not single-select cards, to expose why *tempting* wrong paths carry risk | Non-punitive: each wrong branch leads to a consequence explanation, then returns to the decision point (does not dead-end or penalize) |
| 9 | Journey map (cumulative review) | Consolidation/metacognition | Map view lets learner see the whole first week at once — ideal shape for "you are now ready" summative framing | No new judgment tests; nodes shown are read-only recaps linking back to where each fact was earned |

**Cross-cutting rule:** wrong answers must always explain *why* the wrong choice is tempting and *what risk it creates* — never just "incorrect, try again." This is the non-punitive/explanatory standard for all judgment-type scenes (2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 8).

**Rules against pixel-hunting:**
- Every interactive object must carry a persistent visible affordance (glow, icon badge, subtle motion) — no invisible hotspots; GAO001Scene01's undiscoverable non-badge objects are the anti-pattern to avoid.
- Cap concurrent undiscovered nodes on screen at 6; if a scene has more content, split into sub-stages (e.g., Scene 4's 6 values already at the cap).
- Provide an optional "guided sequence" affordance (numbered order suggestion or first-available-node highlight) for learners who freeze — discovery order is never graded, only completion is.
- No scene may require more than 2 failed attempts before surfacing a direct hint (avoid infinite trial-and-error loops).
- Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for all ambient/idle animation per the recon gap — required, not optional, for the 9 new scenes.

## 5. RETENTION DEVICES

- **Scene 6 → Scene 8 callback**: the flow-sequence protocol learned in Scene 6 must be the exact procedure the learner applies in Scene 8's Torres bruising branch — Scene 8 does not re-teach it, it tests transfer. Field Notes in Scene 8 may reference "recall the reporting sequence from your first week" rather than restate steps.
- **Mission terms resurface in Scenarios**: Scene 8's decision feedback must name the specific mission phrase or value that the correct action embodies (e.g., Chen scenario feedback cites "regulatory integrity" and §484.60), reinforcing Scene 2/4 vocabulary in a new applied context.
- **Scene 9 as cumulative retrieval, not new content**: the journey map presents prior nodes as retrieval cues (learner recalls what was behind each map marker) rather than re-displaying full text — this is deliberate spaced retrieval, not review-by-rereading.
- **Judgment-over-recall principle**: every scenario (Scenes 2, 6, 7, 8) must present the correct action alongside at least one *plausible* wrong action that requires applying a principle to distinguish — never a recall-only "what did the text say" question dressed as a scenario. Retrieval-under-light-pressure moments (distributed into Scenes 2, 5, and 9) remain deliberately recall-based, ungraded, and low-stakes, distinct from Scene 7 and Scene 8's judgment-based, higher-stakes narrative scenarios.

## 6. REDUNDANCY-AS-PEDAGOGY RULES

**Harmful duplication (forbidden):** identical sentence/phrase appearing verbatim in scene UI copy + Field Notes + Reference Notes + narration for the same node. Example anti-pattern already in codebase: GAO001Scene01's "ALEX / RN • NEW HIRE" badge text duplicated verbatim in two components — same fact, same wording, two places, drifts silently. Any writer finding the same sentence in two of {scene surface, Field Notes, Reference Notes, narration} for one node must cut one.

**Useful spaced re-exposure (required):** the same *concept* re-earned through a different representation or context:
- Scene 2 table row "when in doubt, report" (compact rule) → Scene 6 flow sequence (procedural depth) → Scene 8 branch (applied judgment) → Scene 9 map (retrieval cue). Four touches, four different cognitive demands, zero duplicated sentences.
- The mandated reporting sentence is the sole exception: it is compliance-mandated to appear **verbatim** in both Scene 6 and Scene 8's narration + Field Notes per project guardrail #5 — this is intentional legal-precision redundancy, not a violation, and must not be paraphrased to "avoid duplication."

**Assignment rule for writers (per project's redundancy-distribution model):**
- Scene surface = experience (the decision/discovery itself, minimal text)
- Field Notes = explains what was just unlocked, in new phrasing
- Reference Notes = citation/surveyor-facing line only (§484.50, §484.60, §484.110, ACHC refs) — never restates Field Notes prose
- Narration = the only tier carrying full instructional completeness (90–100% concept coverage); it is allowed to restate a concept across tiers because it is the accessibility/TTS/training-record backbone, not a duplicate of on-screen text
